The user discarded a fully-drafted opening Prose (Varys/Valia intimate dawn scene, single
POV, 4,323 words) for four stated reasons:

1. **No world-building** — no sense of kingdoms, cities, or the wider world.
2. **Did not read like a cultivation-technology novel** — technically correct but
   thematically flat, no impact or feeling.
3. **Too slow-paced.**
4. **The story needs to establish where it begins and give the audience context** before or
   alongside the character-level scene.

**Why:** Two Canon changes were made in direct response — the v4.34 Pacing Law Amendment
(which cut the word bands and imposed a 3000-word absolute hard cap, i.e. the rejected draft
was ~1,300 words over the new wall) and the new Tier 0 thematic skill
`ssc-story-control-narration-novel-thematic-law`. The complaint is therefore not a matter of
taste — it has been written into Canon and will be audited.

**How to apply:** When planning any unit, three things are load-bearing rather than nice to
have. (a) Plan a deliberate world-building beat with actual scale — do not default an
opening to a closed-room two-hander. (b) Plan an explicit progression-axis beat per unit
(P30-14): cultivation, technology, named-move, training, or dual-track integration. A quiet
scene still needs one; "characters talk feelingly at dawn" is what got rejected. (c) Budget
beats against the word band up front and tell the Prose Maker that if a beat will not fit,
the fix is to cut the beat or split the unit — never to exceed the cap. Related:
[[prose-1-structural-decisions]].
