## Area 11 — Consequence Resolution Pathways

### 11.1 — Active Repair

Where the consequences of an action produced material, relational, or systemic damage that is repairable, active repair is the most direct resolution pathway:

**Conditions for Active Repair:**
- The damaged party is alive and reachable
- The damage, while severe, is recoverable with sufficient investment
- The repairing party has the resources and capability to provide what repair requires
- The damaged party is willing to receive repair (some damages create enmity that refuses repair)

**The Repair Process:**
Repair is not a single action — it is a sustained process proportional to the CSS of the original consequence. A CSS-3 relational breach may be repaired by a sincere acknowledgment and a kept commitment. A CSS-6 betrayal may require years of demonstrated changed behavior before the relationship can be partially rebuilt. CSS-8+ consequences may not be fully repairable within a single character's lifetime — they may initiate repair processes that must be continued by those who come after.

### 11.2 — Atonement

When direct repair is impossible — because the damaged party is dead, the damage is permanent, or the resource requirements for repair are unavailable — atonement is the resolution pathway. Atonement does not restore what was destroyed. It changes the character's relationship to what they did in a way that allows continued meaningful function:

**Atonement Mechanisms:**
- *Prevention:* Dedicating cultivation and capability to preventing the same harm from being inflicted by others
- *Testimony:* Carrying and transmitting the truth of what happened, ensuring it is not forgotten or distorted
- *Redirection:* Redirecting resources, capability, and influence toward the people who bear the consequences of what was done
- *Witness:* Simply remaining present to what was done without fleeing, numbing, or destroying the self — continuing to live in full awareness of the consequence

**The Limit of Atonement:**
Atonement does not eliminate the original consequence. The dead are still dead. The destroyed sect is still gone. Atonement changes what the actor does in response; it does not change what was done. This distinction must be maintained in narrative: atonement is genuine and significant, but it is not a consequence-erasing reset.

### 11.3 — Acceptance of Irreversibility

For certain CSS-7+ consequences, the resolution pathway is acceptance of what cannot be changed — not resignation or defeat, but genuine integration of the irreversible fact into the character's ongoing existence:

**What Acceptance Requires:**
- Full acknowledgment of the fact (no minimization, rationalization, or dissociation)
- Release of the demand that things be different (not emotional acceptance but cognitive clarity: "This is what is true")
- The capacity to continue existing and acting meaningfully despite the irreversible fact

**What Acceptance Does NOT Require:**
- Approval of what happened
- Absence of grief (grief for irreversible loss is appropriate and ongoing)
- Forgetting
- Forgiveness of those who caused the irreversible consequence (acceptance is the character's relationship to what is, not a judgment on who caused it)

### 11.4 — Exile and Displacement as Consequence Resolution

Some consequences cannot be resolved in place — the damaged system (relationship, faction, location) no longer has room for the actor. The only resolution pathway available is departure:

**Voluntary Exile:**
The character recognizes that their continued presence perpetuates harm — to those they have wronged, to the systems they have damaged, or to their own continued engagement with the consequences they cannot yet resolve in place. They leave. This is not escape (they carry all unresolved consequences with them) but it is sometimes the most honest available response.

**Forced Exile:**
The systemic consequence of their actions is formal expulsion — from sect, from faction, from civilization, from the cultivation world. This is a CSS-4 to CSS-7 consequence event in itself. The expelled character must now exist without the social infrastructure they depended on, which typically intensifies all other consequence cascades simultaneously.
