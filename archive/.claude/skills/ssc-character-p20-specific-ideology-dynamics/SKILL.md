## Area 5 — Specific Ideology Dynamics

### 5.1 — The Ideology of Good Characters

"Good" in the Starward Saga is not a single ideology — it is a quality that can be achieved through multiple ideological frameworks. Good character behavior is characterized by:
- Genuine consideration for the wellbeing of others (not just their usefulness)
- Willingness to be accountable for harm caused
- Operating from a moral orientation that includes others in its circle of concern
- The capacity to restrain their own power in recognition of its cost

JCI, PCI, LBI (Principled variant), and TSI (with sufficient expansiveness of the transcendent goal) can all generate genuinely good characters. The same categories can produce terrible characters under different context conditions.

**Rule 20.5.1.1 — Good Is Not Simple:** Good characters in the Starward Saga are not morally uncomplicated. A genuinely good character has ideological blind spots, has caused harm, has rationalized actions that their ideology did not fully support. The difference between good and evil characters in this series is not purity of record but quality of orientation and willingness to account.

### 5.2 — The Ideology of Evil Characters

"Evil" in the Starward Saga emerges when an ideological framework achieves the following specific configuration:
- Others outside the character's circle of concern are without moral status — they do not count
- The character's goals justify any means required to achieve them — consequence to others is irrelevant
- The character has no genuine accountability mechanism — they neither acknowledge harm nor allow its acknowledgment to affect their behavior

SDI (Pure-Force), ADI (Dominion variant), VAI (Collective), and OMI (Cultivation-Hierarchy in its most exploitative expression) most frequently produce the ideological substrate for genuinely evil behavior.

**Rule 20.5.2.1 — Evil Characters Believe Their Ideology Is Right:** The SSC Canon does not permit cartoonishly self-aware evil. An evil character does not experience themselves as evil — they experience their ideology as correct and their actions as justified. The narrative renders their internal coherence while making the cost to others visible.

**Rule 20.5.2.2 — Evil Has a Formative History:** Every evil character has an Ideology Ledger that traces how they arrived at their current ideological position. That position was not inevitable. There were pivot points at which a different ideological event could have produced a different outcome. The presence of those pivot points is not an excuse — it is a complexity that makes the evil character fully human.

### 5.3 — Neutral Characters and Moral Ambiguity

Neutral characters are not ideologically empty — they hold ideologies that produce outcomes across the moral spectrum depending on context. Common neutral ideological profiles:

- **Pragmatic-Stability OMI**: produces order that can be just or unjust depending on what order is being maintained
- **Network-Power SDI**: produces effective strategic actors who support or undermine larger causes based entirely on self-interest calculation
- **Code-Loyalty LBI**: produces honor-bound characters whose honor code may or may not align with wider moral frameworks
- **Empirical-TRI**: produces truth-seekers whose discoveries may help or harm depending on what is done with them

**Rule 20.5.3.1 — Neutral Characters Are Ideologically Active:** Neutral does not mean passive or undecided. A neutral character has a clear ideology — that ideology simply does not produce consistently good or consistently evil outcomes. The moral weight of a neutral character's actions is context-dependent, not ideologically neutral.

### 5.4 — The Ideology of Holy and Sacred Characters

The SSC Canon uses "holy" and "sacred" as ideological orientations rather than metaphysical states. A holy character is one whose ideology and behavior are aligned with a principle recognized as transcendently valuable — and whose alignment is genuine rather than performed.

Holy character ideological profiles frequently combine:
- SCI (Sacred-Covenant) at Polarity-4 or 5 — genuine covenant relationship
- JCI (Justice-Centered, Cosmic variant) — alignment with a cosmic moral order
- TSI (Transcendence, Enlightenment variant) — a transcendent goal that encompass the wellbeing of others

**Rule 20.5.4.1 — Holy Is Earned Through Consistency:** Holiness as a narrative quality is demonstrated through ideological consistency under pressure. A character who proclaims holy values and maintains them when those values cost them everything has established genuine holiness. A character who proclaims holy values and abandons them under pressure has established performed holiness — a form of Surface ideology fraud.

### 5.5 — Lying, Cheating, and Stealing as Ideological Expression

Characters who lie, cheat, and steal are not necessarily evil — they are expressing specific ideological frameworks in which honesty, rule-following, and property rights either have no status or have reduced status under specific conditions.

**Ideological Roots of Deception:**

| Behavior | Most Common Ideological Roots | Potential Justification Within Framework |
|----------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Systematic lying** | SDI (Network-Power), ADI (Status-Advancement), SCI (Destiny-Covenant where truth is subordinate to covenant fulfillment) | "Truth is a tool; its value is situational" |
| **Tactical deception** | JCI (Retributive, Structural), PCI (any variant), LBI (Institutional, Code) | "Deception of the enemy to protect the innocent is not lying — it is strategy" |
| **Cheating systems** | FSI (Anarchic-Political), TRI (Historical — the system's rules are false), JCI (Structural — unjust rules have no legitimacy) | "The rules are designed to maintain unjust outcomes; refusing them is correct" |
| **Theft** | PCI (survival of protection domain justifies it), VAI (taking from the enemy is restitution), HLI (experience-maximizing) | "What belongs to my enemy can belong to me" |

**Rule 20.5.5.1 — Lying As Character Revelation:** A character who lies does not simply lie — the way they lie, what they lie about, and what they will not lie about reveals their Core ideology more reliably than their honest speech. The lies that feel natural to a character are expressions of their deepest beliefs.

### 5.6 — Ideological Hypocrisy

Ideological hypocrisy — the gap between professed ideology and enacted ideology — is one of the most narratively productive character structures in the series.

**Types of Ideological Hypocrisy:**

| Type | Description | Narrative Potential |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Innocent Hypocrisy** | Character genuinely does not know the gap exists; Surface and Core are authentically disconnected | Revelation scenes; crisis when gap is exposed |
| **Convenient Hypocrisy** | Character knows the gap but rationalizes it case by case | The escalating rationalization arc; the moment the rationalization fails |
| **Structural Hypocrisy** | The ideology itself is internally contradictory — the character cannot be consistent | Philosophical crisis; the ideology must evolve or collapse |
| **Strategic Hypocrisy** | The Surface ideology is consciously performed while Core ideology differs — this is strategic positioning | The long con; the deep-cover character |
| **Recovering Hypocrisy** | The character has identified the gap and is working to close it — either by changing behavior or by genuinely updating the ideology | The redemption arc; the honest reckoning |
