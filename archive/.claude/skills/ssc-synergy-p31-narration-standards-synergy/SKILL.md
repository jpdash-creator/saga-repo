## Area 17 — Narration Standards for Synergy

### 17.1 — Rendering Synergistic Effects in Prose

**Authors depicting synergy should:**

- **Establish both tracks before depicting synergy:** Readers should understand the civilization's cultivation tier and technology tier before synergy effects are described. Synergy is more powerful when the reader knows it is bringing together two independently established capabilities.
- **Use the appropriate integration model vocabulary:** Model A (parallel co-operation), Model B (augmentation — cultivation powering/enhancing technology), Model C (symbiotic — co-designed architecture), Model D (unified — indistinguishable substrate). These terms carry specific meaning per this Part and should be used consistently.
- **Distinguish synergistic output from additive output:** At Model C and D, synergy is not the sum of the two tracks. Prose should reflect that the combined output is qualitatively different from what either track produces — not described as "cultivation force PLUS technology force" but as "something neither produced alone."
- **Reference the three pillars:** Synergy scenes gain world-building depth from establishing the civilization tier, CPM grade available, and location context that make the synergy possible. A Tier 8 CI Technology system drawing on Core CPM inside a natural Sacred Ground is a specific, grounded, world-law-consistent synergy event — more satisfying than an uncontextualized synergy claim.

---

### 17.2 — Model Identification in Prose

**Model A prose signature:**
- Both tracks are visibly separate in action — cultivators doing cultivation things, technology doing technology things, no energy connection between them
- The combined effect is the sum of independent contributions: the cultivator attacked; simultaneously the weapon system fired
- *Correct: "Wei Lin's sword qi swept the left flank while the orbital strike landed on the right."*
- *Incorrect for Model A: "Wei Lin's sword qi fed the orbital strike's guidance system."*

**Model B prose signature:**
- CPM visibly powering or enhancing technology — cultivation energy flowing into technology systems; technology responding to cultivation state
- *Correct for Model B: "She pushed a fragment of Power Crystal into the receiver slot; the formation circuit blazed to life, its output now doubled by the cultivation energy feed."*

**Model C prose signature:**
- Architecture integration — the technology and cultivation components are not separable; the system cannot function if either is removed
- *Correct for Model C: "The suit had been built around him, literally — its energy channels were threaded through the same meridian paths he had spent thirty years cultivating; to remove one was to dismantle the other."*

**Model D prose signature:**
- Indistinguishable energy substrate — the reader cannot tell where cultivation ends and technology begins; they are one system
- *Correct for Model D: "Whether it was qi or current that flowed through him in that moment was a question without an answer. The distinction had dissolved six years ago, somewhere in the third calibration. What flowed now was simply power — his."*

---

### 17.3 — Integrated Practitioner Narration Standards

**Establishing Integrated Practitioner Identity:**
When an Integrated Practitioner first appears, their dual nature should be established: readers should understand they are not merely a cultivator with gadgets, nor merely a technologist who happens to cultivate. Their cultivation architecture and technology system are co-constitutive.

**The Practitioner's Perspective:**
Integrated Practitioners experience the synergy from inside — they feel both the cultivation energy flows and the technology system's operational state simultaneously. Prose from their perspective can render this dual perception: the cultivator sense of qi flowing, and the technology operator's awareness of system status, as a single unified experiential state at Model D.

---

### 17.4 — Non-Combat Synergy in World-Building Prose

Non-combat synergy is as important to world-building texture as combat synergy. Authors should:

- Depict technology-enhanced cultivation chambers as part of urban environments in integrated civilizations — they are as common as hospitals or schools
- Show CPM trade as an economic activity with prices, supply chains, and commercial relationships
- Include formation-array-integrated buildings as architectural features of high-integration-tier civilizations
- Depict Integrated Practitioners in non-combat professional roles — research, infrastructure management, medical integration, cultivation education
- Show the social tension of the Equalizer Effect through non-combat scenes — the cultivator who realizes a non-cultivator Integrated Practitioner operates more effectively in a professional context than they do
