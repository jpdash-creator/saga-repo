## Area 13 — Soft Correspondence Tendency Table

### 13.1 — The Tendency Table and Its Interpretation

The Soft Correspondence Tendency Table describes observed co-occurrence patterns between cultivation realm and technology tier across many civilizations in the Starward Saga world. It is a world-building reference and statistical description — not a governance rule, a requirement, or a restriction.

**What the Table Is:**
- A description of what typically co-occurs in civilizations that have developed both tracks under broadly similar conditions
- A world-building calibration tool for authors assessing whether a civilization's cultivation-technology profile is typical or exceptional
- A reference for narrative framing — civilizations within the tendency range require no special explanation; civilizations outside the range have exceptional histories that are narratively significant

**What the Table Is Not:**
- Not a requirement: a civilization at Technology Tier X is not required to have cultivators at Cultivation Level Y
- Not a guarantee: a civilization with cultivators at Cultivation Level Y will not automatically have Technology Tier X
- Not a ceiling: civilizations cannot be prevented from having technology tiers higher than the range suggests
- Not a floor: civilizations cannot be prevented from having cultivation realms higher than the range suggests

**The Tendency Range:** The ranges in the table are intentionally wide. A civilization at the lower end of its tendency range has systematically deprioritized one track. A civilization at the upper end has systematically invested in one track ahead of the other. Both are valid.

---

### 13.2 — The Full Soft Correspondence Tendency Table

| Cultivation Realm | Typical Technology Tier Range | Notes on Tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Mortal Realm (all stages) | Tier 1–5 | Mortal-realm civilizations span from primitive settlement to early interplanetary; wide range because Mortal cultivation provides no systematic energy or power advantages that drive technology above Tier 5 |
| Xiantian Realm (all stages) | Tier 3–7 | Xiantian cultivation energy begins intersecting systematically with technology; CPM access at scale; early integration models; wide range because Xiantian civilizations vary enormously in technology investment |
| Core Formation Realm | Tier 5–9 | Core Formation civilizations tend to be multi-planetary to multi-stellar; Core energy density drives CPM extraction that enables advanced integration |
| Core Realm (all stages) | Tier 6–11 | Established Core civilizations may span from inner solar to galactic; wide range because Core cultivation has broad power utility and civilizational motivations for technology investment vary |
| Divine Realm (lower stages) | Tier 9–13 | Lower Divine cultivation often correlates with multi-stellar to multi-galactic civilization scale; lower Divine cultivators motivate galactic-scale technology deployment |
| Divine Realm (upper stages) | Tier 11–16 | Upper Divine and peak Divine correlates with galactic to universal civilization scales |
| Post-Realm Ceiling / Phase Break | Tier 14–18 | Trans-galactic to multi-universal civilization scales; post-breakthrough cultivation corresponds to technology operating beyond conventional cosmic scales |
| Absolute Cultivation | Tier 19–20 | Dimensional and absolute cultivation corresponds to the dimensional and absolute technology scales |

**Important Interpretation Note:** The ranges above are intentionally wide. A Xiantian-realm cultivation civilization at Tier 7 is entirely consistent with these tendencies. A Core-realm cultivation civilization at Tier 4 is also consistent — it has deprioritized technology in favor of cultivation. Authors should treat any civilization within 3 tiers of its cultivation correspondence range as requiring no special explanation. Civilizations more than 3 tiers outside the range in either direction represent exceptional development histories that are narratively significant — and those exceptional cases are often the most interesting narrative environments.

---

### 13.3 — Civilization Type and Tendency Deviation

**Cultivation-Predominant Civilizations (Below Tendency Range in Technology):**
Cultivation-predominant civilizations solve problems through cultivation alternatives. They face energy shortages by developing more efficient CPM extraction, not necessarily fusion reactors. Their Technology Tier tends toward the lower end of the tendency range or below it. Their total effective power (cultivation + limited technology) may still exceed a pure-technology civilization of comparable scale when cultivation power is very high.

**Technology-Predominant Civilizations (Below Tendency Range in Cultivation):**
Technology-predominant civilizations must solve all problems through technological means. Their overall Technology Ceiling per civilization tier may be higher than cultivation-predominant peers because they have invested uniformly in technology domains without allocation to cultivation. Their effective power is concentrated entirely in the technology track.

**Balanced Civilizations (At or Near Tendency Range Center):**
Civilizations where cultivation and technology have developed in parallel and each has influenced the other. These are the Starward Saga's primary narrative civilizational model. They develop breakthrough capabilities through synergy — capabilities that neither pure-cultivation nor pure-technology civilizations can achieve.

**The Narrative Value of Tendency Deviation:**
Civilizations significantly outside the tendency range are narratively compelling precisely because of the deviation. The tension created by a high-technology, low-cultivation civilization confronting a low-technology, high-cultivation civilization is one of the Starward Saga's primary narrative collision spaces.
