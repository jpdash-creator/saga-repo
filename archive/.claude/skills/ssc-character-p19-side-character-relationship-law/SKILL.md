## Area 7 — Side Character Relationship Law

### 7.1 — Side Character Relationship to the Hero

**Rule 19.7.1.1 — Side Characters Must Change the Hero:** Every Tier A side character must, through their relationship with the hero, change the hero in some meaningful way — shift the hero's ideology, challenge their psychological assumptions, expand their worldview, or force a consequence reckoning. A Tier A side character who exists through the entire arc without changing the hero is a narration violation.

**Rule 19.7.1.2 — Side Characters Are Changed by the Hero:** Equally, the hero changes the side character. The direction of change is not predetermined — the hero may make a side character better, or worse, or simply different. A Tier A mentor who is completely unaffected by the hero they mentored has not been in a real relationship.

**Rule 19.7.1.3 — Side Character Relationships Have Natural Limits:** A side character relationship that serves the hero perfectly, never creates friction, and is always available when needed is not a relationship — it is a resource allocation. Real relationships have limits: mentors have their own struggles, allies have their own agendas, rivals have their own costs. These limits are narrative assets, not narrative problems.

### 7.2 — Side Character Relationship to the Villain

Side characters who are connected to villains — subordinates, former allies, objects of the villain's consequence arc, or characters living in the villain's shadow — are governed under Part 19 for their individual character governance and Part 18 for their relationship to the villain's agenda.

**Rule 19.7.2.1 — Villain-Connected Side Characters Are Not Villain Agents:** A side character who serves a villain faction is not governed as a villain unless they independently meet the criteria for Part 18 governance (named antagonist in three or more scenes whose opposition to the protagonist drives the conflict). A soldier in a villain's army is a Tier B or C side character. The general who actively opposes the hero across the arc is a Part 18 villain.

**Rule 19.7.2.2 — Side Character Defection from Villain Is a Major Arc Event:** When a Tier A side character who has been serving or affiliated with a villain defects to the hero's side — or is liberated from the villain's control — this is simultaneously a Part 18 CSS event for the villain, a Part 27 CSS event for the side character, and a Part 20 IE event for all parties involved.

### 7.3 — Side Character Relationships to Each Other

Side characters have relationships with each other that are not merely mediated through the hero. Two allies may conflict. A mentor may have history with a rival. A world carrier may have a family relationship with an information carrier. These inter-side-character relationships are governed by Part 23 (Affiliation Law) and add depth to the world's sense of being populated by people rather than by plot functions.

**Rule 19.7.3.1 — Inter-Side-Character Relationships Must Be Consistent:** If two Tier A side characters have a defined relationship, that relationship must be consistent with their individual ideology profiles, psychological profiles, and history. Two characters whose ideological profiles make close alliance impossible but who are narrated as close allies are a canon violation.
