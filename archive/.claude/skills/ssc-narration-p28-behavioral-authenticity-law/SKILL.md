## Area 6 — Behavioral Authenticity Law

### 6.1 — The Behavioral Freedom Principle

Characters, factions, civilizations, and institutions may behave in any way that is consistent with their governed identity. There is no behavioral script. The Canon governs what characters are (classification, ideology, psychology, consequence arc) — it does not specify what they do in any given moment. Behavioral freedom is the freedom to invent specific behavioral instances within governed categories.

### 6.2 — Character Behavioral Authenticity

Character behavior must be authentic to the character's full governed profile — not to a simplified version of that profile. A character is not merely "ambitious" or "compassionate" or "cold." A character has: a specific ideology category with specific variant (Part 20); a specific cultivation specialization type (Part 16); a specific psychological architecture with current PIS tier (Part 26); a specific Heart state (Part 4); a specific relationship web (Parts 17-19); and a specific consequence arc (Part 27). Authentic behavior is behavior that expresses the full complexity of this profile in the specific moment — not the simplified version.

**The Complexity Mandate:** Characters may not be reduced to their dominant trait in behavioral rendering. A character who is primarily SDI-Conquest in ideology may still experience moments of genuine tenderness toward a mentor — because their psychology (Part 26) has a relational architecture that predates and coexists with their ideological hardening. That tenderness is not inconsistent with their ideology — it is the complexity beneath the ideology. Behavioral authenticity renders both.

### 6.3 — Faction Behavioral Authenticity

Factions may take actions that surprise readers, provided those actions are consistent with the faction's: (a) Part 22 classification type and governance archetype; (b) Part 20 ideological orientation of its leadership; (c) Part 25 cultural paradigm type; (d) Part 23 current affiliation positions; and (e) Part 21 civilization tier context. Factions have internal politics, competing sub-factions, conflicting loyalties, and individual members who may act against faction policy. This internal complexity is behavioral authenticity — it makes factions feel like institutions populated by humans rather than monolithic entities with uniform will.

### 6.4 — Civilizational Behavioral Authenticity

Civilizations may respond to events in ways that reflect their CCT tier, civilization type, cultural paradigm, and current ideological state — and may behave in ways that readers find surprising within those parameters. A Stellar-tier civilization under ideological stress may behave with the paranoia and irrationality of a much lower-tier civilization. A Subsistence-tier civilization with extraordinary ideological coherence may achieve strategic outcomes that seem disproportionate to its resource base. These behavioral variations are governed — they must be traceable to the civilization's full canonical profile — but they are not pre-specified. The author determines which facets of the profile are dominant in any given moment.

### 6.5 — The Prohibition on Robotic Behavior

Behavioral authenticity forbids mechanical character rendering — the treatment of characters as governed entities executing their category definitions rather than as persons. A cultivator who always behaves exactly as their ideology category predicts, who never surprises themselves or the reader, who never experiences the gap between what they believe and what they feel — is a governance output, not a person. Part 28 requires that characters be rendered as persons: the gap between their public behavior and their private experience, between their ideology and their emotions, between what they say and what they mean, must be present in the prose. The Canon governs the architecture. The author peoples it with the irreducible specificity of personhood.
