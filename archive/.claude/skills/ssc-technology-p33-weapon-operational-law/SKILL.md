## Area 10 — Weapon Operational Law

### 10.1 — Weapon Deployment Authorization Hierarchy

Every weapon deployment occurs within a defined authorization hierarchy that determines who may authorize weapon use at each scale. Authorization hierarchy is a joint governance construct between Part 33 (weapon rules), Part 22 (faction governance), Part 21 (civilization governance), and Part 5 (combat escalation).

| Weapon WS | Minimum Deployment Authorization | Escalation Implication |
|---|---|---|
| WS-I | Individual operator self-determination in active combat | E1–E2 — no faction authorization required |
| WS-II | Unit commander authorization | E2–E3 |
| WS-III | Garrison or tactical command authorization | E3–E4 |
| WS-IV | Faction military authority authorization | E4–E5 — faction asset engagement |
| WS-V | Faction strategic command plus civilian authority | E5 — full faction-level conflict |
| WS-VI | Civilization governance authorization (Part 21 council) | E6–E7 — civilization-level conflict; Three-Tier Gap Rule implications |
| WS-VII | Absolute Authorization Protocol only | E7 — existential-class conflict |

### 10.2 — Weapon Maintenance Doctrine

**Rule 33.10.1 — Maintenance Cycle:** All technology weapons require periodic maintenance to remain operational. The maintenance interval scales inversely with weapon tier — higher-tier weapons require more frequent and more skilled maintenance. A Tier 7 weapon that has not received maintenance for 30 years is operating at degraded capability regardless of storage conditions.

**Rule 33.10.2 — Maintenance Competency Requirement:** Maintaining a weapon requires technology practitioners whose individual skill tier meets or exceeds the weapon's construction tier. A Tier 8 weapon cannot be maintained by a civilization operating at Tier 5 technology — the maintenance will be inadequate regardless of effort.

**Rule 33.10.3 — Legacy Weapon Degradation:** Weapons from destroyed or regressed civilizations that lack their maintenance ecosystem degrade at a rate of one effective tier per century (approximate — varies by weapon type and storage quality). A Tier 10 weapon discovered after 500 years without maintenance is functionally operating at Tier 5 capability regardless of its design specifications.

### 10.3 — Weapon Transfer and Arms Trade Governance

**Rule 33.10.4 — Cross-Tier Transfer Risk:** Transferring weapons to a civilization or faction more than two technology tiers below the weapon's construction tier creates a proliferation event under Part 21's Three-Tier Gap Rule. The receiving party gains weapons they cannot maintain, cannot reproduce, and cannot safely operate at full capacity. The transferring party bears legal liability under Part 22's faction accountability rules.

**Rule 33.10.5 — Weapons and Faction Classification:** Faction weapon arsenals are classified assets under Part 24 (Information and Secrecy Law). Weapon inventory disclosure requirements, deception doctrines governing weapon capabilities, and intelligence operations targeting weapon stockpiles are Part 24 governance events operating alongside Part 33.
