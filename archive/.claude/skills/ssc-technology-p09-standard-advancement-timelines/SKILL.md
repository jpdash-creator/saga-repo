## Area 5 — Standard Advancement Timelines

### 5.1 — Timeline Definitions

**Standard Advancement Timeline** is the expected duration for a civilization to advance one tier in its majority-domain, given:
- Sustained institutional investment at a reasonable historical rate (not at acceleration conditions, not under deceleration events)
- Full satisfaction of the Advancement Depth Requirement (Area 3.3) before the advancement period begins
- No significant external disruption

Standard timelines are expressed in **generations** (approximately 25–30 years per generation for mortal-lifespan civilizations; adjusted for civilizations with cultivation-extended lifespans). In cultivation-heavy civilizations where high-realm leaders have multi-century lifespans, "generation" in an institutional continuity sense is better measured in what the longest-lived practitioner class experiences, which can be much longer than the mortal generation.

### 5.2 — Standard Advancement Timeline Table

| Current Tier | Standard Timeline (Majority Domain Advancement) | Typical Limiting Factor | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 3–8 generations | Resource surplus generation; organized labor | Short because the total knowledge base is small and problems are concrete |
| Tier 2 | 4–10 generations | Fuel/energy infrastructure; metallurgical knowledge | Chemical and mechanical science accumulates across many practitioners |
| Tier 3 | 3–8 generations | Electrical grid buildout; semiconductor development | Digital transition is among the faster tier advances because AI begins accelerating other domains |
| Tier 4 | 2–5 generations | Quantum computing science; molecular engineering | The shortest standard timeline for non-trivial transitions; AI-enabled research acceleration begins here |
| Tier 5 | 3–8 generations | Multi-world infrastructure; space industrial base | First tier requiring multi-world institutional presence; social identity change required |
| Tier 6 | 5–12 generations | Outer system engineering; gas giant extraction | Outer solar system operations require multi-generation institutional commitment |
| Tier 7 | 8–20 generations | Stellar energy capture; megastructure engineering | Dyson sphere or equivalent is a multi-century minimum project |
| Tier 8 | 8–20 generations | FTL physics breakthrough; interstellar fleet | FTL is a scientific prerequisite that cannot be forced by resource investment; discovery timelines are variable |
| Tier 9 | 15–50 generations | Multi-stellar logistics; cluster-scale governance | Governance of stellar-scale populations requires institutional evolution, not just technology investment |
| Tier 10 | 30–100 generations | Galactic physics; dark matter/energy theory | First tier where scientific prerequisites may have no known engineering pathway in advance |
| Tier 11 | 50–200 generations | Galactic identity formation; cross-galactic infrastructure | Cross-galactic institutional continuity across light-speed communication lag is an unprecedented governance challenge |
| Tier 12–15 | 100–500 generations each | Scale of physics required; multi-galactic coordination | Each sub-tier requires a civilization-scale paradigm shift, not just an advancement investment |
| Tier 16–20 | Narrative-mythological | Physics-of-existence science; dimensional theory | These transitions are not producible on any predictable timeline; they represent civilizational ascension events |

### 5.3 — Intermediate Tier Advancement Properties

The 8 intermediate tiers (6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17) added in Part 8 v2.0 have distinct advancement properties from anchor tiers:

**Intermediate Tier Characteristic:** Intermediate tiers represent consolidation and deepening of capabilities between major anchor-tier paradigm shifts. They are typically faster to advance through than the anchor tiers on either side because:
1. The scientific paradigm has already been established by the preceding anchor tier
2. The primary challenge is infrastructure deployment and institutional deepening, not scientific breakthrough
3. The leading-edge practitioners of the preceding anchor tier are typically still active, providing institutional continuity and tacit knowledge transmission

**Intermediate Tier Standard Timeline Adjustment:** Reduce the anchor tier standard timeline by 30–50% for intermediate tier transitions. A civilization that took 15 generations to advance from Tier 5 to Tier 8 (anchor) will typically take 8–10 generations to advance through Tier 6 and Tier 7 (intermediate) combined.

**Exception:** Intermediate tiers that require specific infrastructure deployment milestones (Tier 6 requires inner solar system multi-world presence; Tier 7 requires outer solar system regular operations) cannot be compressed below the minimum infrastructure buildout timeline, regardless of scientific and institutional readiness.
