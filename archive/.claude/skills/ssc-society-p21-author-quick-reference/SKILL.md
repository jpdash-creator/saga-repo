## Area 21 — Author Quick Reference

### 21.1 — Five Questions Before Writing a Civilization

**Question 1: What is this civilization's Designation?**
Use the CCT synthesis formula (Area 4) or assign directly from the tier profile descriptions (Area 8). Examples: Stellar, Clusteral, Galactic.

**Question 2: What is this civilization's Type?**
CS-Type, TS-Type, BP-Type, ES-Type, LS-Type, or CP-Type? The type shapes the civilization's character, strengths, and vulnerabilities.

**Question 3: Which axes are imbalanced?**
Identify any axis significantly above or below the CCT. That gap is the civilization's narrative identity — where its tension lives.

**Question 4: What CCT level is the civilization interacting with?**
The tier gap determines whether interaction is diplomatic, client-state, or existence-threatening.

**Question 5: Is this a Remnant, Nascent, Fractured, or Standard civilization?**
Special classifications (Area 7) add narrative texture and governance nuance.

### 21.2 — Common Author Errors

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Calling a civilization "Civ-7" or "Hold-7" | Use CTD: "Stellar" or "CCT-7" |
| Assigning CCT based on highest individual cultivator | CA is distributional; peak realm is one criterion among four |
| Treating LA-territorial claims as LA actual value | LA = administered territory, not claimed territory |
| Treating SA-0 as a civilization deficiency | SA-0 pure civilizations are structurally complete; they face an upper cap, not a penalty |
| Depicting CCT-4 and CCT-9 civilizations as diplomatic equals | The tier gap creates a power asymmetry; use Rule 10.1.1 |
| Advancing CCT after a single breakthrough event | CCT advancement requires 2-generation sustaining (Rule 4.4.2) |

### 21.3 — CTD to Narrative Scale

| Designation (CCT) | What the Narrative Feels Like |
|---|---|
| Subsistence to Industrial (CCT-1 to CCT-3) | Intimate; personal power matters enormously; institutions are human-scale |
| Planetary to Solarian (CCT-4 to CCT-6) | Planetary to stellar; institutions are vast; individual power is still pivotal but context is larger |
| Stellar to Clusteral (CCT-7 to CCT-10) | Stellar to cluster; civilizational architecture is the backdrop; extraordinary individuals shape civilizations |
| Galactic to Superclusteral (CCT-11 to CCT-14) | Galactic and beyond; individual scale is dwarfed; themes are civilizational and cosmological |
| Liminal-Universal and above (CCT-15+) | Part 28 territory; the physics of the universe are story elements |
