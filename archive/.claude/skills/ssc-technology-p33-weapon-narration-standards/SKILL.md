## Area 16 — Weapon Narration Standards

### N33-1 — Weapon Tier Grounding

Every technology weapon in prose must be grounded in its tier band. A Tier 3 firearm does not operate with Tier 7 precision targeting. A Tier 6 orbital cannon does not appear on a primitive planet without establishing how it arrived and who maintains it. Weapon presence always implies weapon ecosystem (Area 7 — supply, maintenance, operator training).

### N33-2 — Damage Vector Consistency

When a weapon inflicts damage in prose, the description must be consistent with the weapon's damage vector profile. A T-KIN kinetic weapon creates physical displacement damage — not radiant burns unless the kinetic energy converts to heat (which happens at high velocity and requires explicit acknowledgment). A T-PSI weapon does not create physical wounds unless a DV-I secondary vector is present. Prose damage must match weapon type.

### N33-3 — Cultivation Defense Rendering

When a cultivator resists a technology weapon, the prose must reflect the weapon tier's force category relative to the cultivator's realm (Area 6.1 — Modes A, B, C). If the weapon is in Mode B (weapon superior), the cultivator should be visibly struggling against overwhelming technological force even if their cultivation is formidable. If in Mode C (complete defense failure), cultivation defense attempting to block a Tier 9+ weapon should read as futile — like a paper umbrella in a hurricane.

### N33-4 — Weapon Scale Class Environmental Description

Weapon WS class determines the environmental description scope. WS-I impacts are personal in scale — a hit on one person or one object. WS-V impacts restructure landscapes — authors must describe continental-scale environmental consequences, not merely individual casualties. Each WS class requires description at its actual scale or the weapon's power is narratively undermined.

### N33-5 — Weapon Depletion Realism

Magazine-governed weapons deplete ammunition. Authors must track weapon shots in combat sequences if the weapon's finite magazine is narratively relevant. Characters should experience ammunition limits as genuine tactical constraints, not forgotten inconveniences. Energy-beam weapons (WE-IV) sourced from continuous power plants have operational endurance determined by power plant capacity — not unlimited fire.

### N33-6 — Autonomous Weapon Narration

Autonomous weapons (AT-1 through AT-4) should be narratively distinguished from operator-controlled weapons. AT-3 and AT-4 weapons make their own decisions — their targeting choices should sometimes be surprising, suboptimal, or chillingly efficient in ways that human operators would not replicate. The autonomous nature of the weapon should be narratively present, not invisible.

### N33-7 — Weapon-Cultivation Interaction as Narrative Tension

The threshold where cultivation defense shifts from Mode A (cultivation superior) to Mode B (technology superior) to Mode C (complete failure) is a primary source of narrative tension in the Starward Saga. Authors should exploit this threshold deliberately. A cultivator facing a Tier 6 weapon for the first time and discovering their realm cannot fully block it is a major narrative moment — it represents the boundary where cultivation's individual supremacy ends and civilization-scale technology begins to win.

### N33-8 — Restricted and Prohibited Weapons as Narrative Weight

When Restricted or Absolute Prohibition weapons appear in prose, they carry narrative weight beyond their mechanical effect. The Soul-Sever Lance is not merely a powerful weapon — it is a war crime, a civilizational taboo, a political detonator. Authors must frame such weapons with the full gravity of their governance classification. A character deploying or threatening such a weapon has committed an act with civilizational-level consequences, not merely tactical ones.
