## Area 1 — The Novel Primacy Mandate

### 1.1 — The Four Foundational Principles

**P36-1 — We Are Writing a Novel.**
The Starward Saga is a novel series. It is not a technical reference, a cultivation theory textbook, a physics manual, a governance document, or an academic treatise on power systems. Every decision about how to render a scene — what to show, what to withhold, what to explain, what to leave as sensory impression — is made in service of the novel. Technical accuracy is mandatory; technical accuracy delivered as a textbook lecture violates this law even when every cultivation fact is correct.

**P36-2 — The Scene Must Be Living.**
Every prose unit must be a living scene. A scene is living when the reader is inside the experience with the characters, not outside observing it; the world feels inhabited by real beings with real stakes, not populated by technical specifications; the events carry emotional and physical weight, not just logical consequence; and the prose moves with the pulse of story, not the cadence of documentation. A living scene is not defined by its length, its technical density, or its compliance with other Parts. It is defined by whether it makes the reader feel that what is happening matters.

**P36-3 — All Canon Parts Serve the Novel.**
Parts 1–35 are the technical architecture that makes the Starward Saga possible at its scale and consistency. Part 36 is the reminder that the architecture serves the novel, not the other way around. When a character's cultivation breakthrough must be narrated, Part 3 governs what is physically true about that breakthrough; Part 36 governs that the breakthrough must feel like one of the most significant moments of that character's life, rendered with the full weight of what it means for the story. The cultivation facts and the emotional truth must coexist. There is no exemption.

**P36-4 — The Novel Must Feel Like a Novel.**
The Starward Saga must feel — across every prose unit, every scene, every exchange — like a work in the tradition of the great cultivation and technology novel series. This tradition is not a stylistic rule — it is a thematic identity. The prose must carry this identity in its bones. The reader must finish each prose unit feeling that they have lived inside a world of cultivators and engineers, not that they have studied one.

### 1.2 — Part 36 Scope and Position

Part 36 is **Authority Tier 0 — Narrative Identity**. This is the highest authority tier in the Canon system for the purpose of narrative feel. It does not override technical accuracy requirements (which are load-bearing Canon law) but it governs how those requirements are fulfilled in prose. When any Part 1–35 requirement can be fulfilled in multiple ways, Part 36 determines which way. When a Part 1–35 requirement can only be fulfilled in one way and that one way conflicts with Part 36's living-scene mandate, the conflict must be escalated to the user via `AskUserQuestion` before drafting proceeds. There is no silent resolution of a Part 36 conflict — even minimal variance must be escalated.

Part 36 operates at every stage of prose production: at the Decision Consultation Gate (Area 13 — DCG Thematic Preview Mandate), at Prose Drafting (Areas 1–12 govern the prose as it is written), at Gate 1 Check #16 (Area 16 — Novel Thematic Compliance Audit), and at Gate 2 verification.
