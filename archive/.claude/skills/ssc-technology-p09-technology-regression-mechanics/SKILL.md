## Area 8 — Technology Regression Mechanics

### 8.1 — Regression Definitions

**Technology Regression** is the reduction of a civilization's effective technology tier below its prior peak. Regression can be:

- **Active Regression:** Civilization is currently operating below its prior peak tier due to an ongoing deceleration condition. Infrastructure that once supported higher-tier operations is still present but non-functional or degraded.
- **Completed Regression:** Deceleration condition has ended; the civilization now operates at a sustainably lower tier than its prior peak. Rebuilding has begun.
- **Fragmentary Regression:** Different domains of the civilization's technology have regressed by different amounts. Some domains retain near-peak capability while others have regressed significantly. This uneven pattern is common following war-driven regression (Military typically regresses less; Environmental and Medical typically regress more).

### 8.2 — Degradation Timeline Table (Technology Above Current Ceiling)

Per Part 8, P8-2.2 — technology above a civilization's current Technology Ceiling degrades without maintenance. The following table governs degradation timelines:

| Tier Gap (Technology Tier above Current Ceiling) | Degradation Rate | Mean Time to Non-Functional |
|---|---|---|
| 1 tier above | Slow (10–20% capacity loss per generation under minimal maintenance) | 5–10 generations if partially maintained; 2–4 if no maintenance |
| 2 tiers above | Moderate (25–35% capacity loss per generation) | 2–4 generations if partially maintained; 1–2 if no maintenance |
| 3 tiers above | Rapid (50–60% capacity loss per generation) | 1–2 generations regardless of maintenance attempt |
| 4+ tiers above | Near-immediate (operational failure within years to decades) | 1–3 generations even with full maintenance attempt at civilization's capability limit |

**Partial Maintenance Exception:** A civilization can employ high-tier personnel (from another civilization, or exceptional individuals produced within the civilization) to slow degradation. This exception is governed by Part 8, P8-5 (Technology Transfer — Operation Without Understanding). Personnel can slow degradation to 20–30% of the standard rate but cannot stop it entirely without the full prerequisite satisfaction of the technology's tier.

### 8.3 — Re-Advancement After Regression

A civilization recovering from technology regression re-advances more quickly than initial first-time advancement, because:

1. **Retained Scientific Knowledge:** The theoretical understanding of what is possible at higher tiers is not lost through regression in most cases (except extreme knowledge-destruction catastrophes). Re-advancement to the prior peak is a matter of rebuilding institutional infrastructure to apply existing knowledge, not re-discovering the knowledge.

2. **Physical Evidence:** Ruins, partially functional artifacts, archived records, and physical infrastructure remnants from the prior peak tier provide concrete reference points for reconstruction. A civilization rebuilding a Dyson sphere has design records and physical structure remnants to reference; a civilization building one for the first time has only theoretical models.

3. **Cultural Memory:** Even when explicit institutional knowledge is lost, cultural memory — myths, practices, taboos, aesthetic traditions — carries implicit technological knowledge in encoded form. Civilizations with strong oral tradition can encode significant technical knowledge in non-technical cultural forms.

**Re-Advancement Timeline Multiplier:** Standard advancement timelines for re-advancement are reduced by:
- 30–50% if scientific knowledge was fully retained
- 15–30% if scientific knowledge was partially retained (some gaps)
- 5–15% if scientific knowledge was mostly lost but physical evidence remains
