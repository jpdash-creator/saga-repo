## Area 10 — Dialogue and Psychology Intersection

### 10.1 — Psychological State in Speech

**Rule 29.10.1.1 — PIS State Is Legible in Speech:** Every character's current PIS state (Part 26) produces specific, governable effects in how they speak. These effects are not generic — they are specific to the PIS category:

| PIS State | Effect on Speech |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **PIS-1 (Healthy)** | Full range of registers available; speech is governed by situation, not by suppression |
| **PIS-2 (Surface Disruption)** | Slight compression in the most vulnerable registers; the character may avoid certain topics or speak about them with unusual brevity |
| **PIS-3 (Active Friction)** | Visible tension in speech around wound-adjacent topics; deflection more frequent; humor as defense mechanism if applicable to character |
| **PIS-4 (Active Wound)** | Significant distortion around wound-adjacent topics; character may speak in ways that reveal the wound while believing they are concealing it; emotional regulation is effortful and visible |
| **PIS-5 (Deep Wound)** | Fundamental architecture of social speech under pressure; certain registers may be inaccessible; speech around wound topics may be either brutally direct (wound overflowing) or completely absent (wound locked) |
| **PIS-6 (Identity Disruption)** | The character may not speak as themselves — they speak as the self they are performing in the absence of the self that has been disrupted; the performance is visible in the effort required to maintain it |
| **PIS-7 (Acute Crisis)** | Speech is significantly altered — shorter, less controlled, more raw; the social management layer of speech is partially removed; the character says things they would not say at PIS-2 |
| **PIS-8 (Dissociation)** | Speech may be strangely detached — the character describes their own situation and inner state from a remove, as if narrating someone else's experience; disconnection from ordinary social response patterns |
| **PIS-9 (Breakdown)** | The ordinary architecture of speech is partially or fully unavailable; communication may fragment, become non-sequential, or rely on non-verbal channels |
| **PIS-10 (Collapse)** | Speech may be nearly impossible; what is said may be incoherent, reduced to repetition, or replaced by silence so total it communicates only the collapse itself |

**Rule 29.10.1.2 — Wound-Adjacent Topics Must Be Rendered Distinctly:** When a conversation touches on a topic that is adjacent to a character's psychological wound (per Part 26 impact categories), the character's response to that topic must differ from their response to non-adjacent topics. The difference is the wound's signature in language.

### 10.2 — Psychological Healing Through Dialogue

**Rule 29.10.2.1 — Dialogue Can Be a Resolution Pathway:** Part 26 Area 10 identifies five resolution pathways for psychological wounds. Two of these — narrative integration (the ability to place the wound in a coherent life narrative) and relational re-anchoring (the restoration of trust through a genuine relational encounter) — occur primarily through dialogue. These healing dialogue events are among the most significant moments in the series. They must be rendered at full weight — never rushed, never resolved in a single exchange, always with the residual presence of the wound even after the dialogue has advanced the healing process.

**Rule 29.10.2.2 — Premature Resolution Is Forbidden:** A character whose wound is at PIS-6 cannot be resolved to PIS-2 through a single conversation, no matter how powerful that conversation is. Resolution follows Part 26 mechanics. Dialogue can advance the resolution process — it can move a character from PIS-6 to PIS-5 through a major relational re-anchoring encounter — but it cannot collapse the resolution arc. The healing must be felt as work.
