## Area 16 — Travel Infrastructure Law

### 16.1 — Infrastructure Tiers

Travel infrastructure represents the organized, civilization-level systems that enable mass movement at scales beyond individual capability. Infrastructure amplifies travel capability across all civilization tiers.

| Infrastructure Tier | CCT Range | Infrastructure Type | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — Local | CCT-1 to CCT-3 | Roads, paths, river navigation, harbors | Surface-only; enables intra-Kingdom trade |
| II — Regional | CCT-3 to CCT-4 | Rail networks, highway systems, organized postal routes | Cross-Kingdom connectivity; enables national logistics |
| III — Global | CCT-4 to CCT-5 | Aviation networks, global shipping lanes, satellite relay | Planetary unification; enables global civilization coordination |
| IV — Orbital | CCT-5 to CCT-6 | Space launch infrastructure, orbital stations, inner-system transit | Extends civilization from one world to multiple worlds |
| V — System | CCT-6 to CCT-7 | Full stellar system transit network, deep space relay beacons | Star system as unified logistics domain |
| VI — Interstellar | CCT-7 to CCT-10 | FTL drive infrastructure, star-system beacon networks, early gates | Multi-star-system civilization |
| VII — Cluster | CCT-10 to CCT-11 | Star gate networks, FTL express lanes, cluster-unified transit | Star Cluster as unified travel zone |
| VIII — Galactic | CCT-11 to CCT-13 | Galaxy-spanning gate chains, wormhole networks, galactic transit authority | Galaxy as unified travel infrastructure |
| IX — Cosmological | CCT-13 to CCT-16 | Universe-scale transit architecture | Universe-spanning civilization logistics |

### 16.2 — Infrastructure Maintenance Law

**Rule 16.2.1 — Infrastructure Requires Continuous Investment.**
A gate network, FTL beacon system, or orbital station network requires continuous investment to maintain. Civilizations that undergo regression (Part 9) or military defeat (Part 5 consequence law) lose infrastructure — and the loss of infrastructure compounds their strategic situation by reducing travel capability.

**Rule 16.2.2 — Infrastructure Is a Strategic Target.**
Star gates, FTL beacons, orbital stations, and formation teleportation array nodes are military targets. Destroying them severs travel connectivity. Military campaigns for control of infrastructure nodes are as decisive as battles for population centers.

**Rule 16.2.3 — Infrastructure Persists Beyond Its Builder.**
Constructed infrastructure outlasts the civilization that built it. An ancient star gate built by a Tier 13 civilization that has collapsed may still function if its energy source is intact — or may be partially functional, waiting for a new civilization to restore it. This creates the ruins world / archaeological discovery narrative space.

### 16.3 — Travel Networks and Political Governance

**Principle 16.3.1 — Network Ownership Is Power.**
The civilization or faction that controls key transit nodes in a travel network controls access to the regions connected by those nodes. This is the equivalent of controlling mountain passes, river crossings, or seaports — amplified to interstellar scale.

**Principle 16.3.2 — Transit Rights Require Governance.**
Civilizations sharing transit infrastructure must establish treaties, tolls, or military dominance hierarchies. Unauthorized gate use is treated as an act of aggression in most CCT-10+ political frameworks.
