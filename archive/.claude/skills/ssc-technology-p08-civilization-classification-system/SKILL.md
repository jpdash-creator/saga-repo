## Area 5 — Civilization Classification System

> **Part 21 Redirect Notice (Active):** Part 21 (Civilization Law — fully authored, Areas 1–22) is now the primary authority for all **overall civilization classification**. The Civ-N tier notation in this Area is Part 8's internal reference for the **Technology Axis (TA)** — one of the five axes (CA, TA, EA, LA, SA) that Part 21 synthesizes into a Composite Civilization Tier (CCT) / Civilization Tier Designation (CTD). The Civ-1 through Civ-20 designations remain valid as TA-axis labels. For any reference to a civilization's overall tier, the Civilization Tier Designation (Part 21) is the canonical term. See Part 21 Area 2 for the full TA axis calibration table and Part 21 Area 3 for the Twenty CTDs (Subsistence through Absolute).

### 5.1 — The Twenty Civilization Tiers

Civilization tier governs what technology tier a civilization can sustainably support as its infrastructure. Technology is infrastructure — and infrastructure requires the organizational, population, energy, and resource capacity to build, staff, and maintain it. The 20-Tier Civilization Classification System maps directly to the 20 Technology Tiers, providing one governance layer per tier. For overall civilization classification combining all five axes, see Part 21 (Civilization Law).

**Rule 5.1.1 — One-Tier Tolerance:** A civilization can sustain technology up to one tier above its own civilization tier in at most three domains. This represents cutting-edge investment that the civilization is making at exceptional cost. Technology two or more tiers above the civilization tier in any domain cannot be sustained without civilizational collapse under the maintenance burden.

**Rule 5.1.2 — Domain Specialization Below Tier:** A civilization may have one or more domains at significantly lower levels than its overall civilization tier — this represents deliberate deprioritization, cultural rejection of specific technology types, or resource constraints in specific areas. This is narratively valid. A civilization's majority-domain tier (7 of 14 domains) determines its overall technology tier.

**Rule 5.1.3 — Civilization Tier Determination for Part 21 (Active):** Part 8's Civilization Classification System provides the **Technology Axis (TA)** input to Part 21 (Civilization Law — fully authored)'s CCT synthesis formula. Part 21 synthesizes the five axes (CA, TA, EA, LA, SA) via weighted average and axis gap penalty into a Composite Civilization Tier (CCT) expressed as a CTD (Civilization Tier Designation). Part 8 Area 5 governs technology tier only — it does not determine the overall civilization classification. For overall civilization tier, the canonical designation is the CTD from Part 21.

### 5.2 — Cultivation Civilization vs. Non-Cultivation Civilization Trajectories

*Detailed governance moved to Part 31 (Cultivation-Technology Synergy Law), Area 13.3 and Area 15 as of v2.1.*

Cultivation and non-cultivation civilizations follow different developmental trajectories through technology tiers. Both paths are fully legitimate and can reach Tier 20. Summary:

**Cultivation-Predominant Civilizations:** Typically higher CI Technology (Type 12) development; may have lower Energy/Space/Industrial tiers than technology-predominant peers; total effective power (cultivation + technology combined) may exceed pure-technology civilizations at same civilization scale.

**Technology-Predominant Civilizations:** Zero CI Technology (Type 12); full capability across all 13 other domains; higher Technology Ceiling per civilization tier than cultivation-predominant peers in those domains.

**Balanced Civilizations:** Parallel development of both tracks; access to synergy capabilities neither pure-cultivation nor pure-technology civilizations can achieve (Part 31, Area 5). The Starward Saga's primary narrative civilizational model.

*For the full governance of trajectory differences, societal dynamics, and the narrative value of civilization type, see Part 31, Area 13.3 and Area 15.*

### 5.3 — Technology Tier Assessment Within a Civilization

**The 7-of-14 Rule:** A civilization's overall technology tier equals the technology level achieved in at least 7 of its 14 technology domains. This "majority domain" rule ensures that genuine civilizational capability breadth is required for tier classification, not merely exceptional investment in one or two domains.

**The Peak Domain Rule:** The single highest-level domain a civilization has achieved is its "Peak Domain Tier." This may be 1–2 tiers above the civilization's majority-domain tier. The Peak Domain Tier represents the civilization's cutting edge — the frontier where exceptional investment has produced exceptional capability. It is the technology level that generates the civilization's most awe-inspiring individual technological achievements.

**The Floor Domain Rule:** The lowest-level domain (of the 14) represents the civilization's most underdeveloped technological area. If any domain is more than 3 tiers below the majority-domain tier, the civilization has a "Technology Gap" in that domain — a narratively significant vulnerability or cultural choice that shapes its civilizational profile.
