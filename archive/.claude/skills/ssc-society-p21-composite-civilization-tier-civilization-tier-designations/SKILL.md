## Area 3 — The Composite Civilization Tier (CCT) — Civilization Tier Designations

### 3.1 — The Twenty Civilization Tier Designations

The twenty Civilization Tier Designations (CTDs) are the canonical tier names in the Starward Saga universe. Each CTD is a structural descriptor — it tells the reader what kind of civilizational architecture the entity has built and commands, expressed in terms derived from the Kardashev classification tradition extended across the full cosmic scale of the Starward Saga universe. CTDs are scientific in origin: they derive from the civilization's measurable energy command, territorial extent, and structural complexity as classified by universal scholars.

| CCT | Designation | Short Form | Structural Identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCT-1 | **Subsistence** | Subsist | A pre-institutional civilization — a tribe, a clan, a single settlement. No formal governance, no organized cultivation institutions, no systematic technology. Energy at subsistence scale. The raw beginning of civilizational form. |
| CCT-2 | **Formative** | Formative | A civilization taking its first organized shape — city-states, early kingdoms, the first formal cultivation sects and technology guilds. The formative phase of structural emergence. |
| CCT-3 | **Industrial** | Industrial | The civilization has crossed into systematic production. Nation-states, industrial infrastructure, institutional cultivation academies, standardized knowledge transmission. The first large-scale organizational architecture. |
| CCT-4 | **Planetary (Type I)** | Planetary | A true Type I civilization by the Kardashev standard: full planetary energy command, planetary-scale governance, and civilization-wide cultivation and technology infrastructure. The first complete civilizational tier. |
| CCT-5 | **Multi-World (Type I+)** | Multi-World | Ascending beyond planetary scale — multi-world presence, interplanetary governance, early Sacred Ground construction as institutional goal. Type I civilization extending its reach across a star system's inner worlds. |
| CCT-6 | **Solarian (Type II−)** | Solarian | The civilization has reached into the void between worlds and taken command of the inner stellar environment. Orbital infrastructure, asteroid exploitation, partial stellar energy capture — a civilization approaching Type II status. Named for the solar scale of energy command it approaches. |
| CCT-7 | **Stellar (Type II)** | Stellar | A true Type II civilization: full stellar system administration, Dyson-scale energy capture, cultivation and technology at deep synergy threshold. The civilization's identity is stellar — it holds and manages its star. |
| CCT-8 | **Interstellar (Type II+)** | Interstellar | The interstellar age has begun. FTL transportation achieved; first neighboring star systems administered. The civilization is no longer bound to a single star. Its identity expands with each new stellar system claimed. |
| CCT-9 | **Expansionary** | Expansionary | The civilization is flooding across the interstellar void — 6 to 50 star systems administered, federated or distributed authority emerging. Multi-stellar governance challenges define this tier. |
| CCT-10 | **Clusteral (Type IIb)** | Clusteral | A stellar cluster civilization — thousands to hundreds of thousands of star systems under unified administration. The first tier at which civilizational sub-entities (Stellar-class sub-civilizations) exist within the larger structure as administrative units rather than independent polities. |
| CCT-11 | **Galactic (Type III)** | Galactic | A true Type III civilization: full galactic governance, Model D synergy operational at civilization scale, Divine Liquid accessible civilization-wide. The apex of what most civilizations across the universe consider "achievable." |
| CCT-12 | **Intergalactic (Type III+)** | Intergalactic | Ascending beyond a single galaxy — multiple galaxies in the Local Group under administration. The civilization's cultural and structural diversity has become almost incomprehensible from within. |
| CCT-13 | **Cosmological (Type IIIb)** | Cosmological | Galaxy cluster governance. The civilization has absorbed or subsumed multiple former Galactic-class entities. Its governance architecture administers a diversity of galactic histories. |
| CCT-14 | **Superclusteral** | Superclusteral | Supercluster-spanning civilization. The distances that once defined the limits of civilizational reach are now administrative corridors. |
| CCT-15 | **Liminal-Universal** | Liminal | The threshold of universal scale. A civilization standing at the edge of administering the observable universe — the liminal state between cosmological and universal existence. Part 28 authorization required for direct narrative participation. |
| CCT-16 | **Universal (Type IV)** | Universal | A Type IV civilization by extended Kardashev classification: full command of universal-scale energy, territory, and governance architecture. The universe is a domain, not a context. |
| CCT-17 | **Transversal (Type IV+)** | Transversal | Trans-universal civilization. The boundary between universes has been crossed. This civilization operates across multiple universal membranes simultaneously. |
| CCT-18 | **Multiversal (Type V)** | Multiversal | A Type V civilization: multiple universes converge in this entity's domain. Governance architecture operates at multiversal scale. |
| CCT-19 | **Dimensional** | Dimensional | The civilization has become a structural feature of dimensional reality itself. It does not merely inhabit space — it is, in part, the substrate that defines the dimensional framework. |
| CCT-20 | **Absolute** | Absolute | The terminal tier. A civilization indistinguishable from the substrate of existence. Energy, matter, space, and law are expressions of its structure. Classification ends here — the Absolute civilization is beyond analytical assessment. |

### 3.2 — CTD Quick Reference

Each Civilization Tier Designation carries three canonical identifiers used in prose:

1. **Full Form:** "a Stellar civilization" / "the Stellar civilization of the Veil Cluster..."
2. **Short Form:** "a Stellar-tier civilization" / "a Clusteral-level faction"
3. **In-World Scholar Form:** "CCT-7 classification" / "CCT-10 standing" / "Kardashev Type II standing"

Authors use Full Form or Short Form in narrative prose. Scholar Form appears in world-building documents, ancient records, classification scrolls, and academic contexts within the novel. The Kardashev Type annotation in Scholar Form is used by in-world scientists; in-world cultivators and diplomats typically use the Designation Name only.

### 3.3 — CTD Phases: Nascent, Established, and Apex

Each Civilization Tier Designation contains three internal developmental phases. These phases are not separate CCT tiers — they are structural positions *within* a given CCT level. They provide narrative granularity between CCT advances: a civilization at the same nominal tier as another may be significantly further along in its structural development without yet reaching the next tier.

**The Three Phases:**
- **Nascent Phase:** The civilization has just achieved the minimum axis thresholds for this CTD. Its institutions are young, its axis values are at or near the floor. Fragile; vulnerable to regression. Narratively: a civilization on the edge of its new tier, not yet comfortable in it.
- **Established Phase:** The civilization has stabilized at this CCT level. Axis values are solidly above the minimum thresholds. Institutions are mature and reproducing. Narratively: the "typical" expression of a tier — what scholars mean when they describe a civilization by its CTD.
- **Apex Phase:** The civilization's axis values have advanced significantly above the minimum thresholds for this CCT — approaching but not yet meeting the minimums for the next tier. Narratively: a civilization on the edge of advancement, powerful relative to its tier peers, often perceived by lower-tier civilizations as already in the next classification.

**Phase Notation:**
| Phase | In-World Scholar Notation | Prose Term |
|---|---|---|
| Nascent | CCT-N (Nascent) / N-CCT-N | "early [Designation]" / "nascent [Designation]" |
| Established | CCT-N (Established) / E-CCT-N | "[Designation]" (default usage; no qualifier needed) |
| Apex | CCT-N (Apex) / A-CCT-N | "deep [Designation]" / "apex [Designation]" / "near-[next Designation]" |

**Phase Examples:**
- A CCT-7 (Nascent) Stellar civilization has just achieved stellar system administration but is still consolidating governance and infrastructure.
- A CCT-7 (Established) Stellar civilization is a fully functioning stellar civilization — the canonical Stellar tier.
- A CCT-7 (Apex) Stellar civilization has all axes well above CCT-7 minimums and is structurally approaching CCT-8 — first interstellar probes are already reaching neighboring Star Systems; FTL research is near completion.

**Phase Assessment Rules:**
- **Nascent:** Any axis is at minimum or within 1 level above the CCT's minimum threshold.
- **Established:** All axes are at least 1 level above minimums; weighted synthesis score is comfortably within the CCT band.
- **Apex:** All axes are 2+ levels above the current CCT's minimum and within 2 levels of the next CCT's minimum threshold.

**Phase and Regression:** Regression from Established to Nascent phase within the same CCT is not a tier regression event — it is an intra-tier weakening. Regression from Nascent phase below the minimum threshold triggers a full CCT regression.

**Narrative Use of Phases:**
Phases appear in prose only when the distinction is narratively relevant — when a civilization's precise developmental position within a tier matters to the story. In most prose contexts, the CTD alone is sufficient. Phases are most valuable for:
- Depicting civilizations in transition (why a Stellar civilization feels "almost Interstellar")
- Showing regression (a Clusteral civilization falling to Nascent Clusteral after a catastrophic war)
- Calibrating tension in first-contact scenarios (an Apex Stellar meeting a Nascent Interstellar)
