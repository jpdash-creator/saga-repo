## Area 10 — Information Lag and the Governance of Distance

### 10.1 — The Four Lag Categories

Information lag — the time between an event and when it is known to a decision-maker — shapes all political, military, and social reality in the Starward Saga. Four categories of lag are canonical:

**Lag Category L1 — Real-Time (negligible):** Information arrives within seconds to minutes of the event. Achievable with: Soul Transmission within soul sense range; FTL communication at Type 2 Level 8+; Formation relay within an active network. Decision loops can be rapid.

**Lag Category L2 — Operational Lag (hours to days):** Information arrives within hours to days. Achievable with: Formation relay across multiple nodes; Technology communication within a star system at Type 2 Level 5–7; Fast cultivator courier over moderate distances. Decision loops can respond to rapidly evolving situations.

**Lag Category L3 — Strategic Lag (weeks to months):** Information arrives within weeks to months. Characteristic of: Physical courier transmission across interstellar distances below FTL travel; pre-FTL civilizations governing multi-system territory. Decision loops are slow; frontier commanders act autonomously.

**Lag Category L4 — Historical Lag (years to centuries):** Information arrives after it has lost operational relevance. Characteristic of: Pre-FTL communication at interstellar distances; civilizations that have lost their relay infrastructure; deliberate information quarantine. The history of wars fought, treaties signed, and civilizations fallen may only become known years after the fact.

### 10.2 — Lag and Governance Design

The information lag category experienced by a civilization fundamentally shapes its governance design:

- L1 civilizations can have coherent centralized governance across their full territory.
- L2 civilizations can have responsive centralized governance with some local discretion requirements.
- L3 civilizations must delegate strategic authority to regional commanders; central authority is advisory at the frontier.
- L4 civilizations function as loosely affiliated entities that may not share coherent governance at all — their political unity is a cultural and historical inheritance, not an operational reality.

**Governance Implication Rule P24-Lag-1:** Authors must not write governance scenarios that are inconsistent with the information lag of the civilization depicted. A CCT-5 civilization without FTL communication cannot have centralized real-time command of interstellar military operations. Frontier commanders must have autonomous authority commensurate with their lag environment.

### 10.3 — Intentional Information Quarantine

A civilization, faction, or individual can deliberately impose L3–L4 lag conditions on a specific information flow by cutting transmission channels to a target area. This is:
- Military information blockade: Destroying relay nodes, intercepting all courier traffic, and maintaining soul-sense patrol perimeters around a target zone
- Political information quarantine: Preventing news of internal crisis from reaching external allies or adversaries
- Cultural information isolation: Deliberately limiting access to outside information to maintain an insular community's beliefs (a technique used by religious and ideological factions to prevent contact with contradicting worldviews)
