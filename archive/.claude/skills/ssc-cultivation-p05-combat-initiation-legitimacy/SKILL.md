## Area 4 — Combat Initiation Legitimacy

### 4.1 — The Legitimacy Principle

Not all violence is combat under this Part's governance. The SSC Canon recognizes distinct categories of violent interaction, each with different governance rules and consequence implications. The category of violence determines which laws apply — combat rules, assassination rules (Part 24), accident rules (Part 27), or combinations thereof.

A violent act is classified as **combat** only when it meets the recognition conditions in Section 4.3. Violence that does not meet these conditions is classified under one of the non-combat categories and governed accordingly. This classification has narrative consequences — a character who commits assault when they claim to be engaging in combat is operating under a false legitimacy frame, which has social, legal, and consequence implications per Parts 25, 22, and 27.

### 4.2 — Force Legitimacy Categories

**Category FC-1 — Recognized Combat:**
Both parties are consciously engaged, mutually aware of hostility, and physically capable of responding. Combat rules apply in full. Both parties bear equal standing under the rules of engagement. This is the standard and default combat category.

**Category FC-2 — Declared Strike:**
The initiating party announces their intent to engage before striking. The target has a window — however brief — to prepare, retreat, or respond. The announcement need not be formal: readying a weapon visibly, releasing aura as a challenge signal, or a shouted warning all qualify. A Declared Strike becomes Recognized Combat the moment the target responds or the strike lands.

**Category FC-3 — Defensive Response:**
A response to a hostile action directed at oneself or one's recognized charges. Defensive Response is always categorized as Recognized Combat for the defending party, regardless of the initiating party's legitimacy status. A target of an assassination attempt may respond with full combat force even if the initiator's action was not recognized combat.

**Category FC-4 — Ambush / Surprise Attack:**
The initiating party achieves operational surprise — the target has no advance awareness of hostile intent. An ambush is tactically legitimate in combat culture but carries cultural and moral weight (Part 25). The moment the ambushed party becomes aware and responds, the engagement becomes Recognized Combat. Note: ambush on a protected person or in a protected zone is not combat — it is assault or assassination regardless of the power level of the parties.

**Category FC-5 — Assassination:**
Targeted killing with no combat engagement — the attacker's intent is to eliminate the target without allowing engagement. Assassination is classified separately from combat and is governed by Part 24 (Information and Secrecy Law) for information mechanics and by Part 27 (Consequence Law) for outcome management. Part 5 governs assassination only in the following respects: a target who survives an assassination attempt may respond under FC-3 (Defensive Response); an assassination that fails and becomes an engagement is reclassified as FC-4 (Ambush) at the moment of mutual awareness.

**Category FC-6 — Collateral Force:**
Power discharge that damages non-combatants or non-combat zones as a consequence of combat. Collateral Force is not combat directed at the non-combatants — it is the spatial consequence of combat actions. Collateral Force is governed by Area 9 (Battlefield Containment) and creates consequence obligations under Part 27. Cultivators are responsible for their Collateral Force regardless of intent.

**Category FC-7 — Power Accident:**
Unintended energy discharge — breakthrough states, Qi Deviation, Core instability, or power loss control that produces destructive force without combative intent. Power Accidents are not classified as combat. They may produce consequences identical to combat damage, but the absence of combat intent removes the initiating party from combat governance and places them under Part 27 alone. A Power Accident does not create an engagement — but it may trigger a Defensive Response (FC-3) from those who perceive it as an attack.

### 4.3 — Combat Recognition Conditions

For a violent interaction to be recognized as combat under this Part, **all three** of the following conditions must be met:

**Condition CR-1 — Capability of Engagement:**
Both parties (or at minimum the initiating party) must be conscious, physically capable of combat action, and not in a recognized protected status at the time of initiation. A sleeping cultivator cannot initiate combat. A surrendered cultivator cannot be engaged in combat (violation of Area 12 surrender rules). A cultivator in mid-breakthrough cannot meaningfully engage in combat (they may emit power — that is Power Accident, not combat).

**Condition CR-2 — Mutual Awareness Threshold:**
At least one party must be aware of the hostile intent of the other. This condition is met by: overt hostile action by either party, aura release with aggressive signature, a formal or informal declaration of intent, or the ambush moment of detection (even one-sided awareness satisfies the threshold because the aware party is now engaged). Purely unconscious collateral damage from an unaware cultivator does not meet this condition.

**Condition CR-3 — Overt Hostile Action:**
At least one party must have taken a hostile action — defined as any physical, energetic, or soul-based action directed toward causing harm to the other party. Positioning, preparation, and aura assessment are not overt hostile actions unless the aura release constitutes a challenge declaration (see Section 4.4). The first technique activation, weapon draw with hostile intent, or physical strike constitutes the overt hostile action threshold.

### 4.4 — Pre-Combat Declaration Doctrine

A cultivator may choose to declare combat intent before taking overt action. This is the norm in cultivation society for fights between recognized cultivators of comparable standing. Declaration forms include:

- **Aura Release Declaration:** Releasing one's full aura signature directed at a specific target is universally recognized in cultivation society as a combat declaration.
- **Formal Verbal Challenge:** An explicit statement of intent to fight ("I challenge you," "Draw your weapon," "This ends now").
- **Weapon Presentation:** Openly drawing a weapon and directing it toward a specific target.
- **Cultivation Technique Activation with Visible Charge:** Activating a technique in preparation (charging energy visibly) before releasing it.

Declaration is not required for combat to be valid — the Ambush classification (FC-4) demonstrates that surprise engagement is legitimate. However, declaration affects the cultural and social standing of the initiating party (Part 25) and may affect the severity of consequences under Part 27 depending on whether the situation warranted silence or whether declaration could have been reasonably expected.

### 4.5 — Protected Status

The following categories of individuals cannot be engaged in combat without violating Force Legitimacy — attacks on protected individuals are classified as assault or assassination regardless of the attacker's intent to call it combat:

**PS-1 — Non-Cultivator Civilians in Non-Declared Combat Zones:**
An ordinary person who has taken no hostile action and is not in a zone declared as a battlefield under Area 9 protocols is protected. A cultivator who attacks such a person is committing assault, not engaging in combat.

**PS-2 — Formally Surrendered Combatants:**
A combatant who has formally and unambiguously surrendered (Area 12) and whose surrender has been formally accepted by the opposing party is under protected status. Further violence against a surrendered combatant is assault, not combat.

**PS-3 — Individuals Under Recognized Truce:**
A truce is a formal or mutually recognized temporary cessation of hostilities. Attacking during a truce is a Truce Violation — classified as assault and carrying maximum consequence weight under Part 27. Truce violations are among the most severe breaches in cultivation society (Part 25).

**PS-4 — Diplomatic Figures Under Recognized Diplomatic Status:**
Envoys, ambassadors, and negotiators operating under recognized diplomatic function are protected from combat engagement while their diplomatic status is active. Attacking a diplomat is not combat — it is a declaration of absolute hostility against the represented party and is treated as an act of war (E6 initiation trigger) regardless of the power level of the attacker.

**PS-5 — Children Below Cultivation Age:**
Those who have not yet entered cultivation and who are not directly participating in combat as combatants retain protected status regardless of the conflict tier. Child soldiers forced into combat do not lose protection retroactively — the culpability transfers to the party that conscripted them.

**PS-6 — Cultivators in Medical Status:**
A cultivator who is receiving active treatment for wounds in a recognized treatment zone (Area 9.3) retains protected status while in that zone. Attacking a treatment zone is not combat — it is an atrocity classified under Part 27 at maximum severity.

### 4.6 — Initiation During Protected Periods

Violence initiated during protected periods — truces, surrender windows, negotiations, diplomatic visits — is never classified as combat under this Part. It is always assault or assassination and carries the full weight of consequence law (Part 27) without the mechanical protections that combat classification provides.

A cultivator who is attacked during a protected period may respond with full combat force under FC-3 (Defensive Response). Their response does not constitute a truce violation — the violation belongs entirely to the initiating party.
