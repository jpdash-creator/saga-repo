## Area 3 — Villain Psychology

### 3.1 — The Villain's Psychological Architecture

Every villain's psychology is governed in full by Part 26. The villain has a four-layer psychological architecture (Conscious / Emotional-Somatic / Identity / Will) that has been shaped by their history. What distinguishes villain psychology from hero psychology is not the presence of wounds — it is the direction in which those wounds have been resolved.

**P18-3.1 — Villain Psychology Drives Method:** The villain's psychology is not merely background. It is the direct driver of how they pursue their ideology. A villain with a deep fear-wound (Part 26 PIS-7 category: Existential Terror) will construct their power architecture differently than a villain with a grief-wound (PIS-6: Loss). Their psychology shapes their tactical choices, their relationship to subordinates, their response to betrayal, and their behavior under pressure.

**Rule 18.3.1.1 — Villain Psychological Wounds Are Real:** The villain's psychological wounds are canonical — they occurred through real events (CSS-governed, IE-governed) and produced real PIS states. The wounds were not healed through maladaptive resolution pathways. This is how a character with real wounds becomes a villain rather than a damaged protagonist working toward healing.

### 3.2 — The Five Villain Psychological Archetypes

Five psychological orientations are most common in Starward Saga villains, though all Part 26 psychological states are available:

| Villain Psychological Archetype | Part 26 Source | Narrative Expression |
|----------------------------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **The Armored** | Identity-layer wound (PIS-6, Identity Fracture) covered by power accumulation | The villain who has built their entire identity on dominance because their authentic self was destroyed; challenge to their dominance is an existential threat |
| **The Consumed** | Grief + Obsession (PIS-7, Obsession Impact Category) | A character who has transferred all their capacity for human connection into the pursuit of their goal; the goal is now a replacement for everything lost |
| **The Calcified** | Chronic PIS-5+ unresolved, Maladaptive pathway (Part 26 Area 9) | A character who experienced severe psychological wounds early and used maladaptive coping until the coping mechanisms became the character; no longer aware they are coping |
| **The Fractured** | Dissociation / Self-Concept Rupture (PIS-8) | A character who has lost coherent self-continuity; operates in compartmentalized modes; may have genuine moments of lucidity that reveal what they were before |
| **The Hollow** | Existential wound + Ideological Void (PIS-9+, near IS-5 or IS-5) | A character who has passed through so much suffering and consequence that the ordinary psychological architecture is no longer present; operates on will alone |

### 3.3 — Villain Psychological Trajectory

**Rule 18.3.3.1 — Villain Psychology Worsens Under Pressure:** Unlike hero psychology, which is structured to move toward healing (Part 26 resolution pathways), villain psychology under the pressure of the hero's resistance typically worsens. PIS states escalate as the villain faces increasing consequence, ideological challenge, and loss of control. This is not universal — a villain who genuinely encounters an IE-7 event may begin a resolution arc — but escalation is the default trajectory.

**Rule 18.3.3.2 — Villain Psychological Degradation Is Visible:** As a villain's PIS state worsens, it must be visible in their behavior. The Part 26 pre-combat psychological state modifiers apply to villains in combat — a villain at PIS-7 is not operating at full capability. This is intentional: the hero who has destabilized the villain psychologically has changed the nature of the final confrontation.

**Rule 18.3.3.3 — Villain Psychological Complexity Prohibits Cartoonish Simplicity:** No villain in the Starward Saga can be narrated as psychologically simple. They cannot simply enjoy cruelty without a psychological structure that explains it. They cannot be fearless without that fearlessness having a reason. They cannot love nothing without a history that made love impossible or dangerous for them. Psychological simplicity in a villain is a narration standard violation (N18-7).
