## Area 12 — Narration Standards for Technology

### 12.1 — Technology Introduction Standards

High-tier technology must be introduced in prose before it is deployed functionally.

**Rule 12.1.1 — Environmental Presence Before Deployment:** Technology's presence in the world is established through environmental description — infrastructure, ambient sounds of machinery, architectural signatures of technological civilization, energy systems in operation — before specific technology is deployed as a narrative instrument. A weapons system in a Tier 8 civilization is set against a backdrop of stellar energy collection arrays, megastructure segments, and civilization-scale AI infrastructure. The technology's world is established first.

**Rule 12.1.2 — Tier-Appropriate Character Reaction:** Technology of a tier significantly above a character's experience produces proportionate reactions. A Mortal Tier 1 cultivator encountering Tier 8 technology for the first time should react with the narrative weight of their first encounter with a peak Divine cultivator — power exists here that is not comprehensible from their current frame of reference.

**Rule 12.1.3 — Named Technology Introduction:** When a specific Named technology system first appears in the narrative, its name appears in context through the same standard as a Named Move or Named Relic introduction.

**Rule 12.1.4 — Technology Density Calibration:** The density of technology description in prose should be proportionate to how central technology is to the scene's narrative purpose. A scene set in a Tier 7 civilization's orbital station should feel differently dense with technology details than a scene in a Tier 2 city. Authors avoid treating all tiers as equivalent in narrative texture.

### 12.2 — Capability Rendering Standards

Technology capabilities must be rendered in strict accordance with their tier. Authors must not:

- Represent Tier 2 technology as capable of Tier 9 outputs
- Introduce plot convenience technology that appears only to solve a specific narrative problem without established tier context
- Use technology to bypass cultivation rules without explicit governance in Part 7 (cultivation-technology interface)
- Have characters casually deploy technology far above the established civilization tier without narrative explanation

**Rule 12.2.1 — Consistency After Establishment:** Once a civilization's technology tier has been established, all subsequent technology references must remain consistent with that tier unless a Part 9 (Growth Law) governed advancement event has occurred.

**Rule 12.2.2 — The 20-Tier Rendering Distinction:** The eight intermediate tiers (6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17) must be rendered distinctly from the anchor tiers they bridge. A Tier 6 civilization feels demonstrably different from a Tier 5 civilization — not merely "better at planetary things" but genuinely operating at a different scale (inner solar system as operational space vs. single planet). Authors should identify the key qualitative difference between each tier and make it visible in prose.

### 12.3 — Technology vs. Cultivation Narrative Balance

*Synergy-side narration standards moved to Part 31 (Cultivation-Technology Synergy Law), Area 17 as of v2.1. See Part 31, Area 17 for narration standards on rendering synergistic effects, integration model identification in prose, Integrated Practitioner narration, and non-combat synergy world-building.*

**Rule 12.3.1 — Neither Track Is Background:** In a cultivation-technology civilization, neither cultivation nor technology serves as narrative backdrop. Both have active, present weight. Technology is not set-dressing for the cultivation story, nor is cultivation a flavor element in a technology story.

**Rule 12.3.2 — Different Narrative Registers:** Cultivation's narrative weight is at the individual-power register. Technology's narrative weight is often at the civilizational and environmental register. These registers complement each other. Part 31, Area 17 governs the synergy narrative register when both tracks integrate.
