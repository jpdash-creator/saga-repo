## Area 8 — Consequence by Action Type

### 8.1 — Combat and Violence Consequences

**Legitimate Combat Consequences (Part 5 — CR classification met):**
Legitimate combat consequences are limited by the force legitimacy framework of Part 5. They produce physical, psychological, and relational consequences but do not trigger criminal, political, or cross-factional systemic consequences beyond those that naturally arise from the power dynamics involved.

**Illegitimate Violence Consequences:**
Violence outside the legitimate combat framework (Part 5 force legitimacy) produces amplified systemic consequences: political classification as aggressor, criminal or outlaw status within relevant jurisdictions, escalated faction response authorization.

**Collateral Damage Consequences:**
Collateral damage — harm to non-combatants or protected entities — produces consequences even when the primary combat action was legitimate:
- CSS-3 to CSS-5: Consequences for moderate collateral damage (standard in most large-scale combat)
- CSS-5 to CSS-7: Consequences for extreme collateral damage (entire districts, villages, or protected locations)
- CSS-8+: Consequences for civilizational-scale collateral damage

**Assassination Consequences:**
The assassination of a significant target — rather than death in open combat — carries amplified political and relational consequences because:
- It bypasses the Part 5 engagement framework entirely
- It demonstrates willingness to act outside the combat legitimacy system
- It produces a different quality of threat signal: "This actor is not constrained by combat culture"
- CSS-5 to CSS-8 depending on the target's significance

### 8.2 — Cultivation Use Consequences

**Forbidden Technique Use:**
Cultivation techniques classified as forbidden under relevant faction or civilizational law (cross-reference Part 25 cultural taboo systems and Part 22 faction law) produce systemic consequences beyond the immediate technique effect:
- Automatic factional and political classification event
- Permanent technique-use record in any jurisdiction that witnessed or records it
- Potential pursuit by enforcement mechanisms

**Qi Deviation-Induced Harm:**
When a character's Qi Deviation (Part 3) causes harm to others, the character bears consequence despite the Qi Deviation being initially involuntary — because the cultivation practice that led to the Qi Deviation was the character's choice. The consequence profile is adjusted for the involuntary nature (CSS reduced relative to deliberate action) but is not zero.

**Overpowered Technique Use Without Escalation Authorization:**
Deploying cultivation techniques at an escalation tier above what the Part 5 context authorized produces consequences equivalent to illegal escalation — the fact that the character had the power to do it does not legitimize having done it.

### 8.3 — Technology Use Consequences

**Weapon Tier Misuse:**
Using weapons above the authorized tier for the conflict context (Part 33, Part 5 joint governance) produces both immediate escalation consequences (Part 5 escalation violation) and systemic consequences (Part 27): political classification as a threat at the weapon's WS class level, arms control response from relevant governance structures, and permanent record modification.

**Autonomous Weapon Deployment Consequences:**
Deploying AT-3 or AT-4 autonomous weapons (Part 33, Area 12) produces faction-level consequences for which the deploying faction bears full accountability per Rule 33.12.1. If the autonomous weapon causes collateral damage, the deploying faction's consequence profile includes all collateral damage consequences.

**Technology-Facilitated Information Violation:**
Using technology to violate information security (Part 24) or to conduct surveillance, espionage, or data theft produces systemic consequences in proportion to the information accessed, the relationship violated, and the use made of the stolen information.

### 8.4 — Betrayal and Broken Commitment Consequences

**Formal Covenant Breach:**
Breaking a formally witnessed covenant (sworn alliance, discipleship commitment, ruling mandate) produces the most severe relational and systemic consequences in the consequence catalog:
- The formal witnesses to the covenant now bear social consequence for having witnessed it — they must respond or become complicit in its violation
- The breaching party loses all political legitimacy derived from the covenant
- CSS-6 to CSS-9 depending on the covenant's scale and the nature of the breach

**Sect/Faction Betrayal:**
Betraying one's own sect or faction — providing information to enemies, sabotaging operations, defecting with stolen knowledge — produces consequences at multiple levels:
- The betrayed faction's consequence response (pursuit, blacklisting, retaliation)
- The receiving faction's consequence relationship (those who benefit from betrayal are tainted by association with betrayers)
- The character's own cultivation consequence: betrayal of core relational commitments is one of the clearest pathways to Heart contamination
- CSS-6 to CSS-8

**Master-Disciple Betrayal:**
The most significant form of relational betrayal in a cultivation world — the betrayal of the cultivation bond between master and disciple — carries exceptional consequence weight because this bond is both personally the deepest and culturally (Part 25) the most sacred. Whether the betrayal runs master-to-disciple (exploitation, abandonment, sacrifice of the disciple for the master's advancement) or disciple-to-master (betrayal of transmitted knowledge, attack on the master, revelation of the master's secrets), the consequence profile is CSS-7 minimum.
