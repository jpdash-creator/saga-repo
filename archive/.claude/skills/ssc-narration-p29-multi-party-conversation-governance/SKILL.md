## Area 8 — Multi-Party Conversation Governance

### 8.1 — The Multi-Party Mandate

**Rule 29.8.1.1 — Multi-Party Scenes Are the Standard:** The Starward Saga is a world of factions, sects, alliances, hierarchies, and ensemble relationships. Most scenes involve more than two characters. The default state of the Starward Saga scene is multi-party. This means the default state of Starward Saga dialogue is multi-party conversation governance.

**Rule 29.8.1.2 — No Character May Be Passive in Their Own Scene:** Every character present in a scene — hero, villain, side character of any tier — has a stake in what is happening. That stake expresses itself through active participation in the dialogue. Passive presence — a named character standing in a scene and saying nothing while others speak — is a violation unless the silence is itself governed (see Area 2.2.2).

### 8.2 — Conversation Dynamics in Groups

**Rule 29.8.2.1 — Social Role Determines Conversational Position:** In any group conversation, each character's social role determines their default conversational position — who initiates, who responds, who defers, who can challenge, who cannot. This social architecture must be reflected in the dialogue but must also be navigated, challenged, or violated by characters who are working against or beyond their assigned role.

**Rule 29.8.2.2 — Private Channels Within Public Conversation:** In multi-party conversations, characters often have things they are communicating specifically to one other party while ostensibly speaking to the group. Through cultivation soul-sense communication, through the specific weight of a glance, through the words chosen to carry double meaning, characters within a group conversation may be simultaneously conducting a second, private conversation. This dual-channel dynamic is a primary feature of elite multi-party Starward Saga dialogue and must be rendered with sufficient precision that the reader can track both channels.

**Rule 29.8.2.3 — Ensemble Ideological Diversity in Dialogue:** Per N19-5 (Part 19), the hero's ensemble must represent at least four distinct Part 20 ideology categories. This diversity must be legible in multi-party dialogue — characters with different ideologies genuinely disagree, genuinely emphasize different things, genuinely reach different conclusions from the same information. A scene in which an ideologically diverse ensemble all respond identically to a situation has failed the ensemble diversity requirement.

### 8.3 — Faction and Institutional Dialogue

When characters speak as representatives of their factions — in faction councils, diplomatic encounters, military briefings, sect deliberations — the dialogue must render both the institutional voice and the individual voice simultaneously.

**Rule 29.8.3.1 — Institutional Speech Contains Personal Stakes:** A character speaking as a faction representative carries both their faction's position and their own personal position. Often these are in tension. The dialogue must render both — what the character is required to say as a faction representative and what they actually believe, want, or fear as an individual — through the friction between official speech and the personal pressures that distort or crack it.

**Rule 29.8.3.2 — Faction Dialogue Reflects Faction Culture:** The way a military sect deliberates is different from the way a scholarly faction debates. The way a criminal organization negotiates is different from the way a diplomatic embassy conducts a formal encounter. Part 22's faction taxonomy and Part 25's cultural norms together determine the specific style of institutional dialogue for every faction type.
