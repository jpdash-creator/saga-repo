## Area 25 — Foundational Principles Addendum: Deep Governance Principles

### 25.1 — P8-9: The Technology Diversity Principle

**P8-9 — Technology Diversity Reflects Civilizational History:**
No two civilizations at the same technology tier have identical domain profiles. The 14-domain structure means that even civilizations at the same majority-domain tier differ significantly in their domain-by-domain strengths, gaps, and specializations. Technology tier describes a ceiling and a floor; the texture within that range is civilizational biography.

A civilization that grew up on a resource-poor world may have exceptional Engineering & Architecture (Type 6) and Transport (Type 9) because survival required built environments — and may have underdeveloped Environmental & Terraforming (Type 11) because there was little natural environment to manage. A cultivation-heavy civilization may have exceptional Cultivation Integration Technology (Type 12) and Medical (Type 5) — because cultivation healing shaped their medical domain — and underdeveloped Military (Type 8) because cultivators handled military needs.

Authors should use domain-profile heterogeneity to give civilizations narrative distinctiveness beyond their tier number.

### 25.2 — P8-10: Technology Tier Is Assessed at Civilization Scale

**P8-10 — Individual Access Is Not Civilizational Tier:**
The technology tier a civilization is classified at describes the technology its institutional infrastructure can produce, sustain, and deploy. An individual's access to technology within the civilization may be much lower (Rule 11.1.1 — access stratification) or in exceptional cases slightly higher (through factional resources, exceptional wealth, or institutional position).

The civilization-level tier is the governance parameter for world-law purposes. Individual technology access is a social and political question — governed by Part 22 (Faction Law) and Part 25 (Culture Law) when those Parts are authored. Part 8 governs civilizational classification, not individual access.

**Application:** A character who is the head of a leading-edge technology faction in a Tier 6 civilization may personally have access to some Tier 7 prototypes. This does not make the civilization Tier 7. The civilization remains Tier 6; the character has exceptional individual access within that civilization's one-tier tolerance (Rule 5.1.1).

### 25.3 — P8-11: The Technology-Cultivation Correspondence Is Bidirectional Tendency Only

**P8-11 — The Tendency Table Is Neither a Requirement nor a Guarantee:**
The Soft Correspondence Tendency Table (now housed in **Part 31, Area 13**) reflects the typical co-occurrence pattern observed across many civilizations where both tracks have developed under broadly similar conditions without systematic acceleration or suppression of either track. It is not a requirement, guarantee, ceiling, or floor — it is a world-building reference.

For the full tendency table, interpretation guidance, narrative use of deviation, and the bidirectional tendency principle: see **Part 31, Area 13**. For the governance statement that the tendency is bidirectional and non-binding: see **Part 31, Area 16.5**.

*Quick summary:* Authors calibrate civilizations against the tendency table, not against a governance rule. Civilizations significantly outside the range are narratively significant precisely because of the deviation.

### 25.4 — P8-12: Technology Does Not Determine Plot

**P8-12 — Technology Tier Governs World-Law Capability Boundaries; Plot Is Governed by Story:**
The Technology Ceiling and tier system govern what is physically and institutionally possible in the Starward Saga world. They do not determine what happens in any narrative arc. Part 8 is a capability governance document, not a plot governance document.

Within the capability boundaries Part 8 establishes, authors have complete creative freedom to construct whatever plot they find compelling. A Tier 5 civilization can have any kind of political structure, any kind of cultivation-technology conflict, any kind of characters, any kind of story. Part 8 ensures that the story's technology capability claims remain consistent with the world's physics and institutional logic — it does not constrain the story's content, emotional arc, character decisions, or thematic exploration.
