The eight Part 29 Voice Determinants (realm/tech tier/CPT/ideology IS-state/PIS/social position/relationship/information state) are read from Character Ledger Sections 2 (Classification), 4 (Ideology Profile), 5 (Psychological Profile), and 7 (Relationships and Faction Positions) during the Pre-Write Ledger Check (Part 1 Area 6.2) before any dialogue-significant prose. Dialogue that constitutes an information revelation event (a character learning something canonically significant) triggers Post-Write Ledger Update (Part 1 Area 6.3) to the ledger-people-society-information folder. The [NEXT PROSE] command (C-4) invokes the Part 29 N29-12 Simultaneous Governance Standard (Parts 29+2+20+25+26+16 all active) as part of its full compliance cycle. |

FCIA runs on every accepted prose unit and at session end; ALCA Mode A runs on every edit to this file; ALCA Mode B runs at session end. [RUN CANON AUDIT] (C-10) manually invokes the FCIA. | Canon-Part 7 | Part 29 → Part 7 — Active | Dialogue about fusion processes, fusion stages, and fusion architecture reflects the character's cultivation realm and Part 7 knowledge; the vocabulary of fusion in speech is cultivation-world vocabulary governed by Part 12.1.1 of this Part |
| Canon-Part 8 | Part 29 ↔ Part 8 — Active | Technology tier governs vocabulary and epistemic assumptions in technology-world dialogue (Part 29 Area 12.2); higher technology tiers have access to conceptual frameworks unavailable at lower tiers; technology dialogue must be tier-appropriate and must not attribute higher-tier conceptual frameworks to lower-tier characters |
| Canon-Part 9 | Part 29 → Part 9 — Active | Dialogue about technology advancement, research progress, and breakthrough events reflects Part 9 mechanics; technology specialists speak about advancement in the specific vocabulary of Part 9 growth mechanisms |
| Canon-Part 10 | Part 29 → Part 10 — Active | Scientific dialogue — discussion of scientific knowledge, research paradigms, and discovery — must reflect Part 10 four-layer knowledge stack; characters speak from within their specific knowledge tier and cannot articulate concepts that belong to knowledge layers above their access level |
| Canon-Part 11 | Part 29 ↔ Part 11 — Active | Dialogue about energy substances (CPM, Sacred Ground resources) uses cultivation-world vocabulary governed by Part 29 Area 12.1; the Part 11 Narrative Introduction Sequence (Area 2) governs when substance states may be named in dialogue — a character cannot reference Divine Liquid in dialogue before the story has introduced it per the narrative sequence |
| Canon-Part 12 | Part 29 → Part 12 — Active | Dialogue about relics, artifacts, and formation arrays uses relic-specific vocabulary; the social weight of discussing a Tier 4 relic vs. a Tier 1 relic must be legible in how characters speak about them |
| Canon-Part 13 | Part 29 ↔ Part 13 — Active | Location-specific dialogue — characters discussing their cosmological location, describing Sacred Grounds, referencing the geography of their world — must reflect Part 13 cosmological hierarchy; the reverence or familiarity with which a character describes a specific location tier reflects their own tier relative to it |
| Canon-Part 14 | Part 29 → Part 14 — Active | Dialogue about distance and travel uses Part 14 CMS units; characters speak of distance in the vocabulary appropriate to their civilization tier (a Mortal Tier 1 character does not reference light-year distances in ordinary speech) |
| Canon-Part 15 | Part 29 → Part 15 — Active | Travel dialogue reflects Part 15 travel law; characters speak about travel modes, gate networks, and transit times using vocabulary consistent with their access to travel technology and cultivation |

| Canon-Part 26 | Part 29 ↔ Part 26 — Bidirectional Active | PIS state governs speech pattern alteration per Part 29 Area 10; PIS-state-specific speech effects are detailed in Part 29 Rule 29.10.1.1 table; healing dialogue (Part 29 Rule 29.10.2.1) is a Part 26 resolution pathway event; Part 26 coping mechanisms (adaptive and maladaptive) are visible in speech patterns — maladaptive humor, intellectualization, deflection, and emotional shutting-down all manifest as specific dialogue behaviors |
| Canon-Part 27 | Part 29 ↔ Part 27 — Active | Dialogue produces CSS consequences; a revelation in conversation is a CSS event; a betrayal spoken aloud is a CSS event; a confession that creates relational rupture is a CSS event; Part 27 consequence tracking must account for dialogue-origin consequences; dialogue in the aftermath of a CSS-5+ event reflects the consequence weight per Part 29 Area 7.3 |

| Canon-Part 33 | Part 29 → Part 33 — Active | Weapon-related dialogue — discussion of WU-class weapons, Named Weapon capabilities, weapon discharge events — uses Part 33 weapon taxonomy vocabulary; a character's relationship to their weapons is revealed in how they speak about them, consistent with their WU-class and cultivation tier |
