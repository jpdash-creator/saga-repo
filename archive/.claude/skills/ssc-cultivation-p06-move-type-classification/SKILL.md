## Area 2 — Move Type Classification

### 2.1 — Overview

Move Types classify the **complexity, rarity, and execution difficulty** of Named Moves. They are the vertical axis of the taxonomy — from the simplest, most reliable techniques to the rarest, most uncertain reality-bending executions. Every Named Move belongs to exactly one of the eight Move Types. Type does not change after a move's creation unless the move undergoes a formal Type Upgrade (Area 9.3).

**The Mastery System:**
Practice and repeated use improve success rates. Each move type has a **base success rate** (achieved by a practitioner with minimal mastery) and a **maximum mastery rate** (achieved after sustained dedicated practice). Between these are five mastery grades:

| Mastery Grade | Definition | Success Rate Modifier |
|--------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Novice | First-learned; mechanically understood but inconsistent | Base rate |
| Practiced | Regular use; execution reliable in optimal conditions | Base + 5% |
| Adept | Internalized; reliable under moderate combat stress | Base + 10% |
| Proficient | Deep familiarity; reliable even under significant pressure | Base + 15% |
| Expert | Near-complete mastery; fails only under extreme conditions | Base + 20% |
| Master | Complete mastery; at maximum mastery rate | Maximum rate |

**Success Rate and Combat Conditions:**
Success rates are stated for optimal combat conditions. The following modifiers apply:
- **Severe injury:** −5% to −15% depending on injury severity
- **Energy depletion (below 30% reserves):** −10% on Intermediate and above
- **Opponent active disruption:** −5% to −20% depending on disruption technique
- **Optimal conditions (full energy, no disruption, preferred terrain):** +5% on all types except Basic
- **Heart State modifiers:** See Area 1.5 for Heart-based success rate adjustments under pressure

### 2.2 — The Eight Move Types

---

#### Type 1 — Basic

**Definition:** The first spark of intentional power expression. Basic moves are the foundational building blocks of all cultivation combat — simple techniques that establish control over energy flow, physical execution, and the fundamental principles of combat engagement. They are not beginner moves in the sense of being limited; they are essential moves in the sense of being the most reliable expressions of a cultivator's power.

**Base Success Rate:** 100% — A cultivator who has learned a Basic technique executes it reliably every time under non-disrupted conditions. This is the only Type where Novice mastery already achieves 100%.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 100% (already achieved at Novice; mastery deepens output refinement and speed, not reliability)

**Nature of Practice:** "Dependent on practice and usage" means the following for Basic moves: practice does not improve the success rate (already 100%) but significantly improves **execution speed**, **energy efficiency**, **output precision**, and **integration with other techniques**. A Master-level Basic move executes in a fraction of the time of a Novice-level Basic move and depletes far less energy for the same output. A cultivator who has used their Basic moves for decades makes them look effortless — because they are.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Accessible from the first moment of cultivation. A Mortal Tier 1 cultivator can have Basic Named Moves (physical techniques). Basic moves remain valid and useful at every cultivation level — a Divine Sea Realm cultivator's Basic techniques are among the most destructive actions possible in the world, simply because they have Divine-Sea-Realm power behind them.

**Move Output:** Scales entirely with the cultivator's current power density and Heavenly Scale Grade. The technique is basic in complexity; the output is proportional to the cultivator's tier.

**Energy Cost:** Minimal to Low — Basic moves are optimized for efficiency. Even Mortal Tier 2 practitioners can deploy Basic moves repeatedly without meaningful energy drain.

**Narrative Role:** Basic moves are the combat vocabulary. They appear most in sustained exchanges, rapid sequences, and situations where reliability matters more than complexity. A master making a Basic move look devastating is a classic cultivation narrative moment — the gap between the cultivator's power and the technique's simplicity reveals just how advanced they are.

**Examples of Basic Move Names:** **Iron Body Strike**, **Swift Stepping Wind**, **Qi Surge Guard**, **Earth Pressure Stance**, **Single Point Release**

---

#### Type 2 — Intermediate

**Definition:** Refined execution adding efficiency and minor tactical depth to the cultivator's combat expression. Intermediate moves require genuine understanding of energy circulation patterns beyond the basic, allowing the cultivator to introduce secondary effects, improved targeting, or efficiency gains that Basic moves cannot achieve.

**Base Success Rate:** 80% — Under standard combat conditions, an Intermediate technique executes successfully in 4 out of 5 attempts. The 20% failure rate at Novice mastery reflects the additional complexity of managing secondary effects and refined energy flows.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 100% — Full mastery eliminates the failure rate. An Intermediate move at Master level is executed with complete reliability, making it functionally equivalent to a Basic move in terms of success guarantee, while retaining its additional tactical depth.

**Nature of Practice:** Intermediate moves reward consistent practice with incremental reliability gains. A cultivator who regularly deploys their Intermediate techniques in combat and training will advance from Novice to Master over months to years (depending on talent and conditions). "Dependent on practice and usage" means that an Intermediate technique which is never used under pressure remains at Novice or Practiced mastery — theoretical knowledge is not the same as combat-conditioned execution.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Accessible from Mortal Tier 2 and above. Exceptional Mortal Tier 1 martial artists operating near their physical peak may develop physical Intermediate techniques, but this is uncommon.

**Secondary Effects:** Intermediate moves typically introduce one secondary effect beyond the primary action — a binding element in a strike, an efficiency multiplier in a defensive technique, a minor terrain interaction in a mobility move, etc.

**Energy Cost:** Low to Moderate.

**Narrative Role:** Intermediate moves are the workhorses of cultivator combat at the lower and middle tiers. They represent the gap between raw strength and genuine combat intelligence. A cultivator who has mastered Intermediate-level techniques before advancing in realm is a significantly more dangerous fighter than one who has not.

**Examples:** **Double Spiral Release**, **Flowing River Guard**, **Qi-Threaded Footstep**, **Twin-Point Pressure Strike**, **Wind Peel Dodge**

---

#### Type 3 — Advanced

**Definition:** Complex techniques with layered effects that begin shaping the battlefield. Advanced moves operate on multiple levels simultaneously — primary effect, secondary effect, and a tertiary influence on the combat environment or on the opponent's cultivation function. They require deep internalization of qi behavior, affinity expression (if applicable), or soul force management.

**Base Success Rate:** 60% — Advanced moves fail in 40% of attempts at Novice mastery. The complexity of managing layered effects introduces genuine execution risk that cannot be eliminated by power alone.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 90% — Even fully mastered Advanced moves retain a 10% failure possibility under optimal conditions. This residual uncertainty reflects the inherent complexity of multi-layered technique management. Under pressure (injury, disruption), Master-level Advanced moves may still fail, making them unsuitable as the sole strategy in crisis situations.

**Nature of Practice:** Advanced moves show the most dramatic improvement from practice — the jump from 60% (Novice) to 90% (Master) is the largest success rate gain in the system. A cultivator who commits to mastering their Advanced techniques transforms their combat profile more noticeably than any other investment. This rewards specialization: a cultivator who focuses on one or two Advanced techniques will dramatically outperform one who collects many without mastering any.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Advanced techniques become reliably accessible at the Xiantian Realm. Exceptional Mortal Tier 2 Monarch-level cultivators with exceptional talent can develop Advanced physical-qi hybrid techniques, but these are uncommon and require extraordinary conditions.

**Battlefield Shaping:** Advanced moves begin to shape the immediate battlefield through their tertiary effects — terrain modification, energy field distortion, cultivation disruption in the immediate area, or persistent environmental interactions that outlast the technique's initial execution.

**Energy Cost:** Moderate to High.

**Narrative Role:** Advanced moves mark the transition from reactive combat to tactical combat. A cultivator who deploys Advanced techniques successfully is thinking several moves ahead. Their failure rate creates genuine narrative tension — will it land?

**Examples:** **Cascading Stone Burial**, **Crimson Tide Dispersal**, **Three-Breath Wind Lock**, **Soul Veil Pressure**, **Fractured Ground Ascent**

---

#### Type 4 — Elite

**Definition:** Highly optimized moves that combine precision, power, and strategic dominance. Elite techniques represent the upper tier of what cultivators in the intermediate cultivation ranges can aspire to — they are the signature capabilities of powerful cultivators, the techniques that define combat reputations and terrify opponents.

**Base Success Rate:** 50% — An Elite technique at Novice mastery succeeds half the time. This is the critical threshold — below 50% and a technique becomes too risky to deploy as a primary strategy. At 50%, it remains viable as a high-risk, high-reward option.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 80% — A Master-level Elite technique succeeds in 4 of 5 attempts under optimal conditions. Under pressure, with Heart State modifiers, a Blazing Heart master may achieve 75% Elite reliability — representing the genuine upper echelon of non-Signature, non-Fusion offensive capability.

**Nature of Practice:** Elite moves require the most deliberate practice methodology of the standard types. Simply deploying them in combat is insufficient — understanding their underlying principle deeply enough to correct execution errors is essential. Many cultivators reach Proficient (70%) and stagnate there, unable to achieve the final insight that pushes to Expert and Master levels. The breakthrough typically requires a conceptual understanding of why the technique works, not just how.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Elite techniques are the natural ceiling of accomplished Xiantian cultivators. Core Realm cultivators develop Elite moves readily. Mortal Tier 2 peak practitioners very rarely achieve genuine Elite-level techniques — when they do, it becomes a defining narrative fact about that character.

**Precision Over Power:** Elite moves are not necessarily the most destructive moves a cultivator uses. What defines them is precision and optimization — the exact right technique at the exact right moment, wasting nothing and achieving the maximum effect with the minimum input. An Elite-level deflection technique might be more decisive than a Catastrophic-level attack in the right combat moment.

**Energy Cost:** High — Elite techniques are among the most energy-intensive standard-type moves. Their efficiency advantage comes from precision, not energy minimization.

**Narrative Role:** Elite moves are the moves that define combat ability. When a cultivator pulls off an Elite move successfully, it is a statement. When they attempt one and fail (50% base), it is a turning point.

**Examples:** **Heaven's Edge Penetration**, **Mirror Void Reversal**, **Ten-Direction Seal**, **Core Pulse Scatter**, **Sovereign Pressure Descent**

---

#### Type 5 — Signature

**Definition:** Unique, identity-bound techniques that define a cultivator's personal legend. A Signature move is not merely a powerful technique — it is an expression of who the cultivator fundamentally is. It embodies their cultivation philosophy, their personal history, their most profound understanding of their own power. No two cultivators can have the same Signature move, because no two cultivators have the same identity.

**Base Success Rate:** 40% — Signature moves succeed less than half the time at Novice mastery level. This is not because they are inherently unstable (like Fusion) but because they draw on something personal and deep that cannot be accessed on command. A cultivator who has not yet fully inhabited their Signature move's principle will fail to connect with it under combat pressure.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 70% — Even a fully mastered Signature move succeeds only 70% of the time under optimal conditions. The residual 30% represents the inherent unpredictability of identity-expression — the Signature move depends not just on technique but on the cultivator's present state, their emotional truth in the moment, their contact with their own deepest self. In moments where that contact is compromised, the Signature move will not fully manifest.

**Identity Binding:** A Signature move belongs to one cultivator. It cannot be learned by another through observation alone or by reading a transmission manual — because the technique's core is not the mechanics but the identity that animates them. Another cultivator might learn the physical form of the technique, but without the originator's specific cultivation history and inner truth, the technique becomes an Advanced-type imitation that lacks the Signature's depth and power. See Area 9 for full Signature Move Identity Law.

**Nature of Practice:** Signature moves develop differently from all other move types. They are not practiced to mechanical perfection — they are deepened through lived experience. Every major combat, every crisis of character, every significant moment in the cultivator's personal journey has the potential to deepen the Signature move's resonance. A cultivator in narrative stagnation will find their Signature move stagnating. A cultivator going through genuine growth will find their Signature move growing with them.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Signature moves can theoretically exist at any cultivation level, but they practically emerge most commonly at Xiantian and above, when the cultivator has enough accumulated cultivation experience to have developed a genuine identity. Mortal Tier 2 cultivators with extraordinary depth of personal experience can develop Signature moves, and when they do, this is a significant character-defining event.

**Energy Cost:** Variable — the Signature move's energy cost is determined by its combat function and type expression, not by a fixed scale. Some Signature moves are surprisingly efficient because the cultivator's deep understanding minimizes waste. Others are enormously costly because they express the full weight of who the cultivator is.

**Narrative Role:** The deployment of a Signature move is always a narrative event. It reveals character, expresses identity, and carries emotional weight beyond its combat function. A Signature move that succeeds is a moment of authentic expression. A Signature move that fails is a crisis of self.

**Examples:** Signature moves are named with deep personal significance. No two are alike. A cultivator who fights with a relentless will might name their Signature move **Unyielding Mountain's Roar**. A cultivator who has survived impossible odds might develop **Still Water After the Storm**. The name encodes the cultivator's story.

---

#### Type 6 — Fusion

**Definition:** Hybrid abilities formed by merging different power streams, affinities, cultivation dimensions, or technique principles into a single move that draws on unpredictable synergy. Fusion moves are not simply "two techniques used at once" — they are the genuine integration of two distinct power streams into a new expression that neither stream could produce alone.

**Base Success Rate:** 20% — Fusion moves succeed only 1 in 5 attempts at Novice mastery. The low success rate reflects the inherent instability of merging distinct power streams, which tend to conflict, cancel, or produce unintended interactions when not perfectly integrated.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 50% — Even Master-level Fusion techniques succeed only half the time. This ceiling exists because the elements being fused are genuinely different from each other — no amount of practice eliminates the fundamental tension between distinct power streams. The ceiling can be raised slightly by exceptional affinity for both elements being fused (a Fire + Lightning dual-affinity cultivator fusing these streams may achieve 55–60% maximum), but the instability never disappears.

**Failure Modes for Fusion:** When a Fusion move fails, the failure is not simply "the technique doesn't work." Fusion failure produces one of four outcomes (determined by the specific elements being fused and the circumstances):
1. **Fizzle:** The integration attempt fails cleanly; the energy disperses harmlessly. Best-case failure.
2. **Partial Expression:** One of the two streams dominates; the technique produces a weaker Advanced-type effect from only one stream.
3. **Distortion:** The streams conflict before integration; the move produces an unintended effect that may be useful, harmful to the user, or neutral.
4. **Backlash:** The streams collide destructively within the cultivator's meridians; energy damage to the cultivator proportional to the move's intended power level. Most dangerous failure mode.

**What Can Be Fused:**
- Two distinct affinity streams (the most common form — Fire + Earth, Space + Time, Life + Death, etc.)
- Physical qi and soul force (creating a simultaneous physical-metaphysical attack)
- Cultivation technique and technology-derived energy (Part 7 governs this at the cultivation-path level; a specific Fusion move is the technique expression of it)
- Two entirely different technique principles (one cultivator's two Elite-level techniques merged into a single deployment)
- Alliance Fusion: two cultivators simultaneously deploying coordinated techniques whose interaction creates a shared Fusion effect

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Fusion techniques require sufficient cultivation development to simultaneously manage two distinct power streams. In practice, this begins at the Xiantian Realm for affinity fusions. Physical + soul fusions require active soul cultivation (Part 4). Technology fusions require access to the technology systems governed by Parts 7–10.

**Energy Cost:** Very High — Fusion moves are among the most energy-intensive techniques in existence. Managing two streams simultaneously costs significantly more than the sum of either stream alone.

**Narrative Role:** A successful Fusion move is a breakthrough moment — the cultivator achieving something genuinely new and unexpected. A failed Fusion move, particularly a Backlash failure, creates the kind of mid-combat crisis that defines combat narratives.

**Examples:** **Thunder-Earth Fracture Pulse** (Thunder + Earth affinity fusion), **Soul-Flame Execution** (physical fire technique + soul strike simultaneous), **Void-Time Obliteration Step** (Space + Time affinity fusion at Harmony level)

---

#### Type 7 — Catastrophic

**Definition:** Devastating techniques capable of overwhelming entire battlefields. Catastrophic moves are defined by their destructive scale — not by their complexity (which may be moderate) but by the sheer volume and intensity of energy deployment that makes them possible. A Catastrophic move is not a refined technique; it is a controlled release of energy at a scale that alters terrain, overwhelms multiple opponents simultaneously, and leaves lasting marks on the environment.

**Base Success Rate:** 20% — Catastrophic moves share the Fusion type's 20% base rate, but for different reasons. Fusion fails due to instability between power streams. Catastrophic moves fail because the energy volume required for their execution often exceeds reliable control capacity. A cultivator attempting a Catastrophic technique is pushing their energy deployment to or beyond their comfortable limit. The failure mode is different from Fusion:
- **Underpowered Expression:** The technique deploys but at reduced scale — Advanced-type output instead of Catastrophic
- **Premature Release:** The energy releases before the technique is fully formed — creates an explosive burst centered on the cultivator rather than at the intended point
- **Collapse:** The technique simply fails to form; massive energy expenditure with no output (devastating resource cost for no effect)

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 40% — Master-level Catastrophic technique execution achieves 40% success. The ceiling is lower than Fusion's maximum because Catastrophic techniques depend on raw energy volume that genuinely approaches the cultivator's sustainable limits. A cultivator who has mastered a Catastrophic technique still faces the physics of deploying more energy than they can fully control with certainty.

**Scale Requirement:** A Catastrophic move must qualify at the battlefield scale appropriate to its user's cultivation level. At Mortal Tier 2 peak, a Catastrophic technique might destroy a building. At Xiantian, it might level a district. At Core, it might devastate a city-scale area. At Divine, it could affect continental geography. The designation "Catastrophic" is relative to the tier — it means this move is at the destructive ceiling of its tier.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** True Catastrophic techniques are accessible from late Xiantian onwards. Mortal Tier 2 cultivators can develop moves that are Catastrophic relative to their tier (building-scale, hundred-person scale) but these are edge cases. Core Realm is the natural tier for Catastrophic move development. At Divine, Catastrophic becomes the standard upper-tier combat tool.

**Energy Cost:** Extreme — Catastrophic techniques consume a significant portion of the cultivator's energy reserves in a single deployment. Multiple Catastrophic deployments in a single combat are uncommon at lower realms. Core Realm and above can sustain more, but even Core Solidification cultivators typically deploy Catastrophic techniques as finishing moves rather than opening gambits.

**Narrative Role:** Catastrophic moves signal that a fight has reached its extreme. Their deployment is a tactical decision — the risk of failure is real, the energy cost is high, but the potential to decisively end a combat justifies the risk. A successful Catastrophic technique usually ends the immediate combat situation.

**Examples:** **Heaven-Sealing Collapse**, **Thousand Sun Detonation**, **Earth-Splitting Divine Fist**, **All-Consuming Void Expansion**, **Core Obliteration Pulse**

#### Type 8 — Mythic

**Definition:** Reality-bending moves that transcend logic and echo across legends. A Mythic technique is not merely powerful — it is an intervention in the structure of reality itself. It operates by principles that lie outside standard cultivation mechanics, drawing on forces or interactions that should not be achievable at any cultivation level. Mythic moves are unique events: they create effects that persist beyond the combat, alter the fabric of the location where they are used, and leave traces in cultivation history.

**Base Success Rate:** 10% — A Mythic technique succeeds 1 in 10 attempts at Novice mastery. This is not a limitation of power — even the most powerful cultivators in the world attempt Mythic techniques at 10% base. The uncertainty is metaphysical: Mythic moves attempt something the universe has a natural resistance to. Reality does not easily permit the kinds of interactions these techniques demand.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 20% — Even the greatest masters of a Mythic technique succeed only 20% of the time. There is no version of mastery that makes Mythic techniques reliable. They remain inherently uncertain at 1 in 5 attempts even when perfectly understood. This is by design — Mythic moves are meant to be events, not tactics. A Mythic technique that could be deployed reliably would break the narrative structure of what these techniques represent.

**Failure Modes for Mythic:** Mythic failure is never trivial:
1. **Reality Rejection:** The universe simply refuses the interaction. The energy dissolves, the technique does not form, and the cultivator is left depleted.
2. **Partial Manifestation:** The technique partially realizes — creating a profound but incomplete effect that may still alter the battlefield significantly.
3. **Uncontrolled Manifestation:** The technique activates but outside the cultivator's control, producing effects the cultivator did not intend and cannot direct.
4. **Backlash of Reality:** The universe's resistance to the technique's demands rebounds into the cultivator, producing damage to their cultivation structure, essence, or lifespan (the cultivation attempt drew on essence rather than cultivated energy).

**What Makes a Move Mythic:**
A technique qualifies as Mythic when it does one or more of the following: reverses a fundamental physical law within its scope, directly interacts with space-time at a level that creates lasting alterations, achieves an effect that cannot be explained by standard cultivation energy mechanics, creates changes to the targeted location that persist permanently and become narrative facts of the world, or operates by drawing on the cultivator's essence or lifespan rather than cultivated energy alone.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Mythic techniques are practically inaccessible below Core Solidification. Theoretical comprehension may exist at lower levels — a cultivator might understand the principle of a Mythic technique without being able to deploy it. In practice, Divine Realm is where Mythic techniques become something that can be genuinely attempted. Even there, only cultivators who have achieved deep enlightenment of the technique's principle approach the 20% maximum mastery rate.

**Mythic Move and the World:**
A successful Mythic technique execution is a world event. It is registered in cultivation records. The location where it occurred is altered. Other cultivators can sense the residue of its reality-interaction for years or millennia afterward. A Part 27 consequence event is mandatory for any successful Mythic technique deployment.

**Energy Cost:** Reality-bending cost — Mythic techniques may deplete not just cultivated energy but draw on the cultivator's fundamental life force, cultivation root, or lifespan. This is why they cannot be casually repeated even by cultivators with enormous energy reserves — the cost is not just energetic but existential.

**Narrative Role:** A Mythic technique is a series-level event. Its deployment should feel like the story has crossed a threshold. Success or failure, the world is not the same after a Mythic technique is attempted.

**Examples:** **World-Shattering Truth**, **Causality's End**, **The Last Heaven**, **Eternal Still Point**, **Unmaking of the Forgotten**
