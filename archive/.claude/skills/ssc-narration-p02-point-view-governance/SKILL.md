## Area 4 — Point of View Governance

### 4.1 — Purpose

Point of View (POV) determines the narrative lens through which the reader experiences each Prose unit. Because immersion is the governing standard of all Prose, POV management is critical — an inconsistent or unexamined POV breaks immersion and fragments the reader's experience.

### 4.2 — POV Consistency Within a Prose Unit

Every Prose unit is written from a single, stable POV. Within a Prose unit, the narrative perspective does not shift. The reader accesses interiority, perception, and emotional experience through one character's lens per Prose. If two characters' interior experiences are both needed within the same scene, the Prose unit is structured to prioritize the POV that most serves the scene's emotional function, with the other character's interior state rendered through external observation (expression, body language, speech subtext).

### 4.3 — POV Transition

When a shift in POV is narratively necessary between Prose units, the transition must be handled through the structural boundary between Areas or Chapters — not within a single continuous Prose. A POV shift within a Prose unit is a violation of immersion. A deliberate POV shift between structural units, handled with clear narrative signaling in the opening lines of the new Prose, is valid.

### 4.4 — Deep POV Standard

All Prose is written in **deep POV** — meaning the reader is not told what a character thinks and feels from a distance but is placed inside the character's perceptual and psychological experience directly. Deep POV eliminates filter language ("she noticed," "he thought," "she felt") in favor of direct experiential rendering ("the air was wrong — too still, too careful, the silence of something holding its breath"). Deep POV is what produces pacing-appropriate prose depth (3500%–5000% per Part 2 Area 6.2.1). Shallow POV produces distance and weakens literature.
