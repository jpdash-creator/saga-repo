## Area 1 — Foundational Principle and Psychological Architecture

### 1.1 — The Psychological System Overview

The psychological dimension of a character in the Starward Saga operates on four interconnected layers:

**Layer 1 — The Conscious Layer (Mind):**
The seat of active thought, decision-making, memory, and rational self-awareness. This is where characters process experiences, form intentions, construct identity narratives, and engage with the world deliberately. Psychological impacts that primarily affect this layer manifest as: intrusive thoughts, decision paralysis, cognitive distortion, memory invasion, and conscious identity crisis.

**Layer 2 — The Emotional-Somatic Layer (Body-Mind):**
The interface between psychological experience and physical body — where emotions manifest as physical sensations, where physiological arousal creates emotional states, and where the body "remembers" trauma even when the conscious mind suppresses it. Psychological impacts at this layer manifest as: involuntary physiological responses (trembling, nausea, dissociative numbness), emotional flooding, fight-flight-freeze reactions, sleep disruption, and the physical weight of unresolved grief.

**Layer 3 — The Identity Layer (Self):**
The deep architecture of how a character understands who they are — their values, their sense of continuity, their relationships, their purpose. Identity-layer damage does not just affect what a character thinks or feels; it fractures the coherent structure of selfhood. Psychological impacts at this layer manifest as: identity dissociation, value inversion (the former protector who becomes destroyer), existential crisis, role collapse (the leader who can no longer lead), and meaning loss.

**Layer 4 — The Will Layer (Cultivation Heart Interface):**
The deepest layer — the engine of motivated action that drives a character to keep moving, keep fighting, keep growing. This layer is the direct interface with the Cultivation Heart (Part 4, Area 3). Psychological impacts at this layer are the most severe: Will Fracture, Will Collapse, Heart Dimming, and Heart Severance. Impacts here do not merely affect performance — they can halt cultivation advancement entirely or cause catastrophic regression.

### 1.2 — The Psychological Impact Scale (PIS)

All psychological impacts are classified on the Psychological Impact Scale from PIS-1 (trivial) to PIS-10 (catastrophic / civilization-altering).

| PIS Grade | Label | Nature | Example Triggers |
|-----------|-------|---------|-----------------|
| PIS-1 | Trivial Friction | Brief discomfort, resolved within hours through ordinary activity | Minor embarrassment, losing a sparring match, casual insult |
| PIS-2 | Mild Stress | Short-duration stress response, resolved within days with rest | Failed technique execution in front of peers, prolonged hunger |
| PIS-3 | Moderate Stress | Noticeable impact on daily function lasting days to weeks; requires active coping | A difficult but survived battle, a failed breakthrough, a betrayal by an acquaintance |
| PIS-4 | Significant Impact | Meaningful disruption to cultivation practice, combat performance, and social function; lasts weeks to months | First kill of a human being, loss of a mentor, serious injury that required extended recovery |
| PIS-5 | Acute Trauma | Disruptive to all functions; cultivation stalls; nightmares, hypervigilance, intrusive states; can last months | Witnessing mass death, losing a close companion, surviving something that should have killed them |
| PIS-6 | Major Trauma | Foundational disruption; identity layer affected; cultivation regression possible; requires active external intervention | Loss of a deeply bonded partner or family member, forced participation in atrocity, prolonged captivity and degradation |
| PIS-7 | Severe Trauma | Identity fracture; Will Layer damage; cultivation completely stalled; dissociation; possible cultivation deviation risk | Betrayal by the most trusted person, witnessing the destruction of one's entire sect, near-death breakthrough failure |
| PIS-8 | Critical Trauma | Will Layer severe damage; Heart Dimming or early severance; possible soul vulnerability; extreme functional collapse | Loss of an entire world of people the character loved, commitment of a profound irreversible moral transgression |
| PIS-9 | Catastrophic Trauma | Heart Fracture; identity dissolution; potential cultivation path destruction; multi-year functional collapse | Genocide of the character's entire people, deliberate soul degradation by an external force, the death of what gave life meaning |
| PIS-10 | Existential Break | Complete psychological collapse; selfhood dissolution; cultivation corruption; if not resolved, permanent psychological death (continued biological existence as a hollow) | Reality-level loss of all that defined the character's existence; the complete and total destruction of everything the character protected, loved, and was |

### 1.3 — Trivial vs. Impactful Psychological Events

**Trivial Events (PIS-1 to PIS-2):**
These are the constant background friction of any character's life. They do not require canon tracking but provide the texture of lived experience. A character who experiences only PIS-5+ events without any trivial events appears as a spectacle rather than a person. The accumulation of trivial events creates the baseline emotional state from which larger events are experienced.

**Impactful Events (PIS-3 and above):**
These are canon-tracked psychological events. Every character with significant narrative presence must have their PIS-3+ events documented in their character record (governed by Part 16). The psychological history of a character is not biographical decoration — it is the accumulated substrate that determines how they respond to future challenges, how deep their Heart can grow, and what their ultimate psychological ceiling or breaking point is.

**Accumulation Effects:**
Multiple PIS-3 events without resolution create additive pressure. Three PIS-4 events unresolved can produce PIS-6 effective impact. Unresolved psychological burden accumulates and eventually forces a reckoning. Characters who suppress without resolving carry growing internal pressure that can trigger sudden decompensation — an apparently stable character who suddenly breaks under what appears to be a "small" final stressor.
