## Area 16 — Relational Address and Respect Law

**Added:** 2026-04-24 (formal Canon amendment — relational address governance expansion).
**Governing Law:** Part 29 Area 16 is the canonical authority for all forms of address between characters — how characters call one another, reference one another, and signal relational position through speech. This Area governs the complete address taxonomy across all relationship categories, hierarchy levels, faction types, and cultural contexts in the Starward Saga. **FCIA Dimension FC-4 (Character Arc Compliance)** and **FC-8 (Ideology and Psychology)** audit address accuracy in all prose units.

---

### 13.1 — Foundational Principles of Relational Address

**P29-13.1 — Address Is Relational Fact, Not Style Choice.**
How a character addresses another character is not a stylistic decision — it is a canonical governance fact. The address form carries the relational, hierarchical, cultural, and psychological state of both parties. An incorrect address form is a canon violation equivalent to using a wrong cultivation realm label.

**P29-13.2 — Two Hierarchies Govern Address: Social Rank and Cultivation Rank.**
The Starward Saga contains two independent authority hierarchies that govern address:
- **Social/Political Rank:** Sovereign title, noble rank, faction position, military rank, guild standing.
- **Cultivation Rank:** Cultivation realm tier as defined by Part 3.

**Rule P29-13.2.1 — Context Determines Which Hierarchy Is Active:** In political and social contexts (court sessions, diplomatic meetings, formal receptions, faction governance), social/political rank governs address. In cultivation and sect contexts (a training hall, a formal duel sequence, sect governance, cultivation examination), cultivation rank governs address. In mixed or ambiguous contexts, the PRIMARY PURPOSE of the event determines the active hierarchy — a political negotiation held in a sect hall uses social rank governance; a cultivation demonstration held in a royal court uses cultivation rank governance.

**P29-13.3 — Address Never Uses Personal Names for Relational Superiors in the Parental-Filial Bond.**
Children never use the personal name of their own parent in any context. This is Locked Canon. The parental bond creates an absolute address prohibition on personal-name use by the child. The only canonical exception is a documented psychological rupture or ideological estrangement event of CSS-7+ magnitude — and even then, the personal-name use must be a deliberate, flagged narrative moment, not an authorial error.

**P29-13.4 — Register Shifts Are Always Legible.**
Every shift in address register — from private (Father) to public (kingdom-specific title) — must be legible to the reader through the narrative context. A character's switch from private register to public register signals that the scene has become public; the switch itself is narratively informative.

**P29-13.5 — Culture-Specific Address Forms Are Negotiable Canon.**
The SPECIFIC WORDS of kingdom-specific sovereign titles, faction-specific address vocabulary, and culture-specific honorifics are Negotiable Canon per Part 28 — they are established when prose first requires them and become immutable at that moment (Establishment Threshold). The RULES governing when such forms are used and which category they belong to are Locked Canon.

**P29-13.6 — Faction-Type Address Operates on Universal Rules Plus Modifiers.**
A universal address framework (this Area) applies to all characters in all factions. Individual faction types (cultivation sects, government bodies, military forces, guilds, religious factions) then apply faction-specific modifiers that supplement or override specific categories. The universal framework is never absent; faction modifiers add to it.

**P29-13.7 — Address Reveals Loyalty, Affiliation, and Psychological State.**
How a character chooses to address another — particularly at a contested or transitional moment — reveals their actual relational position beyond what they may state explicitly. A character who switches from private register to formal register in a private setting is performing distance. A character who uses a personal name where a title is expected is claiming intimacy. These are narratively significant acts, not neutral variations.

---

### 13.2 — The Seven Address Categories

All address relationships in the Starward Saga are governed by one of seven primary categories. Each category has defined private-register, public-register, and interior-thought rules.

| Category | Description | Governing Section |
|---|---|---|
| CAT-1 | Parental Address — children → own parents | 13.3 |
| CAT-2 | Fictive Kinship Address — children → parents' peer-adults | 13.4 |
| CAT-3 | Child-to-Peer-Child Address — noble children → children of equivalent rank | 13.5 |
| CAT-4 | Peer Sovereign Address — sovereigns → peer-rank sovereigns | 13.6 |
| CAT-5 | Hierarchical: Lower → Higher — lower-rank characters → sovereigns, elders, masters | 13.7 |
| CAT-6 | Hierarchical: Higher → Lower — sovereigns, masters → those below them | 13.8 |
| CAT-7 | Cultivation-Sect Internal Address — sect members within their sect hierarchy | 13.9 |

---

### 13.3 — CAT-1: Parental Address (Children → Own Parents)

**Definition:** Address and reference used by a child when speaking to or about their own biological or legally adopted parent.

#### 13.3.1 — Private and Intimate Register

| Context | Father Form | Mother Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct address (child speaking to parent) | "Father" | "Mother" | Absolute base form |
| Indirect reference in dialogue | "my father" / "Father said..." | "my mother" / "Mother said..." | Title capitalized when functioning as name-substitute |
| Interior thought (all contexts) | "Father" / "my father" | "Mother" / "my mother" | Interior thought always uses private register regardless of setting |
| Third-person narration (child's POV) | "her father" / "his father" | "her mother" / "his mother" | Lower-case in narrative attribution; capitalized when direct reference |

**Rule P29-13.3.1 — Personal Name Absolute Prohibition:** A child NEVER uses their parent's personal name (e.g., Varya never says "Varys"; Aegon never says "Aeries") in any dialogue or interior thought. This prohibition is Locked Canon. Violation is a CRITICAL FCIA failure.

**Rule P29-13.3.2 — Variants Are Permitted:** Natural variations of "Father" and "Mother" are permitted within the private register:
- Affectionate diminutives if culturally established (to be named in prose when first used — Establishment Threshold applies)
- "my father" / "my mother" as indirect forms in the same register
- Context-embedded reference ("he said..." rather than "Father said..." in rapid dialogue) is acceptable when the referent is unambiguous

#### 13.3.2 — Public and Formal Register

In public/formal court settings — when a child is addressing or referencing their parent in an official capacity before an audience — the address shifts to the kingdom-specific formal title.

**Rule P29-13.3.3 — Kingdom-Specific Formal Parent Titles Are Negotiable Canon:** The specific formal title words are Negotiable Canon, established in prose when the first public-court scene requires them. The RULE that such titles exist and differ from the private register is Locked Canon.

**Azure Sky Kingdom:** Kingdom-specific formal title for Varys and Valia — to be established in prose. Reflects the Azure Sky dual-track paired-duality cultural paradigm (CPT per Part 25).

**Gale Kingdom:** Kingdom-specific formal title for Aeries and Aalia — to be established in prose. Reflects the Gale Kingdom wind-court/Dragon-Human cultivation-dominant cultural paradigm (CPT per Part 25).

**Rule P29-13.3.4 — Interior Thought Does Not Switch to Public Register:** Even in a public court scene, Varya's interior thoughts use "Father" and "Mother" — the private register. The public-register form is an external, social-performance act, not an internal cognitive state.

### 13.4 — CAT-2: Fictive Kinship Address (Children → Parents' Peer-Adults)

**Definition:** Address used by a child toward adults who are not their parents but are their parents' close peers and designated fictive kin (honorary uncle/aunt). This category applies to the cross-family relationships between the Azure Sky and Gale Kingdom royal families.

#### 13.4.1 — Canonical Fictive Kin Relationships (Eight Hero Families)

| Child | Parent | Fictive Uncle/Aunt | Private Form | Public Form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Varya | Varys, Valia | Aeries | "Uncle Aeries" | "King Aeries" |
| Varya | Varys, Valia | Aalia | "Aunt Aalia" | "Queen Aalia" |
| Aegon | Aeries, Aalia | Varys | "Uncle Varys" | "King Varys" |
| Aegon | Aeries, Aalia | Valia | "Aunt Valia" | "Queen Valia" |

**Rule P29-13.4.1 — Title + Name in Private Register:** In private and informal settings, fictive kinship address uses the full form: [Kinship Title] + [First Name]. "Uncle Aeries" is the canonical private-register form for Varya addressing Aeries — not "Uncle" alone (which risks ambiguity if multiple uncles exist) and not "Aeries" alone (which violates the relational respect boundary).

**Rule P29-13.4.2 — Formal Title in Public Register:** In public, formal, or multi-party official contexts, the child uses the adult's formal sovereign title. Varya says "King Aeries" not "Uncle Aeries" in a public court gathering.

**Rule P29-13.4.3 — Fictive Kinship Requires Establishment:** The fictive kinship relationship (Uncle/Aunt designation) is established when the first prose unit depicts the child using this form in the private register. At that moment of first use, the form becomes Negotiable Canon under the Establishment Threshold. The relationship between the Azure Sky and Gale Kingdom families at story start presupposes this designation is already in place — it does not need to be established in prose for the first time; it may simply be used.

**Rule P29-13.4.4 — Error Class:** A child character addressing a fictive uncle/aunt by personal name alone (e.g., Aegon saying "Aeries" when referring to Varys — **ERROR: Aeries is Aegon's FATHER, not his fictive uncle**) is a Critical FCIA failure. **The most common error in this category is confusing which character is a parent vs. fictive kin.** Always verify the relational mapping before assigning an address form.

> **Canonical relational check for Aegon:** Aeries = Father (not uncle). Aalia = Mother (not aunt). Varys = Uncle Varys. Valia = Aunt Valia.
> **Canonical relational check for Varya:** Varys = Father (not uncle). Valia = Mother (not aunt). Aeries = Uncle Aeries. Aalia = Aunt Aalia.

### 13.5 — CAT-3: Child-to-Peer-Child Address

**Definition:** Address used by noble children toward other children of equivalent or similar noble rank who are not family.

**Default form:** First name. Children of equivalent noble rank address each other by given name in all informal and working contexts.

**Rule P29-13.5.1 — First Name in Default Register:** Varya addresses Aegon as "Aegon." Aegon addresses Varya as "Varya." No title required in private or working settings.

**Rule P29-13.5.2 — Rank Title in Public Formal Gatherings:** In a formal public gathering where ranks are being observed (a court reception, a formal bilateral session, a ceremonial introduction), children address each other by rank title: "Prince Aegon," "Princess Varya." The public register activates when the scene is formally structured.

**Rule P29-13.5.3 — Peer Children Outside the Family Circle:** When noble children encounter peer children they do not know personally, the default is first name once an introduction has been made. Before introduction: title or deferential address ("my lord," "my lady") depending on the kingdom's CPT. After introduction: first name becomes appropriate.

---

### 13.6 — CAT-4: Peer Sovereign Address (Sovereigns → Peer Sovereigns)

**Definition:** Address used between sovereigns of equivalent rank — kings addressing kings, queens addressing queens, co-rulers addressing each other's counterparts.

#### 13.6.1 — Private and Working Register

Peer sovereigns in private, informal, or working contexts use **first names only**.

- Varys and Aeries in private: "Varys" / "Aeries"
- Valia and Aalia in private working session: "Valia" / "Aalia"
- Varys and Valia as co-regents in private: "Varys" / "Valia"

**Rule P29-13.6.1 — Private First-Name Use Is Unconditional:** Among peer sovereigns with established personal relationships (Varys-Aeries, Valia-Aalia), private first-name use requires no qualifier and applies in all working contexts regardless of the formal setting's name. A bilateral session room is formally structured but the session itself is private; first names apply once the working conversation begins.

#### 13.6.2 — Formal Session Opening and Public Gatherings

**Rule P29-13.6.2 — Opening Title Protocol:** At the opening of a formal bilateral session or public joint ceremony, peer sovereigns use formal titles for the opening acknowledgment. This is a brief, ritual performance of the formal context. The transition to first-name working address follows naturally once the session enters its working phase.

- Opening: *"King Aeries."* / *"King Varys."*
- Working transition: *"Aeries."* / *"Varys."* (first names resume)

**Rule P29-13.6.3 — Open Public Gatherings:** When peer sovereigns are interacting before an audience that includes subjects, foreign observers, or formal witnesses, formal titles are maintained throughout the public interaction: "King Aeries," "Queen Valia," "King Varys," "Queen Aalia."

**Rule P29-13.6.4 — Third-Party Presence:** When a third party who is not part of the bilateral peer relationship is present in a meeting (a court official, a formal witness, a scribe), the address reverts to formal titles for all exchanges made before that third party, even in an otherwise private setting.

---

### 13.7 — CAT-5: Hierarchical Address — Lower-Rank → Sovereign/Elder/Master

**Definition:** Address used by lower-rank characters (court officials, palace staff, military officers, guild members, disciples) when addressing or referring to sovereign-rank, elder-rank, or master-rank characters.

**Rule P29-13.7.1 — Culture-Specific Always:** Lower-rank characters' address vocabulary for sovereigns is culture-specific — governed by the CPT (Cultural Paradigm Type, Part 25) of the civilization, kingdom, or faction they belong to. There is no single universal form. Azure Sky Kingdom has its own lower-to-sovereign address vocabulary; Gale Kingdom has its own. Cultivation sects have their own. Military hierarchies have their own.

**Rule P29-13.7.2 — Culture-Specific Forms Are Negotiable Canon:** The specific forms (the exact words used by Azure Sky court officials addressing King Varys, for example) are Negotiable Canon — established in prose when first required. The RULE that culture-specific forms exist and must be used consistently is Locked Canon.

**Rule P29-13.7.3 — Rank Gap Governs Formality Depth:** The greater the rank differential, the more formal the address. A senior court official addressing the king has a moderate formality floor. A new palace staff member addressing the queen has a high formality floor. A commoner petitioner in a formal court session has the highest formality floor. These gradations are consistent with the CPT of the relevant kingdom.

**Rule P29-13.7.4 — Private Service Relationships:** Long-established private service relationships (a personal attendant of many years, a childhood tutor, a trusted healer) may develop a modified address register — not informality, but a kind of respectful intimacy that acknowledges the personal history. Such a modification must be established in prose; it cannot be assumed.

---

### 13.8 — CAT-6: Hierarchical Address — Higher-Rank → Lower-Rank

**Definition:** Address used by sovereigns, elders, masters, and other high-rank characters when addressing those below them in the hierarchy.

**Rule P29-13.8.1 — Adults to Children of Peer Adults (Private):** In private or informal settings, adults address their peers' children by first name: Varys addresses Aegon as "Aegon"; Aeries addresses Varya as "Varya." An optional affectionate modifier may be added as a relationship develops in prose (e.g., a nickname or endearment). These modifications are Negotiable Canon — named when they appear.

**Rule P29-13.8.2 — Adults to Children of Peer Adults (Public/Formal):** In public or formal settings, adults address their peers' children by rank title: "Prince Aegon," "Princess Varya." The public-formal register activates in the same contexts as described in CAT-3 (13.5).

**Rule P29-13.8.3 — Sovereigns to Staff and Officials:** Sovereigns address known staff and officials by name or role-title depending on the context:
- Private / working context: Name preferred (when personal relationship exists) or role-title
- Formal court context: Role-title or formal address appropriate to the official's rank

**Rule P29-13.8.4 — Sovereigns to Petitioners:** In formal court petition contexts (Sword Court, Signet Court), sovereigns address petitioners by name or role, depending on the petition type. The sovereign-to-petitioner address is dignified and impersonal unless the petitioner is known to the sovereign.

### 13.9 — CAT-7: Cultivation-Sect Internal Address

**Definition:** Address vocabulary used among members of cultivation sects, governed by their internal hierarchy. This vocabulary is distinct from social/political hierarchy address and takes precedence in cultivation-context settings (Rule P29-13.2.1).

| Role Title | Who Bears It | Used By | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sect Master** (or equivalent) | The leader of the sect | All sect members | Formal title; used in all contexts including private sect meetings |
| **Elder** | Senior sect members with governance authority | All lower members | Abbreviated to "Elder [Name]" when multiple elders are present |
| **Master** | A recognized cultivation instructor with formal transmission responsibility | Their disciples and students | "Master [Name]" in all contexts; becomes "my master" in indirect reference |
| **Senior Disciple** / **Senior [Name]** | A disciple who has achieved a higher cultivation stage than those they interact with | Their juniors in the same sect generation | "Senior [Name]" is the standard form; conveys both relational seniority and cultivation acknowledgment |
| **Fellow Disciple** / **Sect Brother** / **Sect Sister** | A peer disciple within the same sect generation | Fellow sect members of the same generation | Peer address; signals same-generation cultivation standing without hierarchy |
| **Junior [Name]** | A less advanced disciple | Their cultivation seniors in the same sect | Affectionate-to-neutral, depending on relationship; not condescending in cultivation culture |

**Rule P29-13.9.1 — Cultivation Address in Non-Sect Contexts:** Cultivation titles follow their holder into non-sect contexts. A character who is a recognized Master in a cultivation sect retains the "Master [Name]" address even in political or social settings when their cultivation identity is relevant to the interaction.

**Rule P29-13.9.2 — Three Moon Sect Address (Phase 2+ Activation Only):** The Three Moon Sects (Fallen Moon, Full Moon, Dark Moon — apex cultivation sects, co-governing the Moon of Saha) have apex-tier address vocabulary. Specific forms are Negotiable Canon — to be established in prose when the sects appear. The RULE that apex cultivation sects have elevated address vocabulary distinct from standard sect hierarchy is Locked Canon. **[Phase 2+ Activation Note per Part 30 Law P30-L8: The Three Moon Sects are Phase 1 Forbidden Entities. This Rule does not activate during Phase 1 prose authoring — Moon Sect characters may not appear in Phase 1 prose in any form. This Rule activates in Phase 2+ when Moon Sect characters first appear on-page.]**

**Rule P29-13.9.3 — Cultivation Address Does Not Override Parental Address:** The cultivation hierarchy address system applies to sect roles, not to family relationships. A cultivation master who is also someone's parent is addressed as "Father" / "Mother" by their child, not as "Master." The CAT-1 address takes priority over CAT-7 in the parent-child relationship.

### 13.10 — Cultivation Rank vs. Social Rank: Context Determines Which Hierarchy

The Starward Saga contains characters whose cultivation rank and social rank diverge significantly. A high-realm cultivator may be of common birth; a low-cultivation noble may hold significant political authority.

**Rule P29-13.10.1 — Context Determines Active Hierarchy:** The type of event and setting determines which hierarchy governs address:

| Context | Active Hierarchy | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Royal court session (political) | Social/political rank | A Warrior-realm king is "Your Majesty" even to a Ruler-realm visiting advisor |
| Sect training hall | Cultivation rank | A Ruler-realm king visiting a sect hall addresses the Core-realm Sect Master as "Sect Master" |
| Cultivation duel | Cultivation rank | Participants address each other by cultivation tier acknowledgment, not political rank |
| Military engagement | Military rank | Command hierarchy addresses apply regardless of cultivation tier |
| Diplomatic reception | Social/political rank | All parties use formal sovereign/official titles |
| Mixed informal setting | Relationship register defaults | If no clear context signal: social register is the default in the Starward Saga world |

**Rule P29-13.10.2 — Cultivation Rank Challenge:** A character who explicitly asserts cultivation authority in a political context ("I stand before you not as a diplomat but as a Ruler-realm cultivator") is invoking a register shift — moving from social to cultivation hierarchy. Such shifts are narratively significant acts requiring characterization support.

### 13.11 — Faction-Type Universal Rules and Modifiers

**Universal Address Rules (apply to ALL faction types):**

1. **Rule U-1 — Internal Hierarchy Produces Vocabulary:** Every faction's internal authority structure generates its own address vocabulary. No two faction types have identical internal address systems.

2. **Rule U-2 — Host Territory Default:** When characters of different faction types meet, the host territory's faction address conventions govern for the meeting context unless the meeting is formally neutral (bilateral compact, neutral arbitration).

3. **Rule U-3 — Address Signals Real Loyalty:** At contested or transitional moments, how a character addresses another reveals their actual relational position. A character who uses a title when first-name address is expected is performing distance. A character who uses first-name address when a title is expected is claiming intimacy or asserting equality.

4. **Rule U-4 — New Encounter Default:** When characters from different factions meet for the first time without prior relationship context, the default address is the most formal applicable register. Informality descends with demonstrated relationship.

**Faction-Type Modifiers:**

| Faction Type | Primary Modifier | Internal Address Vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| Cultivator Sects | Cultivation rank governs internal address (CAT-7, Section 13.9) | Sect Master, Elder, Master, Senior/Junior Disciple, Fellow Disciple |
| Government Bodies | Social/political rank governs | Sovereign title + kingdom-specific forms; official role-title for all below sovereign |
| Military and Security Forces | Military rank hierarchy governs | Marshal, General, Captain, Lieutenant, Sergeant; "sir" / "ma'am" equivalents |
| Space Forces | Military rank + void-culture modifier | Rank title + void-oath acknowledgment forms (Negotiable Canon, established in prose) |
| Guilds / Technology Factions | Professional rank governs | Master Craftsman / Hall Director / Guild Head + name; journeyman / apprentice forms |
| Alchemical / Pharmaceutical | Seniority and formula-lineage rank | Grand Alchemist, Master Alchemist, Apprentice; "Formula Elder" for lineage seniors |
| Intelligence / Espionage | Operational rank; often covert | Cover-appropriate address; true internal address known only within the organization |
| Religious / Ideological | Covenant rank | Covenant-title forms (specific to each religious tradition; Negotiable Canon) |
| Noble / Aristocratic Houses | Lineage and blood rank | Lord, Lady, High Lord, House title; inherited address forms |
| Criminal / Underworld | Loyalty hierarchy | Dark Patriarch, Branch Lord, Veteran Hand; address forms earned through demonstrated loyalty |
| Diplomatic / Political | Formal title always | Ambassador, Envoy, Compact-Holder; formal title in all interactions |
| Synergy Integration Institutes | Integration Model rank + professional rank | Integration Master, Model-C Practitioner; hybrid professional-cultivation address |

### 13.12 — Cross-Kingdom Formal Session Address

When sovereigns of different kingdoms convene in a formally structured bilateral or multilateral session:

**Rule P29-13.12.1 — Opening Title Protocol:** All parties open with formal title address. Azure Sky and Gale Kingdom bilateral sessions: "King Varys" / "King Aeries" / "Queen Valia" / "Queen Aalia" as the opening acknowledgment.

**Rule P29-13.12.2 — Transition to First Names:** Once a bilateral session between established-relationship sovereigns enters its working phase, address transitions to first names. The transition is signaled by the first use of a first name after the formal opening.

**Rule P29-13.12.3 — Third Party Presence Reverts to Formal:** If a third party (official, scribe, witness) is present, all exchanges remain in formal-title register for the duration of that party's presence.

**Rule P29-13.12.4 — New Relationship Bilateral Sessions:** When sovereigns who do not have an established personal relationship meet formally for the first time, formal titles are maintained throughout the initial session. Transition to first-name address in subsequent sessions is a relationship development milestone.

### 13.14 — FCIA Audit Hook and Error Classification

**FCIA Dimension:** FC-4 (Character Arc Compliance) and FC-8 (Ideology and Psychology) audit address accuracy in every prose unit. The following error classes apply:

| Error Type | FCIA Severity | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Child uses parent's personal name | CRITICAL | Rule P29-13.3.1 violation — absolute prohibition; e.g., Varya saying "Varys" when referring to her father |
| Child uses own parent's form for fictive uncle/aunt | CRITICAL | Relational mapping error — most common error class; e.g., Aegon calling his own father Aeries "Uncle Aeries" |
| Fictive uncle/aunt form used in formal public setting | MAJOR | Varya saying "Uncle Aeries" in a formal court assembly rather than "King Aeries" |
| Private first-name form between sovereigns in public assembly | MAJOR | Varys saying "Aeries" rather than "King Aeries" in a public formal gathering with audience |
| Formal title used between peer sovereigns in confirmed-private working setting | MINOR | Unnecessarily formal register in a private meeting signals either character distance (check if intentional) or authorial error |
| Sect address used in political/social context without register-shift signal | MINOR | A character using "Elder [Name]" in a court context without narrative acknowledgment that a cultivation-hierarchy register is active |
| Unknown character addressed by first name before introduction | MINOR | Characters should use title or deferential form before introduction |
