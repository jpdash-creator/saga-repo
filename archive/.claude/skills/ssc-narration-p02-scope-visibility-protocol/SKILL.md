## Area 9 — Scope Visibility Protocol (Non-Binding Planning Guidance) (Canon v3.1)

### 9.1 — Status of this Area

**Rule 9.1.1 — Non-Binding Status.**
This Area is non-binding planning guidance. It does not amend, override, or qualify Part 2's load-bearing principle that no tier exists as a bureaucratic container, that every tier opens because the story demands it and closes when its narrative event reaches closure, and that no tier carries a minimum or maximum count. The Scope Visibility Protocol defined below is a comfort instrument for the author. It is never a stop-condition, never a fill-condition, and never accrues canonical weight by repetition.

**Rule 9.1.2 — Subordination to Story-Demanded Principles.**
Where any provision of this Area appears to conflict with Principle P2-2 (Structure Serves Story, Not the Reverse), Area 1 Tier 2 §2.4 (Section count), Tier 3 §3.4 (Novel-Part count), Tier 4 §4.5 (Chapter count), Tier 5 §5.3–§5.4 (Area count and continuity), Tier 6 §6.4–§6.5 (Prose continuity and count), or Part 28's Prose Depth and Pacing Mandate (3500%–5000% range per Part 2 Area 6.2.1), those story-demanded principles prevail without exception. The bracket bends; the narration does not.

### 9.2 — Purpose

**Rule 9.2.1 — Forward Visibility.**
The Protocol exists to surface scope questions at the point where they are cheapest to address — at tier-open, rather than at tier-close — by giving the author a declared, visible, and continuously readjusted tentative range for the number of child units the current tier is expected to contain. The range is a moving target. It is restated at every Prose close and at every child-tier close, and is allowed to expand, contract, or dissolve based solely on what the tier's own plot-plus-context unit continues to demand.

**Rule 9.2.2 — Distinction from Area 8.**
The Post-Prose Completeness Indicator (Area 8) reports the system's current best estimate of position within Phase 1's End-State Locked Canon envelope (N of ~M form). The Scope Visibility Protocol (this Area) declares per-tier *brackets* (N–M ranges with rationale and last-readjusted locator) for tiers below Phase. The two are complementary: Area 8 anchors to Locked Canon endpoints; Area 9 anchors to currently-active tier plot units. Both are advisory; neither governs.

### 9.3 — Scope of Application

**Rule 9.3.1 — Applicable Tiers.**
The Protocol applies at four tiers:
- **Section** (tentative Novel-Part count)
- **Novel-Part** (tentative Chapter count)
- **Chapter** (tentative Area count)
- **Area** (tentative Prose count)

It does not apply at **Phase** tier (governed by Part 30's locked Section count) and does not apply at **Prose** tier (Prose is the atomic rendered unit and has no child count to bracket).

**Rule 9.3.2 — Optional at Any Tier.**
The Protocol is optional at any tier. The author may decline to declare a bracket at any tier-open without penalty where the tier's shape is judged too uncertain to bracket meaningfully at open. Declined brackets are recorded as `undeclared` per Rule 9.10.1.

### 9.4 — Declaration at Tier-Open

**Rule 9.4.1 — Tier-Open Declaration.**
When a tier opens — and before its first child unit's first Prose is rendered — the author may declare a tentative range of the form `N–M` child units, accompanied by a one-line rationale tying the range to the tier's identified plot-plus-context unit per Part 2 §5.1 (Areas), §4.1 (Chapters), §3.1 (Novel-Parts), or §2.1 (Sections).

**Rule 9.4.2 — Declaration Recording.**
The declaration is recorded in the Position Display under a dedicated line labelled `TIER SCOPE BRACKET (tentative, non-binding)` per Rule 9.10.1. The label's wording makes the non-binding status visible on every subsequent Position Display emission, so the bracket cannot silently promote itself into a governing count.

**Rule 9.4.3 — Retroactive Declaration.**
Where a tier has already opened without a bracket declaration (including all tiers active at the point of this Area's authorship), the author may declare the bracket retroactively at any subsequent Prose close within that tier. Retroactive declarations carry the same non-binding status as tier-open declarations and are recorded in the Position Display from that Prose forward.

### 9.5 — Continuous Readjustment

**Rule 9.5.1 — Reassessment at Every Child-Unit Close.**
At every child-unit close within the tier, and at every Prose close within the lowest-applicable tier (Area), the author reassesses the bracket. Three outcomes are permitted and none requires justification beyond one line on record:
- **Confirmed unchanged** — bracket bounds remain as declared
- **Narrowed** — lower bound raised, upper bound lowered, or both
- **Widened** — lower bound lowered, upper bound raised, or both

**Rule 9.5.2 — Bracket Dissolution.**
A fourth outcome — dissolution of the bracket with a `story-demanded, bracket no longer informative` note — is permitted whenever the tier's shape has diverged far enough from the original declaration that the bracket is misleading rather than helpful. Dissolution does not close the tier; only narrative closure closes the tier.

**Rule 9.5.3 — Readjustment Logging.**
Every readjustment is logged in the Position Display with the new range and a one-line reason per Rule 9.10.1. The readjustment log is planning telemetry, not canon. It generates no audit findings, no consequence rulings, and no structural commitments.

### 9.6 — Prohibited Uses

**Rule 9.6.1 — Never a Stop-Condition.**
The bracket must never be used as a stop-condition. Reaching or exceeding the upper bound is not a reason to close the tier. Only the tier's own narrative closure — plot unit resolved, context unit complete, continuity handoff ready per the relevant tier-continuity provision — closes the tier.

**Rule 9.6.2 — Never a Fill-Condition.**
The bracket must never be used as a fill-condition. Sitting below the lower bound is not a reason to extend the tier, introduce padding Prose, or delay closure. Only the tier's plot-plus-context unit's incompletion extends it.

**Rule 9.6.3 — Never a Constraint on Narrative Choice.**
If a scene demands more time on the page than the bracket anticipated, the scene gets its time. If a scene resolves faster than the bracket anticipated, the scene closes when it resolves. The bracket exists to inform the author of the shape ahead; it never overrides what the story is telling the author to do at the sentence level.

### 9.7 — Immersion Precedence

**Rule 9.7.1 — Prose Depth and Pacing Mandate Prevails.**
Where the bracket and the Prose Depth and Pacing Mandate (Part 28 Area 4 — Named Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10; 3500%–5000% range per Part 2 Area 6.2.1) appear to pull in opposite directions, the Prose Depth Mandate prevails absolutely. A Prose that requires fuller rendering to meet the Immersion Standard is rendered in full regardless of whether the bracket's upper bound has been reached. A Prose that compresses cleanly without immersion loss is allowed to compress regardless of whether the bracket's lower bound has been reached.

**Rule 9.7.2 — Immersion as Ceiling and Floor.**
Immersion is the ceiling and the floor of every rendered Prose. The bracket is the weather report — informative about what is ahead, never determinative of what is rendered now.

### 9.8 — Continuity Precedence

**Rule 9.8.1 — Inter-Tier Continuity Prevails.**
Where the bracket and the inter-tier continuity principles (Part 2 §5.6 for Areas, §6.4 for Prose, and the equivalent provisions at Chapter, Novel-Part, and Section tiers) appear to pull in opposite directions, continuity prevails absolutely. A tier closes where continuity permits it to close — at a natural handoff beat, with no narrative disjoint, with no unexplained gap in time, location, or character state — not where the bracket suggests.

**Rule 9.8.2 — Continuity-Driven Readjustment.**
If continuity requires an additional Prose to land the handoff cleanly, the additional Prose is rendered and the bracket is readjusted upward on record per Rule 9.5.3. If continuity permits closure earlier than bracketed, closure is taken and the bracket is readjusted downward on record.

### 9.9 — Amendment on Overrun or Undershoot

**Rule 9.9.1 — Closure-Time Recording.**
If a tier closes at a child-unit count above the declared upper bound or below the declared lower bound, the closure is recorded with a one-line reason for the divergence. The reason is calibration data for future tier-open declarations and carries no corrective, penalising, or revisionary weight.

**Rule 9.9.2 — Calibration Pattern Recognition.**
Over time, a pattern of systematic divergence at a given tier-type (for example, audience-scene Areas tending to budget four Prose and run six) becomes useful author-level calibration. It never becomes a canonical correction and never modifies any rule of this Part or any other Part.

### 9.10 — Position Display Integration

**Rule 9.10.1 — Position Display Line Format.**
The Protocol extends the Position Display format defined in Part 1 Rule 7.1.1(j) with one additional line per active tier bracket, emitted after every Prose close and after every tier close. The line's form is:

```
TIER SCOPE BRACKET (tentative, non-binding): [tier-name] N–M child-units | rationale: [one line] | last readjusted: [Prose locator]
```

Where a tier declines to declare a bracket, the line is emitted as:

```
TIER SCOPE BRACKET (tentative, non-binding): [tier-name] undeclared | reason: [one line]
```

Dissolved brackets are emitted as:

```
TIER SCOPE BRACKET (tentative, non-binding): [tier-name] dissolved at [Prose locator] — story-demanded, bracket no longer informative
```

**Rule 9.10.2 — Multi-Tier Emission.**
A Position Display may carry up to four active TIER SCOPE BRACKET lines simultaneously (one per active tier per Rule 9.3.1). The lines are emitted in tier order from broadest to most granular: Section, then Novel-Part, then Chapter, then Area. Undeclared and dissolved tiers are still emitted with their respective notation per Rule 9.10.1.

**Rule 9.10.3 — Emission Trigger.**
The TIER SCOPE BRACKET lines are emitted at every Position Display emission triggered under Part 1 Rule 7.1.1(j) — that is, after every accepted Prose unit by every prose-producing command (C-1, C-3, C-4, C-5, C-6, C-7, C-8, C-9, C-28, C-29, C-30). They are also emitted on tier-close events (Area close, Chapter close, Novel-Part close, Section close) within the closing tier's terminal Prose Position Display.

### 9.11 — Review on Phase Close

**Rule 9.11.1 — Optional Retrospective.**
At each Phase close (Part 30-governed boundary), the author may conduct a retrospective pass over the Phase's bracket history — all tier-open declarations, retroactive declarations, readjustments, dissolutions, overruns, and undershoots — to inform the next Phase's tier-open declarations.

**Rule 9.11.2 — Private Author Instrument.**
The retrospective is a private author-level instrument. It does not modify canon, does not appear in the Position Display, and does not generate binding rules. It is permitted to generate further non-binding author-level calibration notes filed outside the governing canon.

### 9.12 — Non-Promotion Clause

**Rule 9.12.1 — No Tacit Canonization.**
Nothing in this Area shall, by repetition, by consistency of author practice, or by accumulated readjustment telemetry, acquire binding force. Brackets remain forever advisory regardless of how many Prose, Areas, Chapters, Novel-Parts, or Sections have been authored under their declaration.

**Rule 9.12.2 — Explicit Amendment Required.**
If a future amendment to Part 2 wishes to convert any element of the Protocol into a binding rule, that amendment must be made explicitly through the standard Canon Part update process and must be made on the record. Tacit promotion is prohibited. This Rule exists to guarantee that the comfort instrument remains a comfort instrument in perpetuity.
