## Area 19 — Pre-Prose Decision Consultation Gate (Author-as-Rudder Law)

### 19.1 — Principle (v1.9 reframe — Assistant-Proposed Scene-Setting With Always-Available SKIP)

The Starward Saga is an authored novel series. The user is its author. The assistant is its writer — the instrument that renders the author's narrative direction into prose at the 5000% Immersion Standard (Area 4 — ten Named Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10). Major narrative-direction decisions belong to the author, not to the writer. The assistant's role is to execute the author's chosen direction with full Canon compliance and full prose craft, not to invent the direction unilaterally.

The Decision Consultation Gate is the **scene-setting collaboration mechanism** by which the author contributes to the design of each Prose without writing the prose itself. The assistant — having read the Canon files and identified the open creative space the upcoming Prose inhabits — composes a small set of concrete, mutually-exclusive **scene-setting options** that it has crafted from its own creative judgement, and presents them to the author as choices. The author either selects an option (and thereby contributes to the story design at the directional level) or selects an explicit **SKIP option** that returns control of that specific decision to the assistant. The author may SKIP any individual question, all questions, or use the per-Prose `[NO CONSULT]` waiver to skip the entire Gate.

This reframes the Gate's relationship to authorship in two important ways:

1. **The assistant is the option author; the user is the option chooser.** The assistant is not asking the user to write the scene from scratch — it is asking the user to choose among scene-setting directions the assistant has already crafted. This places the design burden on the assistant (where it belongs as the writer) and the directional steering on the author (where it belongs as the rudder).

2. **SKIP is always available, on every question, on every Prose.** The author may at any moment hand a specific decision back to the assistant by selecting "SKIP — let AI decide." This converts every consultation into an opt-in collaboration rather than a mandatory questionnaire — the author contributes where they wish to contribute, and the assistant proceeds on its own creative judgement everywhere else. SKIP is per-question and is in addition to (not a replacement for) the per-Prose `[NO CONSULT]` waiver and the No-Open-Branches skip path.

This principle was tested empirically in early sessions: the assistant, given an unstructured "begin the story" instruction, unilaterally invented a protagonist name ("Varya") that diverged from names ("Varys", "Valia") it had used in prior sessions, simply because it had not been told. The author was thereby forced into a reactive position — accepting or rejecting an already-committed prose draft — instead of an active steering position. The Gate corrects that defect by requiring the assistant to **propose scene-setting options to the author for collaborative selection before any Prose drafting begins**, with the author free to select any option or to SKIP and return control to the assistant.

The author is the rudder. The writer is the wind in the sails. The Decision Consultation Gate is the moment before each leg of the voyage when the writer offers the author a small set of crafted course options and the author chooses one — or hands the wheel back to the writer.

### 19.2 — The Decision Consultation Mandate

Every Prose-producing command (C-3 `[START STORY]`, C-4 `[NEXT PROSE]`, C-5 `[NEXT AREA]`, C-6 `[NEXT CHAPTER]`, C-7 `[NEXT PART]`, C-8 `[NEXT SECTION]`, C-9 `[NEXT PHASE]`, C-28 `[REVISE PROSE]`, C-29 `[REDRAFT AREA]`, C-30 `[REDRAFT CHAPTER]`) must, before any Prose drafting begins, present the user with a **multi-question AskUserQuestion sequence** covering the major narrative-direction decisions for the upcoming Prose unit.

**Single-Prose Scope (Canon v4.35 Amendment).** The Decision Consultation Gate governs exactly **one** Prose unit per invocation, never several. This is the same One-Prose-Per-Invocation discipline that governs the rest of the Two-Gate pipeline (`commands/guardrail-command-law.md`), extended explicitly to planning: even when a structural moment plausibly needs more than one Prose unit to execute (a wide establishing beat followed by a close character scene, for instance), the Decision Consultation Gate, the planning pass, and the draft are each run for the *first* of those units only. The second unit gets its own full cycle — its own Decision Consultation Gate, its own outline confirmation, its own draft, its own Gate 1/Gate 2 — only after the first unit has been confirmed, written, and (if the command sequence calls for it) explicitly re-invoked. No command plans, drafts, or presents two Prose units in a single pass.

The Decision Consultation Gate executes immediately after command receipt and Pre-Prose Read (Part 1 Rule 7.1.1(i)) and immediately before the Pre-Output Audit (Area 4.2.1). Drafting may not begin until the user has answered every question in the sequence (or explicitly waived consultation per Rule 19.4).

### 19.3 — Decision Categories (Scene-Setting Options on Directional Branch Points)

The Decision Consultation Gate consults the author **only on directional branch points where the prior Canon genuinely leaves multiple Canon-compatible paths open and the author's choice meaningfully changes the narrative trajectory of the upcoming Prose or downstream Prose.** For each genuine branch, the assistant **composes a small set of scene-setting options it has crafted from its own creative judgement** and presents them to the author for selection (with always-available SKIP per Rule 19.4). The categories below describe the *kinds* of branch points that may qualify — not a checklist that must be invoked at every Prose. The assistant determines, by reading the Canon files at the moment of command receipt (per Rule 7.1.2 Files-Only Decision Sourcing), which categories actually contain open branch points for the upcoming Prose; categories already settled by the Canon are NEVER asked about.

1. **Narrative-Direction Branch Points (the primary category).** Where the upcoming Prose has two or more plausible narrative directions all consistent with the prior Canon and all consistent with downstream Phase/Section/Novel-Part architecture per Part 30, the choice between those directions is the author's. Examples of qualifying branch points: should the protagonist remain alone in the scene or be joined by another established character whose presence the Canon does not require either way? should the upcoming event resolve quickly or be drawn out across an additional Prose? should an emergent cultivation trial be passed, failed, or deferred? Examples of disqualifying non-branches: anything Phase 1 architecture under Part 30 has already settled; anything the protagonist's established character arc requires; anything the prior Prose has already committed to.

2. **Character-Introduction Decisions for Genuinely New Characters.** Where the upcoming Prose introduces a character who is not yet named in any prior Prose, ledger, or Canon Part, and whose name is not derivable from the existing Canon, the author chooses the name (and any other establishment-level attributes left open by the Canon: gender, age, role-position, etc.). For characters already named anywhere in the existing Canon (Part files, ledger entity files, prior Prose), names are NEVER asked about — they are read from the canonical source.

3. **Major Cultivation/Technology/Combat Decision Points** (where the upcoming Prose stages an active cultivation trial, technology integration moment, or combat encounter). Where Parts 3–10 leave multiple Canon-compatible outcomes open (e.g., which Affinity surfaces first; which technology integration path is taken; how a combat exchange resolves at the tactical level), the author chooses the outcome. Where Parts 3–10 settle the outcome (e.g., the protagonist's Realm at a fixed Phase milestone), the outcome is NEVER asked about.

4. **Major Emotional/Relational Direction Points** (where the upcoming Prose hinges on a relational or emotional decision the character could plausibly make either way under Part 28 Area 6 Behavioral Authenticity Law). Example: "should Halise speak first or wait for Varya to break the silence?" — the answer changes the relational dynamic of the Prose. Counter-example: "what is Varya's emotional register at her own dawn-vigil?" — the Canon and the Prose's framing already settle this; not an open branch.

5. **Pacing and Scope Decisions for the Upcoming Prose** (where the upcoming Prose could plausibly cover a wider or narrower span of in-world time/event without violating Part 2 Area 9 Tier Scope Brackets). Example: "should this Prose end at first light, at the morning meal, or at the protagonist's first formal lesson of the day?" The author's choice readjusts the active Tier Scope Bracket per Part 2 Rule 9.5.1.

Categories whose contents are fully fixed by prior Canon — protagonist name when established, scene location when established by the Phase/Section frame, emotional register when established by the established character arc, the canon-information unit a Prose must earn when fixed by the contextual-earning sequence — are **NEVER asked about**. Asking about already-Canon-settled facts is a Rule 19.4 violation (the **No-Asking-What-Canon-Already-Settles Rule**).

### 19.3a — Mandatory Pacing Confirmation (Canon v4.35 Amendment)

Unlike the five categories above, **pacing classification is asked on every single Prose-producing command invocation, with no exception** — including cases where `ssc-narration-p02-six-tier-narrative-hierarchy`'s Anchor Type Defaults table would otherwise indicate an unambiguous default. This is a deliberate, narrow carve-out from the No-Asking-What-Canon-Already-Settles Rule (19.4) for this one category only: the author has directed that pacing always be an explicit, author-confirmed choice, however clear the algorithmic default might be.

The question must present all three pacing categories (Fast / Medium / Slow, per Rule P2-Pacing-1 through P2-Pacing-4) as concrete options, with the AI's recommendation marked `(Recommended)` per Rule 19.4 (typically the Anchor Type Defaults table's indicated category, stated with its rationale), plus the mandatory `SKIP — let AI decide` option. The author's selection (including SKIP, which defers to the AI's stated recommendation) governs the pacing classification recorded in the Gate 1 Position Header Block. This question is never omitted, never folded silently into a larger multi-topic question, and never skipped under the zero-open-branch carve-out in Rule 19.4 below — that carve-out applies only to the five narrative-direction categories in 19.3, never to this one.

### 19.4 — Question Authoring Standards

- **Always-Available SKIP Option (load-bearing — v1.9).** Every question presented in any AskUserQuestion call under this Gate must include an explicit option labelled `SKIP — let AI decide` (or a near-equivalent phrasing that unambiguously hands the decision back to the assistant). The SKIP option is mandatory **on every question, on every Prose, with no exceptions**, and is in addition to (not a replacement for) the implicit "Other" escape hatch the AskUserQuestion tool provides automatically. Selection of SKIP by the author returns control of that specific decision to the assistant, which then proceeds on its own Canon-grounded creative judgement for that decision. SKIP is per-question (single decision returns to assistant), distinct from the per-Prose `[NO CONSULT]` waiver (entire Gate is bypassed for one Prose). An AskUserQuestion call that omits SKIP from any of its questions is a Rule 19.4 violation — Failure Mode C below.
- **Assistant-Proposed Scene-Setting Options.** The assistant — having read the Canon files and identified the open creative space — composes the option set for each question from its own creative judgement. Options should be concrete scene-setting choices (e.g., "Open in Varya's chamber at first light" / "Open at the practice yard with Aegon already arrived" / "Open in the Sun Hall during morning audience"), not abstract framings. The assistant is doing the design work; the author is doing the directional selection.
- **No-Asking-What-Canon-Already-Settles Rule (load-bearing).** Before composing any AskUserQuestion call, the assistant must read the relevant Canon Part files, ledger entity files, and prior Prose files at the moment of command receipt (per Rule 7.1.2 Files-Only Decision Sourcing) to determine which decisions are already fixed by the Canon and which are genuinely open. **Already-settled facts are NEVER asked about.** This includes, non-exhaustively: protagonist name when established by Part 30 Phase architecture, ledger entry, or prior Prose; scene-opening location when fixed by the Section/Novel-Part/Chapter/Area frame; emotional register when fixed by the established character arc and the Prose's place in the Contextual Earning sequence; the canon-information unit a Prose must earn when fixed by the prior-Prose continuity and the upcoming Prose's position in the Area's earning trajectory; cultivation outcomes settled by Parts 3–10 milestones; cosmological/world details settled by Parts 13–15. A Decision Consultation that surfaces already-Canon-settled facts as if they were open choices is a Rule 19.4 violation and re-frames the author as a redundant decision-maker rather than as the rudder. **Empirical test:** if the answer to the candidate question is already discoverable by reading the existing Canon files, the question must not be asked.
- **Directional decisions only.** The Decision Consultation Gate consults on **directional branch points** per Rule 19.3 — choices that meaningfully change the narrative trajectory of the upcoming Prose or downstream Prose. Micro-prose decisions — word choice, sentence rhythm, paragraph structure, dialogue phrasing, internal-monologue voice — remain with the assistant as part of the writing craft. Equally, Canon-settled facts remain with the Canon and are not surfaced as questions.
- **Concrete options, not open-ended prompts.** Each question presents 2–4 mutually-exclusive concrete scene-setting options plus the mandatory SKIP option plus the implicit "Other" escape hatch (which the AskUserQuestion tool provides automatically). The total option count per question is therefore typically 3–5 visible options (2–4 scene-setting + 1 SKIP), with "Other" implicit. Open-ended "what should happen?" prompts are non-compliant because they fail to give the author a starting frame for choice.
- **Persistent consultation until 100% clarity (Canon v4.35 Amendment — Exhaustive Clarity Mandate).** A single AskUserQuestion call carries 1–4 questions (the tool maximum). The assistant issues as many follow-up AskUserQuestion calls as are needed — the count is bounded by *completeness*, not by convenience or proportionality. If an author's answer to one question reveals a new genuine open branch (per Rule 19.3's five categories, or a scope/naming/tone consequence the chosen direction now creates), that new branch is surfaced in a further round rather than resolved silently by the assistant. Consultation continues, round after round, until no genuine open branch point remains — either because the author answered it or because the author explicitly selected SKIP for it. **Pacing (Rule 19.3a) is asked every time regardless of how many or how few narrative-direction branches exist.** Where every one of the five 19.3 categories is Canon-settled (zero open narrative branches) and pacing has been confirmed per 19.3a, the assistant may skip the narrative-branch portion of the AskUserQuestion call, declare that portion satisfied via "no open narrative branch points found," and proceed to sequence step 1 (Pre-Prose Read) — but the pacing question itself is never part of that skip. Skipping the narrative-branch portion for genuine cause is not a Rule 19.2 violation; asking about non-branches, terminating consultation while a genuine branch remains open, or omitting the pacing question, are all violations.
- **Recommended option marked.** When the assistant has a Canon-grounded recommendation among the offered scene-setting options (e.g., for Canon-coherence reasons or for narrative-craft reasons), it may mark that option `(Recommended)` and place it first per AskUserQuestion convention. The recommendation does not bind the author; it surfaces the assistant's craft judgement for the author's information. The SKIP option is never marked `(Recommended)` — its purpose is to hand the decision back to the assistant unmarked.
- **Per-Prose Waiver.** The author may waive Decision Consultation for an entire Prose by issuing the explicit token `[NO CONSULT]` after the Prose-producing command but before the assistant emits the AskUserQuestion call. A waived Prose proceeds directly to Pre-Output Audit and Drafting; the assistant uses its best Canon-grounded judgement on all categories. `[NO CONSULT]` is per-Prose only and does not establish a session-level waiver. The per-question SKIP option (above) is the lighter-weight equivalent for handing back individual decisions.

### 19.5 — Sequencing Within the Two-Gate Output

The full Prose-production sequence under Part 1 Rule 7.1.1 + this Area 19 is:

```
0. Decision Consultation Gate (this Area — multi-step AskUserQuestion)
1. Pre-Prose Read (Part 1 Rule 7.1.1(i))
2. Pre-Output Audit (Part 1 Area 4.2.1)
3. Prose Drafting (Pacing-Appropriate Depth Standard — Part 28 Area 4.1 + Part 2 Area 6.2.1, Canon v4.34 Pacing Law Amendment — all ten Depth Pillars (DP-1 through DP-10) simultaneously mandatory; word count: Fast 1400–1800/2000 ceiling / Medium 1900–2300/2500 ceiling / Slow 2400–2700/3000 ceiling / absolute hard cap 3000 words for all categories)
4. Post-Draft Audit / Audit Pass 1 of 2 (Part 1 Rule 7.1.1(k))
5. Gate 1 Chat Delivery (Part 1 Rule 7.1.1)
   — Position Header Block + Prose body + Contextual Earning Declaration + Post-Draft Audit Report
6. Inter-Gate Confirmation Prompt
7. Gate 2 File Writes (on [CONFIRM WRITE])
8. Post-Write Audit / Audit Pass 2 of 2
9. Post-Write Ledger Update
10. Post-Prose Completeness Indicator + Tier Scope Brackets
```

The Decision Consultation Gate is sequence step 0 — the first thing that happens after command receipt and before any Canon-validation or drafting work begins. Its outputs (the user's answers) become drafting inputs that the Pre-Output Audit (step 2) then validates against the full Canon. If a Decision Consultation answer is itself Canon-incompatible (e.g., the user picks a name that violates Part 28 Area 18 register/length, or picks a location that violates Phase 1 Locked Canon under Part 30), the Pre-Output Audit FAILS and the assistant returns to Decision Consultation with a re-framed question that surfaces the Canon constraint to the user.

### 19.6 — Failure Grades

Two distinct failure modes are graded:

**Failure Mode A — Missed Consultation on a Genuine Branch.**
- **Minor.** Missing one open directional branch point that should have been consulted. Correctable by issuing the missed-branch AskUserQuestion call before drafting begins.
- **Major.** Missing multiple open branch points, or proceeding past sequence step 0 to step 1 without any consultation in a Prose that contains open branches. Correctable only by halting drafting, issuing the consultation, and restarting the sequence at step 0.
- **Critical.** Drafting and writing a Prose all the way through Gate 2 file writes without ever having presented a Decision Consultation, in a Prose that contained open branches. The on-disk Prose files are flagged for [REDRAFT PROSE] (C-28); the Ledger Update is rolled back; the prior consultation-skipped draft is preserved as an archive but does not enter the canonical body.

**Failure Mode B — Asking About Already-Canon-Settled Facts (Rule 19.4 No-Asking-What-Canon-Already-Settles violation).**
- **Minor.** One question in an otherwise valid AskUserQuestion call surfaces a fact that is already Canon-settled. Correctable by acknowledging the misframe to the author and re-issuing only the genuinely-open questions.
- **Major.** A majority of questions in an AskUserQuestion call surface already-settled facts, treating the author as a redundant decision-maker rather than as the rudder. Correctable by acknowledging the misframe, reading the Canon files to map open branches, and re-issuing a corrected consultation.
- **Critical.** Repeated Failure Mode B violations across consecutive Prose-producing commands within a single session, indicating a systemic failure to read the Canon files at command receipt before composing the consultation. Triggers a session-level review against Rule 7.1.2 (Files-Only Decision Sourcing) and a re-grounding of the consultation pipeline.

**Failure Mode C — Missing Mandatory SKIP Option (Rule 19.4 Always-Available SKIP violation, v1.9).**
- **Minor.** One question in an AskUserQuestion call omits the explicit SKIP option. Correctable by re-issuing the AskUserQuestion call with the SKIP option restored on the missing question.
- **Major.** Multiple questions in a single call omit SKIP, or an entire call presents only forced-choice options without a SKIP path. Correctable by re-issuing the entire AskUserQuestion call with SKIP on every question.
- **Critical.** Drafting and writing a Prose all the way through Gate 2 file writes after a consultation in which the author was denied SKIP options on all questions, thereby being structurally forced to either pick a scene-setting option the assistant proposed or to type free-text into "Other." Treated equivalently to Failure Mode A Critical: the on-disk Prose files are flagged for `[REDRAFT PROSE]` (C-28), the Ledger Update is rolled back, and the consultation is re-issued with proper SKIP options before re-drafting begins.

### 19.7 — Interaction With Other Rules

- **Part 1 Rule 7.1.1 (Two-Gate Output Mandate):** Extended in Part 1 v2.12 with Rule 7.1.1(l) Decision Consultation Gate as sequence step 0. The Two-Gate Output continues to govern Gate 1 / Gate 2; Area 19 governs the new pre-Gate-0 consultation.
- **Part 1 Rule 7.1.1(k) (Two-Audit Mandate):** Audit Pass 1 scope extended to include a Decision Consultation Compliance Check — Audit Pass 1 FAILS if no Decision Consultation was performed for this Prose and no `[NO CONSULT]` waiver was issued.
- **Part 28 Area 3 (Naming Freedom Law):** Character names introduced via Decision Consultation become Base Information (B) in the relevant ledger upon delivery, per Area 3 Establishment.
- **Part 28 Area 15 (Contextual Earning Law):** The "Canon-Information Unit to Earn" decision category (Rule 19.3 #4) directly feeds the Three-Beat Test and the Contextual Earning Declaration emitted at Gate 1.
- **Part 28 Area 18 (Structural Naming Mandate):** The Decision Consultation Gate is the natural authoring moment for Novel-Part / Chapter / Area / Prose names that are introduced for the first time in the upcoming Prose; consultation outputs propagate directly into the Position Header Block and the Prose Title Block.
- **Part 2 Area 9 (Tier Scope Brackets):** Decision Consultation answers may readjust active Tier Scope Brackets (e.g., a chosen narrative branch widens the projected Area Prose-count); readjustments are logged per Part 2 Rule 9.10.1.
- **Part 36 Area 13 (DCG Thematic Preview Mandate):** Every DCG option must include a 1–2 sentence narrative preview in Part 36 thematic voice. Exposition Risk options must be flagged. Part 36 Autonomous Matters (sensory palette, tone register, living scene criteria) must not be asked as DCG questions.

**Part 36 Sovereignty Delineation (Canon v4.31):** Part 28 governs the **structural presence** of what must be in prose (Ten Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10, Contextual Earning Law, Naming Mandates, Lore Creation Law, Behavioral Authenticity Law, Scene Density Law). Part 36 governs the **thematic quality** of how what is required by Part 28 feels in prose — Living Scene Standard, Eight Cardinal Sins, Exposition Ceiling, Narrative Rhythm, Combat Texture, Emotional Authenticity, Sensory Palette, Dual-Track Identity, Tone Register. Both must be satisfied simultaneously. Part 28 compliance does not satisfy Part 36. Part 36 compliance does not satisfy Part 28. DP-3 (Lore and World-Building Integration) compliance is distinct from Part 36 WB-1 through WB-3 compliance. DP-4 (Sensory Environment) compliance is distinct from Part 36 Sensory Palette Law. DP-5 (Internal POV Architecture) compliance is distinct from Part 36 EA-1 through EA-4. A prose unit must pass both the structural test (Part 28) and the thematic test (Part 36).
