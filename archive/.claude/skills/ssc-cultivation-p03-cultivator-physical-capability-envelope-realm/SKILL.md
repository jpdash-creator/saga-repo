## Area 21 — Cultivator Physical Capability Envelope by Realm

### 21.1 — Purpose

This Area provides a consolidated reference for the physical capability parameters at each cultivation realm: passive speed, raw strike force, structural durability, sensory acuity, and environmental mobility. It is the baseline physical truth that underlies Part 5's combat mechanics, Part 6's move expression parameters, and Part 4's affinity environmental integration rules.

These parameters represent the cultivator's capability without any Named Move deployed — their passive physical baseline. Named Move execution (Part 6) augments these baselines through technique architecture; this Area governs the foundation on which those moves operate.

**Principle P3-21.1: Passive Capability Is the Combat Baseline.**
Before any technique is deployed, the cultivator's passive physical capability at their realm is a combat-relevant factor. Two cultivators engaging in physical contact without Named Move deployment are still operating at entirely different physical scales. This Area establishes those scales. Part 5 uses these baselines when assessing unstructured physical contact during combat (e.g., what happens when a Void God and a Champion physically collide). Part 6 uses these baselines to anchor what it means for a technique to produce "above-baseline" output at each realm.

### 21.7 — Cross-Part Application Rules

**Rule 21.7.1 — Capability Envelope Is the Floor of Combat Output.**
The values in Areas 21.2–21.6 represent passive baseline capability — what the cultivator can achieve without deploying any Named Move. Named Move deployment (Part 6) extends above these baselines through technique architecture. But these baselines determine what happens in physical contact outside of structured technique execution.

**Rule 21.7.2 — Move Expressions Layer on Capability Envelope.**
Part 6's move expressions at each realm (Part 6, Area 1.1) describe what Named Moves achieve within each realm tier. Those expressions are consistent with the capability envelopes here — a Named Move's output is always above the cultivator's passive baseline capability. Part 3 owns the baseline; Part 6 owns the technique amplification above it.

**Rule 21.7.3 — Affinity Environmental Signatures Layer on Capability Envelope.**
When a cultivator with active affinity executes movement or passive aura expression, Part 4's affinity environmental signatures layer on top of the baseline Heavenly Scale environmental signature (established in Part 3, Area 3.3 and the footprint table in Area 20). The base environmental effect is governed by Grade; the affinity-character of that effect is governed by Part 4, Area 1.7. Both must be satisfied simultaneously in prose.

**Rule 21.7.4 — Part 5 Battlefield Footprint Is Derived from Capability Envelope + Area 20.**
Part 5's battlefield spatial constraints (Part 5, Area 9.2) for minimum safe engagement distances and collateral damage zones are derived from the combination of passive capability (this Area) and spatial aura footprint (Area 20). The full picture requires both: Area 20 gives the aura and environmental footprint; this Area gives the physical action range of an unrestrained cultivator at each realm.

**Rule 21.7.5 — Capability Envelope and Cross-Realm Categorical Impossibility.**
The Categorical Impossibility Law (Part 3, Area 17.3) is grounded in the capability envelopes here. The reason Mortal Tier 2 attacks cannot harm an Xiantian cultivator is not an arbitrary rule — it is the direct consequence of the capability gap between Grades 1–5 (passive durability) and Grade 6+ (qi field immunity to Grade-1–5 force). The capability envelope is the physical evidence base for the impossibility law.
