## Area 12 — Combat Termination & Victory Conditions

### 12.1 — Termination Triggers

Combat terminates — and the engagement state ends — when **any one** of the following conditions is met:

**TT-1 — All Opposing Parties Defeated:**
Every party on one side has been rendered unable to continue through death, incapacitation, or complete loss of combat capacity.

**TT-2 — Formal Surrender Accepted:**
One or more parties have offered formal surrender and that surrender has been formally accepted by the opposing party (Section 12.5).

**TT-3 — Mutual Exhaustion:**
All parties have reached a state where continued combat is physically impossible due to energy depletion, injury accumulation, or resource exhaustion. Mutual Exhaustion produces a Draw condition (Section 12.4).

**TT-4 — Objective Achievement:**
The initiating party's objectives have been achieved — the territory secured, the resource seized, the person captured or killed, the message delivered — and the initiating party ceases hostile action. If the opposing party also ceases, combat terminates. If the opposing party continues hostile pursuit, combat is not terminated from their side.

**TT-5 — External Termination:**
A force external to the combat imposes termination — overwhelming presence of a higher-power entity whose combat-ending authority cannot be refused, natural catastrophe that makes continuation impossible, or institutional intervention (a recognized authority with the power to enforce cessation).

**TT-6 — Mutual Disengagement:**
Both parties disengage from active hostile contact and neither pursues or re-initiates. This is the most ambiguous termination trigger — it requires that both parties have genuinely ceased hostile intent, not merely tactical pause. Mutual Disengagement terminates the engagement but not necessarily the conflict (the larger conflict may continue).

### 12.2 — Victory Classification

**VC-1 — Total Victory:**
The opposing force has been completely eliminated (killed, incapacitated, or destroyed) with no retreat. Objectives are fully secured. The opponent cannot reconstitute or respond. Maximum consequence and social weight. Most rare.

**VC-2 — Decisive Victory:**
The opposing force has been decisively defeated — forced to retreat or surrender, objectives fully secured, the opponent's capacity to resume the conflict in the immediate theater is eliminated. The opponent survives but cannot contest the outcome.

**VC-3 — Strategic Victory:**
The overall strategic objectives have been achieved even if individual tactical exchanges were lost. The winning party's position in the larger conflict is significantly improved. Victory is not through total engagement domination but through strategic positioning.

**VC-4 — Technical Victory:**
The opponent formally concedes through surrender or treaty, acknowledging the winning party's superior position without requiring the complete elimination of the opposing force. The outcome is recognized by both parties.

**VC-5 — Pyrrhic Victory:**
Victory conditions are achieved but at a cost that materially weakens the winning party. The winner has achieved the objective but is significantly worse off than before the conflict in terms of forces, resources, political standing, or cultivation capacity. Pyrrhic victories are victories in consequence but defeats in effect.

**VC-6 — No Outcome (Draw):**
Neither party achieves decisive objectives. The conflict terminates through mutual exhaustion, external termination, or deliberate cessation without a recognized victor. See Section 12.4.

### 12.3 — Defeat Classification

**DC-1 — Catastrophic Defeat:**
The defeated faction's military capacity is destroyed, leadership is eliminated, objectives are lost, and no recovery path exists within the current narrative timeframe. The most severe defeat classification.

**DC-2 — Strategic Defeat:**
The faction is forced to withdraw from the theater or conflict; objectives are lost; but the faction's core capacity to eventually recover remains intact. Strategic defeat changes the strategic situation decisively against the defeated party.

**DC-3 — Tactical Defeat:**
A specific engagement or battle is lost, but the larger conflict is not resolved. The faction retains capacity to continue prosecuting the campaign. Tactical defeats are learning events as much as setbacks.

**DC-4 — Honorable Defeat:**
The losing party fought fully and well, lost clearly, and exits with their honor and cultural standing intact. Honorable defeat is not a lesser classification — it is a specific recognized outcome in cultivation society that carries no social shame (Part 25). A faction that fought to its last capacity before accepting defeat under Honorable conditions is not considered diminished.

**DC-5 — Disgraceful Defeat:**
Flight without fighting, surrender before genuine engagement, or violation of rules of engagement during a defeat that was then imposed by the opposing party. Disgraceful defeat carries maximum social and cultural weight (Part 25). Its consequences under Part 27 include reputation damage that persists beyond the immediate conflict.

### 12.4 — Draw Conditions

A **Draw** is recognized when:
1. Mutual Exhaustion (TT-3) terminates combat without objective achievement by either party
2. External Termination (TT-5) ends combat before either party achieves objectives
3. Both parties withdraw simultaneously without one side having achieved decisive advantage
4. A Representative Duel (FD-Type 5) or Formal Duel ends without a recognized winner (both combatants simultaneously incapacitated or the duel is invalidated)

Draws are recognized outcomes, not failures of the classification system. They carry their own consequence weight under Part 27 — a draw that follows a catastrophic battle has different consequences than a draw following a brief skirmish.

### 12.5 — Surrender Protocol

**Individual Surrender:**
A cultivator communicates surrender through: explicit verbal declaration, weapon cast down and hands raised, aura fully and visibly withdrawn and collapsed, kneeling with head bowed (cultural form governed by Part 25), or other universally recognized incapacitation signals. Surrender must be **unambiguous** — a cultivator who is recovering while appearing to have surrendered has not surrendered.

**Force Surrender:**
A command authority surrenders their forces on behalf of all members of that force. Force surrender requires the surrendering authority to have legitimate command over those forces. Force surrender takes effect when: the opposing party formally accepts it, the surrender terms are established, and the surrendering forces visibly stand down.

**Conditional Surrender:**
Surrender with terms attached. The surrendering party proposes conditions (treatment of prisoners, retention of certain assets, immunity of certain individuals). The opposing party may accept, counter-propose, or reject the conditions. Rejection of conditions does not automatically resume combat — the surrendering party may choose to offer unconditional surrender or to resume combat.

**Unconditional Surrender:**
Surrender without terms. The surrendering party accepts the winning party's determination of the post-combat situation. Unconditional surrender is the most extreme form of surrender and is recognized as ending the conflict definitively. The winning party bears no obligation other than those imposed by Part 25 (cultural norms of treatment of defeated) and Part 27 (consequences of how the defeated are treated).

**Surrender Acceptance Requirement:**
The winning party must formally accept a surrender for it to be binding. A surrender that is not accepted (the opposing party continues combat despite a valid surrender offer) is not a valid termination. However, rejecting a valid surrender offer and continuing combat carries severe consequence weight under Part 27.

**Post-Surrender Protections:**
Once surrender is accepted, the surrendered parties gain Protected Status (PS-2, Area 4.5). Continued violence against them is assault, not combat, and carries the full weight of Part 27's consequence law.

### 12.6 — Post-Combat Obligations

After combat terminates, the following obligations apply regardless of victory or defeat classification:

**PO-1 — Wounded Treatment:**
Wounded combatants — of all parties — are entitled to basic treatment under the cultural norms of Part 25. Denying treatment to wounded combatants as a deliberate act is a Part 27 violation.

**PO-2 — Prisoner Handling:**
Captured combatants who have surrendered or been incapacitated retain protected status. Their treatment is governed by Part 25 cultural norms and Part 23 affiliation obligations.

**PO-3 — Body Recognition:**
The dead of all parties are to be recognized — identified if possible, handled according to their cultural death customs (Part 25). Desecration of the dead is a severe cultural violation with consequence implications under Part 27.

**PO-4 — Ceasefire Maintenance:**
Once combat terminates, both parties maintain the termination state until new initiation conditions are met. Re-initiation of combat against the same party requires meeting the conditions of Area 4.3 fresh — the previous conflict does not maintain a permanent state of engagement.
