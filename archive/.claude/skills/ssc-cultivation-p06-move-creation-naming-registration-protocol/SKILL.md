## Area 6 — Move Creation, Naming, and Registration Protocol

### 6.1 — Why Naming Is Mandatory

In the Starward Saga's world, the naming of a cultivation technique is not merely cultural convention — it is the act that transforms raw power application into transmissible knowledge. An unnamed technique is applied force: it works, it may be powerful, but it is singular. It cannot be taught without showing it in person. It cannot be referenced in records. It cannot be developed systematically because it has no anchor for analysis. It is born when the cultivator uses it and dies when the cultivator dies.

A Named Move is codified principle: it encodes the underlying energy-circulation pattern, force-application structure, and philosophical principle of the technique into a transmissible form. The name is the identifier that allows the move to exist as a canonical object in the cultivation world — to be recorded in sect manuals, referenced by those who have seen it, studied by those who have experienced it, and improved upon across generations.

This is why Part 2, Area 3 Rule 3.2.1 mandates ***bold-italic*** formatting: the bold-italic signals to the reader that what is happening in the narrative is not casual force application but the invocation of a specific, canon-registered technique with history, properties, and classification. The bold-italic is visually distinct from both plain narration and italic dialogue — it marks the exact moment a canon-registered Named Move enters the scene.

**Principle of Naming:** A technique is named by its creator (in original creation), by its lineage (in inherited techniques), or by the community of cultivators who have observed and recorded it (in the rare case of observed-but-unclaimed techniques). A cultivator does not need to formally declare a name in a ceremony — the naming is complete when the cultivator has consistently referred to the technique by a specific name in their own cultivation practice and the technique has stabilized into a repeatable form.

### 6.2 — Move Naming Conventions

Named Moves in cultivation society follow several traditional naming patterns. None of these are mandatory — any name that the cultivator consistently uses for a codified technique is valid. However, the following conventions govern most named techniques:

**Pattern 1 — Principle + Action:**
Describes the underlying principle followed by the action it produces. Names the "why" and the "what." Examples: **Iron Bone Shattering Stamp** (Iron Bone principle + Stamp action), **Heaven Piercing Thrust** (Heaven-Piercing principle + Thrust action), **Still Water Mirror Slash** (Still Water principle + Slash action).

**Pattern 2 — Natural Force + Technique Form:**
Invokes a natural force or phenomenon as the primary descriptor, followed by the technique form. Common for affinity-aligned moves. Examples: **Crimson Tide Dispersal** (fire/red force + dispersal form), **Thunder Step Advance** (thunder force + advance form), **Void Space Collapse** (void principle + collapse form).

**Pattern 3 — Creator Reference:**
Names the technique after its creator (personal name, title, or legacy identifier) plus a descriptor. Most common for Signature moves and lineage-defining Elite techniques. Examples: **Elder Fen's Mountain Root Stance**, **Lin Shen's Unbreakable Fist**, **Seven Ancestors' Heaven Sealing**.

**Pattern 4 — Scale or Number + Effect:**
Invokes a scale reference or numerical principle. Common for Area-function moves and Catastrophic types. Examples: **Thousand Sun Detonation**, **Ten-Direction Seal**, **Ten-Thousand Needle Rain**, **Single Point Release**.

**Pattern 5 — State or Condition + Consequence:**
Names the cultivator's state or condition and the consequence it produces. Common for Signature moves with deep personal resonance. Examples: **Unyielding Mountain's Roar**, **Still Water After the Storm**, **Grief That Burns No Flame**.

**Invalid Names:** A move name is invalid if it is: identical to an already-registered move within the same sect/faction canon record without differentiation, non-specific to the point of being meaningless (a name like "Big Strike" has no principle-encoding function and is not treated as a formally named technique), or deliberately deceptive in a way that hides the technique's true nature (false naming to deceive opponents about technique function is a cultural violation per Part 25, though the technique remains valid — the naming violation is social, not mechanical).

### 6.3 — The Move Creation Process

**Stage 1 — Principle Identification:**
The cultivator identifies the underlying principle they are trying to express — the specific energy pattern, force-application structure, or affinity expression that defines the technique. This is the conceptual foundation. Without a clear principle, the technique cannot be stabilized into a Named Move.

**Stage 2 — Form Development:**
The cultivator physically develops the technique — experimenting with energy circulation paths, body mechanics, affinity expression, and output geometry until the technique can be executed consistently. This stage is the practice phase. The technique exists but is not yet named (and thus not yet classified as a Named Move).

**Stage 3 — Stabilization:**
The technique has been executed enough times with sufficient consistency that its form is stable — it produces the same type of effect each time, even if success rate varies. A technique that produces wildly different effects each time is not stabilized and cannot be named.

**Stage 4 — Naming and Type/Function Assignment:**
The cultivator names the technique. At this moment, the technique becomes a Named Move and is subject to this Part's classification system. The cultivator assigns (consciously or intuitively through cultural convention) the technique's Type and Function. These classifications should reflect what the technique actually is, not what the cultivator wishes it to be.

**Stage 5 — Registration (Optional but Recommended):**
In most cultivation societies, Named Moves of intermediate-level and above are registered with the cultivator's sect, clan, or cultivation institution. Registration creates a canonical record: the move's name, creator, classification, energy substrate, and primary/secondary functions. Registration allows the technique to be transmitted formally, claimed as intellectual property by the sect, and included in lineage records. Unregistered Named Moves are still Valid Named Moves — registration affects their cultural and legal status (Part 23 — Affiliation Law), not their canonical classification under this Part.

### 6.4 — Move Name Stability

Once a technique is named, the name is stable. A cultivator who changes their technique's name loses the accumulated mastery record associated with the old name in cultivation records (though their personal mastery is retained — it is the social/historical record that is disrupted). Renaming a technique is treated as creating a new Named Move from the existing technique's form; the technique's mastery history in institutional records begins again from the new name's registration date.

If a technique evolves in Type (Area 9.3 — Type Upgrade), the evolved technique typically receives a new name or a named addition to its existing name (prefix or suffix indicating elevation). The original technique's record is preserved as the ancestor of the evolved form.
