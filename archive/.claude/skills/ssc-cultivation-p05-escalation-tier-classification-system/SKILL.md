## Area 5 — Escalation Tier Classification System

### 5.1 — Overview

All conflicts in the Starward Saga are classified into one of seven escalation tiers (E1 through E7). This classification is not cosmetic — it determines the intensity ceiling, third-party rules, termination conditions, and battlefield containment requirements that apply to the conflict. The tier reflects the actual scale and nature of the conflict, not the power of the participants.

**Critical Principle:** Escalation tier and cultivation level are independent variables. A fight between two Divine Realm cultivators can be classified as E1 (Personal Combat) if it meets the E1 parameters. A war between Mortal Tier 2 factions can be E6 if it meets the E6 parameters. Tier classification is based on participant count, scope, duration, faction commitment, and stated objectives — not on the power level of those involved.

The **Formal Duel** is a special combat classification that exists alongside the escalation tier system. It can occur within any tier (most commonly E1) but carries its own governance rules independent of tier.

### 5.2 — The Seven Escalation Tiers

---

#### E1 — Personal Combat

**Participant Count:** 2 to 10 combatants total
**Spatial Scope:** Immediate personal space; single location
**Duration:** Seconds to hours
**Nature:** Personal grievance, survival response, honor conflict, territorial dispute at personal scale, criminal encounter
**Faction Involvement:** Participants may represent factions but no formal faction-level commitment is required or implied
**Objectives:** Personal in nature — defeating, capturing, driving away, or killing specific individuals
**Power Expression Ceiling:** All cultivation levels may participate; intensity must remain proportional to the immediate combat space (see Area 7)
**Collateral Obligation:** Minimize collateral force to immediate combat zone; no acceptable civilian casualties beyond incidental
**Initiation Legitimacy Requirements:** Standard conditions under Area 4.3
**Examples:** A street fight between two cultivators, a survival response to an ambush, a disciple conflict over a resource, a confrontation in a marketplace

**E1 Escalation Trigger to E2:** Participant count grows beyond 10 from either side through entry of additional parties, OR the conflict relocates while maintaining active engagement, OR duration extends with sustained hostile intent across multiple locations.

---

#### E2 — Skirmish

**Participant Count:** 2 to 50 combatants total
**Spatial Scope:** Localized area; single or closely adjacent locations
**Duration:** Hours to days
**Nature:** Contested local resource, border tension, patrol engagement, ambush response with reinforcement, local territorial claim
**Faction Involvement:** Possible but unofficial; participants may represent factions without their factions having formally committed forces
**Objectives:** Tactically defined — control of a specific position, elimination of a specific group, assertion of territorial presence
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Participants fight to their individual capability within the spatial footprint constraint; no deliberate strategic-level destruction intended
**Collateral Obligation:** Active measures to limit collateral force to the immediate engagement zone; civilian evacuation of the immediate area is expected if time permits
**Examples:** Border patrol clash, rival sect disciples fighting over a sacred spring, a caravan defense against a bandit group, a hunting party ambush

**E2 Escalation Trigger to E3:** Participant count grows beyond 50 from either side, OR faction leadership formally commits units to the engagement, OR the conflict persists beyond 48 hours with continuous hostile intent.

---

#### E3 — Engagement

**Participant Count:** 50 to 500 combatants
**Spatial Scope:** Single location or tightly connected multi-site; recognizable front lines possible
**Duration:** Days to weeks
**Nature:** Contested territory, punitive strike, resource seizure, systematic elimination of opposition presence in an area
**Faction Involvement:** Recognized faction participation; mid-tier leaders directly commit units; the faction is aware of and directing the engagement
**Objectives:** Tactical objectives with strategic implications — control of a location, destruction of an opposing formation, seizure of resources
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Strategic-scale destruction permitted within the declared engagement zone; civilian displacement is an expected consequence and must be factored into pre-engagement planning
**Battlefield Declaration Requirement:** Formal or informal designation of the engagement zone is required under Area 9 before major actions begin
**Collateral Obligation:** Civilian areas outside the declared engagement zone must be protected
**Examples:** Sect occupation of contested territory, a city guard response to a criminal organization, military enforcement operation in a rebellious district

**E3 Escalation Trigger to E4:** Participant count grows beyond 500, OR the conflict develops strategic objectives that require sustained multi-week prosecution, OR top-tier faction leadership directly assumes command of forces.

---

#### E4 — Battle

**Participant Count:** 500 to 50,000 combatants
**Spatial Scope:** Regional; multiple positions, established front lines, supply considerations
**Duration:** Days to months for a single battle; a battle may be one event within an E5 Campaign
**Nature:** Major strategic confrontation, decisive territorial contest, large-scale elimination of opposing military capacity
**Faction Involvement:** Major faction commitment; leadership-level decision to prosecute the battle; the battle represents a significant resource and personnel commitment
**Objectives:** Strategic — seizing or defending territory, destroying a significant portion of an opposing faction's military capacity, achieving a decisive blow that alters the strategic situation
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Theater-scale destruction capabilities permitted within designated battlefield; no attacks on non-combat population centers outside the military objective
**Battlefield Declaration Requirement:** Full battlefield declaration protocol under Area 9 required; protected zones must be formally identified before engagement
**Command Structure Requirement:** At least one side must have an identifiable command hierarchy for the battle to be classified as E4 rather than large-scale E3
**Examples:** Major sect war battles, kingdom-versus-kingdom confrontations, large-scale military expeditions

**E4 Escalation Trigger to E5:** Multiple battles under unified strategic intent occur, OR the conflict develops into a sustained multi-month prosecution with multiple fronts, OR the conflict encompasses a regional strategic theater.

---

#### E5 — Campaign

**Participant Count:** Defined by constituent battles (no fixed cap; effectively unlimited within campaign scope)
**Spatial Scope:** Continental or multi-regional; multiple theaters of operation
**Duration:** Months to years
**Nature:** Systematic strategic conquest, sustained defensive campaign, prosecuted cause over multiple battles and operational theaters
**Faction Involvement:** Complete faction mobilization; economy of scale considerations; entire institutional apparatus engaged
**Objectives:** Campaign-level — territorial conquest, regime change, systematic destruction of opposing faction's capacity to resist, protection of homeland against systematic invasion
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Any within the Phase ceiling; campaign doctrine governs deployment of extreme power (see Area 15)
**Formal Recognition:** By E5, all parties involved recognize the state of active campaign; it is no longer ambiguous
**Examples:** Sect alliance wars, continental unification campaigns, divine realm cultivator-led conquest operations

**E5 Escalation Trigger to E6:** Formal war declaration between political entities, OR the conflict develops into existential competition between major political powers with no exit condition below total victory or total defeat.

---

#### E6 — War

**Participant Count:** Effectively unlimited; entire political entities engaged
**Spatial Scope:** Continental or multi-continental; affects entire populations
**Duration:** Years to generations
**Nature:** Existential competition for dominance, resources, ideology, or survival; involves every level of society, not just military forces
**Faction Involvement:** All available resources committed; war is the dominant fact of the political entity's existence during the conflict
**Objectives:** War-level — regime change, territorial conquest at civilizational scale, ideological victory, or destruction of the opposing entity's capacity to ever threaten again
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Any within Phase ceiling; divine realm combat may occur; no artificial intensity ceiling other than Phase constraints
**Formal Declaration Requirement:** E6 requires either an explicit formal declaration of war between recognized political entities OR an escalation of hostilities so total that formal declaration is simply recognition of existing reality
**Peace Requirement:** E6 can only be terminated by formal peace — a recognized agreement that ends the state of war. See Area 12 for termination conditions.
**Examples:** Civilization-spanning wars, divine realm power bloc conflicts, wars of extinction between major political entities

**E6 Escalation Trigger to E7:** One or more parties declare or demonstrate the intent to entirely eliminate the opposing entity's existence — not defeat but obliteration.

---

#### E7 — Annihilation Conflict

**Participant Count:** Unlimited
**Spatial Scope:** Unlimited; may affect cosmological structures
**Duration:** Unlimited
**Nature:** One or more parties seek the total elimination of the opposing entity — not military defeat, not territorial conquest, but the absolute end of the opposing force's existence
**Faction Involvement:** Existential commitment — every resource, every life, every asset is relevant
**Objectives:** Annihilation — the opposing entity ceases to exist in any meaningful form
**Power Expression Ceiling:** None under this Part; Phase ceiling (Area 15) remains applicable
**Formal Classification Trigger:** A conflict is reclassified as E7 when: a party publicly declares intent to annihilate the opposing entity, OR actions taken make annihilation the only logical conclusion even without declaration, OR the conflict's scale has exceeded all possibility of conventional termination
**Termination:** An E7 conflict can only terminate through: total annihilation of one party, intervention by a power so overwhelming that further conflict is impossible, or a paradigm-shifting event that fundamentally eliminates the cause of the conflict
**De-escalation:** E7 cannot be voluntarily de-escalated by either party once classified. The conditions that created E7 cannot be un-created by declaration alone.
**Examples:** Genocidal campaigns backed by divine realm power, cosmological-scale conflicts between existential forces, wars of extinction between divine-tier civilizations

---

### 5.3 — Formal Duel Protocol (Special Classification)

A **Formal Duel** is a distinct combat classification that operates parallel to, not within, the escalation tier system. A Formal Duel is always personal in scale (two combatants or two small representative groups) and is governed by pre-agreed rules. It is most commonly, but not exclusively, classified at E1.

**Conditions for Formal Duel Recognition:**
1. A challenge is issued — explicitly, publicly, or through recognized cultural ritual (Part 25 governs the cultural forms by faction)
2. The challenge is accepted — the challenged party acknowledges the duel
3. Terms are established — victory conditions, power ceiling agreement if any, life/honor/wager stakes, and time/place
4. Witnesses are designated — at least one recognized witness per side, or a mutually recognized neutral witness
5. The duel proceeds under the established terms

**The Five Formal Duel Types:**

**FD-Type 1 — Life Duel:**
Fought to the death. Surrender is not an accepted termination. The duel ends only when one combatant is killed or so thoroughly defeated that death is imminent and inevitable. Life Duels are governed under the strictest honor codes — interruption by any party (including allies of the combatants) is a severe violation. Life Duels have the highest cultural significance and consequence weight.

**FD-Type 2 — Submission Duel:**
Fought until one party cannot continue fighting or formally submits. Submission may be physical (incapacitation, inability to rise) or explicit (spoken surrender, weapon cast down, aura fully withdrawn). This is the most common formal duel type. Victory requires clear, unambiguous incapacitation or submission — a cultivator who is injured but still capable of fighting has not yet submitted.

**FD-Type 3 — Honor Duel:**
Fought to demonstrate the superior skill, cultivation, or technique of one party over another. The goal is not to kill or incapacitate but to make the comparative quality unmistakable. An Honor Duel may be terminated when: one combatant acknowledges the other's demonstrated superiority, a recognized audience consensus declares a winner, or a designated judge calls the outcome. Honor Duels are the most culturally stylized form of duel.

**FD-Type 4 — Wager Duel:**
Stakes are agreed before combat — territory, resources, disciples, information, oaths, or any other agreed currency. Victory transfers the agreed stakes to the winner. The duel type (Submission, Honor, etc.) is agreed alongside the stakes. The Wager Duel is binding — the losing party's faction, sect, or recognized authority is obligated by the outcome.

**FD-Type 5 — Representative Duel:**
Two parties with a larger conflict resolve it through single combat between designated champions. The outcome of the representative duel binds both sides as if it were the outcome of the larger conflict. A Representative Duel requires: mutual agreement on the binding nature of the outcome, designation of champions by each side, and recognized witnesses to the agreement. A party that refuses to honor a Representative Duel's outcome retroactively escalates the conflict by one tier.

**Duel Invalidation Conditions:**
A formal duel is invalidated if: either party violates the agreed terms (fighting style, power ceiling, technique restrictions), the witness structure is compromised (witnesses removed or threatened), third-party interference occurs with the consent or direction of one party, or either party engages outside the declared duel space. Invalidation of a duel caused by one party is equivalent to that party's defeat under the original terms.

**The Right to Challenge:**
Any cultivator may formally challenge any other cultivator. Cultural norms (Part 25) govern whether a particular challenge is honorable or dishonorable by power differential. Challenging someone significantly weaker than oneself without proportional stakes is culturally dishonorable in most cultivation societies. The challenged party has the right to: accept under the proposed terms, propose modified terms, or refuse. Refusal carries cultural weight — in many cultivation societies, refusing a challenge is a form of concession. The specific cultural interpretation of refusal is governed by Part 25.

### 5.4 — Escalation Tier Determination Rules

**Rule 5.4.1 — Classification at Initiation:**
Every conflict is classified at its tier at the moment of initiation based on the immediately visible parameters: participant count, faction commitment level, stated objectives, and spatial scope. The classification may be revised as the conflict develops.

**Rule 5.4.2 — Upward Revision:**
A conflict may be reclassified to a higher tier when the escalation trigger conditions for the current tier are met. Upward revision is automatic — once the trigger conditions are objectively met, the new tier applies regardless of whether either party formally acknowledges the escalation.

**Rule 5.4.3 — Downward Revision Prohibition:**
A conflict may not be reclassified to a lower tier without meeting the full termination conditions of the current tier. A war cannot become a skirmish by declaration alone. De-escalation requires meeting the termination conditions of the current tier and formally re-establishing a lower engagement level through agreement.

**Rule 5.4.4 — Retroactive Classification:**
Events that were not initially classified may be retroactively assigned a tier based on their actual parameters. Retroactive classification does not change the events themselves but establishes their canonical tier for consequence and cross-event consistency purposes.

**Rule 5.4.5 — Disputed Classification:**
If the tier of a conflict is narratively disputed between parties (e.g., one faction calls it a "border skirmish" while the other calls it an act of war), Part 1's domain routing determines the canonical classification based on the objective parameters defined in this Part, not on either party's political framing.

### 5.5 — Tier Interaction with Cultivation Level

**Rule 5.5.1 — Proportional Force Obligation:**
A cultivator engaging in a conflict classified below their combat capability must limit their force expression to what is consistent with that tier's intensity ceiling (Area 7). A Divine Realm cultivator in an E1 personal combat cannot casually discharge continental-scale destruction. The tier governs the rules; the cultivator's power governs their capability. The two are independent.

**Rule 5.5.2 — Involuntary Escalation:**
If a cultivator cannot control their power output — due to breakthrough state, Deviant Breakthrough, emotional breakdown, or Core destabilization — the conflict's effective tier automatically elevates to match the intensity being expressed, regardless of the initiating tier. Involuntary Escalation is a canon event with consequences under Part 27; it does not excuse the escalation.

**Rule 5.5.3 — Escalation Consent:**
Deliberately escalating a conflict's tier beyond the current classification requires at minimum the implicit consent of at least one other party through their actions. A unilateral escalation that draws unwilling third parties into a higher-tier conflict is a Force Legitimacy violation and carries maximum consequence weight under Part 27.
