## Area 12 — Integrated Practitioner Governance

### 12.1 — Definition and Classification of Integrated Practitioners

An Integrated Practitioner is an individual who personally operates cultivation-technology integration at Model C (Symbiotic) or Model D (Unified). They are neither pure cultivators nor pure technologists — they operate both tracks simultaneously as a unified practice.

**Distinction from Cultivators Who Use Technology:**
Any cultivator in a CCT-5 (Multi-World) or higher civilization uses some technology (cultivation chambers, monitoring systems, transport). Using technology does not make a practitioner an Integrated Practitioner. Integrated Practitioner status requires personal operation of Model C or D integration — the practitioner's cultivation architecture and the technology system's CI Technology architecture are co-designed around each other. The practitioner and the system have a permanent architectural relationship.

**Distinction from Technologists Who Use Cultivation Resources:**
A technology operator in an integrated civilization may use CPM-powered systems and operate within cultivation-enhanced environments. Using cultivation resources through technology does not make a person an Integrated Practitioner. The practitioner must themselves have the cultivation capability (Part 7's cultivation-side thresholds) to participate as the cultivation-side of the integration.

**The Integrated Practitioner Requirement:**
Integrated Practitioner status requires simultaneously:
1. Cultivation realm meeting Part 7, Area 6.2 threshold for the claimed integration model
2. CI Technology operational access meeting Part 8, Area 6.2 threshold for the claimed integration model
3. Personal architecture integration — the practitioner's cultivation architecture (Core, meridians, affinity) is directly co-designed with the CI Technology system they operate

---

### 12.2 — Practitioner-Level Synergy Assessment

**Technology Tier of an Integrated Practitioner:**
An Integrated Practitioner has a CI Technology operational level — not a civilization technology tier. This level is bounded by:
- The highest CI Technology level available in their civilization's infrastructure (Part 8, Area 5 governs what is available)
- The cultivation realm threshold for CI Technology operation (Part 8, Rule 15.12.2)
- Their personal cultivation realm (the cultivation-side interface)

The Integrated Practitioner's personal synergy capability is assessed by:
1. Their cultivation realm → what integration model the cultivation side supports (Part 7, Area 6.2)
2. Their CI Technology access level → what integration model the technology side supports (Part 8, Area 6.2)
3. Effective integration model = lower of the two threshold assessments

**The Personal Synergy Profile:**
An Integrated Practitioner's synergy capability is described by their personal synergy profile:
- Integration Model (A/B/C/D) — the effective model they can operate
- CI Technology Level — the domain level within Domain 12 they have personal access to
- Cultivation Realm — their cultivation tier from Part 3
- Affinity Types — the affinities whose typed CPM they can use with affinity preservation
- Affiliated System — the specific CI Technology infrastructure they are architecturally integrated with (if any)

### 12.3 — The Asymmetric Integrated Practitioner

Not all Integrated Practitioners have balanced cultivation realm and CI Technology access. Asymmetric Integrated Practitioners have one track significantly more advanced than the other.

**High Cultivation / Low Technology Access:**
A Divine-realm cultivator in a Tier 5 civilization has cultivation capability far exceeding what the civilization's CI Technology can support. Their integration model is bounded by the technology side — Model B at best, regardless of their cultivation tier. Their cultivation capability is not wasted — they achieve full cultivation output; the integration simply cannot reach its potential because the technology side is insufficient. Their cultivation side may wait years for the civilization's CI Technology to advance to match their cultivation tier.

**High Technology Access / Low Cultivation:**
A Tier 8 technology civilization's integration operator who has Xiantian cultivation is asymmetric in the other direction. The technology side can support Model C; the cultivation side is limited to Model B (Xiantian cultivation maximum per Part 7, Area 6.2). The technology operates at full Tier 8 capability; the integration remains bounded by the cultivation-side threshold. This practitioner's cultivation advancement is their primary path to deeper integration capability.

**The Asymmetry Motivation:**
Asymmetric Integrated Practitioners are powerful motivating forces in narrative. The High Cultivation / Low Technology practitioner has reason to invest in the civilization's CI Technology development — their personal capability is limited by institutional infrastructure, not individual talent. The High Technology / Low Cultivation practitioner has reason to advance their cultivation despite being in a technology-dominant civilization — their technology access has already outrun their cultivation and awaits its partner.

### 12.4 — Social and Factional Position of Integrated Practitioners

**Economic and Political Value:**
Integrated Practitioners at Model C and D are rare and their skills are irreplaceable. In integrated civilizations, they are:
- Essential operators of CI Technology systems requiring cultivation participation
- Primary CPM-quality assessors and conversion operators for high-grade CPM
- Strategic assets whose loss degrades a civilization's effective integration capability below what technology alone can maintain
- Frequently targets of factional recruitment, institutional loyalty programs, and political pressure

**The New Social Class:**
The emergence of Integrated Practitioners at civilization scale (Model C or D) represents a civilizational transformation point. Before their emergence, cultivators and technologists are distinct social categories. After their emergence, a new category exists that belongs fully to neither and uniquely to both. This produces social tension (Part 25 — Culture and Social Law — fully authored, Areas 1–28; New Elite Problem governed by Part 25 social class axes CC-Class, Ac-Class, W-Class simultaneously) as existing power structures adapt to the new class. Part 22 (Faction Law — fully authored, Areas 1–30) governs the political structures that emerge, including the formation of Synergy Integration Institutes (SII, Part 22 Area 9.3) as the primary faction-level synergy governance structure.

**Factional Specialization:**
Civilizations with significant Integrated Practitioner populations typically develop factions organized around integration specialization:
- Integration Institutes that train Integrated Practitioners and develop CI Technology alongside cultivation
- Hybrid Schools that combine cultivation lineage with technological specialization
- Integration Guard units that provide military capability through Model C and D combat integration
- Integration Council structures where Integrated Practitioners have institutional authority over synergy-related policy
