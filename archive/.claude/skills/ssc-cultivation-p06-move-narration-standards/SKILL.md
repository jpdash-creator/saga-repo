## Area 11 — Move Narration Standards (DP-9 Named Move Architecture — Canon v4.24)

### 11.0 — DP-9 Named Move Architecture Integration

Area 11 governs the prose rendering of every Named Move. As of Canon v4.24, all Named Move narration must simultaneously satisfy **Part 28 Named Depth Pillar DP-9 — Named Move Architecture**, which mandates that every Named Move appearing in a Prose unit is rendered with three-layer depth: (a) **physical execution layer** — the cultivator's body mechanics, qi direction, meridian activation, and technique trigger; (b) **sensory output layer** — what the technique looks, sounds, smells, feels, or tastes like at the point of expression, rendered through the POV character's senses; and (c) **consequence propagation layer** — what the technique changes in the environment, the target, or the deploying character's internal state in the immediate aftermath. A Named Move rendered with only one or two layers is a DP-9 violation and an Audit Pass 1 FAIL. Named Move narration that satisfies DP-9 automatically satisfies the bold mandate (Rule 11.1), the success/failure rendering standards (Rules 11.3–11.4), the soul dual-layer standard (Rule 11.6), and the affinity narration standard (Rule 11.7) — these rules are not superseded but are expressions of the same DP-9 depth requirement. The hard pacing-determined word count (2000–3800; Part 2 Area 6.2.1) per Prose unit (Part 28 Area 4.2) ensures that Named Move sequences have sufficient prose space to render all three DP-9 layers without compression.

### 11.1 — The Bold Mandate in Practice

Part 2, Area 3.2.1 establishes that all Named Moves are bolded in prose. Part 6 governs the naming conditions that make a technique a Named Move. The combined requirement is: every consciously executed, named cultivation technique is written in **bold** at every point of use in prose. This includes:

- The first use of a technique in a scene
- Every subsequent use of the same technique in the same Prose unit
- Uses in internal monologue when a character is thinking about a specific technique by name
- References in dialogue when characters name specific techniques

**What Is NOT Bolded:**
- General descriptions of force application without a specific technique name ("he sent qi forward," "she reinforced her body")
- Sensory descriptions of technique effects ("the fire consumed the wooden structure")
- Post-technique environmental descriptions
- Unnamed force applications

### 11.2 — Move Name First Appearance

When a Named Move appears for the first time in the novel — the first time the reader encounters this specific technique — the prose must provide sufficient descriptive context for the reader to understand what the technique does. This does not mean explaining it mechanically (which would break immersion) but rendering it experientially: what does it look, feel, and sound like? What is its immediate effect? Who deployed it and what was the intent?

Subsequent uses of the same technique in the same novel can be rendered more concisely — the reader has context. But they should never be purely mechanical ("he used **Crimson Tide Dispersal** again"). Each use should carry the scene's emotional and tactical charge.

### 11.3 — Move Success and Failure Narration

When a Named Move is deployed in prose, the outcome (success or failure, based on the Type's success rate and the cultivator's mastery) must be explicitly rendered through consequences. The prose cannot leave a Named Move's deployment ambiguous.

**Success Narration:** The technique's intended effect manifests at the appropriate level for the cultivator's power density and mastery. Environmental signature is consistent with the Heavenly Scale Grade (Part 3, Area 3.3). Physical and affinity effects are rendered with sensory precision.

**Failure Narration:** When a technique fails (the author decides based on type success rates, mastery, and narrative needs that this deployment does not succeed), the failure mode is rendered explicitly:
- For Type 2–5 (Intermediate through Signature): a "near-miss," incomplete formation, or partially expressed effect
- For Type 6 (Fusion): one of the four failure modes, rendered with appropriate intensity
- For Type 7 (Catastrophic): underpowered expression, premature release, or collapse — each with distinct physical description
- For Type 8 (Mythic): reality rejection, partial manifestation, uncontrolled manifestation, or backlash of reality — each rendered with the gravity a Mythic failure deserves

**High-Complexity Failure Narration Standards (Rule 11.3.1):**
The failure of a Catastrophic or Mythic move is a **major prose event** — it deserves the same narrative weight as a success. The following standards govern how each failure mode is rendered for high-complexity move types:

*Catastrophic Type Failures:*
- **Underpowered Expression:** The technique begins to form — the cultivator feels the energy moving, the environment responds partially (terrain shudders, a pressure wave starts) — and then the formation collapses before reaching critical threshold. The result is a visibly impressive but sub-Advanced effect. The narration must convey the cultivator's internal sensation of the technique "dying in their hands" and the opponent's recognition that the Catastrophic attempt failed. This is a turning point — the energy cost was paid; the opponent now knows the cultivator is depleted.
- **Premature Release:** The technique releases before complete formation, centering the explosion on or near the cultivator instead of the intended target. Narration must render the self-damage (meridian strain, knockback, disorientation), the cultivator's immediate recovery attempt, and any incidental environmental destruction that the release creates. The opponent may be partially affected if in close proximity but not at the intended scale.
- **Collapse:** The technique simply does not form. The energy expenditure vanishes into nothing. Narration renders the cultivator's moment of internal silence — the expected surge that never arrived — and then the sinking recognition that the reserves were depleted for nothing. This is the most psychologically devastating failure mode and should carry that weight.

*Mythic Type Failures:*
- **Reality Rejection:** The technique approaches formation — the cultivator may sense reality "bending around" the attempt, seeing the edges of the effect begin to manifest — and then the universe simply refuses. The energy disperses without creating any effect. Narration should convey the metaphysical resistance: not the technique failing, but reality actively declining to permit the interaction. The cultivator is left with the existential weight of having reached for something the universe refused.
- **Partial Manifestation:** Perhaps the most narratively complex outcome — the technique manifests at a fraction of its intended scope or in an altered form. The effect is real but incomplete: a technique intended to alter a battlefield creates only a localized, personal-scale reality disturbance. The prose must show what was intended versus what manifested, and the cultivator's attempt to determine if the partial result is salvageable.
- **Uncontrolled Manifestation:** The full technique activates but disconnected from the cultivator's intent. The effect unfolds according to the technique's principle rather than the cultivator's direction — a space-altering Mythic technique might create a spatial anomaly that affects friend and foe alike; a time-interaction Mythic might slow everything in a radius including the user. Narration renders the cultivator's horror as they watch something they cannot control unfold.
- **Backlash of Reality:** The most dangerous outcome — reality's resistance rebounds directly into the cultivator. Narration must render the physical sensation of cultivation damage (essence erosion, root strain, lifespan reduction), the visible signs visible to observers (the cultivator aging visibly, their qi signature destabilizing), and the profound assessment moment afterward: how much did this attempt cost, and is what remains sufficient to survive the current situation?

**Narrative Pacing of Failure Scenes:** A high-complexity move failure scene should not be rushed. The attempt, the moment of failure recognition, the immediate consequence, and the cultivator's next decision are each meaningful beats. Compressing all four into a single sentence is a narration violation — it signals to the reader that the system's stakes do not actually apply.

### 11.4 — Move Name in Combat Sequences

In extended combat sequences with multiple Named Move uses, the prose maintains the bold standard throughout. The sequence creates a rhythm — each named technique a distinct beat in the combat's pulse. The author should vary the descriptive depth around each named technique to prevent repetition: some techniques are rendered in full sensory depth; others are rendered more briefly when pace demands it. But the bold itself never disappears.

### 11.5 — Multi-Cultivator Move Naming

When multiple cultivators are using Named Moves simultaneously (a common condition in large-scale battles at E3+), the prose must maintain clarity about which Named Move belongs to which cultivator. The attribution is handled through prose conventions (the cultivator is identified immediately before the bold move name, or the move name's description identifies its source through its energy signature) rather than parenthetical annotations.

### 11.6 — Soul Move Special Narration

Soul moves require a dual-layer narration standard (per Part 4, Area 2.8). When a Named Move is a soul technique, the prose renders both the physical layer (what observers see: nothing, a faint shimmer, a visible soul manifestation) and the metaphysical layer (what the targeted cultivator experiences: the soul-force contact, the pressure against their soul structure, the specific effect the soul technique produces). Both layers are rendered in the same Prose unit without breaking the POV standard of Part 2, Area 4.

### 11.7 — Affinity Move Narration

When a Named Move is affinity-typed (involves a cultivator's affinity resonance), the narration must incorporate the affinity's environmental response appropriate to the cultivator's affinity level:
- At Resonance/Attunement (Levels 4–5): secondary environmental response layered onto the move's direct effect
- At Harmony+ (Level 6+): the affinity's environmental response is pronounced and may dominate the visual description of the technique
- At Sovereignty/Origin Convergence (Levels 8–9): the affinity expression is so total that the technique's physical mechanics may be secondary in the prose to the affinity's manifestation

All affinity narration standards must simultaneously satisfy Part 4, Area 1.7 (affinity narration rules) and this Area's move narration standards.
