## Area 29 — Narrative Consistency Standards for the 20-Tier Scale

### 29.1 — Managing Scale in Prose

The 20-Tier Scale spans from pre-civilization (Tier 1) to absolute existence (Tier 20). Writing prose that accurately renders each tier's distinctive capability without becoming either too technical or too vague requires calibration.

**Rule 29.1.1 — Use Concrete Sensory Anchors:**
Tier descriptions should not be rendered in prose as abstract statistics ("they had 10^26 watts of energy"). They should be grounded in sensory and experiential concrete details. Render Tier 8 not as "they had Dyson sphere energy" but as "the star above them was no longer fully visible — the vast hexagonal collector arrays blocked direct stellar light, turning the system's sky into an intricate lattice of engineered panels that stretched across the heavens in every direction." The statistic is the law-document description; the prose is the embodied experience.

**Rule 29.1.2 — Let Scale Imply Itself:**
Characters who live in a Tier 11 civilization don't narrate that their civilization is galactic-scale. They reference it obliquely through what they treat as unremarkable: the news from the other galactic arm arriving, the logistics of attending a political event three thousand light-years away, the centuries-long projects their civilization has running. Scale is established through what is treated as normal, not through explicit statement.

**Rule 29.1.3 — Tier Transitions as Character Experience:**
When a character from a lower-tier civilization encounters higher-tier technology for the first time, their reaction establishes the tier gap for the reader without requiring explicit tier-number exposition. A character from a Tier 5 civilization encountering Tier 9 technology should be rendered in a state of fundamental disorientation — not merely impressed, but structurally unable to process what they are seeing within their existing frame of reference.

### 29.2 — Technology Tier in Dialogue and Description

**Rule 29.2.1 — Characters Do Not Cite Technology Tiers in Dialogue:**
In-narrative characters do not refer to "Tier 8 technology" or "Type 7 Energy at Level 11." They refer to specific technology by name, function, or description. Tier notation is a governance tool; the narrative renders the technology experientially. Authors use tier notation in planning and consistency checks — not in prose.

**Rule 29.2.2 — Technology Expertise Is Characterization:**
A character who understands the technology they are using renders it differently than a character who doesn't. A Tier 8 weapons systems engineer describes their weapon with technical precision and appropriate confidence. A Tier 3 cultivator examining a captured Tier 8 weapon describes it through confusion, awe, and grasping analogy. The technology's tier is constant; the character's relationship to it varies and reveals character.

**Rule 29.2.3 — Technology Has a Voice in Each Era:**
The way characters discuss technology reflects the era's relationship to technology. In Tier 1–3 civilizations, technology is practical, immediate, and material — tools, machines, weapons. In Tier 5–7 civilizations, technology is ambient infrastructure and personal augmentation — it is part of identity and social structure. In Tier 11+ civilizations, technology is civilizational context — it is the world itself, too pervasive and complex to be discussed as a separate category.
