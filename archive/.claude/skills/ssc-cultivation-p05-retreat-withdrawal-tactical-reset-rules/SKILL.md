## Area 14 — Retreat, Withdrawal & Tactical Reset Rules

### 14.1 — The Three Forms of Leaving Combat

A combatant or faction may leave an active combat state through three distinct mechanisms with different canonical meanings and consequences:

**Form 1 — Tactical Retreat:**
Withdrawal to a more defensible position within the same conflict. The retreating party remains in the engagement — they are changing their position, not leaving the fight. Combat rules remain in full effect. The opposing party may pursue.

**Form 2 — Strategic Withdrawal:**
Deliberate departure from the engagement theater with organized force preservation. The withdrawing party is exiting the current engagement but not surrendering the broader conflict. The engagement terminates from the withdrawing party's side; the larger conflict (if E5+) continues. The opposing party may pursue under pursuit rules but the engagement has technically ended from the withdrawing party's side.

**Form 3 — Combat Flight:**
Disorganized departure under existential pressure — the forced abandonment of position and order under overwhelming assault, where preservation of life takes priority over organized military structure. The most psychologically and socially costly form of departure. Combat Flight is not tactical; it is survival. The fleeing party retains all rights to reconsolidate, regroup, and return unless they have offered and accepted surrender.

### 14.2 — Legitimacy of All Three Forms

All three forms of leaving combat are legitimate under this Part. There is no canon rule that requires a cultivator or force to remain in combat until death or incapacitation. Retreat is not surrender. Withdrawal is not defeat. Combat Flight is not dishonor under canon law, though cultivation society's cultural norms (Part 25) may assign social weight to it.

### 14.3 — Legitimate Retreat Mechanics

**Tactical Retreat Requirements:**
- The retreating party must maintain hostile intent — they are retreating to a better position, not abandoning the fight
- The retreating party's force must remain organized and capable of re-engagement
- The retreating party signals retreat in a recognizable way (movement away from the opponent, defensive formation, etc.)

**Strategic Withdrawal Requirements:**
- The withdrawing party's command authority makes the withdrawal decision
- The withdrawal is executed with organized force preservation as the priority
- The withdrawing party's hostile intent toward the opponent ends with the withdrawal

### 14.4 — Pursuit Rules

**Pursuit Legitimacy:**
Pursuing a retreating or withdrawing force is canonically legitimate in combat. The retreat does not create immunity from being followed and re-engaged. Pursuit is part of combat, not a separate act.

**Pursuit Limits:**
1. **Distance Limit:** Pursuit of a retreating or withdrawing force is legitimate for a distance up to three times the original engagement zone's effective radius. Beyond this distance, continued pursuit constitutes a new initiation event (requiring Area 4.3 conditions) rather than continuation of the original engagement.
2. **Protected Zone Limit:** Pursuit cannot continue into protected zones (Area 9.3). A retreating force that reaches a civilian settlement, sacred ground, or treaty territory gains the protections of that zone. Pursuit that violates a protected zone is a Force Legitimacy violation.
3. **Surrender Response Limit:** If a retreating party formally offers surrender during their retreat (Combat Flight into surrender), the pursuing party must respond — continued pursuit of a party actively surrendering is an assault, not pursuit combat.
4. **Power Differential Limit:** A significantly superior-grade cultivator pursuing an overwhelmed fleeing cultivator must recognize the cultivator cannot defend themselves. The 3-Level Rule (Part 3, Area 7.2) does not obligate the superior to stop pursuing, but continuing to pursue and attack a combatant more than 3 levels below oneself who cannot meaningfully respond may constitute Force Legitimacy violation under certain cultural frameworks (Part 25).

### 14.5 — Tactical Reset

A **Tactical Reset** is a mutually recognized (or independently executed) pause within an ongoing engagement for both parties to recover, regroup, and reassess — without either party intending to terminate the engagement.

**Tactical Reset Characteristics:**
- Both parties disengage from immediate hostile contact
- Neither party pursues the other during the reset
- Both parties retain their engagement status — the fight is not over
- The reset ends when either party re-initiates hostile contact

**Tactical Reset Recognition:**
A Tactical Reset is most clearly recognized in cultivation combat involving Core Realm and above participants, where Core depletion necessitates recovery periods before continued high-output exchange. Both parties may independently recognize the reset without explicit agreement. The test is intent: if both parties have genuinely stopped for recovery and not for withdrawal, it is a Tactical Reset.

**Tactical Reset vs Strategic Withdrawal:**
The distinction is intent. A Tactical Reset preserves engagement — both parties expect to continue. A Strategic Withdrawal exits engagement — the withdrawing party is done with the fight, even if they may re-engage at a later time under a new initiation. A force that claims Tactical Reset and then completes a Strategic Withdrawal during the reset period has violated the reset's implicit terms and is treated as having executed a deceptive withdrawal (Force Legitimacy implication).
