## Area 5 — Systemic Consequences

### 5.1 — Faction Consequence Architecture

Actions by individuals and their factions produce consequence cascades at the faction level (governed by Part 22 for faction mechanics; this Part for the consequence framework):

**Individual Action → Faction Consequence:**
When a character who is identified with a faction takes a significant action, the faction shares in the consequence:
- A disciple who commits a public atrocity implicates the sect that trained them
- A faction commander whose military action causes excessive collateral damage implicates the entire faction's political standing
- A faction founder's founding act defines the faction's entire consequence relationship with the world — the consequences of that founding act persist indefinitely

**The Faction Reputation Consequence:**
Faction reputation is one of the most significant long-term consequence currencies. A faction's reputation for reliability, for honoring commitments, for treating others with specific standards — or its reputation for betrayal, for cruelty, for opportunism — is a systemic consequence of accumulated actions. This reputation propagates forward in time (IC-4 to IC-5 timescale) as a permanent structural modifier of how other factions interact with it.

**Faction Collapse as Consequence Cascade:**
Major faction destruction events are not simply the consequence of the immediate defeat — they are the culmination of accumulated unresolved consequences across every domain:
- Broken alliances that were never repaired
- Moral injury in members that was never addressed
- Environmental damage that created enemy populations
- Internal contradictions between stated values and actual behavior that eroded internal loyalty
- Resource overextension that left no resilience margin
Faction collapse is always, in the canonical Starward Saga world, a consequence-structured event — not a narrative convenience but the climax of a long cascade.

### 5.2 — Civilizational Consequence Architecture

At the civilizational scale (Part 21), consequence law operates through the civilization's Hold Level and development trajectory:

**Hold Regression as Consequence:**
A civilization's Hold Level (Part 21 CCT system) can regress as a consequence of:
- Military overextension that depletes the resources and willpower required for Hold maintenance
- Political collapse from accumulated governance failures
- Cultural destruction that removes the collective identity cohesion necessary for civilizational function
- Environmental catastrophe from unchecked high-power events
Hold regression is never instantaneous at the civilizational scale — it is always the culmination of a long IC-4 or IC-5 consequence cascade.

**Civilizational Legacy Consequence:**
Civilizations that commit certain categories of action — civilizational genocide, sacred ground destruction, fundamental violation of the cosmological laws — carry legacy consequences that persist across their full historical existence and affect how they are perceived, treated, and responded to by other civilizations across generational timescales.

### 5.3 — Location and Environmental Consequence

**Sacred Ground Desecration:**
Sacred Ground desecration (Part 13, Area 14 governs Sacred Grounds; Part 26/27 governs the consequences) is a consequence category of exceptional significance:
- *Immediate:* The Sacred Ground's energy field is disrupted or destroyed; formations dependent on it fail; sacred cultivation environments are lost
- *Short-to-medium:* Spiritual consequences propagate — cultivation communities dependent on the Sacred Ground lose their cultivation advantage; the surrounding region loses the positive qi influence
- *Long-term:* Cultural grief for the destroyed Sacred Ground propagates through every civilization that held it sacred; pilgrimage routes terminate; cultural practices dependent on it become impossible; the loss becomes a defining narrative event in the cultural memory of affected communities
- *Political:* The desecrator becomes the object of cross-civilizational hostility in proportion to how broadly the Sacred Ground was held sacred

**Dimensional Anomaly Consequence:**
Actions that produce dimensional anomalies — unstable spatial regions created by specific high-power cultivation or technology events — create persistent environmental consequences:
- Navigation hazard (IC-3 to IC-5): the anomaly remains a hazard for travelers long after the event
- Cultivation disruption (IC-3): the anomaly's energy field disrupts normal qi formation in surrounding regions
- Resource depletion: regions near anomalies see reduced cultivation resource quality
