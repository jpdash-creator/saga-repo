## Area 18 — Bipedal Sentient Species Governance Law

### 18.1 — Definition and Scope

Area 18 governs non-human sentient species whose natural form includes bipedal locomotion and whose members qualify as characters under Area 1.1 sentience criterion. This Area is distinct from:
- **Awakened Cultivated Beasts (Area 12.2)** — beasts that have reached sufficient cultivation to develop human-equivalent cognition; a Cultivated Beast may *assume* bipedal form as part of cultivation but is governed by the Cultivated-Beast framework, not Area 18;
- **Hybrid Sentients (Area 17)** — a bipedal-species member who is also a cross-species or integrated hybrid is additionally governed by Area 17;
- **Other Sentient Beings (Area 19)** — non-bipedal sentients (incorporeal, collective, planar, etc.) are governed by Area 19.

Area 18 specifically governs sentient species whose natural form includes bipedal locomotion and whose anatomical and cultural distinctness from human-lineage characters warrants its own governance track. The canonical reference case within the current canon is the Minotaur species (referenced in `bison.md` as the hero Bison's species); other bipedal sentient species entering the prose in any Phase receive Area 18 governance by default.

### 18.2 — Origin Archetypes

Bipedal sentient species in the Starward Saga cosmos trace to one of five origin archetypes. Each origin archetype has implications for species-cultural identity (Part 25), cosmological positioning (Part 30 macro story cross-reference), and canonical genealogy.

**18.2.1 — Ancestral-Divergent Origin:** The species diverged from a common ancestral lineage with other sentient species in distant cosmological past. This is the most common origin archetype for bipedal sentients in the canon. Ancestral-Divergent species typically carry mythic origin stories emphasizing ancient lineage; their cultural identity is grounded in continuity with ancestral tradition.

**18.2.2 — Uplifted Origin:** The species was biologically or cultivationally uplifted from a non-sentient ancestral state by an external agent (a divine being, an ancient civilization, a cultivation working) — gaining sentience, language, and cultural capability through the uplift event. Uplifted species typically carry founding-myth narratives tied to the uplift event; their relationship to the uplift agent (reverence, resentment, indifference) is a central species-cultural axis.

**18.2.3 — Created/Constructed-Biological Origin:** The species was biologically engineered or cultivationally constructed by an agent who designed them with intent. Created species typically carry complex identity questions: what relationship do they have to their creators? what do they owe? what are they free from? This origin generates rich arc potential for individual members wrestling with species-origin questions.

**18.2.4 — Dimensional-Native Origin:** The species originated in a cosmological region (a different plane, dimension, parallel world, ancient-era reality) and entered the current cosmological space through a canonical transition event. Dimensional-Native species typically carry cultural markers of their origin plane and may maintain ties to a home-plane identity alongside their integration into the current cosmological frame.

**18.2.5 — Post-Awakening-Evolved Origin:** The species evolved from a pre-sentient ancestor through natural cultivation awakening at the species level (distinct from individual beast awakening per Area 12.2). Post-Awakening-Evolved species typically show transitional traits between pre-awakened ancestor form and fully sentient modern form; their relationship to non-awakened cousins of their ancestral species is a species-cultural tension axis.

### 18.3 — Anatomical Classification Axes

Bipedal sentient species are classified along four anatomical axes that determine their governance-relevant physical profile.

**18.3.1 — Humanoid-Baseline:** The species' anatomy closely parallels human baseline (two arms, two legs, head with standard sensory organs, roughly human-scale proportions) with relatively minor anatomical distinctives (ear shape, skin coloration, eye phenotype, musculature density). Humanoid-Baseline species integrate most readily with human-majority factions; most of their governance matches human governance with minor species-specific modifiers.

**18.3.2 — Augmented-Bipedal:** The species carries human-baseline anatomy augmented with additional distinctive anatomical features — horns, tails, wings, claws, extra digits, augmented sensory organs, altered dentition, or similar structural additions. Augmented-Bipedal species are the most common source of Species-Innate Moves (Part 6 Area 12.3) because the augmented anatomy provides the substrate for species-innate techniques. The canonical reference: the Minotaur species (human-baseline plus horns, tail, augmented musculature) with Species-Innate moves **Chaprachup** and **Chalakudhaal**.

**18.3.3 — Composite-Bipedal:** The species' anatomy combines multiple distinct anatomical lineages in an integrated form (a species whose anatomy shows both reptilian and mammalian characteristics, for example). Composite-Bipedal species may carry multiple distinct Species-Innate Move sets drawn from their constituent anatomical lineages. Their cultural identity is often organized around the anatomical fusion rather than either single origin.

**18.3.4 — Mythic-Bipedal:** The species' anatomy includes elements that exceed standard biological plausibility under the current cosmological physics — reality-bending anatomical features (anatomical space-fold capabilities, elemental-embodied limbs, anatomy that scales with emotional state, etc.). Mythic-Bipedal species are rare and typically carry cosmological significance (Part 30 macro story cross-reference). Their Species-Innate Moves can reach Catastrophic or Mythic Type (Part 6 Area 2) because the anatomical substrate itself carries cosmological weight.

### 18.4 — Species Cultural Carrier Rules

Bipedal sentient species carry culture as an integral part of species-identity. Area 18.4 establishes what every species-culture must carry and how species-cultural elements interact with broader cultural governance (Part 25).

**Rule 18.18.4.1 — Species Language Mandate:** Every canonical bipedal sentient species carries a distinct language (or language family) that predates or distinguishes from the cultivation-world common tongue. Species languages are the source of Species-Cultural Proper Name conventions (Part 6 Area 12.3.2) — the Minotaur-language words **Chaprachup** and **Chalakudhaal** are the canonical reference. A species without its own language is under-specified for Area 18 purposes and requires canonical language establishment before extensive prose appearances.

**Rule 18.18.4.2 — Species Lineage Rites:** Every canonical bipedal sentient species carries lineage rites — cultural practices through which species-identity is transmitted across generations. Lineage rites may include coming-of-age ceremonies, name-giving rituals, bonding-to-ancestor practices, species-cultural apprenticeships, or combat-rite initiations. Individual species-members' relationship to their species lineage rites is a central ideological axis (Part 20 cross-reference).

**Rule 18.18.4.3 — Species Collective Memory:** Every canonical bipedal sentient species carries forms of collective memory — oral traditions, written lineage chronicles, cultivation-encoded ancestral records, or species-psychic collective substrates — through which past generations' experience reaches present members. Access to species collective memory is a species-cultural capability; loss of such access (through exile, cultural suppression, or the character's own choice) carries specific narrative and ideological weight.

**Rule 18.18.4.4 — Species-Cultural Depth Preservation:** Prose involving a bipedal sentient species character must render the species-cultural layer truthfully alongside the cultivation-world layer. A Minotaur hero character functions both within cultivation-world society (where cultivation realm and sect affiliation dominate) and within species-cultural reality (where horn-lineage, species-language, and lineage-rite obligations matter). The prose must honor both layers; flattening a species-distinct character into cultivation-world standard is a violation of N16-12 (below).

### 18.5 — Species Anatomical Development Scale (SADS) and Cultivation Access Profile

The **Species Anatomical Development Scale (SADS)** is Area 18's answer to the Part 3 Realm ladder for species-innate capability. SADS measures a species-member's anatomical maturity — the developmental state of the species-specific anatomy that governs S-3 Species-Innate Substrate move output per Part 6 Area 12.3.3.

**18.5.1 — SADS Tiers (Species-Agnostic Framework):**

The following tiers apply to every catalogued bipedal sentient species with species-specific scaling values:

- **SADS-1 Juvenile:** The species-anatomy is present but immature; Species-Innate moves are accessible but at reduced output.
- **SADS-2 Adolescent:** The species-anatomy approaches maturity; Species-Innate move output approaches full species-typical scaling.
- **SADS-3 Mature:** Full species-typical anatomical maturity; Species-Innate move output at full species-typical baseline.
- **SADS-4 Peak:** Exceptional anatomical development beyond species-typical; Species-Innate move output scaled above baseline.
- **SADS-5 Legendary:** Rare anatomical development representing species-legendary capability; Species-Innate moves may reach Catastrophic or Mythic Type at this tier.

**18.5.2 — SADS and Cultivation Realm Orthogonality:**

SADS and cultivation realm are orthogonal. A SADS-3 Mature Minotaur may be at Mortal Tier 2 Warrior cultivation or at Core Realm cultivation; the SADS tier reflects anatomical maturity only. Cultivation realm affects S-1 moves (per Area 4 Access Table) and conditions the body generally (per Part 3 Area 11), but SADS tier is the direct governance axis for S-3 moves.

**18.5.3 — Cultivation Access Profile for Bipedal Sentient Species:**

Every bipedal sentient species carries a Cultivation Access Profile documenting:
- Species-typical affinity clusters (which Part 4 Area 1 affinities are common, rare, or absent for members of this species)
- Meridian architecture variations (whether the species' meridians match human-standard patterns or diverge)
- Soul cultivation accessibility (whether species-typical soul cultivation follows standard Part 4 Area 2 patterns)
- Body cultivation advantages and penalties (whether species-anatomy provides cultivation-relevant advantages — a Minotaur's dense musculature provides a body-cultivation affinity)
- Cultivation technique learning modifiers (whether species-cognition, species-memory, or species-cultural factors affect Area 7 learning speed)

A species' Cultivation Access Profile is recorded in the species entry within ledger-people-society-civilization (each sentient species with canonical prose appearance is a civilization-ledger entity).

### 18.6 — Species-Innate Anatomy Interface (Cross-Reference to Part 6 Area 12.3)

Part 6 Area 12.3 governs the Species-Innate Substrate (S-3) at the move-taxonomy level. Part 16 Area 18.6 governs the species-side interface:

**Rule 18.18.6.1 — Species-Specific S-3 Catalog.** Every canonical bipedal sentient species with narrative-active S-3 moves maintains a Species-Specific Innate Move Catalog within the species entry in ledger-people-society-civilization. The catalog enumerates all species-cultural codified innate moves (meeting the Area 12.3.1 anatomical-derivation test) with Move Name, Anatomical Derivation, SADS availability profile, species-cultural significance, and transmission protocol (per Area 12.7).

> **[Repo layout note]** `ledger-people-society-civilization` above refers to `.claude/story-memory/ledger-people-society-civilization` in the current repo layout (post-.claude/ migration).

**Rule 18.18.6.2 — Individual Member S-3 Registration.** Individual species-members who carry specific S-3 moves register those moves in their character ledger Section 3 "Innate Moves (Part 6 Area 12)" subsection per Part 1 Area 5.4. The individual's registration cross-references the species catalog and notes individual-specific variations (mastery grade, anatomical particulars, training lineage within the species).

**Rule 18.18.6.3 — Species-Cultural Transmission of S-3 Moves.** Intra-species transmission of S-3 moves follows species-specific cultural protocols. Some species treat S-3 moves as sacred and transmit them through rite-of-maturity only (a Minotaur receives **Chalakudhaal** instruction at a specific coming-of-age event). Other species transmit S-3 moves communally (every species-member learns the standard catalog as part of general education). Other species hold certain S-3 moves as clan-secret, limited to specific species-lineages. These cultural protocols are species-level Part 25 governance operationalized through Area 18.6 with species-specific entries in the civilization ledger.

### 18.7 — Interspecies Civilization Interfaces

Bipedal sentient species rarely exist in isolation. Their relationship to other sentient populations — human-majority cultivation civilizations, other bipedal sentient species, non-bipedal sentients (Area 19), Awakened Cultivated Beast populations — is governed at the civilization-level by Part 22 (Faction Law) and Part 24 (Civilization Law) with Area 18.7 providing species-specific interface rules.

**Rule 18.18.7.1 — Species-Civilization Participation Modes.** A bipedal sentient species may participate in broader civilization through one of five modes: (a) Integrated — full participation in the dominant civilization with species-distinctness preserved; (b) Federated — species maintains its own civilization alongside and in treaty with the dominant; (c) Enclaved — species maintains distinct territories within the dominant civilization; (d) Diasporic — species is dispersed through multiple civilizations without a home territory; (e) Hidden — species maintains concealed existence within or alongside other civilizations. Each mode produces distinct character archetypes for species-members navigating the civilization interface.

**Rule 18.18.7.2 — Species Political Representation.** Where a bipedal sentient species participates politically in broader civilization structures, Area 18.7.2 governs the forms that representation may take: species-delegate structures, species-seat reservations, dual-citizenship frameworks, or informal-influence networks. Part 22 Faction Law handles the broader political architecture; Area 18.7.2 handles the species-specific shape of species representation.

**Rule 18.18.7.3 — Interspecies Tension Types.** Interspecies relationships canonically generate four typical tension types: (a) Resource-Competition Tensions, (b) Cultural-Incommensurability Tensions, (c) Historical-Grievance Tensions, (d) Cosmological-Origin Tensions. These tensions manifest at individual-character level through specific dramatic pressure patterns that the prose must render with species-specific nuance rather than generic "tribes-at-odds" treatment.

### 18.8 — Narration Standards (N16-12 through N16-14)

**N16-12 — Species-Cultural Layer Rendering.** Prose involving a bipedal sentient species character must render the species-cultural layer as present alongside the cultivation-world layer. Species-specific idiom, lineage-rite references, species-language terms, species-anatomical reality (how it feels to have horns in doorways sized for humans; how it feels to walk with a tail behind you), and species-cultural memory all must find scene-by-scene prose presence. Flattening to cultivation-world-standard is a N16-12 violation.

**N16-13 — Species-Anatomical Embodiment.** Prose involving an Augmented-Bipedal or Composite-Bipedal species-member must render the species-anatomical embodiment in sensory specificity. The reader must feel the character's anatomy — its weight, its mechanics, its sensory envelope, its social consequences. A Minotaur character in prose is not a human in costume; they are a Minotaur, and the prose must embody that.

**N16-14 — Species-Language Name Rendering.** When a species-language term enters the prose — a Species-Cultural Proper Name move (Part 6 N6-14), a species-specific concept, a lineage-rite designation — the prose uses the species-language term in italics (or bold in the case of Named Moves) without in-prose translation. Translation may occur in narrator exposition when the POV character needs the concept explained, but the species-language term is preserved as itself. This preserves species-cultural distinctness at the prose level.
