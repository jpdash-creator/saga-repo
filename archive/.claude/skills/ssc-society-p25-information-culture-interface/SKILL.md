## Area 15 — The Information-Culture Interface

### 15.1 — Secrecy Culture Variations

Different civilizations and factions have radically different cultural norms around information, privacy, and disclosure:

| Secrecy Culture Type | Description | Associated CPT | Social Convention |
|---------------------|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Open-Share (OSC) | Information sharing is a cultural virtue; hoarding information is taboo | CPT-5 Syncretic-Adaptive | Voluntary disclosure of relevant information before asking; information-as-gift custom |
| Earned-Trust (ETC) | Information is shared only after relationship depth is established | CPT-1 Hierarchical-Sacral | Staged disclosure protocol; information as intimacy marker |
| Commercial-Confidential (CCC) | Information has explicit exchange value; disclosure requires compensation | CPT-3 Mercantile-Exchange | Information purchase convention; NDAs as social contract |
| Sovereign-Secret (SSC) | Information ownership is sovereign right; unauthorized access is violation | CPT-4 Isolationist-Purity | No disclosure obligation; questioning is offensive |
| Covenant-Protected (CPC) | Certain information is sacred covenant matter; disclosure is apostasy | CPT-6 Theocratic-Covenant | Confession as controlled disclosure; sacred secret oath |

### 15.2 — Oral Tradition as Cultural Identity

At CCT-1 through CCT-3 civilizations (and in rural/isolated communities of higher-tier civilizations), oral tradition (Part 24 ISS-10) is not merely a storage method — it is a cultural practice and identity marker. The oral tradition keeper (bard, chronicler, griot, elder storyteller) is a distinct high-prestige social role in low-technology or isolation communities. The destruction of oral tradition practitioners is recognized as cultural genocide even by otherwise neutral cultural observers.
