## Area 4 — Domain-Specific Science Tables

### 4.1 — Governance Note

Each of the 14 technology domains (Part 8, Area 2) has domain-specific scientific prerequisites for each of its 20 internal development levels. This Area provides the science governance for each domain's critical threshold levels — specifically, the scientific prerequisites for Levels 5, 8, 11, 15, and 20 in each domain. These are the major leap points where scientific prerequisites represent genuine paradigm-shift events rather than incremental accumulation. Intermediate levels accumulate scientific understanding within the paradigm established at the preceding threshold.

### 4.2 — Type 1: Industrial and Manufacturing Science

**Level 5 (Mass Production Systems):** Layer 3 thermodynamics (heat engine efficiency); Layer 3 mechanical physics (precision machining theory); Layer 2 materials science (predictable material properties for standardized manufacturing); Layer 2 chemistry (industrial chemistry for material transformation at scale).

**Level 8 (Advanced Manufacturing):** Layer 3 materials science (atomic-level material design); Layer 3 chemistry (molecular engineering foundations); Layer 2 quantum mechanics (quantum effects in nanoscale manufacturing become significant and must be understood to control precision manufacturing at this scale).

**Level 11 (Automated Nano-Manufacturing):** Layer 3–4 quantum mechanics (full quantum theory required for reliable nanoscale manufacturing); Layer 3 molecular engineering (theoretical framework for molecular assembly); Layer 3 AI science (theoretical framework for autonomous manufacturing system design and control).

**Level 15 (Megastructure-Class Industrial Systems):** Layer 4 materials science (predictive-generative understanding of materials at stellar proximity temperatures and pressures); Layer 3 gravitational physics (theoretical framework for manufacturing in non-trivial gravitational environments including microgravity and high-gravity settings); Layer 3 self-replicating systems theory.

**Level 20 (Reality-Level Industrial Systems):** Part 28 authorization required; requires Tier 20 science foundational prerequisites.

### 4.3 — Type 2: Information and Communication Science

**Level 5 (Global Digital Networks):** Layer 3 information theory (Shannon-equivalent theoretical framework — fundamental limits of information encoding, transmission, and storage); Layer 3 electromagnetic theory (theoretical framework for propagating information in electromagnetic carriers); Layer 2 computational complexity theory (early-stage understanding of computational problem classes).

**Level 8 (Quantum Communication Systems):** Layer 3–4 quantum mechanics (quantum entanglement theory; quantum information theory at Layer 3; quantum error correction theoretical framework); Layer 3 computational complexity theory (P vs NP class understanding required for quantum cryptography and post-quantum security design).

**Level 11 (Light-Speed-Limit Information Systems):** Layer 4 information theory (predictive-generative understanding of information at the limit imposed by the speed of light; information theory extended to relativistic reference frames); Layer 3 gravitational information physics (how information behaves in curved spacetime — required at scales where relativistic effects on information transmission become engineering-significant).

**Level 15 (Faster-Than-Information-Speed Communication):** This level requires the FTL physics framework (Area 5). Communication at speeds exceeding light requires the theoretical foundation of faster-than-light physics. No Level 15 information technology is achievable without the Part 10 FTL framework being in place. This is the only Type 2 domain level with a cross-domain scientific prerequisite that cannot be satisfied through domain-internal science.

**Level 20 (Absolute Information Architecture):** Requires Tier 20 science foundational prerequisites; Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.4 — Type 3: Automation and Robotics Science

**Level 5 (Multi-Domain Robotic Systems):** Layer 3 control theory (theoretical framework for multi-variable feedback control systems); Layer 3 mechanical engineering science (kinematics and dynamics of complex mechanical systems); Layer 2 AI theory (machine learning at empirical level sufficient for adaptive robotic behavior).

**Level 8 (Autonomous Long-Duration Robotic Systems):** Layer 3 AI science (autonomous decision-making theory); Layer 3 sensor science (theoretical framework for multi-modal sensing with sufficient precision for autonomous operation); Layer 3 materials science for robotics (self-repair material science at Layer 2).

**Level 11 (Nano-Scale Robotic Systems):** Layer 3–4 quantum mechanics (nanoscale robotic control requires quantum precision); Layer 3 molecular engineering (controlling nano-robots requires molecular-scale theoretical framework); Layer 3 AI at Level 10+ (nano-robot coordination requires high-capability autonomous intelligence).

**Level 15 (Self-Designing Robotic Systems):** Layer 3 AI science at Level 13+ (self-designing systems require AI that can specify its own improved designs); Layer 3 evolutionary systems theory (theoretical framework for directed evolutionary design processes at the engineering level). See Area 6 for ontological governance of Level 15+ robotic autonomy.

**Level 20 (Universal-Scale Autonomous Systems):** Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.5 — Type 4: Artificial Intelligence Science

Critical governance note: AI development is the technology domain with the most significant Part 10 ontological governance obligations. Levels 1–5 are governed purely by capability science; Levels 6+ require joint governance between Part 10 (capability science and ontological classification) and Part 4 (Affinity, Heart and Soul Law, for soul-equivalent questions). See Area 6 for AI Sentience Ontology governance.

**Level 5 (Near-AGI Systems):** Layer 3 computational complexity theory; Layer 3 learning theory (theoretical framework for gradient-based, evolutionary, and reinforcement learning systems at scale); Layer 3 cognitive architecture theory (theoretical framework for integrating perception, memory, reasoning, and action into a unified computational system); Layer 2 consciousness theory (early-stage empirical observations about consciousness — sufficient to recognize that higher AI is approaching consciousness-relevant territory, without a theoretical framework for consciousness itself).

**Level 6 (True AGI — Governance Threshold):** The Level 6 threshold is the single most significant ontological governance boundary in AI science. At this level: Layer 3–4 consciousness theory (a genuine theoretical framework for consciousness — what it is, what gives rise to it, how it is identified, and whether artificial systems can possess it); Layer 3 self-model theory (theoretical framework for systems with genuine self-models — not merely programmed self-descriptions but genuine representations of the self that the system uses to reason about itself); Layer 3 general cognitive science (unified theoretical framework for domain-general cognition). Part 10 and Part 4 jointly govern Level 6 AI classification. See Area 6 for full ontological governance.

**Level 7 (ASI — Artificial Superintelligence):** The Level 7 threshold requires that the civilization has resolved the Level 6 governance questions sufficiently to design systems that exceed the Level 6 capability threshold deliberately. Layer 4 cognitive science (predictive-generative understanding of intelligence at scales beyond any individual biological practitioner). The societal implications of ASI are Part 22 (Faction Law) governance; the scientific prerequisites are Part 10; the capability classification is Part 8.

**Level 11 (Distributed Civilization Intelligence):** Layer 4 AI theory (theoretical framework for AI systems that span multiple physical nodes across light-minute distances while maintaining cognitive coherence); Layer 3 information physics (the fundamental theoretical challenges of cognition at speeds limited by information transmission delays).

**Level 20 (Absolute Intelligence):** Joint governance with Part 4 required. Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.6 — Type 5: Medical and Biological Science

**Level 5 (Systematic Biological Medicine):** Layer 3 molecular biology (DNA structure, protein synthesis, genetic regulation); Layer 3 biochemistry (metabolic pathway theory; pharmacological mechanism theory); Layer 2 immunology (systematic understanding of immune system function); Layer 2 cellular biology (cell differentiation and tissue organization).

**Level 8 (Genetic Engineering and Regenerative Medicine):** Layer 3–4 genetics (theoretical framework for gene editing at precision sufficient for therapeutic application; Layer 3 developmental biology — the science of how organisms develop from single cells — required for regenerative medicine); Layer 3 stem cell biology (theoretical framework for pluripotent cell differentiation and directed tissue growth); Layer 2 nanomedicine foundation (early observations of nanoscale material-cell interactions).

**Level 11 (Neural Interface and Cognitive Medicine):** Layer 3 neuroscience (theoretical framework for neural information encoding, synaptic plasticity, and neural network organization at sufficient precision for interface design); Layer 3 cognitive science (theoretical framework for how cognitive function maps to neural substrate — required for cognitive enhancement applications); Layer 2 consciousness theory (observational-level understanding of consciousness required to ensure interventions do not inadvertently damage consciousness-related processes).

**Level 15 (Lifespan Extension and Post-Biological Medicine):** Layer 3–4 aging biology (theoretical framework for the mechanisms of biological aging — telomere theory, senescence, mitochondrial degradation — sufficient to design systematic anti-aging interventions); Layer 3 consciousness theory at Layer 3 (required to govern consciousness continuity across biological substrate replacement); Layer 3 substrate-independence science (theoretical framework for cognition that operates across different biological substrates — required for the most advanced life extension technologies).

**Level 20 (Absolute Biological Mastery):** Part 28 authorization required; joint governance with Part 4 for questions of soul-biology interaction.

### 4.7 — Type 6: Engineering and Architecture Science

**Level 5 (Large-Scale Civil Engineering):** Layer 3 structural mechanics (theoretical framework for large-scale structure design under all relevant load cases); Layer 3 fluid dynamics (theoretical framework for fluid-structure interaction and hydraulic engineering); Layer 3 geotechnical science (theoretical framework for foundation design on varied geological substrates).

**Level 8 (Planetary-Scale Engineering):** Layer 3 planetary science (theoretical framework for planetary geology, atmosphere, hydrology, and climate as engineering environments); Layer 3 orbital mechanics (engineering in orbital environments); Layer 3 megastructure structural science (theoretical framework for structures at scales of thousands of kilometers — material science at this scale, structural wave propagation, and long-term gravitational influence on structure).

**Level 11 (Multi-Planetary and Space Industrial Engineering):** Layer 3–4 materials science (theoretical framework for materials that perform across the full range of space environments — vacuum, radiation, micrometeorite impact, extreme temperature cycles); Layer 3 self-assembling structure theory (theoretical framework for structures that assemble without human supervision in remote environments).

**Level 15 (Stellar-Scale Megastructure Engineering):** Layer 4 materials science (predictive-generative understanding of stellar-proximity materials); Layer 4 stellar engineering science (theoretical framework for the structural, thermal, and radiation challenges of building at stellar scale — the Dyson sphere framework requires this level).

**Level 20 (Universal-Scale Engineering):** Part 28 authorization required; requires Tier 16+ science foundational prerequisites.

### 4.8 — Type 7: Energy Science

Energy is the most scientifically demanding domain at the highest tiers. Every major tier transition in the Energy domain has a corresponding paradigm-shift scientific prerequisite.

**Level 5 (Fusion Power):** Layer 3 nuclear physics (theoretical framework for sustained thermonuclear fusion — plasma confinement theory, magnetic field geometry for confinement stability, radiation management, net energy extraction design); Layer 3 plasma physics (theoretical framework for high-energy plasma behavior at the temperatures and densities of fusion conditions).

**Level 8 (Exotic Energy Capture):** Layer 3 quantum field theory (theoretical framework for vacuum energy, Casimir effect, and early zero-point energy exploration — Layer 2 observations at Level 7 becoming theoretical understanding at Level 8); Layer 3 general relativity as engineering tool (theoretical framework for gravitational energy capture — black hole accretion, Penrose process type mechanisms).

**Level 11 (Stellar-Scale Energy Capture):** Layer 4 stellar physics (predictive-generative understanding of stellar energy output, magnetic field structure, and the engineering of structures in stellar proximity); Layer 4 megastructure structural science (the Dyson construction framework as engineering science).

**Level 15 (Dark Matter Energy Interaction):** Layer 3 dark matter theory (theoretical framework for what dark matter is and how it can be made to interact with energy technology systems — this is the first energy level at which dark matter becomes an engineering medium rather than merely an obstacle or gravitational contributor).

**Level 20 (Absolute Energy Mastery — Dark Energy and Vacuum):** Requires the dark energy theoretical framework at Layer 4; dimensional energy theory at Layer 3–4; Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.9 — Type 8: Military and Defense Science

Military science prerequisites are in most cases derivative of prerequisites in other domains — military technology requires the underlying science of whatever physical principles its weapons and defenses employ. The specific scientific prerequisites unique to military science are:

**Level 5 (Strategic Weapon Systems):** Layer 3 ballistics and guided weapons science (theoretical framework for precision guidance systems, terminal ballistics, and area-effect weapons design); Layer 2 electronic warfare science (early observations of electronic signal disruption).

**Level 8 (Energy Weapon Systems):** Layer 3 directed energy theory (theoretical framework for coherent energy beam propagation, atmospheric interaction, and targeting through turbulent media); Layer 3 materials science for energy absorption/reflection (theoretical framework for armor and shielding against directed energy weapons).

**Level 11 (AI-Coordinated Warfare Science):** Layer 3 AI science at Level 9+ (required for autonomous tactical systems); Layer 3 network warfare theory (theoretical framework for attacks on information infrastructure at civilization scale).

**Level 15 (Stellar-Scale Weapons Science):** Layer 4 stellar physics (required to design weapons that harness stellar energies or target stellar systems); Layer 3 planetary engineering science (required to design and defend against planetary-scale weapons).

**Level 20:** Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.10 — Type 9: Transportation and Travel Science

**Level 5 (Planetary-Scale Transport):** Layer 3 aerodynamics and atmospheric flight science (theoretical framework for powered flight in planetary atmospheres at high speeds); Layer 3 orbital mechanics at Layer 2 (early observational framework for orbital transfer — sufficient for early spacecraft, not yet sufficient for interplanetary operations).

**Level 8 (Interplanetary Transport):** Layer 3 orbital mechanics at Layer 3 (theoretical framework for multi-body orbital transfer optimization — Lagrange point operations, low-energy transfer trajectories, gravity assist maneuvers); Layer 3 life support science (theoretical framework for sustaining biological practitioners during multi-month transit without resupply); Layer 3 re-entry and landing science (theoretical framework for landing on diverse planetary bodies).

**Level 11 (Inner System Industrial Transport):** Layer 3–4 space propulsion science (theoretical framework for high-specific-impulse propulsion systems — nuclear thermal, nuclear electric, solar sail at scale, and ion drive systems capable of commercial-scale cargo transport).

**Level 15 (Near-FTL Transit Science):** Layer 3–4 relativistic physics (theoretical framework for near-light-speed transit and its consequences — time dilation, relativistic mass increase, communication latency at high fractions of c); Layer 2 FTL physics foundation (early observational and theoretical foundations that precede the full FTL paradigm-shift — see Area 5).

**Level 20 (Trans-Universal Transit):** Requires the full FTL physics framework at Layer 4; requires trans-universal physics at Layer 3+; Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.11 — Type 10: Space and Cosmological Science

**Level 5 (Inner System Astronomy):** Layer 3 planetary science (theoretical framework for the host planetary system's composition, history, and current properties); Layer 3 stellar physics at Layer 2 (systematic empirical understanding of the host star — sufficient for energy budget planning and prediction of solar weather events).

**Level 8 (Stellar Science):** Layer 3 stellar physics at Layer 3 (theoretical framework for stellar lifecycle, stellar types, and stellar structure — required for detailed stellar system resource assessment and megastructure planning); Layer 3 exoplanet science (theoretical framework for detecting and characterizing distant planetary systems — required for planning interstellar colonization targets).

**Level 11 (Galactic Science):** Layer 3 galactic astronomy (theoretical framework for galactic structure — disk, bulge, halo, dark matter distribution — as an engineering context); Layer 2 dark matter science (beginning to develop theoretical models for dark matter properties).

**Level 15 (Cosmological Science):** Layer 3 cosmological physics (theoretical framework for the large-scale structure of the universe — cosmic microwave background, baryon acoustic oscillations, large-scale structure formation); Layer 2 dark energy observational framework (systematic empirical characterization of dark energy's effects).

**Level 20 (Absolute Cosmological Science):** Requires trans-universal physics at Layer 4; Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.12 — Type 11: Environmental and Terraforming Science

**Level 5 (Planetary Systems Science):** Layer 3 atmospheric science (theoretical framework for planetary atmosphere chemistry, dynamics, and energy balance — required for managing human environments on a planetary scale); Layer 3 ecological science (theoretical framework for ecological systems as functional infrastructure — required for managing food production, water cycles, and biological resource systems); Layer 3 climate science (theoretical framework for planetary energy balance and the engineering of it).

**Level 8 (Terraforming Foundation Science):** Layer 3–4 planetary science (theoretical framework for planetary atmospheric modification — the science of what happens when you deliberately change a planet's atmospheric composition, pressure, and energy balance over centuries); Layer 3 bioengineering ecology (theoretical framework for introducing biological systems to new planetary environments and managing their spread and evolution deliberately).

**Level 11 (Multi-World Terraforming Science):** Layer 4 planetary science (predictive-generative understanding of planetary system dynamics at sufficient precision to plan terraforming of multiple worlds with known interaction effects); Layer 3 large-scale biological engineering (theoretical framework for directing ecosphere development across multiple worlds simultaneously).

**Level 15 (Star-System Scale Environmental Science):** Layer 4 stellar physics (understanding star-system evolution as an engineering problem); Layer 3 megastructure environmental science (theoretical framework for managing the thermal and radiative environment of a stellar system under active megastructure construction).

**Level 20:** Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.13 — Type 12: Cultivation Integration Technology Science

CI Technology (Type 12) is the only domain where scientific prerequisites are inherently joint between technology science (this Part) and cultivation science (Part 3). The scientific prerequisites for CI Technology advancement include both the technology-side physics of interfacing with qi phenomena AND the cultivation-science understanding of how qi phenomena operate.

**Level 5 (Basic Qi Interface Technology):** Layer 2 qi physics empirical observations (systematic empirical observation of qi manifestation, distribution, and behavior — sufficient to design instruments that reliably detect qi without theoretical framework for WHY qi behaves as it does); Layer 3 cultivation-adjacent materials science (theoretical framework for materials that interact with qi fields in predictable ways — based on empirical testing in cultivation environments); Layer 2 biometric qi detection (systematic characterization of practitioner qi signatures sufficient for reliable detection and authentication).

**Level 8 (Advanced CI Technology — CPM Interface):** Layer 3 qi physics framework (theoretical framework for qi as a physical phenomenon — the specific properties that determine how qi flows, concentrates, is stored, and is released; this is the first level where technology science makes genuine theoretical claims about cultivation phenomena, requiring joint governance with Part 4); Layer 3 CPM interaction science (theoretical framework for how CPM substance states interact with technology systems — based on systematic experimental data from controlled CPM-technology interface experiments); Layer 2 cultivation environment engineering (empirical observations sufficient to design reliable cultivation training environment enhancement systems).

**Level 11 (CI Technology — Soul Interface Level):** Layer 3 soul-interaction physics (theoretical framework for how technology interfaces with soul-level cultivation phenomena — this is the highest-sensitivity joint governance area between Part 10 and Part 4; any technology that claims to interact with soul phenomena must have scientific prerequisites that Part 4 has reviewed and approved); Layer 3 affinity-typed energy science (theoretical framework for the 40+ affinity types as technology-side energy signatures that CI Technology can interface with).

**Level 15 (Advanced Symbiotic CI Technology):** Layer 4 cultivation physics (predictive-generative understanding of qi, soul, and affinity phenomena at a level that enables deliberate engineering of cultivation-integration systems at Model D complexity); Layer 4 cross-system physics (theoretical framework for the cultivation-technology interface as a unified physical system rather than two separate systems interacting — the science required for the most advanced Integration Model D systems). This level requires Part 4 review and approval of all CI Technology Level 15 scientific prerequisites before those prerequisites are treated as canon.

**Level 20:** Part 4 and Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.14 — Type 13: Sensory and Detection Science

**Level 5 (Advanced Multi-Spectrum Detection):** Layer 3 electromagnetic spectrum science (theoretical framework for all bands of the electromagnetic spectrum and their interaction with matter — enabling the design of detectors across the full spectrum); Layer 3 signal processing science (theoretical framework for extracting information from weak or noisy signals).

**Level 8 (Quantum Sensing):** Layer 3–4 quantum mechanics (theoretical framework for quantum-limited sensing — using quantum systems to detect phenomena at or near the Heisenberg limit); Layer 3 gravitational wave science (theoretical framework for gravitational wave detection and interpretation — required for detecting gravity-wave-emitting events such as stellar mergers and black hole collisions).

**Level 11 (Dimensional Detection):** Layer 3 quantum field theory (detecting vacuum fluctuations and quantum field properties requires Layer 3 QFT); Layer 2 dark matter detection science (early observations of dark matter's detectable interactions with ordinary matter).

**Level 15 (Cosmological-Scale Sensing):** Layer 3 cosmological physics (detecting the large-scale structure of the universe requires the same theoretical framework as studying it); Layer 2 extra-dimensional detection empirics (early observational evidence for extra-dimensional phenomena, preceding the theoretical framework of dimensional science).

**Level 20:** Part 28 authorization required.

### 4.15 — Type 14: Materials Science and Nanotechnology Science

**Level 5 (Composite and Advanced Materials):** Layer 3 solid state physics (theoretical framework for material properties derived from atomic and molecular structure); Layer 3 surface science (theoretical framework for material interfaces — required for advanced coatings, catalysts, and nanoscale materials).

**Level 8 (Designer Nanomaterials):** Layer 3–4 quantum mechanics (nanomaterial properties are quantum-dominated — full Layer 3 quantum theory required); Layer 3 molecular engineering science (theoretical framework for deliberate molecular-scale material design).

**Level 11 (Exotic State Materials):** Layer 3 condensed matter physics (theoretical framework for exotic quantum states — superconductors, superfluids, topological materials — required for materials at this level); Layer 3 high-energy density materials science (theoretical framework for materials that function at extreme energy densities — plasma-adjacent, stellar-adjacent).

**Level 15 (Neutronium-Class and Stellar Materials):** Layer 4 stellar interior physics (theoretical framework for matter under stellar core conditions — neutronium, quark-gluon plasma, and degenerate matter science); Layer 4 materials at relativistic conditions (theoretical framework for material behavior under conditions where relativistic effects are engineering-significant).

**Level 20 (Absolute Materials Science — Reality-Level):** Requires spacetime engineering theory at Layer 3+; Part 28 authorization required.
