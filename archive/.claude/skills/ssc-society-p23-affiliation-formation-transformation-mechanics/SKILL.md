## Area 10 — Affiliation Formation and Transformation Mechanics

### 10.1 — Relationship Formation Events

New inter-faction relationships of any formal type require a formation event — a specific occasion at which the relationship is established, its terms are defined, and it becomes binding.

**Formation Event Types:**

| Formation Type | Description | Required Elements | Formality Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grand Summit | Both faction leaderships meet formally; terms negotiated in person; formal charter signed | Both senior leaders; formal charter; witnesses | Maximum |
| Emissary Treaty | Empowered representatives negotiate and sign on behalf of faction leadership | Empowered emissaries with explicit authorization; signed charter | High |
| Exchange Ceremony | Formal exchange of tokens, pledges, or hostages establishing relationship | Cultural exchange appropriate to faction types; no written charter required | Medium-High |
| Battlefield Compact | Formed under combat conditions; verbal or minimal written agreement; less formal but binding | Verbal declaration before witnesses; good faith assumption | Medium |
| Covert Formation | Hidden relationship established through covert communication; no formal documents | Pre-agreed signals; operational understanding; deniable | Low formal, High operational |
| Gradual Emergence | No single formation event; relationship develops over time through cumulative cooperative interactions until both parties implicitly treat it as established | Consistent cooperative behavior pattern | Informal |

**Rule 10.1.1 — Formation Events Are Narrative Anchors:**
The circumstances under which a relationship is formed define its character throughout its existence. A mutual defense treaty signed during a crisis moment — when both factions were on the verge of destruction — carries different emotional weight than one signed in a comfortable summit. Authors treat formation events as character-revealing and relationship-defining.

### 10.2 — Relationship Evolution Stages

Relationships between factions typically pass through recognizable evolution stages:

**Stage 1 — Emergence:** First awareness of the other faction; initial assessment; no formal relationship
**Stage 2 — Contact:** First direct interaction; tone-setting incident; initial posture established
**Stage 3 — Assessment:** Both factions study each other; intelligence gathering; FPT comparison; interest identification
**Stage 4 — Initial Formalization:** First formal position on the spectrum established; may be minimal (non-aggression pact) or substantial
**Stage 5 — Operational Reality:** The formal relationship is tested against actual events; both factions discover whether the other honors commitments
**Stage 6 — Deepening or Deterioration:** Based on Stage 5 experience, the relationship either deepens (toward higher cooperation) or deteriorates (toward higher hostility)
**Stage 7 — Stability or Crisis:** The relationship either reaches a stable equilibrium or enters a crisis that may transform it fundamentally
**Stage 8 — Transformation or Continuation:** Crisis resolved = fundamentally transformed relationship; crisis unresolved = continuation until next crisis or dissolution

### 10.3 — Relationship Dissolution Events

Formal affiliations dissolve through defined mechanisms:

| Dissolution Type | Trigger | Process | Aftermath |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mutual Termination | Both parties agree to end the relationship | Formal notification; negotiated termination; transition period | Clean break; possibility of future relationship |
| Unilateral Termination | One party invokes termination clause | Formal notice to other party; minimum notice period; obligations during notice period | Depends on terms and circumstances |
| Violation Termination | One party's breach of terms triggers the other's right to terminate | Formal violation declaration; response window; termination if not resolved | High-hostility transition; violation may justify conflict |
| Crisis Collapse | An external crisis or internal event destroys the relationship without formal process | Sudden; chaotic; each party's response to the crisis damages the other's trust | Often transition to open hostility |
| Absorption/Merger | One party absorbs or merges with the other; bilateral relationship ends because both parties become one | Formal agreement or forced absorption | End of separate relationship; new internal dynamics |
| Death Dissolution | One party faction is destroyed; relationship ends by extinction | None required | Surviving party's relationships with third parties are affected |
