## Area 1 — Foundational Principle and Consequence Architecture

### 1.1 — The Consequence System Overview

Consequences in the Starward Saga operate across five domains, each with its own propagation mechanics, timescales, and resolution pathways:

**Domain 1 — Physical Consequences:**
The direct, material outcomes of actions on bodies, locations, objects, and the physical environment. Physical consequences are the fastest-propagating and most immediately visible. They are governed by the physical laws of Parts 3, 8, 11, 12, 13, and 14.

**Domain 2 — Psychological Consequences:**
The internal impacts of actions on the mental and emotional architecture of the actor and those affected. Psychological consequences are governed jointly by this Part (as consequences) and Part 26 (as psychological states). Part 26 governs the internal experience of psychological consequences; this Part governs them as outputs of action in the consequence framework.

**Domain 3 — Relational Consequences:**
The impacts of actions on relationships — bonds, alliances, rivalries, enmities, and affiliations. Relational consequences propagate through the social network of everyone affected by the action, governed jointly with Parts 22, 23, and 25.

**Domain 4 — Systemic Consequences:**
The impacts of actions on organized systems — factions, civilizations, power hierarchies, information networks, and cultivation institutions. Systemic consequences are the most complex and the slowest to fully manifest, but they are often the most significant in long-term narrative impact.

**Domain 5 — Cultivation and Power Consequences:**
The specific consequences that actions have on cultivation capacity, Heart development, soul architecture, and power development. This includes both adverse consequences (cultivation regression, Heart damage, path limitation) and enabling consequences (breakthrough enabling, Heart awakening, cultivation acceleration from earned experience).

### 1.2 — The Consequence Severity Scale (CSS)

All consequences are classified on the Consequence Severity Scale from CSS-1 (trivial) to CSS-10 (civilizational/existential):

| CSS Grade | Label | Nature | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|---------|
| CSS-1 | Trivial | Minimal impact, resolved in hours to days | Minor social slight; small environmental damage |
| CSS-2 | Minor | Small but real impact; resolved in days to weeks | Failed promise; property damage; brief reputation harm |
| CSS-3 | Moderate | Meaningful impact requiring weeks to months to resolve | Broken minor alliance; significant property destruction; reputation damage requiring active repair |
| CSS-4 | Significant | Substantial impact requiring months to years to resolve | Loss of significant faction trust; serious injury to an important relationship; moderate environmental harm requiring recovery time |
| CSS-5 | Major | Major impact with multi-year resolution timeline; domain-crossing propagation | Betrayal of a primary faction alliance; significant collateral damage from a high-power event; reputation destruction within a community |
| CSS-6 | Severe | Severe impact with decade-scale resolution possibility; identity-domain effects | Major faction destruction or crippling; significant location damage; the end of a primary relationship through unrecoverable breach |
| CSS-7 | Critical | Critical structural damage to major social, physical, or power systems | Destruction of a significant faction; severe sacred ground damage; cultivator's cultivation path severely compromised |
| CSS-8 | Catastrophic | Catastrophic scale; world-level or civilization-level structural damage | Destruction of a world's civilization; permanent spiritual contamination of a sacred region; civilizational-scale political collapse |
| CSS-9 | Apocalyptic | Apocalyptic scale; multiple-world or civilization system destruction | Destruction of a civilization across multiple worlds; collapse of a galactic-scale power structure |
| CSS-10 | Existential | Existential scale; cosmological, reality-level, or multi-civilizational | Actions with consequences that reshape the fundamental conditions of existence for large regions of the cosmos |

### 1.3 — Consequence Immediacy Classes

Consequences are further classified by their propagation timeline:

| Class | Label | Manifestation Timeline |
|-------|-------|----------------------|
| IC-1 | Instant | Within seconds to minutes of the action |
| IC-2 | Short | Within hours to days |
| IC-3 | Medium | Within weeks to months |
| IC-4 | Long | Within years to decades |
| IC-5 | Generational | Across generations (decades to centuries) |
| IC-6 | Historical | Across epochs (centuries to millennia) |

**The Delayed Consequence Rule:**
Characters (and authors) cannot assume that the absence of IC-1 or IC-2 consequences means an action was consequence-free. Many of the most significant consequences are IC-3 through IC-5 — they are building in systems and relationships long before they surface as visible events.
