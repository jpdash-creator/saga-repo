## Area 6 — Engagement Recognition & Mutuality Law

### 6.1 — Definition of Engagement

An **engagement** is the canonical state in which two or more parties are actively committed to hostile action against each other. Engagement is distinct from:

- **Encounter:** Two parties are in proximity but have not taken hostile action
- **Surveillance / Pursuit:** One party is tracking another with no overt hostile action
- **Positioning / Preparation:** One or both parties are preparing for combat without initiating it
- **Standoff:** Both parties have the capability and intent to fight but neither has acted — this is pre-engagement, not engagement

Engagement begins at the moment the **engagement entry conditions** (Section 6.2) are met. Engagement ends at the moment the **termination conditions** (Area 12) are met. The period between these moments is the canonical combat state.

### 6.2 — Engagement Entry Conditions

A party enters engagement when **any one** of the following occurs:

**EC-1 — Overt Hostile Action:**
The party takes a physical, energetic, or soul-based action directed at causing harm to another party. This is the clearest entry condition and the most common.

**EC-2 — Formal Challenge Accepted:**
A formal challenge is issued and accepted — both parties are in engagement from the moment of acceptance, even before physical action begins.

**EC-3 — Aura Release as Declaration:**
A cultivator releases their full combat aura specifically directed at another cultivator in a manner recognizable as a combat declaration (not accidental aura expression). At Grade 6 and above, aura release of this type is universally recognized as engagement declaration. Below Grade 6, context determines whether aura release constitutes a declaration or a display.

**EC-4 — Response to Hostile Action:**
A party that is attacked under any Force Legitimacy category (including ambush) enters engagement the moment they consciously respond with hostile intent. A defensive action that produces counter-force constitutes engagement entry for the defender — their response is not passive resistance but active combat participation.

**EC-5 — Third-Party Activation:**
A party designated as an active combatant by alliance activation (Area 8.6) enters engagement at the moment of activation, even before physical contact with the opposing force.

### 6.3 — One-Sided Engagement

In an ambush or assassination attempt, the attacking party enters engagement from the moment of initiation (they meet EC-1). The target may not be in engagement until they become aware and respond. This creates a window — however brief — in which the engagement is technically one-sided.

**Consequences of One-Sided Engagement:**
- The ambushing party is fully subject to all combat rules (escalation tier, intensity, etc.) from the moment of initiation
- The target is not bound by combat rules until they enter engagement through EC-4
- A target who remains unaware and takes no action is not in engagement; damage to them is processed under assault/consequence rules rather than combat rules
- The moment the target responds, mutual engagement is established

### 6.4 — Mutuality Threshold

Engagement becomes **mutual** when both parties have met at least one engagement entry condition. Mutual engagement is the standard combat state governed by this Part in full.

**One-Sided Engagement Rules:**
When engagement is one-sided (ambush condition), the combat rules still apply to the attacking party. The defending party's rights — including the right to respond with full Defensive Response (FC-3) force — are active from the moment they become aware. There is no advantage to the ambushing party from the engagement recognition perspective; they simply initiated first.

### 6.5 — Disengagement vs. Termination

**Disengagement** is the act of withdrawing from active hostile contact without meeting termination conditions. A disengaged combatant is no longer in immediate combat but remains in the engagement's scope — they can be pursued, re-engaged, and are still subject to the conflict's tier classification. Disengagement rules are governed by Area 14.

**Termination** is the canonical end of the engagement under the conditions defined in Area 12. A terminated engagement cannot be "re-opened" without a new initiation event meeting the conditions of Area 4.3.

### 6.6 — Engagement in Multi-Combatant Scenarios

In fights involving more than two parties:

**Rule 6.6.1 — Individual Engagement Tracking:**
Each party's engagement status is tracked independently. Party A may be in engagement with Party B and C, while Party D is an observer who has not entered engagement. Party D's transition from observer to combatant occurs when they meet one of the entry conditions (EC-1 through EC-5).

**Rule 6.6.2 — Entry Announcement in Formal Conflicts:**
In E3 and above conflicts, a party entering the engagement must be identifiable — their entry cannot be ambiguous. Unknown combatants without clear faction alignment are treated as hostile to all parties until identification is established (Area 10.3).

**Rule 6.6.3 — Exit Tracking:**
A party exits engagement through disengagement (Area 14) or termination conditions (Area 12). Exit is tracked per party — the overall engagement continues as long as any two parties remain in mutual engagement.
