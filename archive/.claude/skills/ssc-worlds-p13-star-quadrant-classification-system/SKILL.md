## Area 18 — Star Quadrant Classification System (Cosmic Tier)

A Star Quadrant consists of multiple Star Clusters forming a navigable region of a Galaxy. Star Quadrants exist within Galaxies only — not within Galaxy Clusters or Galaxy Superclusters.

### 18.1 — Star Quadrant Internal Structure

A Star Quadrant contains: Quadrant Core Clusters (highest-class Star Clusters, typically the most developed civilizations and highest Sacred Ground density per unit volume), Quadrant Standard Clusters (the bulk of the Quadrant's population and civilization), Quadrant Outer Clusters (least developed, most frontier), and Inter-Cluster Void Spaces (largely empty space between Clusters within the Quadrant).

### 18.2 — Star Quadrant Class Table

| Class | Designation | Ambient Energy Profile | Affinity Profile | Special Zone Status | Dimensional Properties | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SQ-1 | Barren Quadrant | Class 1 dominant | Neutral | None at Quadrant scale | Standard | A Star Quadrant with no meaningful aggregate energy character. Navigation hazard rather than civilization hub. |
| SQ-2 | Sparse Quadrant | Class 1–2 | Neutral | None at Quadrant scale | Standard | Minimal Quadrant-level energy profile. Individual Clusters may vary but the Quadrant as a unit is unremarkable. |
| SQ-3 | Common Quadrant | Class 2 | Neutral or mild | None at Quadrant scale | Standard | The baseline inhabited Star Quadrant. Adequate conditions, no special character at Quadrant scale. |
| SQ-4 | Affinity Quadrant | Class 2–3 | Active affinity across significant portion | Cluster-distributed Formation Zones | Standard | A Star Quadrant with a dominant affinity character manifesting across multiple constituent Clusters. The Quadrant has a recognized energy identity. |
| SQ-5 | Rich Quadrant | Class 3–4 | Neutral or affinity | Formation Zones distributed across constituent Clusters at notable density | Standard | An energy-rich Star Quadrant. The density of Formation Zones across its constituent Clusters makes it noticeably favorable. |
| SQ-6 | Vortex Quadrant | Class 4–5 | Affinity aligned | Active Vortex Zones across multiple Star Clusters; Possible: Quadrant-scale energy phenomenon | Standard | A Star Quadrant with distributed high-energy Vortex Formation Zones across constituent Clusters. High ambient density at Quadrant-wide scale. |
| SQ-7 | Sacred Quadrant (Minor) | Class 6–7 in Sacred zones | Affinity aligned | Sacred Grounds distributed across multiple Star Clusters | Minor dimensional alteration in Sacred zones | A Star Quadrant where Sacred Grounds are present across multiple constituent Clusters. The distributed Sacred presence gives the Quadrant a sacred reputation at Galaxy scale. |
| SQ-8 | Sacred Quadrant (Major) | Class 7 in Sacred zones | Affinity aligned | High-grade Sacred Grounds (S5–S7) across multiple Clusters; Quadrant-scale energy convergence | Moderate dimensional alteration | A Star Quadrant of major sacred significance at Galaxy scale. High-grade Sacred presence distributed across constituent Clusters. Known across its Galaxy. |
| SQ-9 | Sacred Quadrant (Apex) | Class 7 (transcendent) | Affinity aligned | S7–S8 Sacred Grounds in multiple Star Clusters; Quadrant-scale sacred convergence | Significant dimensional overlap across the Quadrant | An apex-sacred Star Quadrant. The density of the highest-grade Sacred Grounds makes the entire Quadrant a sacred zone of galactic significance. |
| SQ-10 | Dimensional Quadrant | Class 4–7 | Variable | Dimensional feature of Quadrant-scale magnitude — inter-cluster or inter-quadrant Stable Rift network, Quadrant-scale Resonance Plane access, or confirmed dimensional layer overlap at Quadrant scale | Active Quadrant-scale dimensional structure | A Star Quadrant defined by a major dimensional feature operating at Quadrant scale. |
