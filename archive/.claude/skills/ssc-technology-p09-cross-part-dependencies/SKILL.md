Technology-Relic Hybrid Class 3 requires CI Level 8 — Part 9 governs how long it takes to reach Level 8 and what growth conditions accelerate or decelerate that timeline |
| Canon-Part 14 | Part 14 informs Part 9 — Active | Part 14 (Distance Law — fully authored, Areas 1–17) provides the spatial scale milestones that define what technology tier corresponds to what civilization footprint. Part 9's growth timeline milestones acquire concrete spatial meaning through Part 14's VNS (Void Notation Scale): Tier 4–5 = first orbital access (Passage scale, ~1.5AU); Tier 8–9 = first neighbor star system access (Drift, ~1–5 light-years); Tier 10–11 = cluster-scale expansion (Scatter, ~100–500 light-years); Tier 14–15 = galactic-scale operations (Immensity, ~100,000 light-years); Tier 18–20 = inter-galactic and cosmological scale (Remnant/Furthest). These VNS milestones are the distance-axis anchors for Part 9's advancement timeline table — growth events that require new spatial access thresholds reference Part 14 for the canonical distance values. FTL communication unlocking at Tier 8+ (Part 14 Area 5.2) is a critical growth-threshold event in Part 9 growth timelines — it removes information lag as a growth deceleration factor. Part 9 sensor and detection capability improvements (advancing through technology tiers) follow the Part 14 Area 5.1 canonical sensor range table |
| Canon-Part 21 | Part 9 ↔ Part 21 — Bidirectional Active | Technology Axis (TA) advancement mechanics (Part 9) are the primary driver of TA-axis changes in Part 21 (Civilization Law — fully authored, Areas 1–22). Technology regression (Part 9, Area 8) reduces the TA axis, which feeds back through the Part 21 CCT synthesis formula with axis gap penalty considerations. Part 21 Area 9 (Advancement Mechanics) governs how TA-axis changes affect the overall CCT / CTD; Part 21 Area 10 (Civilization Interaction Laws) governs technology transfer asymmetry by CCT gap; Part 9 provides the TA-axis movement mechanics; Part 21 provides the CCT synthesis that translates those movements into overall CTD advancement or regression |
| Canon-Part 23 | Part 9 informs Part 23 — Active | Competitive rivalry affiliations (AFF-8) between technology factions drive Part 9's factional competition acceleration mechanism (Part 9 Area 6.3 — 10–20% growth rate increase when multiple factions compete in the same domain); co-development alliances (Part 23 Area 4.6) are the primary Part 9 joint development governance events — shared research investment and co-development alliance structures are the institutional form through which Part 9's Cross-Civilizational Transfer mechanism operates at faction scale; crisis-driven innovation (Part 9 Area 6.4) is frequently triggered by AFF-12 open conflict affiliation events |
| Canon-Part 22 | Bidirectional — Active | Part 9 informs Part 22 (Faction Law): factional technology advancement races, technology transfer between factions (including governance of factional leading-edge advancement in Part 9 Area 9.3), and political governance of breakthroughs are all governed in Part 22's factional framework; Part 9 provides the growth mechanics that factions compete over; technology breakthrough events (Part 9 Area 6.1) are faction-level events with political consequences governed by Part 22 Alliance Law and Conflict Law. Part 22 informs Part 9: factional competition is a technology acceleration mechanism (Part 9 Area 6.3 — multiple factions competing in the same domain accelerate advancement by 10–20%); factional suppression of research factions (STF, ACR) is a deceleration mechanism (Part 9 Area 7.4); Science/Technology Factions (STF) and Academic/Research Factions (ACR) are the institutional actors executing Part 9's growth mechanics — their organizational stability, resource access, and political protection directly affect Part 9 growth timeline outcomes; faction-level technology gate networks (FPT-8+ factions, Part 22 Area 27.2) require Tier 10 achievement that is a Part 9 growth milestone |
| Canon-Part 25 | Part 25 ↔ Part 9 — Bidirectional Active | Cultural attitudes toward science, experimentation, and technology are the social conditions within which Part 9's growth mechanics either flourish or are suppressed — this is a substantive bidirectional governance relationship. Part 9's Institutional Prerequisites Category III (Area 4.1) are built on cultural foundations governed by Part 25: a civilization whose dominant CPT is CPT-1 Hierarchical-Sacral may actively resist scientific inquiry that challenges the cultivation hierarchy's explanatory monopoly; a CPT-3 Mercantile-Exchange civilization will fund research that produces commercial advantage; a CPT-2 Meritocratic-Martial civilization will prioritize military science (Type 8) over medical or environmental science. The four-phase technological culture disruption model in Part 25 (Area 13 — Novelty Shock, Cultural Negotiation, Cultural Normalization, Cultural Dependence) is the cultural reverse-side of Part 9's advancement timeline: when Part 9 governs when technology advances, Part 25 governs how the cultural environment processes and integrates that advance; a civilization that has undergone Cultural Normalization of a technology tier advances faster through Part 9's Institutional Prerequisites because resistance to the technology has dissolved. Technology revolution events (Part 9 breakthrough events — FTL travel invention, AI sentience first-confirmation, CPM-technology interface achievement) are not merely technical milestones — they are cultural revolution triggers that produce Part 25 cultural inflection points; the invention of FTL travel permanently reorders what a civilization considers possible, restructuring its cultural value hierarchy, its prestige economy (CPT-type pivot), and its social stratification (A-Class affiliation shift) in ways that feed back into Part 9's subsequent growth trajectory |
| Canon-Part 27 | Part 9 feeds Part 27 | Technology growth events with narrative consequence — especially accidental discovery events that harm bystanders, crisis-driven innovation that causes social disruption, regression events that leave populations without critical infrastructure — produce consequences governed by Part 27 (Consequence Law) |
| Canon-Part 30 | Part 30 informs Part 9 | Part 30 (Novel Macro Story Law) provides story rhythm guidance on when technology growth events are narratively appropriate to depict; it does not impose restrictions on what growth events are physically possible in the world; Part 9 governs the in-world physics of growth; Part 30 governs story rhythm guidance for when growth events serve the narrative |
| Canon-Part 31 | Bidirectional | Part 31 governs Part 9: cultivation-technology synergy conditions (Part 31, Areas 2–4) determine what cultivation-assisted discovery is available to technology research programs; the Three Pillars of Synergy determine how much cultivation can contribute to technology growth; the CPM Growth Cycle (Part 9, Area 10.3) is the growth-mechanics face of the synergy dynamics governed in Part 31. Part 9 governs Part 31 by context: technology tier advancement timeline (Part 9) determines when civilization-tier synergy access levels unlock (Part 31, Area 8 — Civilization Tier Synergy Access Table); growth acceleration from cultivation (Part 9, Area 6.6) requires the synergy infrastructure conditions defined in Part 31 |
| Canon-Part 15 | Part 15 feeds Part 9 — active | Part 15 (Travel Law — fully authored, Areas 1–20) defines travel capability milestones that are civilization-scale technology growth benchmarks in Part 9. The five Part 15 technology travel paradigm bands correspond to Part 9 growth threshold events: first powered flight (Surface paradigm, Tier 1–5) is an early growth milestone; orbital access (Orbital/Inner-System, Tier 5–7) marks the first space-capable tier transition; first sub-FTL drive (Interstellar paradigm, Tier 8) is a critical advancement threshold; star gate network construction (Tier 10) is a civilization-defining growth event; galactic gate grids (Galactic paradigm, Tier 11–14) are landmark civilization achievements. Part 9's growth timeline models must be consistent with Part 15's paradigm band thresholds — it is a contradiction for Part 9 to describe a Tier 10 civilization without star gate capability. Part 15 FTL unlock (Tier 8) removes information lag as a growth deceleration factor (already noted in Part 14 cross-reference); this is reinforced by Part 15's separate treatment of FTL communications vs. FTL travel (Part 15 Area 14.5) |
| Canon-Part 33 | Part 33 feeds Part 9 — Weapon stockpile as growth indicator | Part 33 (Technology — Weapon Law — fully authored, Areas 1–18) connects to Part 9 as the weapon inventory advancement indicator. Weapon stockpile composition within a civilization's arsenal is a Part 9 growth output: the presence of Tier 5+ weapons in a faction arsenal signals TB-Gamma civilization minimum; Tier 8 IW-Band weapons signal the IW-Band capability milestone (the same FTL milestone signaled by IS-Band vehicles in Part 32); WS-VI civilization-class weapons signal Tier 11+ civilization floor. Part 9 governs HOW a civilization advances its Type 8 Military domain; Part 33 governs WHAT weapons that advancement produces. Weapon production infrastructure regression (Part 9 Active Regression events) is narrated through Part 33's weapon taxonomy — a civilization that loses Tier 8 weapon production has regressed its Type 8 domain below Level 8. Weapon research prototypes (Part 33 Area 8.3, referencing Part 9 Area 4.1.1) operate under Part 9 prototype governance: weapons above the civilization's current Technology Ceiling cannot be reproduced at scale until ceiling advancement is achieved |
| Canon-Part 32 | Part 32 feeds Part 9 — Vehicle fleet as growth indicator | Part 32 (Technology — Vehicle Law — fully authored, Areas 1–17) connects to Part 9 in the specific domain of transportation fleet quality as a civilization advancement indicator. Vehicle tier distribution within a civilization's fleet is a Part 9 growth output: the composition of a civilization's vehicle fleet (what proportion of vehicles are ST-Band, OS-Band, IS-Band, GT-Band, or CT-Band) is a practical indicator of its Type 9 Transportation Technology advancement tier. Part 9 governs HOW a civilization advances its Type 9 domain; Part 32 governs WHAT vehicles that advancement produces. The appearance of IS-Band vehicles (Tier 8–10 Traversal Vessels and Jump-Drive vessels) in a civilization's fleet is the Part 32 observable that signals Part 9's FTL advancement milestone has been reached — the vehicle fleet is the downstream manifestation of Part 9 growth mechanics. CHV vehicle class availability follows the same relationship: the appearance of CHV-C vehicles in a civilization's production capacity is the observable that signals Part 9's CI Technology Level 5 threshold (required for Formation Drive Vessel construction) has been achieved. Fleet regression (loss of higher-tier vehicles through Part 9 Active Regression events) is narrated through Part 32's vehicle taxonomy — a civilization that loses IS-Band capability is a civilization whose Type 9 domain has regressed to sub-Tier-8 |
| Canon-Part 24 | Part 24 governs information security of technology growth events | Technology theft and industrial espionage (Part 24 Area 15 — AM-4 Technical Infiltration, AM-5 Institutional Compromise) are the canonical Part 24 information acquisition mechanisms that accelerate Part 9 Cross-Civilizational Transfer (Part 9 Area 2.4) through illicit means — a civilization that successfully acquires research data through espionage compresses its advancement timeline by the equivalent of the stolen research investment. Proprietary research findings and scientific discovery timelines are Tier II–III classified information objects (Part 24 Area 2 Vanguard Intelligence / Operational Cipher) in most technology factions; breakthrough research that establishes a new capability tier is Tier I Sovereign Secret Category C once achieved. Research secrecy (Part 24 Area 18 Doctrine SD-2 Strategic Secrecy or SD-5 Commercial Confidentiality) governs what research programs can be publicly announced — academic suppression (Part 24 Area 17.3 Propaganda) creates artificial knowledge access gaps that function as deceleration factors in Part 9 Area 7 terms. Institutional Amnesia laws (Part 24 Area 11 — IA-1 through IA-3) govern how technology knowledge degrades when research institutions collapse (Part 9 Area 8 regression) — the rate of knowledge loss in Part 9 regression events follows Part 24 IA-1 Amnesia Scales with Transmission Gap |
| Canon-Part 18 | Part 9 informs Part 18 | Villain technology advancement follows Part 9 growth trajectories; a villain's technology tier ceiling is governed by the civilization context they operate within; villain technology acceleration events (stolen research, crisis-driven military innovation, exploitation of conquered territories' research infrastructure) are Part 9 acceleration mechanism applications in the villain's context; villain technology regression as a consequence of their conflict arc (Part 27) is narrated through Part 9 regression mechanics; the Villain Ledger Section 3 tracks technology growth dimension in Part 9 terms |
| Canon-Part 19 | Part 9 informs Part 19 | Tier A side character technology advancement follows Part 9 growth trajectories; technology specialist mentors operate within their own technology tier ceiling for knowledge transmission; side character technology tier advancement events may be Side Character Profile update triggers if they represent significant capability shifts; ally contributions to a hero's technological development (access to research facilities, cross-civilizational technology transfer) are Part 9 growth mechanism applications within the Part 19 Ally function framework |
| Canon-Part 17 | Part 9 informs Part 17 | Hero technology advancement follows Part 9 growth trajectories and civilization tier constraints; significant technology upgrades achieved by the hero (accessing new generation weapons, achieving new prototype capability, surviving a technology crisis that forces regression) are Hero Ledger update triggers; a hero's personal technology growth arc (what they build, acquire, or master) is a Power Growth Dimension tracked in Hero Ledger Section 3 and is subject to the same civilization-tier ceilings Part 9 defines |
| Canon-Part 28 | Part 9 ↔ Part 28 | The four Technology Growth Mechanisms (Systematic Research, Experimental Accident, Cultivation-Assisted Discovery, Cross-Civilizational Transfer) and the Technology Regression Law are Part 28 Locked Canon — growth events must follow Part 9's governed pathways; specific institution names, technology organization names, research facility names, and named innovation events are Part 28 Negotiable Canon; Part 28's prohibition on causally unsupported technology jumps (Area 12, Prohibited Act 2) is the Part 28 enforcement mechanism for Part 9's growth pathway requirements — no technology advancement may appear without the Part 9 growth pathway being legible in the prose |
| Canon-Part 29 | Part 9 → Part 29 — Active | Dialogue about technology advancement, research progress, and breakthrough events reflects Part 9 growth mechanics — characters speak about discovery using the vocabulary of Part 9's four growth mechanisms (Systematic Research, Experimental Accident, Cultivation-Assisted Discovery, Cross-Civilizational Transfer); technology researchers and scientists speak about their work using vocabulary consistent with their knowledge tier; Part 29 Rule 29.12.2.2 (Technology Dialogue Is Precision-Oriented) applies to all technology-specialist dialogue — characters describe what they know, what they have measured, and what the data shows |
