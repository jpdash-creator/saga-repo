## Area 13 — Dialogue Narration Standards

### 13.1 — Twelve Narration Standards for Dialogue

**N29-1 — The Voice Standard:** Every named character must have a voice distinguishable from every other named character. If a line of dialogue can be given to a different character without noticing, the voice has failed. Voice is built from the intersection of cultivation tier, cultural origin, ideology, psychology, and social position — and must be consistently maintained across scenes while evolving across the arc.

**N29-2 — The Depth-Match Standard (DP-2 Dialogue Threading):** Dialogue must match the depth of the surrounding prose and must satisfy the **DP-2 Dialogue Threading** dimension of Part 28's Pacing-Appropriate Prose Depth (3500%–5000%; Part 2 Area 6.2.1) Mandate (Part 28 Area 4, Named Depth Pillar DP-2). A scene written at the Pacing-Appropriate Prose Depth (3500%–5000%; Part 2 Area 6.2.1) Mandate (Part 2) cannot contain dialogue that sounds like a functional information exchange. Every line of dialogue must simultaneously: (a) carry the scene's full emotional and thematic weight; (b) advance at least one of the twelve Content Registers; (c) thread through the POV character's internal architecture (Part 28 DP-5 Internal POV Architecture) so that the dialogue and the character's unspoken reaction constitute a single narrative unit; (d) register against the character's power system or cultivation state where applicable (Part 28 DP-6 Power System Precision); and (e) emerge from and enrich the cultural-social context of the speaking character (Part 28 DP-8 Cultural-Social Register). Dialogue that passes N29-2 in isolation but fails DP-2 in the context of the full Prose is a Rule 7.1.1(k) Audit Pass 1 FAIL.

**N29-3 — The Subtext Standard:** Every significant dialogue exchange must contain subtext. The reader must be able to detect what is being communicated beneath what is being said. Dialogue without subtext is surface without depth — it communicates the event but not the meaning.

**N29-4 — The Multi-Register Standard:** No scene's dialogue may be confined to a single content register. A scene of combat strategy may carry both Register 11 (Strategy) and Register 5 (Fear) and Register 8 (Revelation) simultaneously. A scene of grief may carry Register 1 (Emotion), Register 7 (Doubt), and Register 4 (Authority — the authority of one's own grief over another's need for comfort) simultaneously. Mono-register dialogue is a form of flatness.

**N29-5 — The Consequence Standard:** Every significant dialogue exchange must produce a consequence. After the conversation, something has changed — a relationship has shifted, an understanding has been altered, a decision has been made, a wound has been opened or touched or tentatively closed. A conversation that ends with all parties in the same state they entered is a conversation that did not happen.

**N29-6 — The Physical Embedding Standard:** Every spoken line is embedded in a physical and environmental reality. The character speaks from a body in a space with other bodies. Their breath, their posture, their hands, the way they hold the space around themselves — all of this is present in the prose that embeds the dialogue. Dialogue without physical embedding is dialogue that floats above the scene rather than living in it.

**N29-7 — The Character-Specific Register Standard:** Every character in a multi-party dialogue must occupy a register-position that is specific to who they are, where they are in their arc, and what their relationship to this specific conversation is. There must be no interchangeable characters in dialogue — each voice must be necessary, carrying something only that character can carry.

**N29-8 — The Power-Legibility Standard:** The power dynamics of every dialogue exchange must be legible to the reader through the dialogue itself. Who holds power, who is challenging it, who has surrendered it, and who is performing power they do not possess — all of this must be readable in how the characters speak.

**N29-9 — The Cultural Specificity Standard:** Every character's cultural origin (Part 25 CPT) must be legible in their speech patterns, their formality register defaults, their assumptions about what is politely said and what is impolitely said, and their response to cultural speech norms from characters whose origins differ from their own.

**N29-10 — The Advancement Standard:** Every dialogue exchange must advance — from the first line to the last, the conversation must move to a new position. Exchanges that circle without advancing, that restate without developing, that return to their starting point without having traversed genuine new ground, have failed the advancement requirement.

**N29-11 — The Silence-as-Speech Standard:** When a character is silent in a moment that calls for speech, that silence must be rendered with the same depth and specificity as the speech it replaces. Silence is a speech act. It carries meaning, it produces consequences, and it must be characterized.

**N29-12 — The Simultaneous Governance Standard:** All dialogue must simultaneously satisfy: Part 29 (this Part — voice, content, subtext, registers, cultural specificity, power legibility, advancement), Part 2 (formatting — italics, embedded paragraphs, no script format), Part 20 (ideology is legible in word choice and assumptions), Part 25 (cultural speech norms are specific to each character's CPT), Part 26 (PIS state alters speech pattern), Part 16 (social position shapes speech register), **and Part 28 Named Depth Pillar DP-2 (Dialogue Threading)** (dialogue is simultaneously threaded through internal POV architecture, power-system precision, cultural-social register, and contributes to Scene Density Law's 4-function minimum). All seven sets of requirements are simultaneously active in every spoken exchange. The dialogue is not valid if it satisfies some but not all.
