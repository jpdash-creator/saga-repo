## Area 4 — Relational Consequences

### 4.1 — Trust Architecture and Its Fragility

Every significant relationship in the Starward Saga has a Trust Architecture — the accumulated structure of commitments, demonstrated reliability, shared experience, and mutual investment that defines what the relationship is and what it can bear.

**Trust Accumulation:**
Trust accumulates through: kept promises (small and large), demonstrated willingness to bear cost for the relationship, consistency between stated values and actual behavior, vulnerability shared and honored, and presence in moments when presence was costly. Each of these accumulates Trust Capital.

**Trust Depletion:**
Trust is depleted through: broken promises (small depletions; large ones can be catastrophic), inconsistency between stated values and behavior, sacrifice of the relationship for self-interest when choice was available, absence in moments when presence was needed, and betrayal. The rate of depletion is not symmetric with accumulation: a significant betrayal can destroy what years of Trust Capital built. This asymmetry is canonical.

### 4.2 — Relationship Consequence Categories

| Action Type | Relationship Consequence | CSS Range |
|------------|--------------------------|-----------|
| Minor broken promise | Small trust depletion; recoverable | CSS-1 to CSS-2 |
| Significant broken promise | Moderate trust depletion; requires active repair | CSS-3 |
| Betrayal in a high-stakes moment | Major trust depletion; permanent architectural change in relationship; trust may be rebuilt but architecture is permanently different | CSS-4 to CSS-5 |
| Betrayal for personal gain | Catastrophic trust depletion; likely relationship-ending; surviving trust cannot be rebuilt without extraordinary demonstration | CSS-5 to CSS-6 |
| Fundamental betrayal (of sect, master, sworn bond) | Trust architecture destroyed; relationship ends or becomes defined by the betrayal | CSS-6 to CSS-7 |
| Active harm to the trusted person | All prior trust destroyed; relationship becomes one of the deepest possible enmity | CSS-7 to CSS-8 |

### 4.3 — Vendetta and Enmity Formation

When a character's action causes the death, humiliation, severe harm, or fundamental betrayal of someone connected to a third party, vendetta formation is a canonical consequence:

**Vendetta Formation Conditions:**
- The aggrieved party perceives the harm as unjust or disproportionate
- The aggrieved party has the capability (cultivation, resources, connections) to pursue consequence
- Cultural norms (Part 25) governing that faction or civilization include vindication obligation or honor codes that mandate response

**Vendetta Persistence:**
Once formed, vendetta is not easily dissolved. It persists across:
- Time (vendetta families maintain grudges across generations — IC-5 timescale)
- Distance (a vendetta target who flees still carries the vendetta)
- Power differential (a vendetta from a lower-power party against a higher-power character creates narrative stakes through the asymmetry — the lower-power party's determination vs. the higher-power character's capability)
- Faction changes (if the character leaves a faction, personal vendettas follow the character, not the faction)

### 4.4 — Alliance and Affiliation Consequence

Inter-faction relationships (governed by Parts 22 and 23) produce specific consequence structures:

**Alliance Breach:**
Breaking an active alliance agreement produces:
- *Immediate:* The breaching party is reclassified from ally to neutral or enemy depending on the severity of the breach
- *Relational cascade:* Other parties observing the breach update their trust assessment of the breacher — the breach demonstrates what the breacher will do when alliance becomes inconvenient
- *Systemic:* Alliance breach events are politically significant (Part 22 governs the faction mechanics; Part 27 governs the consequence cascade)
- *CSS-4 to CSS-7* depending on the scale of the alliance and the nature of the breach

**Broken Oath:**
A sworn oath — a publicly or privately witnessed binding commitment — carries heavier consequences than a broken promise because it involves social witnesses and often cultural or spiritual weight (Part 25):
- The social witnesses are now engaged: their own honor is implicated in whether the oath-breaker faces consequence
- Cultural and political institutions that maintained the oath's legitimacy are obligated to respond
