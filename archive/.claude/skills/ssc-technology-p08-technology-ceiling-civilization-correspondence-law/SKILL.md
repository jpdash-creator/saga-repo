## Area 4 — Technology Ceiling and Civilization Correspondence Law

### 4.1 — The Technology Ceiling

A civilization's Technology Ceiling is determined by its overall development tier (Area 5 — Civilization Classification System). The ceiling defines the maximum technology tier a civilization can sustainably construct, maintain, and operate given its current institutional, infrastructural, and knowledge infrastructure.

Technology Ceiling and cultivation tier operate on separate independent scales. They are not locked to each other, and neither determines the other. Area 4.2 provides a Soft Correspondence Tendency Table — a world-building reference for what tends to co-occur in typical civilizations, not a governance rule prescribing what must co-occur.

**Rule 4.1.1 — Cultivation Tier Does Not Set the Technology Ceiling:**
A civilization of entirely Mortal-tier cultivators may have a Tier 5, 6, or 7 Technology Ceiling if it has satisfied the civilizational prerequisites in Area 10. The Technology Ceiling is a function of civilizational infrastructure, scientific knowledge, and resources — not of cultivation realm achievement.

**Rule 4.1.2 — Technology Ceiling Does Not Set the Realm Ceiling:**
A civilization's technology tier does not restrict the realm its cultivators can reach. Cultivators in a Tier 2 technology civilization can be Divine realm practitioners — the technology tier does not constrain cultivation advancement. The realm ceiling is a physical cultivation law governed entirely by Part 3 (Cultivation Power Law).

**Rule 4.1.3 — The Soft Correspondence Is Tendency, Not Law:**
The Soft Correspondence Tendency Table represents what happens in civilizations where technology and cultivation have developed under broadly comparable circumstances and neither has been artificially accelerated or suppressed. Exceptional civilizations — those that have specifically invested in one track over the other, or that have had access to catastrophic technology transfer events, ancient ruins, or exceptional cultivation breakthroughs — may be far outside the tendency range. This is narratively valid and requires no special justification.

### 4.2 — Soft Correspondence Tendency Table

*Moved to Part 31 (Cultivation-Technology Synergy Law), Area 13 as of v2.1.*

The Soft Correspondence Tendency Table — which maps cultivation realms to typical associated technology tier ranges — is now governed exclusively by Part 31, Area 13. This Part retains Rules 4.1.1 through 4.1.3 (the governance principles) and defers to Part 31 for the table and its detailed interpretation.

**Summary rule (retained here for quick reference):** Technology tier and cultivation realm are independent. The tendency table is a world-building calibration reference — not a requirement, a ceiling, or a floor. See Part 31, Area 13.2 for the full table and Part 31, Area 13.1 for interpretation guidance.

### 4.3 — The Technology Ceiling and Legacy Technology

Technology built at a higher tier than the current civilization's ceiling is subject to P8-2.3 (Technology Ceiling Suppression). Key narrative applications:

**Ancient Civilization Ruins:** Technology from a prior era at a higher tier than the current civilization's ceiling functions below designed capacity. This creates narrative opportunities for discovering artifacts that are awe-inspiring but only partially functional.

**Sealed Technology Vaults:** Technology from a higher-tier civilization stored and preserved may become fully relevant when the receiving civilization's Technology Ceiling rises to match its tier. The vault represents potential waiting to be unlocked.

**Imported Technology:** Technology brought from a civilization operating at a higher tier functions normally in the hands of the importing civilization as long as the importing civilization can maintain it — but the importer cannot reproduce, repair at full specification, or understand the technology's principles without independent scientific development (Part 10).

### 4.4 — The 20-Tier Scale and Narrative Phase

Technology Ceiling is determined by civilization infrastructure (Area 5 and Area 10), not by any Part 30 narrative phase restriction. Part 30 (Novel Macro Story Law) provides story rhythm and escalation guidance. It does not set specific technology tier values, does not lock civilizations to specific tiers per narrative phase, and does not restrict authors from depicting any technology tier that the civilization has validly earned through its civilizational development. The Correspondence Table (Area 4.2) is Part 8's own framework — Part 30 is consulted for escalation pacing guidance only.
