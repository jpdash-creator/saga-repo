## Area 9 — Dialogue and Ideology Intersection

### 9.1 — Ideology in Speech

**Rule 29.9.1.1 — Ideology Is Visible in Word Choice:** A character governed by SDI (Strength-Dominant Ideology) uses different language to describe the same event than a character governed by JCI (Justice-Centered Ideology) or PCI (Protection-Centered Ideology). The specific vocabulary, the specific assumptions embedded in word choice, the specific things each character treats as obvious that another treats as worthy of question — all of this must reflect the character's Part 20 ideology. Ideology is not stated in dialogue. It is visible in the assumptions the dialogue makes.

**Rule 29.9.1.2 — Ideological Confrontation Produces the Most Significant Dialogues:** When two characters with genuinely different Core Ideologies (Part 20 Area 1.1) engage in direct conversation about the things their ideologies make them disagree about, the dialogue becomes the ideological confrontation of the story made literal. These dialogues must be rendered at their full weight — not rushed, not summarized, not resolved with one character simply winning the argument. Ideological arguments in the Starward Saga do not end with defeat. They end with the two positions remaining in tension, each having been genuinely expressed, each having genuinely challenged the other. The resolution of that tension is the work of the arc, not the dialogue.

**Rule 29.9.1.3 — IS State Alters Ideological Speech:** A character at IS-3 (Tested-Stable) speaks about their ideology differently than a character at IS-5 (Ideological Void). A character at IS-4 (Fragmented) may not be able to finish the sentences that begin from their original Core Ideology — the fracture is visible in what they cannot say. IS states must be perceptible in how a character speaks about the things their ideology used to govern with certainty.

### 9.2 — Ideology Change Events in Dialogue

**Rule 29.9.2.1 — IE-4+ Events May Occur During Dialogue:** Ideological Impact Events at IE-4 (Core Activation) and above may be triggered by what is said in a conversation. A revelation, a question, a truth spoken at the exact moment a character is vulnerable — these are valid triggers for IE events per Part 20 Area 4. When an IE-4+ event is triggered by dialogue, the moment must be rendered with the full weight that a Core-level ideology event requires. The character must visibly receive the impact. The conversation must be permanently altered. The scene must acknowledge what has happened.

**Rule 29.9.2.2 — Post-IE Dialogue Is Altered Dialogue:** A character who has just experienced an IE-4+ event speaks differently in the remainder of the scene — and in every subsequent scene until their ideological state stabilizes or resolves. The change must be legible. The reader must be able to trace what shifted in the character's language after the event.
