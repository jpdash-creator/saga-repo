## Area 7 — Consequence Propagation Mechanics

### 7.1 — The Propagation Rules

**Rule P27-1: Consequences Propagate Through Proximity and Dependency.**
Consequences propagate from the action point outward through every system that was proximate to the action or dependent on the actor. The strength of propagation diminishes with distance — in relationship distance, social distance, and physical distance — but it does not reach zero within meaningful narrative scope.

**Rule P27-2: Silence Is Not Consequence Absorption.**
A consequence that has not yet produced visible effects has not been absorbed — it is in propagation. Characters who believe they "got away with it" because no visible consequence has arrived are carrying unmanifested consequences. The distinction between "no consequence yet arrived" and "no consequence will arrive" is a canonical distinction that must be maintained in the narrative.

**Rule P27-3: Power Does Not Prevent Consequence — It Modifies Its Form.**
A character with sufficient power can prevent the physical manifestation of some consequences — they can kill potential pursuers, destroy evidence, outrun retribution. But power does not prevent the psychological consequence of their actions (Part 26), does not prevent the relational consequence (trust is depleted regardless of whether the aggrieved party can act on it), and does not prevent the systemic consequence (the world's political and cultural landscape has been permanently modified). Power modifies the form of consequence expression; it does not eliminate consequence.

**Rule P27-4: Collective Actions Produce Collective Consequences.**
When a group acts together — a faction in war, a sect executing a disciplinary action, a civilization implementing a policy — the consequences are distributed across the collective. No individual member escapes all consequences by arguing they were not personally the actor. Membership in the acting collective creates consequence-sharing. The individual share is modified by their specific role in the action, their degree of agency, and their expression of dissent or compliance.

### 7.2 — Consequence Amplification

Certain conditions amplify consequences beyond their baseline CSS:

**Power Amplification:**
The higher the actor's cultivation realm or technology tier, the greater the amplification of their actions' consequences. A Mortal Tier 2 cultivator who attacks a village causes CSS-3 to CSS-4 consequences. A Core Formation cultivator who attacks the same village causes CSS-6 to CSS-7 consequences. The same action at greater power produces proportionally greater consequences. This amplification applies in both directions: greater power means greater consequences of harm and greater consequences of beneficence.

**Role Amplification:**
Characters who hold positions of authority and trust — elders, leaders, protectors — have amplified consequences for betrayals of that authority. A master who betrays a disciple produces greater consequences than a peer who betrays a peer. The trust invested in authority, and the vulnerability that trust creates, amplifies the consequence of its violation.

**Deliberateness Amplification:**
Deliberate actions produce greater consequences than accidental ones of equivalent physical magnitude — not in the physical domain (the physical damage is the same) but in the relational and systemic domains. A deliberate betrayal is worse than an inadvertent one. Deliberateness is a CSS amplifier in all non-physical consequence domains.

### 7.3 — CSS Scaling by Cultivation Tier Differential

The CSS weight of an action is not fixed — it scales based on the cultivation tier differential between actor and target. An action taken by a vastly more powerful cultivator against a weaker target amplifies consequence weight beyond the base CSS because the power differential changes the moral, relational, and systemic meaning of the action.

**The Tier Differential Multiplier Table:**

| Actor-Target Tier Gap | CSS Amplifier | Narrative Rationale |
|----------------------|---------------|---------------------|
| Same tier (±1 sub-realm) | ×1.0 (baseline) | Peers engaging as peers; standard consequence weight |
| 1–2 sub-realms above target | ×1.25 | Meaningful advantage; moderately amplified |
| 1 Major Realm above target | ×1.5 | Significant power advantage; "bullying" amplification |
| 2 Major Realms above target | ×2.0 | Structural domination; CSS weight doubles |
| 3+ Major Realms above target | ×2.5 to ×3.0 | Absolute domination; approaching Part 3 Categorical Impossibility zone |
| God-Tier acting on Mortal | ×3.0 minimum | Cosmological-scale moral weight amplification |

**Rule P27-CSS-T1 — Tier Differential Amplification Applies in All Consequence Domains:** The amplifier applies to physical, psychological, relational, and systemic consequences simultaneously. A Ruler-tier cultivator assassinating a Mortal-tier target does not carry a CSS-5 consequence that would apply to a peer assassination — the consequence is CSS-5 × 2.0 = CSS-10 for the tier differential alone. This is the governance mechanism that makes "bullying upward" (stronger attacking weaker) one of the heaviest consequence generators in the Canon.

**Rule P27-CSS-T2 — Tier Differential Amplification Is Reversed for Enabling Actions:** When a higher-tier character takes consequentially significant *positive* action toward a lower-tier target (defending, sacrificing for, sustaining at great cost), the tier differential amplifier applies to *positive* CSS events as well. A god protecting a mortal at great personal cost generates maximum positive consequence weight. Both positive and negative CSS events scale with tier differential.

**Rule P27-CSS-T3 — Inverse Amplification for Combat Upward:** When a lower-tier character genuinely threatens or defeats a higher-tier character, the consequence is amplified in the *lower*-tier character's positive CSS profile and in the *higher*-tier character's negative CSS profile. A Mortal defeating a Ruler creates a seismic consequence event for both: massive positive reputation/consequence event for the Mortal (Part 6 combat validation, Part 22 faction response, Part 20 ideology shift in observers), and massive negative consequence for the Ruler whose power credibility has been shattered.

### 7.4 — Positive CSS Saturation Mechanics

The Part 27 system primarily governs harmful consequence propagation. However, the Consequence Severity Scale (CSS) also applies to positive consequence events — acts of great sacrifice, protection, healing, or justice that generate positive consequence weight in the actor's profile. This Area governs the limits and mechanics of positive CSS accumulation.

**Rule P27-PCS-1 — Positive CSS Events Are Real:** Just as harmful actions generate CSS consequence debt, genuinely consequential positive actions generate positive CSS weight — reputation, relational trust, factional standing, civilizational recognition, and narrative moral authority. The same CSS-1 through CSS-10 scale applies: a CSS-1 positive act is minor helpfulness; a CSS-10 positive act is civilization-altering sacrifice.

**Rule P27-PCS-2 — Positive CSS Saturation Threshold:** Positive CSS events cannot cancel ongoing negative CSS consequence debt in a simple subtraction model. A CSS-7 sacrifice does not eliminate an outstanding CSS-7 consequence debt — they exist on separate ledger lines. The positive event shifts the character's *standing* in the world's moral economy but does not satisfy the specific relational, physical, or systemic debts generated by negative events. Positive consequences and negative consequences are processed through separate pathways.

**Rule P27-PCS-3 — The Saturation Ceiling:** A character can accumulate significant positive CSS weight, but beyond a threshold it saturates — further positive acts generate diminishing additional positive weight because the moral authority they produce is already fully extended. A character with CSS-10 positive standing is at the saturation ceiling; additional positive acts maintain that standing but do not amplify it further. The saturation ceiling is set at the maximum achievable positive moral authority for a character of their tier and role within their civilizational context.

**Rule P27-PCS-4 — Positive CSS Weight Can Be Spent:** Positive CSS weight can be spent — consumed in the act of obtaining something of equivalent weight. A character with enormous positive CSS standing who uses that standing to demand a favor of equal weight (a pardon, a military alliance, access to a restricted location) depletes their positive standing proportionally. Spending positive CSS is a narrative event with clear governance: the expenditure must be proportional, the spending must be acknowledged by the character who "receives" the positive standing transfer, and both ledgers must be updated.

**Rule P27-PCS-5 — Positive CSS Cannot Prevent All Negative Consequences:** No amount of accumulated positive CSS weight exempts a character from negative consequences for a negative act. A character who has done enormous good and then commits an atrocity still carries the full CSS consequence of the atrocity. The positive standing may modify how quickly, severely, or permanently the negative consequence manifests — long-standing positive actors may receive more benefit of the doubt, slower retribution, and greater social forgiveness — but positive CSS is not a negative consequence immunity shield.

### 7.5 — Cross-Generational Consequence Debt

Some consequences do not fully resolve within a single character's lifetime or arc. The Starward Saga Canon governs these cross-generational consequence patterns — where the actions of one generation create the consequence landscape that the next generation inherits and must navigate.

**Rule P27-CGD-1 — Consequence Debt Can Pass to Successors:** When a character who has accumulated significant CSS consequence debt dies, is defeated, or exits the story without fully meeting their consequences, that debt does not simply disappear. It passes — in whole or in part — to the structures that inherited the actor's position: their disciples, their faction, their bloodline, their ideology. The mechanism of transfer depends on what the consequence was:
- **Relational debt:** Passes to those who claimed the actor's legacy or relationships
- **Systemic debt:** Passes to the political/factional structure the actor controlled
- **Civilizational debt:** Passes to the civilization itself, expressed as historical injustice requiring reckoning

**Rule P27-CGD-2 — Cross-Generational Debt Creates Narrative Inheritance:** Characters who inherit a cross-generational consequence debt did not create the original harm. Their consequence position is: they have inherited a moral debt that makes them the target of justified grievance without being personally culpable for the original act. This is one of the most complex and nuanced consequence states in the Canon — governing it requires simultaneous acknowledgment of the inherited character's lack of personal guilt and the legitimacy of the grievance directed toward them.

**Rule P27-CGD-3 — The Second Generation Cannot Simply Deny Inherited Debt:** A character who inherits cross-generational consequence debt and responds by denying the original harm or refusing to acknowledge the debt accelerates their negative CSS accumulation — the denial is itself a new harmful act in the relational and systemic domains. The denial compounds the original debt with a new CSS event (CSS-4 to CSS-6 depending on scale).

**Rule P27-CGD-4 — Reckoning Pathways for Inherited Debt:** Characters who carry inherited consequence debt have governed pathways for reckoning with it:
- **Acknowledgment:** Formal recognition of the original harm without personal guilt — CSS-positive event for the inheriting character
- **Restitution:** Taking action to repair the inherited harm where possible — CSS-positive event proportional to the scale and sincerity of the repair
- **Reform:** Restructuring the systems or relationships that perpetuated the harm — CSS-positive event; the most significant form of cross-generational reckoning
- **Sacrifice:** The inheriting character accepts personal cost in service of addressing the inherited harm — may dramatically shift the cross-generational consequence arc

**Rule P27-CGD-5 — Cross-Generational Debt Is a World-Building Asset:** The civilizations, factions, and cultural institutions of the Starward Saga carry real historical consequence debts. These are not abstract or merely political — they are governed by Part 27 as ongoing CSS events in the "Systemic/Civilizational" consequence domain. When the story visits an institution, location, or faction that carries historical consequence debt, the narrative must not ignore it. The debt's presence is part of the world's texture.
