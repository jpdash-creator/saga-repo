## Area 19 — Travel Narration Standards

### 19.1 — Core Narration Rules

**N15-1 — Travel Method Must Match Character's Existence Tier.**
Authors may not grant a Mortal Tier 2 cultivator spatial fold travel or a Tier 5 civilization FTL gate transit. The travel method must be consistent with the character's cultivation realm and the civilization's technology tier.

**N15-2 — Travel Time Must Be Internally Consistent.**
A journey that takes two days in chapter 1 cannot take two hours in chapter 3 unless the character's realm or the civilization's infrastructure has meaningfully changed between those chapters.

**N15-3 — Travel Cost Is Not Freely Ignored.**
A character who flies at top speed across a star system does not immediately enter combat at full cultivation capacity. Travel exhaustion is real. Authors acknowledge the qi, energy, or time cost in prose — even if only with a sentence noting the character's depleted state or recovery pause.

**N15-4 — Distance Scale Is Stated or Implied by Context.**
All travel scenes provide sufficient context for the reader to understand the scale. "He flew across the city" sets a different scale than "He folded across the star cluster." Authors establish the CMS scale of the journey in context, even without stating a specific number.

**N15-5 — Infrastructure Is Presented as World-Building.**
When characters use star gates, FTL lanes, or formation array portals, the author presents the infrastructure as world-building — acknowledging it exists, has a cost (even if paid by someone else), and has a political/economic context. Travel infrastructure is not invisible.

**N15-6 — Spatial Fold Narration (Cultivation).**
When a Heaven Treading or higher cultivator performs a spatial fold, the prose must convey: (a) the preparation time (even if brief), (b) the subjective experience of transit (not just "they appeared"), and (c) the spatial disturbance effect on the environment if there are witnesses. Spatial folds are extraordinary — they should feel extraordinary in the prose.

**N15-7 — FTL Travel Has a Phenomenology.**
FTL transit in a vessel is not invisible to passengers. Different FTL architectures produce different sensory experiences: warp travel may produce light-distortion outside viewports; hyperspace may produce a distinctive visual signature; jump drives may produce instantaneous disorientation. Authors give FTL travel a felt experience, not just a navigation log entry.

**N15-8 — Travel as Character Revelation.**
How a character travels — and what it costs them — reveals character. A Xiantian cultivator who pushes past their qi limit to arrive in time reveals priorities. A Tier 9 civilization that can afford to maintain gate infrastructure but chooses to operate point-to-point FTL for secrecy reveals strategic calculus. Authors use travel as a lens on character and civilization as much as a logistics problem.
