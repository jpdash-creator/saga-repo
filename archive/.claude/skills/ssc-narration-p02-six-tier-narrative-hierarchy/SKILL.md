## Rule Text (Verbatim Source — Authoritative)
### Overview

Every novel in the Starward Saga is organized through six structural tiers, arranged from broadest to most granular:

```
PHASE
  └── SECTION
        └── PART
              └── CHAPTER
                    └── AREA
                          └── PROSE
```

Each tier has a distinct narrative function. No tier may perform the function of another tier. Skipping tiers is not permitted. The entire novel exists within this hierarchy and only within this hierarchy.

---

### Tier 1 — Phase

#### 1.1 — Definition

A Phase is the broadest structural unit of a Starward Saga novel or novel series. It represents a **major story epoch** — a sustained period of the narrative in which the fundamental parameters of the world are fixed at a specific state. These parameters include:

- **Power Ceiling:** The maximum cultivation tier achievable or encountered within the Phase. No character may exceed the Phase's power ceiling within the Phase's narrative. A higher power ceiling belongs to the next Phase.
- **Technology Ceiling:** The maximum technological scale and capability present within the Phase. Technology that exists beyond the Phase's technology ceiling is unknown, inaccessible, or not yet discovered.
- **Political Scale:** The scope of political entities, conflicts, and power structures that are operative within the Phase. A Phase may be city-scale, regional-scale, continental-scale, or world-scale. The political scale defines the stakes and the reach of narrative events.

#### 1.2 — Phase Function

A Phase is not a chapter. It is not a story arc. It is an epoch — a geological layer of the narrative. Its function is to:

- Establish a coherent world-state against which the story's events take place.
- Define the hard limits of what is possible within its span.
- Create the context within which all lower structural tiers operate.

A Phase answers the implicit question every reader carries: "What kind of world is this, and how powerful are the forces at play?" The Phase answer must be consistent from its opening to its close.

#### 1.3 — Phase Immutability

Phases are **never renamed, merged, or retroactively altered once created.** This rule exists because:

- The Phase defines the canon-valid state of the world for all narrative output within its span.
- Retroactively altering a Phase would potentially invalidate all previously validated narrative output within it.
- Phase-level changes require a canonical in-world event that opens the next Phase — they are not editorial revisions.

If the story requires a change to the world's power ceiling, technology ceiling, or political scale, that change is not a Phase revision. It is the opening event of the next Phase.

#### 1.4 — Phase Transitions

A Phase ends when a world-state change of sufficient magnitude has permanently altered at least two of the three Phase parameters (power ceiling, technology ceiling, political scale). The transition event is itself narrative — it is the climax or turning point that concludes one Phase and opens the next. Phase transitions are among the most significant moments in the entire series. They must be earned through narrative events, not declared arbitrarily.

#### 1.5 — Phase Scope

A Phase may span a single novel or multiple novels. A single novel may contain multiple Phases, though this is rare and indicates a story of extraordinary scope and compressed transformation. A Phase with a single Section is valid. A Phase with many Sections is equally valid. There are no minimum or maximum Section counts per Phase.

---

### Tier 2 — Section

#### 2.1 — Definition

A Section is the second structural tier and represents a **location-anchored major arc** within a Phase. A Section is defined by its **dominant location** — the primary geographical, political, or cosmological setting in which the arc's events unfold. The location gives the Section its identity, its atmosphere, and its physical context.

Sections are **location-anchored** in the sense that the dominant location is constant enough to define the Section's character — but the story is not required to remain in a single room or even a single city for the entire Section. Movement within a location's sphere of influence (a city and its surrounding territory, a realm and its adjacent zones, a palace complex and its grounds) is valid within a single Section. What ends a Section is not minor movement — it is a fundamental relocation of the narrative's center of gravity to a new and different place.

#### 2.2 — Section Function

A Section's narrative function is to develop a **major arc within the Phase** — a sustained story development that has its own internal logic, conflict, escalation, and resolution (or deliberate irresolution that feeds the next Section). A Section may encompass:

- The development of a major relationship or enmity
- The investigation or revelation of a major secret
- A campaign, siege, or extended conflict
- A period of training, growth, or transformation
- A political struggle, negotiation, or collapse
- An exploration or discovery journey

A Section is large enough to contain significant narrative weight but is bounded by its location anchor. When the story must move to a fundamentally new setting to continue its development, a new Section begins.

#### 2.3 — Inter-Section Relationships

A Phase must feel whole. This wholeness is produced by ensuring **novelic relationships between Sections within the same Phase**. This means:

- No Section exists in narrative isolation. Each Section must carry consequences from the previous Section and plant seeds that the next Section will develop.
- The Sections of a Phase collectively tell a story that is larger than any individual Section's arc.
- The emotional and thematic journey of the Phase is the sum of its Sections in deliberate sequence.

#### 2.4 — Section Transitions

A Section does not begin as a narrative unit announcing itself. It begins **thematically** — as the story demands, following the thematic journey of the previous Section's close. The reader should feel the shift in location and arc as a natural story development, not as a chapter announcement or scene heading. The transition is produced through prose, through character momentum, through the pull of the new setting and its demands. The structural label "Section" never appears in the text.

---

### Tier 3 — Part (Novel-Part)

#### 3.1 — Definition

A Novel-Part is the third structural tier and represents a **thematic progression** within a Section. Where a Section is location-anchored, a Novel-Part is thematically anchored. It develops a specific thematic thread, emotional phase, or narrative movement within the broader arc of its Section.

#### 3.2 — Novel-Part Function

A Novel-Part exists to:

- Preserve **immersion** by organizing the narrative into thematically coherent blocks that do not overload the reader with tonal or contextual whiplash.
- Govern **pacing** by defining natural points of thematic completion or escalation that allow the story to breathe without losing momentum.
- Carry the reader through a specific emotional or intellectual journey within the Section, completing it sufficiently that the next Novel-Part begins from a new and evolved thematic position.

There are no artificial limits on the number of Novel-Parts within a Section. A Section requires as many Novel-Parts as its story demands — no more, no fewer.

#### 3.3 — Inter-Part Relationships

A Section must feel whole. Wholeness requires **novelic relationships between Novel-Parts within the same Section**:

- Each Novel-Part must emerge from the thematic state left by the previous Novel-Part's close.
- The transition between Novel-Parts must feel organic, not editorial. The new Novel-Part begins because the story's thematic journey has entered a new phase, not because a structural container has been filled.
- The Novel-Parts of a Section collectively develop the Section's arc in a way that feels like a single sustained experience of escalating depth.

#### 3.4 — Novel-Part Transitions

Novel-Part transitions follow the same principle as all structural transitions in this hierarchy: they begin **thematically as the story demands** after the previous Novel-Part ended in its thematic journey. No Novel-Part begins with a narrative clean-slate. It always inherits the emotional residue, the unresolved tensions, and the character states that the previous Novel-Part produced.

---

### Tier 4 — Chapter

#### 4.1 — Definition

A Chapter is the fourth structural tier and represents a **continuous narrative unit** — the largest structural unit in which the narrative is experienced as a single, unbroken reading experience. A Chapter has a beginning condition, a developing middle, and a closing condition. It is not closed until **narrative closure is achieved**.

#### 4.2 — Narrative Closure

Narrative closure does not mean resolution or happiness. It means the Chapter has reached a state in which the reader experiences a meaningful pause — a moment of consequence, revelation, transformation, transition, or resting tension that completes the immediate narrative thread. A Chapter may close on:

- A victory and its cost
- A defeat and its emotional aftermath
- A revelation that recontextualizes everything preceding
- A departure or arrival
- A moment of quiet after sustained intensity
- A cliffhanger whose immediate emotional charge has peaked

A Chapter does not close mid-tension, mid-action, mid-conversation, or mid-realization. It closes when the reader has been delivered to a complete experiential moment, even if the broader story is unresolved.

#### 4.3 — Chapter Length

There are no artificial limits on Chapter length. A Chapter is as long as its narrative unit requires. A Chapter that ends early because of a perceived length limit has violated narrative closure. A Chapter that continues past its natural closure point because of a perceived length requirement has violated pacing. Length is always a function of story.

#### 4.4 — Inter-Chapter Relationships

A Novel-Part must feel whole. Wholeness requires **thematic relationships between Chapters within the same Novel-Part**:

- Each Chapter must continue the thematic development established by the previous Chapter.
- The transition between Chapters must carry thematic momentum — the new Chapter begins from the emotional state in which the previous Chapter left the story.
- Chapters within a Novel-Part collectively develop its thematic arc in a sequence of escalating or deepening narrative units.

#### 4.5 — Chapter Transitions

Every Chapter begins **thematically as the story demands** after the previous Chapter ended in its thematic journey. No Chapter begins as if the previous Chapter did not occur. The opening prose of every Chapter must be oriented in the emotional, psychological, and situational reality established by the previous Chapter's close.

---

### Tier 5 — Area

#### 5.1 — Definition

An Area is the fifth structural tier and represents a **Plot and Context Unit** within a Chapter. An Area defines the boundary of a discrete narrative event: what happens, why it matters in the context of the story, and who is involved. An Area is the organizational unit that ensures a Chapter's plot is fully and correctly executed — every development the Chapter requires has a dedicated Area in which it occurs.

#### 5.2 — Area Function

An Area provides the **contextual container** within which one or more Prose units are written. Its function is to:

- Define with precision what narrative event is occurring within its span.
- Establish who is present, where the action is located, and what the stakes of the event are.
- Ensure that the Chapter's total plot requirements are accounted for across its complete set of Areas.
- Provide the author (and the canon audit system) with a clear unit for tracking narrative completeness.

An Area is not a summary of what happens. It is the context that makes the subsequent Prose possible and necessary. The Prose is where the story lives — the Area is the frame that makes that life coherent.

#### 5.3 — Area Sequencing

Areas within a Chapter must **flow sequentially**. This means:

- Area 2 of a Chapter begins from the narrative position where Area 1 ended.
- No Area may skip over narrative events required for continuity. If Event B follows Event A, there must be an Area that contains Event A and a subsequent Area (or the same Area) that contains Event B, with no narrative gap between them.
- There is no return to a previous Area once the sequence has advanced. The narrative moves forward.

#### 5.4 — Area Count

There can be as many Areas in a Chapter as required to ensure the Chapter's plot is completely and correctly executed. A single-Area Chapter is valid if the Chapter's narrative event is unified. A many-Area Chapter is equally valid if the Chapter requires multiple distinct plot developments. There is no artificial limit.

#### 5.5 — Area-to-Prose Relationship

An Area provides the context within which multiple Prose units can be written. The Area defines what is happening. The Prose realizes it at full literary depth. An Area without at least one Prose is incomplete. A Prose without an Area context is architecturally unmoored.

#### 5.6 — Inter-Area Continuity

Each Area must begin where the last ended to make the Chapter feel whole and complete. There must be no narrative disjoint between consecutive Areas — no unexplained gaps in time, location, or character state. If a transition in time, location, or character state occurs between Areas, that transition must be produced by the final Prose of the preceding Area or the opening Prose of the new Area.

---

### Tier 6 — Prose

#### 6.1 — Definition

Prose is the sixth and final structural tier. It is the **single scene** — the fundamental unit of the novel as literature. All structure above Prose exists to place the reader in the best possible position to receive each Prose. The Prose is where the novel is made. It is not a summary, a sketch, a placeholder, or a draft. Every Prose unit is final-grade literature executed at the highest achievable standard.

#### 6.2 — Prose Depth and Pacing Mandate (Canon v4.27)

Every Prose must be written at **pacing-appropriate prose depth (Part 28 Prose Depth and Pacing Mandate — Named Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10 simultaneously mandatory at all pacing levels; word count governed by pacing classification per Area 6.2.1; immersion range 3500%–5000% pacing-dependent — Part 28 Area 4).** This is not a metaphor for "write well." It is a specific operational standard defined by the following requirements, every one of which is mandatory in every Prose regardless of pacing category. Prose immersion is achieved only when every unit of canon information within the Prose satisfies the **Contextual Earning Law (Part 28 Area 15)**. Sensory precision, tonal fidelity, and depth of rendering are necessary but not sufficient conditions for immersion. Information that is accurate but unearned breaks immersion as surely as information that is tonally wrong.

**Named Depth Pillars at all pacing levels:** The ten Named Depth Pillars (DP-1 through DP-10, Part 28 Area 4) remain simultaneously mandatory regardless of pacing category. At fast pace the depth is expressed in concise, concentrated form — the mandate does not change, only the canvas. Compression in fast-paced prose means concision and efficiency; it never means omission of any of the ten pillars.

#### 6.2.1 — Pacing Classification Law

Every Prose unit is classified into one of three pacing categories before drafting begins. The pacing category governs the word count target range and the immersion depth range for that Prose unit. The AI author declares the pacing category in the Gate 1 Position Header before the prose body.

**The Three Pacing Categories (Canon v4.34 — Pacing Law Amendment):**

| Pacing Category | Word Count Range | Max Ceiling | Immersion % Range | Narrative Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fast** | 1400–1800 words | 2000 words | 3500%–4000% | High-momentum, action-driven, rapid exchange; information delivered at speed; depth expressed through concision |
| **Medium** | 1900–2300 words | 2500 words | 4000%–4500% | Balanced scene; dialogue-rich, mixed interiority and event; standard narrative pace |
| **Slow** | 2400–2700 words | 3000 words | 4500%–5000% | Deliberate, immersive; world-building, character establishment, psychological depth, cultivation milestones |

**Flexible Ceiling (All Categories):** On designated milestone prose within any pacing category — Phase-climax events, first cultivation breakthrough or awakening, saga-turning confrontations, first rendering of a major location — the AI author may organically extend up to that category's **Max Ceiling** (Fast: 2000, Medium: 2500, Slow: 3000) without user override.

**Absolute Hard Cap (Canon v4.34):** No Prose unit, regardless of pacing category or milestone status, may exceed **3000 words** under any circumstances without the user's explicit, on-record permission. 3000 words is the ceiling of the entire pacing system, not just the Slow category's ceiling.

**Pacing Determination:** The AI author determines pacing based on the content type of the prose unit using the Anchor Type Defaults table below and organic narrative assessment. The determination is made before drafting and disclosed in the Gate 1 Position Header.

**Gate 1 Pacing Declaration Format (Rule P2-Pacing-2):** The Gate 1 Position Header must include the pacing declaration immediately below the Prose title:
> **Pacing: [Fast / Medium / Slow] | Target: [word range] | Immersion: [%–% range]**

**Anchor Type Defaults:**

The following content types have canonical default pacing assignments. Mixed-content prose units use the dominant content type as the primary determinant; the AI may adjust by one tier when a scene's secondary content significantly shifts the balance.

| Content Type | Default Pacing | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Pure combat / active battle sequence | **Fast** (1400–1800) | Maximum momentum; depth through action precision |
| Chase sequence / physical pursuit / flight | **Fast** (1400–1800) | Urgency governs; sensory compression required |
| Travel / journey montage | **Fast** (1400–1800) | Coverage of ground without dwelling; environmental depth in efficient passes |
| Diplomatic / bilateral session / formal court exchange | **Medium** (1900–2300) | Multi-register dialogue; political subtext; character positioning |
| Character interior / psychological depth scene | **Medium** (1900–2300) | Interiority requires space but not the full slow render |
| Social / domestic scene / casual interaction | **Medium** (1900–2300) | Relationship texture at standard pace |
| Information reveal / intelligence gathering / intrigue | **Medium** (1900–2300) | Strategic disclosure requires care but not full-slow immersion |
| Tactical planning / strategic deliberation | **Medium** (1900–2300) | Dialogue and interiority balanced; planning texture important |
| World / location establishment (first full rendering) | **Slow** (2400–2700) | Immersive world-building; sensory + cultural + historical depth required |
| Cultivation breakthrough / awakening / realm milestone | **Slow** (2400–2700) | Identity-level transformation; full psychological + physical rendering |
| First on-page appearance of a major character | **Slow** (2400–2700) | Character establishment; voice, interiority, physical presence all required |
| Phase-climax or saga-turning confrontation | **Slow** (2400–3000 flex) | Maximum depth; milestone prose; flexible ceiling applies, subject to the 3000-word absolute hard cap |
| Opening prose of a chapter or section | **Slow** (2400–2700) | Setting, register, and world state must all be established |

**Rule P2-Pacing-1 — All 10 Named Depth Pillars at All Pacing Levels:** The Named Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10 (Part 28 Area 4) remain simultaneously mandatory at all pacing levels. Fast-paced prose expresses each pillar more efficiently — the sensory detail is sharper, the interiority more concentrated, the cultural texture compressed into specific action — but no pillar is omitted. A 2000-word combat prose must still carry simultaneous sensory precision, psychological ground, ideological texture, relational weight, environmental specificity, historical resonance, power honesty, consequence threading, authentic voice, and cultural embeddedness. The compression is in execution, never in coverage.

**Rule P2-Pacing-2 — Gate 1 Pacing Declaration is Mandatory:** The pacing category, word count target, and immersion % range must appear in every Gate 1 Position Header. Failure to declare pacing before drafting is an Audit Pass 1 failure.

**Rule P2-Pacing-3 — Mandatory Pacing Confirmation, User Override (Canon v4.35 Amendment):** Pacing is no longer a silently-applied AI default subject only to optional override — per `ssc-narration-p28-pre-prose-decision-consultation-gate` Rule 19.3a, the pacing question is asked via AskUserQuestion before every single Prose-producing command invocation, with no exception, regardless of how unambiguous the Anchor Type Defaults table's indicated category is. The AI's Anchor-Type-Defaults-derived category is presented as the recommended option; the user may accept it, choose a different category, or select SKIP (which defers to the AI's recommendation). The user may also declare pacing directly in the command invocation (e.g., `[NEXT PROSE] slow`), which pre-answers the mandatory question rather than bypassing it. The user's declared pacing overrides all anchor type defaults.

**Rule P2-Pacing-4 — No Floor Below 1400 Words (Canon v4.34):** 1400 words is the absolute minimum. No prose unit may be written below 1400 words regardless of content type. If a scene can be completed at full depth in under 1400 words, it is either not a complete scene or is not being rendered at pacing-appropriate depth.

---

- **No Range Descriptions.** Prose does not describe what a scene is approximately like. It describes exactly what is happening, with sensory precision, physical detail, and experiential immediacy. "He was powerful" is forbidden. "The weight of his presence compressed the air around him, and she felt her own cultivation instinctively recoil — a cold, involuntary retreat of her qi like smoke before a flame she could not see" is correct.

- **No Shortcuts.** Prose does not skip events, compress them into summaries, or bracket them with ellipses of implication. Every moment that matters happens on the page. If a negotiation is critical to the plot, the reader witnesses the negotiation. If a walk through a hostile district matters, the reader walks it with the character.

- **No Tonal Collapse.** The prose voice must remain sustained and consistent within a Prose unit. A scene of profound tension does not suddenly lapse into flat description. A scene of quiet melancholy does not abruptly adopt an action-prose rhythm. The tone is established by the scene's emotional context and held throughout.

#### 6.3 — Mandatory Prose Elements

The following elements must appear in every Prose where they are contextually relevant. This does not mean every element appears in every Prose — it means that when the context calls for them, they may not be omitted:

- **Dialogue:** Conversations happen on the page. Characters speak to each other in full, contextually grounded exchanges. (Full rules in Area 2.)
- **Lore Depth:** The world's history, systems, and knowledge are woven into the prose through character awareness, environmental observation, and narrative voice. Lore is not dumped — it is revealed through the story as the story requires it.
- **Armor, Clothing, and Equipment:** When a character's physical presentation matters — in combat, in formal encounters, in social contexts — their armor, clothing, insignia, weapons, and tools are described with specificity. Names, materials, cultural significance, and functional purpose are embedded in the description.
- **Weapon Names and Usage:** Named weapons are identified by name. Their usage — grip style, draw, technique, weight, cultural history, the specific way a character has adapted to them — is rendered with precision.
- **Canon Information Delivery:** All canon information delivered in prose — power levels, classification vocabulary of every category, location references, faction references, relationship state, cultural detail, mechanics, and any other canon fact — must pass the **Three-Beat Test defined in Part 28 Area 15.4**. The **Heavenly Scale sensory system (Part 3 Area 3.3)** remains the canonical delivery mechanism for cultivation-power encounter and is the preferred register whenever power is sensed rather than named. Raw numerical multipliers remain prohibited without exception. Information that is tonally correct but scene-standalone is a violation of the Contextual Earning Law regardless of its technical accuracy. Explicit naming of any classification is permitted only as a Section 15.5 "consequential naming" subcase — where the naming itself does scene-work.
- **Psychology and Emotional State:** Every character's internal world — what they feel, what they fear, what they want, what they are concealing, what they are discovering about themselves — is present in the prose. Psychology is not told. It is rendered through action, micro-behavior, dialogue subtext, and physical sensation.
- **Cause and Consequence:** Every event in a Prose produces effects. Those effects begin to manifest within the same Prose when narratively appropriate. Consequence is not deferred indefinitely. If a character makes a choice that changes the world, the world begins to respond within the scene.
- **Discovery and Realization:** When a character learns something, understands something they previously misunderstood, or realizes the true nature of a situation or person — that moment is given full narrative weight. Discovery is not a parenthetical. It is a scene.
- **Combat:** When combat occurs, it is rendered with the full combat prose standard defined in Area 3. Combat is never compressed, skipped, or summarized.

#### 6.4 — Prose Continuity

Every Prose must relate to the preceding Prose in the same Area, or to the final Prose of the preceding Area. The narrative must maintain continuous flow. There must be no disjoint between consecutive Prose units — no unexplained gaps, no sudden changes in location or character state without transitional grounding, no tonal discontinuities. The reader must be able to read every Prose in sequence and experience a single unbroken narrative journey.

Each Prose must begin **where the last ended** — in the same space, the same moment, the same emotional register — and advance from there. The story always moves forward from a concrete narrative position.

#### 6.5 — Prose Count

There can be multiple Prose units within a single Area. The number required is determined entirely by what the Area's narrative event demands. A simple, focused event may require one Prose. A complex, multi-beat event may require many. There is no minimum or maximum. The Area is complete when all its narrative content has been fully realized at the pacing-appropriate prose depth standard (Part 2 Area 6.2.1 — 3500%–5000% range based on pacing classification).
