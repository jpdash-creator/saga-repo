## Area 14 — Technology Travel: FTL Mechanics and Navigation

### 14.1 — FTL Drive Types

Multiple FTL drive architectures exist in the Starward Saga universe. The specific drive type used by a civilization is a setting design choice; Part 15 governs the mechanics for all of them.

**Recognized FTL Drive Architectures:**
1. **Warp Drive:** Contracts space ahead and expands it behind the vessel; the vessel sits in a "warp bubble" and is not technically moving through space — space moves around it. Navigation is continuous; no fixed transit points required.
2. **Hyperspace Drive:** The vessel transitions to an alternative spatial dimension (hyperspace) where distances are compressed; travels through hyperspace and emerges at destination. Hyperspace has its own hazards. Navigation requires hyperspace charts.
3. **Jump Drive (Point-to-Point):** The vessel performs a near-instantaneous dimensional jump between two known gravity points. Each jump covers a fixed distance determined by drive tier. Navigation requires mapped jump points.
4. **Fold Drive:** Similar to cultivation spatial fold — the drive compresses space rather than moving through it. Most energy-intensive but most precise. Navigation requires destination coordinates.
5. **Gate Transit:** Not a drive — infrastructure-dependent traversal (governed in Area 13).

**Rule 14.1.1 — Drive Type Determines Navigation Method.**
The navigation requirements, precision, and failure modes differ by drive type. A civilization's FTL history and drive selection is a world-building choice, but it must be consistent within a given civilization's narrative.

### 14.2 — FTL Navigation Precision

FTL navigation is not perfectly precise. The farther the transit, the larger the potential landing error.

| Technology Tier | Navigation Beacon Network | Arrival Precision (1 ly transit) | Arrival Precision (100 ly transit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 8 (early FTL) | None required | ±1 AU | ±100 AU |
| Tier 9 (standard FTL) | Beacons at departure + destination | ±0.1 AU | ±10 AU |
| Tier 10 (mature FTL) | Dense beacon network | ±0.01 AU | ±1 AU |
| Tier 11+ (advanced FTL) | Full galactic beacon grid | ±0.001 AU | ±0.1 AU |

**Navigational consequence:** Arriving 10 AU off-target in a hostile star system can mean arriving in the outer system's vacuum with no refueling capability rather than near the inhabited inner system. Navigation precision is a survival and tactical variable.

### 14.3 — FTL Interdiction

**FTL Interdiction** — preventing an enemy vessel from entering or exiting FTL within a controlled zone — is a military capability that emerges at Tier 9+.

**Interdiction Methods:**
- **Gravity wells:** Natural or artificial massive bodies that make FTL entry/exit dangerous within their gravitational radius (~0.01–0.1 AU depending on body mass and FTL type)
- **Interdiction fields (Tier 9+):** Technology systems that generate localized FTL-suppression zones; forces enemy vessels to drop from FTL within the field
- **Beacon jamming (Tier 10+):** Disrupting FTL navigation signals to prevent accurate jump targeting

**Rule 14.3.1 — FTL Cannot Be Used Within Interdiction Fields.**
Within an active interdiction field or near a significant gravity well, FTL drive activation fails. Vessels must use subluminal engines. This creates the tactical chokepoint mechanic: controlling the approach to a gate, star system, or installation requires either destroying the interdiction infrastructure or physically defeating the defending fleet.

### 14.4 — FTL Communication vs. FTL Travel

FTL communication (transmitting data faster-than-light) and FTL travel are separate capabilities that develop at different rates:

| Tier | Communication | Travel |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 8 | FTL data transmission first achieved (limited range and bandwidth) | Early prototype FTL travel; generation ships still dominant |
| Tier 9 | Multi-system FTL comm network; usable governance tool | FTL travel normalized; multi-system travel practical |
| Tier 10 | Cluster-wide real-time FTL comm | FTL travel rapid; cluster-unified travel |
| Tier 11+ | Galactic FTL comm infrastructure | FTL travel plus gate networks |

**Rule 14.4.1 — Communication Lag Remains Meaningful Below Tier 10.**
Even with FTL communication at Tier 8, message transmission takes hours to days to cross multi-light-year distances at early FTL comm speeds. Real-time governance requires Tier 9+ communication infrastructure at multi-system scale.
