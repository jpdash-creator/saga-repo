## Area 3 — Psychological Consequences

### 3.1 — Internal Consequence as a Separate Domain

Psychological consequences — the impacts of actions on a character's internal psychological architecture — are governed jointly by this Part (as consequences) and Part 26 (as psychological states). The governance boundary:
- **Part 27 governs:** That a psychological consequence occurred, its CSS severity, and what triggered it — the causal architecture of psychological consequence
- **Part 26 governs:** What the psychological state looks like internally, how it manifests in behavior, and how it is coped with and resolved

**The Rule of Internal Non-Immunity:**
No character is psychologically immune to the consequences of their actions. This rule interacts directly with Part 26's Principle P26-1. A character who has killed ten thousand people "without guilt" is not a character without psychological consequence — they are a character in denial, dissociation, or pathological moral inversion. The psychological consequence is present in their inability to form genuine connections, their compulsive escalation, their hollowness, or their eventual decompensation.

### 3.2 — Consequence of Killing — The Full Cascade

The complete consequence cascade of a significant killing event:

**Immediate (IC-1):**
- Physical: The body, the blood, the cessation of movement
- Psychological (Part 26, Area 2.1.1): The first-kill threshold event, or the continued accumulation if not the first

**Short-term (IC-2):**
- Relational: Those who were bonded to the deceased now know who killed them — grief, grief-converted-to-rage, and vendetta formation in survivors
- Factional (CSS-3 to CSS-6 depending on victim's position): The deceased's faction assesses the kill for appropriate response
- Legal/political: Under Part 5's force legitimacy framework, the kill is classified as legitimate combat, murder, execution, or assassination — each with different consequence profiles

**Medium-term (IC-3):**
- Reputation consequence: The character becomes "the person who killed [victim]" in relevant social circles — whether this is a positive or negative consequence depends on the victim, the circumstances, and the culture
- Vendetta consequence: If survivors are connected to powerful organizations, active pursuit may begin
- Psychological chronic state: Unresolved first-kill guilt becomes a chronic psychological configuration (Part 26)

**Long-term (IC-4):**
- Legacy consequence: The kill is part of the character's permanent history — it may be remembered by those who survive generations, by historical records, by the victim's descendants
- Cultivation consequence: Moral injury from unjustified killing creates cultivation Heart ceiling (Part 26, Area 3.3); the ceiling remains until resolved

### 3.3 — Consequence Feedback Loops

Psychological consequences create secondary consequence cascades:

**The Rage Displacement Loop:**
Character experiences major adverse consequence (CSS-4+) → psychological pain exceeds coping capacity → rage displacement activated (Part 26, Area 6.3.2) → displaced rage produces actions against external targets → those actions produce new CSS-3+ consequences → further adverse consequence → cycle continues. This is the canonical pathway by which a single major adverse consequence can produce an escalating cascade of further consequences across months or years.

**The Suppression Decompensation Cascade:**
Character experiences CSS-5+ adverse consequence → suppression coping (Part 26, Area 6.3.1) appears to contain it → character functions normally for months or years → accumulated suppression reaches threshold → catastrophic decompensation event → all suppressed psychological material floods simultaneously → character enters acute crisis → their consequent actions in that state produce a new major consequence cascade.
