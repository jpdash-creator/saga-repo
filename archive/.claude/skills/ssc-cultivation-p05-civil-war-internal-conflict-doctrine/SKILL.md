## Area 13 — Civil War & Internal Conflict Doctrine

### 13.1 — Civil War Definition

A **Civil War** is an internal conflict classified at E5 or above that takes place within a single political entity (empire, kingdom, sect, faction, clan system) between parties who both claim legitimate authority over that entity. Civil War is distinguished from:

- **External War (E5–E7):** Between separate political entities
- **Internal Purge / Suppression (E3–E4):** A dominant authority suppressing a rebellion without acknowledging the rebellion's legitimacy claims
- **Factional Conflict (E1–E4):** Internal political violence that has not reached E5 scale

### 13.2 — Civil War Initiation Conditions

A conflict within a political entity escalates to Civil War status when **all three** of the following conditions are met:

**CW-1 — Competing Authority Claims:**
At least two parties within the political entity make active, recognized claims to legitimate authority over the entity. Claims need not be legally valid — they must be recognized as sincere claims by a significant portion of the entity's population or forces.

**CW-2 — Armed Mobilization:**
Both competing authority claims have organized military forces committed to prosecuting the conflict. Unilateral armed mobilization against unresisting rivals is not a Civil War — it is a suppression.

**CW-3 — E5 Threshold:**
The internal conflict has reached or exceeded E5 scale — multiple battles, sustained multi-month prosecution, complete mobilization of institutional resources on at least one side.

### 13.3 — Civil War Combat Governance

All escalation tiers (Area 5), force legitimacy rules (Area 4), third-party rules (Area 8), and termination conditions (Area 12) apply to Civil War as they do to external war. Additional specific rules:

**Rule 13.3.1 — Enhanced Non-Combatant Protection:**
In a Civil War, the non-combatant population of the contested political entity retains full protected status (PS-1). The shared political entity means that combatants on both sides share a common civilian population — attacks on civilians are attacks on the political entity's foundation and carry maximum consequence weight under Part 27.

**Rule 13.3.2 — Internal Heritage Protection:**
Historical, cultural, and institutional sites of the contested political entity must be protected by all parties unless designated as military objectives under formal E4+ battlefield declaration. Destruction of a political entity's own cultural heritage in the course of a Civil War is an irreversible consequence that Part 27 treats with maximum severity.

**Rule 13.3.3 — No External Acceleration:**
A Civil War's escalation tier cannot be artificially accelerated by external parties. If an external faction's intervention causes the Civil War to escalate beyond E5, the external party bears co-responsibility for the escalation (Section 13.4).

### 13.4 — Neutral Party Status in Civil War

A faction, sect, clan, or individual entity within the contested political entity may declare **neutrality** during the Civil War. To maintain recognized neutral status:

**Neutrality Requirements:**
1. Non-participation: the neutral party takes no hostile action against any Civil War combatant
2. Non-support: the neutral party provides no direct material support — forces, weapons, resources, intelligence — to either side (humanitarian aid may be provided without losing neutral status)
3. Public declaration: neutrality is publicly communicated to both Civil War parties so that neither misidentifies the neutral party as a hidden ally of the other

**Neutral Party Protections:**
Recognized neutral parties within a Civil War are protected from targeting by either side. Attacking a declared neutral party constitutes Force Legitimacy violation and may trigger external intervention rights for the neutral party's defensive response.

**Neutral Party Loss of Status:**
A neutral party loses neutral status the moment they take any action that qualifies as direct support of either Civil War party. Once lost, neutral status cannot be immediately reclaimed — the party must re-declare through a publicly recognized process and must be accepted as neutral by both parties.

### 13.5 — External Intervention in Civil Wars

An external faction that intervenes in a Civil War becomes a full party to the conflict at the civil war's current escalation tier. External intervention rules (Area 8) apply. Additionally:

- External intervention in an E5 Civil War is classified as E6 (cross-entity conflict) from the intervening external faction's perspective
- External intervention triggers the Alliance Activation rules (Section 8.6) for any party that has alliance obligations with the intervening faction
- An external faction that intervenes cannot subsequently claim non-combatant status or attempt neutrality declaration — intervention is a full and irreversible engagement commitment

### 13.6 — Civil War Resolution

A Civil War terminates when one party achieves control over the contested authority structure — recognized control, not merely military dominance. The termination of military hostilities without political resolution leaves the Civil War in a de facto ceasefire state rather than a genuine termination.

Formal Civil War resolution requires: recognition of the winning authority claim by the surviving losing party (or its remnants), establishment of the new authority's legitimacy claim, and a recognized peace-making act (formal surrender, peace treaty, accession agreement) that creates a stable post-war political state.
