## Area 3 — The Ideology Ledger

### 3.1 — What the Ideology Ledger Is

The **Ideology Ledger** is the canonical record of every character's ideological state and its history of change. It is not a narrative document — it is a governance document. Authors consult the Ideology Ledger before writing significant character decisions to ensure those decisions are ideologically coherent or ideologically justified deviations.

**The Ideology Ledger Contains:**
1. **Current Ideology Profile** — the character's present ideological position: Primary Category, Secondary Category, Tertiary Category, and Polarity level for each
2. **Core / Operational / Surface Ideology Mapping** — what the character's deepest beliefs are versus what they act from day to day versus what they publicly express
3. **Ideology Event History** — the chronological record of every ideological shift, challenge, and confirmation
4. **Active Contradictions** — unresolved tensions between layers or categories that the character has not yet integrated
5. **Ideological Blindspots** — beliefs the character holds without awareness; things the character cannot see because their ideology does not give them the tools to see it

**Rule 20.3.1.1 — Ledger Mandatory for Named Characters:** Every named character who appears in more than three scenes must have an Ideology Ledger entry. Characters without Ledger entries are restricted to background or reactive roles.

**Rule 20.3.1.2 — Ledger Primacy Over Momentary Inspiration:** If an authorial impulse to have a character act in a certain way conflicts with the Ideology Ledger, the Ledger governs. Either (a) the action must be grounded in the Ideology Ledger's current state, (b) the action triggers an Ideology Ledger update (the character is changing), or (c) the action is an ideological rupture event (the character is acting against their ideology under pressure, which has its own governance rules in Area 3.5).

**Rule 20.3.1.3 — Protagonist Ledgers Are Not Protected:** The protagonist's ideology is not exempt from challenge, contradiction, or change. Protagonist narrative immunity in ideological matters violates the same principle as protagonist narrative immunity in consequence matters (Part 27 Rule 27.5.1). The protagonist's ideology must be genuinely tested, not merely affirmed.

### 3.3 — Ideology Event Types

Every event recorded in the Ideology Ledger is classified by type:

| Event Type | Description | Minimum Impact Level |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **IE-1 — Confirmation Event** | An experience that confirms the character's current ideology | PIS-2 |
| **IE-2 — Challenge Event** | An experience that challenges but does not change the current ideology | PIS-3 |
| **IE-3 — Expansion Event** | An experience that adds nuance, new categories, or depth without replacing core beliefs | PIS-4 |
| **IE-4 — Partial Shift Event** | An experience that modifies Operational Ideology without touching Core Ideology | PIS-5 |
| **IE-5 — Core Shift Event** | An experience that modifies Core Ideology | PIS-7 |
| **IE-6 — Ideology Crisis Event** | An experience that destabilizes the entire ideology structure simultaneously | PIS-8 |
| **IE-7 — Ideology Collapse Event** | An experience that destroys the character's operating ideology, requiring complete reconstruction | PIS-9 |
| **IE-8 — Ideology Crystallization Event** | An experience that permanently fixes a specific belief domain at Polarity-5 | PIS-6 |
| **IE-9 — Ideology Reconciliation Event** | An experience that resolves a recorded Active Contradiction | PIS-5 |
| **IE-10 — Ideological Revelation Event** | The character becomes consciously aware of a previously unconscious ideological structure | PIS-4 |

### 3.4 — Ideology Change Mechanics

Ideology does not change arbitrarily. It changes through specific types of experience that meet threshold criteria.

**Ideology Change Threshold Table:**

| Layer | Minimum Event | Minimum Experience Intensity |
|-------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Surface Ideology | IE-1 | PIS-1 (any perception that surface performance is being challenged) |
| Operational Ideology | IE-3 or IE-4 | PIS-4 (significant repeated experiences or one powerful experience) |
| Core Ideology | IE-5 | PIS-7 (experiences that strike the foundational identity layer) |
| Polarity Increase | IE-8 | PIS-6 (experiences that produce ideological commitment hardening) |
| Polarity Decrease | IE-3 + IE-10 | PIS-4 + self-reflection (difficult to achieve; requires both challenge and awareness) |

**Rule 20.3.4.1 — Core Ideology Cannot Be Changed by Argument Alone:** Intellectual counter-argument, however brilliant, cannot change Core Ideology. Core Ideology is changed only by lived experience of sufficient PIS intensity. Characters who appear to change their Core Ideology through argument alone are (a) changing only their Surface Ideology, (b) experiencing an IE-10 revelation facilitated by the argument, or (c) in mid-crisis already (IE-6 or IE-7 in progress).

**Rule 20.3.4.2 — Repeated Small Events Accumulate:** Multiple IE-2 (Challenge) events in the same ideological domain accumulate to eventually trigger an IE-3 (Expansion) or IE-4 (Partial Shift). The accumulation threshold is: five IE-2 events in the same domain within a comparable narrative period = one IE-4 event.

**Rule 20.3.4.3 — Ideology Does Not Flip:** Even IE-7 (Ideology Collapse) does not produce an immediate opposite ideology. A character who held SDI (Strength-Dominant) through Polarity-4 and experiences an IE-7 collapses into ideological disorientation — they may trend toward JCI or TSI in reconstruction, but the reconstruction process takes narrative time and passes through an ideology-void phase (governed jointly by Part 26 Area 9 — Identity Crisis, and Part 20 Area 8 — Ideological Void).

### 3.5 — Ideological Rupture Events

An **Ideological Rupture Event** is when a character acts against their own recorded ideology under extreme pressure, deception, or psychological crisis. This is not a Ledger error — it is a canonical event type.

**Ideological Rupture Conditions:**
- Psychological state at PIS-8 or higher overrides ideological processing (Part 26 Area 10 — combat psychology override)
- Complete information deprivation: the character acts on false information that, had it been correct, would have been ideologically coherent
- Extreme duress where the character's survival instinct overrides all ideological processing
- Soul or mind control technique (Part 4 Area 2.3 — Convert Soul) that temporarily replaces ideological processing

**Rule 20.3.5.1 — Rupture Events Have Consequences:** An Ideological Rupture Event is recorded in the Ideology Ledger and produces either (a) an IE-5 or IE-6 based on the character's retrospective confrontation with the rupture, or (b) a Core Ideology rationalization — the character retroactively reconstructs an ideological explanation for the ruptured behavior. Rationalizations are recorded as Active Contradictions.

**Rule 20.3.5.2 — Rupture Cannot Be Forgotten:** Part 20 prohibits authorial erasure of Ideological Rupture Events. The character may choose to forget, suppress, or rationalize — but the Ideology Ledger retains the event, and it is available for later narrative activation.

### 3.6 — Ideological Blindspot Revelation and Removal Law

An **Ideological Blindspot** (Ideology Ledger entry type 5 per Area 3.2) is a belief or assumption the character holds without awareness — something their ideology structurally prevents them from seeing. Blindspots are among the most powerful narrative assets in the series. This Area governs how blindspots are revealed, challenged, and removed.

**Rule 20.3.6.1 — Blindspots Are Structurally Generated:** A blindspot is not a failure of intelligence or perception — it is a failure of ideological framing. The character cannot see what their ideology has no category for. A Strength-Dominant character cannot perceive relational obligation as a real force because their ideology has no framework for obligation that is not ultimately reducible to power. Blindspots arise from the internal logic of the character's Core Ideology and must be consistent with that logic.

**Rule 20.3.6.2 — Blindspot Revelation Triggers an IE Event:** When a character becomes consciously aware of a blindspot — either through confrontation, lived experience, or the specific IE-10 Revelation Event — this is always a Part 20 IE event:
- **Confrontation-Triggered Revelation:** Another character explicitly exposes the blindspot in dialogue or action; IE-4 minimum (Core Activation)
- **Experience-Triggered Revelation:** The blindspot causes a consequence so significant the character cannot rationalize it away; IE-5 to IE-6 depending on severity
- **Crisis-Triggered Revelation:** The blindspot causes the character to fail at a fundamental level (Phase 4 arc context); IE-7 or higher
- **IE-10 Ideological Revelation Event:** The purest form — the character achieves conscious awareness of a previously unconscious structure; always accompanied by PIS-4+ (Active Wound) from the psychological impact of self-recognition

**Rule 20.3.6.3 — Revelation Does Not Equal Removal:** Becoming aware of a blindspot does not remove it. A character who now knows they have a blindspot around relational obligation may still be unable to act on that knowledge because their Core Ideology has not changed. Revelation creates cognitive dissonance (an Active Contradiction per Area 3.2 entry type 4) that must be resolved through further IE events. The gap between revelation and genuine removal is a productive narrative space.

**Rule 20.3.6.4 — Blindspot Removal Requires Core Ideology Shift:** A blindspot is fully removed only when the Core Ideology shifts to include the category the blindspot excluded. This requires a minimum IE-6 (Ideology Crisis) event — the same threshold as Core Ideology change per Rule 20.3.4.1. Partial blindspot removal (the character can now sometimes see what they previously couldn't, under favorable conditions) is a valid intermediate state and should be tracked in the Ideology Ledger as a "Partially Removed" blindspot entry.

**Rule 20.3.6.5 — Blindspot Replacement:** When a blindspot is removed, it is often replaced by a new blindspot generated by the character's new ideological position. An ideologically evolving character does not simply accumulate wisdom — they trade one set of blindspots for another. The new blindspot must be tracked. A character who has overcome SDI blindness to relational obligation may now have a blindspot around the costs of their new relational priority.

**Rule 20.3.6.6 — Narration Standard for Blindspot Revelation:** The narrative must not editorialize when a blindspot is exposed. The prose renders the consequence of the blindspot operating — the character's failure, confusion, surprise, or damage — without imposing authorial judgment that they "should have known." The reader perceives the blindspot through the character's behavior and its consequences, not through narrative pronouncement.

### 3.7 — Active Contradiction Resolution Law

An **Active Contradiction** (Ideology Ledger entry type 4 per Area 3.2) is an unresolved tension between two layers or positions within a character's ideology that the character has not yet integrated. Active Contradictions are canonical — they are not authorial errors; they are the structured governance of internal ideological complexity. This Area governs how contradictions are resolved, sustained, or weaponized.

**Rule 20.3.7.1 — Active Contradictions Must Be Recorded:** Every named character whose Ideology Ledger contains an Active Contradiction must have that contradiction specifically documented: what the two positions are, which layers they occupy (Core/Operational/Surface), when they entered the Ledger as a contradiction, and what triggered the contradiction's emergence. Undocumented contradictions are authorial convenience, not governed ideology.

**Rule 20.3.7.2 — Contradictions Under Pressure Are Story Engines:** An Active Contradiction is most useful when it is placed under narrative pressure — when the story forces the character into a situation where both sides of the contradiction cannot simultaneously be honored. The character must choose. That choice is both a character definition moment and an IE event. This pressure should not be arbitrary — the best contradiction pressure emerges organically from the narrative circumstances the character's own actions have created.

**Rule 20.3.7.3 — Contradiction Resolution Pathways:** Active Contradictions can resolve through four canonical pathways:

| Resolution Pathway | Description | IE Event |
|-------------------|-------------|---------|
| **Integration** | The character finds a framework that genuinely reconciles both positions; neither is discarded | IE-9 Ideology Reconciliation Event; hardest and most significant resolution |
| **Dominance** | One side of the contradiction overwhelms the other; the dominated position is discarded or suppressed into a new blindspot | IE-5 to IE-6; the character becomes more coherent but loses something |
| **Sublimation** | The contradiction is redirected — rather than resolving the tension, the character directs its energy into action, cultivation, or creativity that channels the unresolved tension productively | IE-4; tension remains but is managed |
| **Collapse** | The contradiction becomes so acute it collapses the character's ideology toward IS-4 (Fragmented) or IS-5 (Void) | IE-7 to IE-8; the contradiction was not resolvable and the ideology fractures |

**Rule 20.3.7.4 — Unresolved Contradictions Accumulate:** A character who carries multiple unresolved Active Contradictions without any IE events processing them is building ideological pressure. This is a governed state — the character may appear fine on the surface while the contradiction count climbs. When the pressure is released (through a sufficiently significant narrative event), the release is proportional to the accumulated weight. A character carrying 4–5 unresolved Active Contradictions who faces a genuine Phase 4 Unmaking event may experience ideology collapse (IS-4 or IS-5) rather than manageable crisis.

**Rule 20.3.7.5 — Contradiction Cannot Be Administratively Removed:** An Active Contradiction cannot be removed from the Ideology Ledger without a corresponding IE event of sufficient magnitude. Authors cannot simply write a contradiction out of existence because it has become inconvenient. The contradiction must be resolved through the narrative — through lived experience, choice under pressure, or consequence — and the resolution event must be logged in the Ideology Ledger.

**Rule 20.3.7.6 — Hero-Villain Contradictions Are Structurally Related:** When a hero and villain share a structural ideological contradiction (the hero's Active Contradiction involves the same tension that the villain has resolved into their Core Ideology, or vice versa), this creates the highest-value ideological confrontation structure in the series. The ideological confrontation between them is the hero confronting what their own contradiction could become. This relationship must be tracked in both the Hero Ledger Section 4 and the Villain Ledger Section 4.
