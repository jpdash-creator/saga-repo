## Area 7 — Dialogue in Combat and High-Stakes Contexts

### 7.1 — Speech in Combat

Combat dialogue is among the most compressed, most charged, and most characterologically dense dialogue in the novel. When characters speak during combat, they reveal things they would never reveal in a controlled social context — what they fear, what they value, what they are willing to lose, and what they need the other person to understand before this is over.

**Rule 29.7.1.1 — Combat Dialogue Must Match Combat Stakes:** Speech during a life-or-death confrontation does not have the leisure of ordinary conversation. Combat dialogue is often shorter, more compressed, more direct. What would take a paragraph of inner reflection in a quieter moment must be achieved in a sentence or two. The pressure of combat strips language to its essentials — which is what makes combat dialogue so revealing.

**Rule 29.7.1.2 — Combat Dialogue Is Ideologically Legible:** The confrontation between hero and villain is fundamentally an ideological confrontation (Part 18, Part 17). The final combat dialogue between them must express this ideological confrontation directly or obliquely — what each character believes, what they are fighting for, what victory and defeat mean beyond the physical outcome. Combat dialogue that only discusses tactics and power output has missed the central meaning of the fight.

**Rule 29.7.1.3 — Combat Taunts, Declarations, and Ultimatums Are Governed:** When a character taunts, declares their intent, or issues an ultimatum during combat, these speech acts are not random. They are governed by the character's ideology (Part 20), psychology (Part 26), and their specific relationship to the person they are speaking to. A villain does not taunt for generic menace — they taunt in a way that is specific to their ideology and to what they have perceived about the hero's weakness.

**Rule 29.7.1.4 — Dialogue and Move Execution Are Simultaneous Governance Events:** When a character speaks during combat and simultaneously executes a Named Move, both the speech act (Part 29) and the move execution (Part 6) must be rendered with full governance compliance. The move is not paused for the speech, and the speech does not pause for the move. The dual rendering is a simultaneous event.

### 7.2 — Speech in Negotiation and Formal Encounter

High-stakes negotiation and formal encounter dialogue operates under its own specific pressures — the need to reveal enough to build trust while concealing enough to preserve leverage, the performance of strength, the testing of the other party's limits, and the management of a room where every word is heard by multiple parties with different stakes.

**Rule 29.7.2.1 — Negotiation Dialogue Must Reveal Power Structure:** Who holds leverage in a negotiation must be legible through the dialogue itself — not through narration that summarizes the power dynamic, but through who speaks first, who concedes, who redirects, who retreats to a better position, and how each party's language shifts as the leverage shifts.

**Rule 29.7.2.2 — Multi-Party Negotiation Governance:** When a negotiation involves three or more parties, the dialogue must render the triangulated power dynamics — each party is managing their relationship to both other parties simultaneously, which produces speech that can seem to address one party while actually communicating to another. This is the dialogue of genuine political complexity and must be rendered at that complexity.

### 7.3 — Speech in Grief and Psychological Crisis

When a character is in acute psychological crisis — at PIS-5 or above per Part 26 — their speech is fundamentally altered. The normal architecture of social speech breaks down or transforms. The crisis reveals itself through language.

**Rule 29.7.3.1 — PIS State Must Alter Speech Pattern:** A character at PIS-7 (Acute Crisis) does not speak the way they speak at PIS-2 (Surface Disruption). The crisis must be visible in their speech — in what they cannot say, in what they say without meaning to, in the way their sentences fracture or become brutally simple, in the way they hear what is said to them differently than it is meant. PIS states are not silent conditions. They alter the entire architecture of speech.

**Rule 29.7.3.2 — Grief Dialogue Requires Its Own Specific Rendering:** Grief is one of the most complex speech states because grief often makes it impossible to say what is most needed. Grief dialogue in the Starward Saga does not settle for "she was overcome with grief and could not speak." It renders the specific physics of grief in language — the sentences that begin and cannot be finished, the strange specific details that the grieving mind fixates on, the way the grieving character reaches for ordinary speech and cannot find it, and the way what they manage to say is simultaneously about the lost person and about what the loss means for who the grieving character now has to be.
