## Area 7 — Emotion-From-Experience Law

### 7.1 — The Emotion-From-Experience Principle

All emotional states experienced by characters must be traceable to specific experiences in the prose. No emotion may be asserted without being grounded. A character does not simply feel grief — they feel grief because the specific person they loved in the specific ways established in the prior prose is now gone, and because their Part 26 psychological architecture, their Part 4 Heart state, and their Part 20 ideology all shape the specific texture of what grief means for this particular person.

### 7.2 — Emotional Authenticity Standards

**Grounding Requirement:** Every emotional state must have a causal chain traceable to prior prose events. The cause may be immediate (the death just witnessed) or deeply historical (the grief is for a person the reader never met, but whose absence has shaped every scene in which this character has appeared). The emotional response must be proportional to the established relationship between the character and the cause.

**Texture Requirement:** Emotions must be rendered with their full sensory, cognitive, and behavioral texture. The body carries emotion — the specific physical manifestations of grief in this cultivator's trained physique, in this soldier's conditioned posture, in this elderly merchant's weathered face — are specific and must be rendered as such. Generic emotional description ("she was sad," "he was angry") fails the texture requirement.

**Duration Requirement:** Significant emotional events — CSS-5+ consequences, IE-4+ ideology events, PIS-4+ psychological impacts — do not resolve immediately. They persist. They alter the character's behavior in scenes that follow. They resurface in unexpected moments. The Part 26 psychological governance framework requires this temporal spread. Part 28 reinforces it: emotions established in the prose must have a duration consistent with their magnitude.

**Interaction Requirement:** Emotional states interact with the character's cultivation, ideology, and tactical choices. A character in acute grief (PIS-7) does not fight at full effectiveness (per Part 26). A character in ideological ecstasy (IE-3 Conviction event) takes risks that their baseline pragmatism would forbid. A character in the first shock of betrayal (IE-5 Disillusionment event) may speak with an honesty that their normal social register would filter. These interactions are not optional — they are Part 26 and Part 20 governance requirements that the emotional rendering must satisfy.

### 7.3 — Prohibited Emotional Rendering

The following are prohibited under the emotion-from-experience law:

- **Asserted emotion:** Telling the reader a character feels something without showing the specific physical, behavioral, and cognitive evidence of that feeling
- **Disproportionate emotion:** A character exhibiting emotional intensity that exceeds what their established relationship with the cause would produce
- **Causally disconnected emotion:** An emotional state with no traceable cause in prior prose
- **Instantaneous resolution:** A significant emotional wound (PIS-4+) resolving within the same scene without a complete Part 26 resolution pathway having been traversed
- **Emotional stasis:** A character who, after a CSS-7+ consequence event, shows no sustained emotional impact in subsequent scenes
- **Emotional uniformity:** A character who responds to all events with the same emotional register, regardless of what the events are and what the character's prior experience has established
