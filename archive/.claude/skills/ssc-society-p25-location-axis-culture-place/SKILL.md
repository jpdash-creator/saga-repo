## Area 4 — Location Axis (LA-C): Culture by Place

### 4.1 — Location as Cultural Bedrock

The physical environment within which a culture forms determines its foundational orientations before any other axis acts. Part 13's cosmological hierarchy from Village through The Primordium provides the location classification; this Part governs what those locations culturally produce.

### 4.3 — Sacred Ground Cultural Formation

Sacred Ground locations (Part 13 Area 14, S-Grade 1–8) generate the most distinct location-specific cultures in the cosmos. Communities that form on Sacred Grounds develop:

- **Custodian Culture (S-Grade 1–3):** The community defines its identity through stewardship of the sacred site. Cultural prestige flows from lineage of custodianship, not conquest or acquisition. Taboo: commerce on the sacred site perimeter. Ritual: seasonal site purification ceremony.

- **Pilgrimage Culture (S-Grade 3–5):** The community's economy and identity is organized around receiving pilgrims. Hospitality to the genuine pilgrim is a sacred obligation; exploitation of pilgrims is a capital cultural violation. Ritual: the Welcome of the Weary (formal pilgrim reception ceremony).

- **Revelation Culture (S-Grade 5–7):** The community expects that the Sacred Ground will produce spiritual revelation. Meditation, isolation retreat, and breakthrough are culturally valorized above all other achievement. Social prestige flows to those who achieve breakthroughs at the site. Taboo: celebrating technological achievement over cultivation breakthrough.

- **Primordial Culture (S-Grade 8):** The community develops an entirely distinct cultural paradigm around the absolute energy environment. Normal cultivation hierarchies are inverted — the community may value purity of relationship to the site over cultivation realm advancement. Extreme cases develop cultures incomprehensible to outside observers. Part 28 consultation required for narrative depiction of S-Grade 8 community cultures.

### 4.4 — Ruined World Culture (Archaeology Culture)

Civilizations that develop on Ruins Worlds (Part 13 Area 8) invariably develop archaeology-based cultural identities:

- Excavation as cultural practice: digging is a sacred act
- Ancient artifact possession as primary social prestige marker (overriding cultivation realm in some cases)
- Deep ambivalence about the civilization that created the ruins: ancestor veneration vs. cautionary narrative
- Professional excavator caste with distinctive cultural markers
- Taboo: destroying ruins for construction material (even when economically rational)
