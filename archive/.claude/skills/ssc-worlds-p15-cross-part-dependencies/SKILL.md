| Canon-Part 10 | Part 15 ← Part 10 | Part 10 (Technology Science Law, Area 5 — FTL Physics Framework) is the upstream scientific authority for all FTL-capable travel; the Minimum Physical Duration rule (Part 10, P10-FTL-1) constrains how fast FTL research can proceed even with maximum investment; Part 10 governs the scientific prerequisite that makes Part 15's FTL travel mechanics achievable; Part 15 governs what FTL achieves when those prerequisites are met |
| Canon-Part 12 | Part 15 ↔ Part 12 | Formation Teleportation Arrays (FTAs) are classified and governed as formation arrays under Part 12 (Area 7 — Formation Arrays as spatial architecture); Part 15 governs the travel mechanics of FTA transit including range, cost, throughput, and failure conditions (Part 15, Area 13.2); Part 12 governs the construction, tier classification, and energy sourcing of the array objects; both Parts are required to fully render a formation gate travel scene |
| Canon-Part 13 | Part 15 ← Part 13 | Part 13 (Location Law) defines the cosmological hierarchy (Village through The Primordium / Absolute Chaos) that Part 15's cross-scale travel governance is calibrated against; the CCT-scale boundaries (Part 13 Area 3) give Part 15 the canonical distance thresholds that define when interstellar, galactic, and cosmological travel becomes relevant; Part 13's Sacred Ground governance (Area 14) creates travel destinations with cultivation amplification effects that are a context for formation gate destinations |
| Canon-Part 14 | Part 15 ← Part 14 | Part 14 (Distance Law) provides all canonical distance values that Part 15's travel time calculations operate on; the CMS (Part 14, Area 2) is the distance axis that Part 15's speed tables operate against; Part 14 FTL governance rules (P14-FTL-1 through P14-FTL-4) are respected in Part 15's FTL mechanics — FTL changes transit time, not physical distance; Part 14 Area 3 canonical distance thresholds are the reference values for Part 15 transit time calculations |
| Canon-Part 21 | Part 15 → Part 21 | CCT tier (Part 21) determines what travel infrastructure a civilization can sustain — a CCT-5 civilization cannot maintain gate networks; CCT tier corresponds to the infrastructure tier in Part 15 Area 16.1; Part 21's Three-Tier Gap Rule (civilizations separated by 3+ CCT tiers interact asymmetrically) applies to travel — a CCT-11 civilization's galactic gate network versus a CCT-7 civilization's FTL transit fleet creates a tactical travel asymmetry with military and political consequences |
| Canon-Part 22 | Part 15 → Part 22 | Faction territorial governance (Part 22) depends on travel accessibility — factions control territory they can reliably supply and defend; transit network control is a major factional power asset (Part 15, Area 16.3); transit rights and gate access treaties are Part 22 political governance matters enabled by Part 15 infrastructure categories |
| Canon-Part 23 | Part 15 → Part 23 — Active | Travel capability is the prerequisite for fulfilling affiliation obligations across distance — a mutual defense treaty (Part 23 Area 4.3) that cannot be physically fulfilled because the ally lacks the travel technology to reach the threatened territory within operationally relevant timeframes is a paper alliance; Part 15 travel paradigm bands (Tiers 1–20) set the real threshold for which alliances are physically executable. Gate network access and control (Part 15 Area 16.3) is one of the primary strategic resources over which factions hold leverage affiliations — a faction controlling a key star gate (Part 15 Area 14) exercises transit dependency power over factions requiring access. FTL capability (Heaven Treading prerequisite at Core Solidification — Part 15 Area 14) is the threshold below which trans-stellar military alliances are not executable — affiliation obligations that require FTL response are void against a faction that has not reached Core Solidification cultivation or equivalent technology tier. Affiliation boundary enforcement (Part 23 Areas 5 and 13) across territory depends on Part 15 patrol and response range governance |
| Canon-Part 25 | Part 15 ↔ Part 25 — Bidirectional Active | Travel infrastructure is one of the most powerful determinants of cultural form and Part 15's five paradigm bands (Surface, Orbital/Inner-System, Interstellar, Galactic, Cosmological) each generate a canonical cultural profile governed by Part 25's Technology Axis (TA-C) in conjunction with the Location Axis (LA-C). A Surface-paradigm civilization (Part 15 Tiers 1–5) generates a locally rooted, culturally homogeneous or sharply divided culture where cultural exchange requires physical travel on foot or beast — trade route culture, merchant caravan culture, and travel-rite-of-passage culture are all Part 25 institutional forms of this paradigm. An Interstellar-paradigm civilization (Part 15 Tiers 8–10) has FTL access, creating instantaneous cultural connection across light-years — but this also creates the stargate-junction culture of transit hubs where all cultures collide, governed by Part 25 as a CPT-3 and CPT-5 hybrid attractor. Travel as a cultural rite of passage (Part 25 Area 15 — Pilgrimage Culture) is a Part 15-enabled cultural institution: the solo journey from one's home sect to the nearest major city is a Mortal → Xiantian rite in many CPT-1 sects; the multi-year voyage to a major Sacred Ground is a higher-realm proving rite; the civilization-crossing journey to meet a legendary figure is a Divine Sea aspirant's cultural necessity. The cultural gulf between a Surface-paradigm civilization and an Interstellar-paradigm civilization is, in Part 25 terms, larger than the technology gap: it represents the difference between cultures that define distance in days-of-travel and cultures that define distance in gate-hops; this is a fundamental value-architecture difference, not merely a technology difference. Gate-network culture (Part 15 Area 13) generates distinct hub-port cultural forms: gate-hub settlements develop as extreme cultural mixing zones with CPT-5 (Syncretic-Adaptive) cultural bias |
| Canon-Part 27 | Part 15 → Part 27 | Travel consequences: failed FTL transit, gate collapse during transit, depleted cultivation qi during space flight, and navigation error consequences carry Part 27 consequence law weight; a stranded starship crew, a cultivator depleted mid-void, or a civilization that loses its gate network are all Part 27 consequence events enabled by Part 15 mechanics |
| Canon-Part 28 | Part 15 → Part 28 | Multi-verse travel, Primordium navigation, and dimensional transit (Part 15, Area 18) are deferred scope requiring Part 28 (Creative Flexibility Law) authorization for any narrative appearance; no author may include multi-verse travel in the current story arc without Part 28 review |
| Canon-Part 31 | Part 15 ↔ Part 31 | Cultivation-Technology Synergy Travel (Part 15, Area 15) is jointly governed with Part 31 (Cultivation-Technology Synergy Law); Part 31 governs the mechanism of synergy between the two tracks; Part 15 governs what travel output that synergy produces; the Integration Model levels from Part 31 (Models A–D) map to the synergy travel categories in Part 15, Area 15.2; Part 31 Rule 2.1.1 (civilization tier gates accessible synergy) applies — the civilization must be at the appropriate CCT to deploy the corresponding synergy travel infrastructure |
| Canon-Part 32 | Part 15 ↔ Part 32 — JOINT AUTHORITY | Part 32 (Technology — Vehicle Law) and Part 15 share joint authority over all technology travel scenes: Part 32 governs the vehicle (its identity, classification, tier, scale class, configuration code, propulsion system, construction prerequisites, and combat status); Part 15 governs the travel mechanics (travel speed by tier, transit time, range, fuel cost, gate network access, FTL mechanics, and infrastructure law); both Parts must be consulted simultaneously for any scene involving a technology vehicle in motion. Part 15 Area 9 (Tier 1–5 Surface/Atmospheric Travel) and Areas 10–12 (Orbital through Cosmological Travel) define the travel speed and range outputs for which Part 32's vehicle tiers are the platform. Part 15's five Transport Paradigm Bands (Surface Tiers 1–5, Orbital/Inner-System Tiers 5–7, Interstellar Tiers 8–10, Galactic Tiers 11–14, Cosmological Tiers 15–20) directly correspond to Part 32's five Transport Paradigm Bands (ST-Band, OS-Band, IS-Band, GT-Band, CT-Band) — the bands are the same canonical structure viewed from two governance angles: Part 15 = travel output; Part 32 = vehicle platform. Part 32's CHV vehicle classes (CHV-A through CHV-E) are the vehicle expression of Part 15 Area 15 synergy travel categories (Categories 1–3); synergy travel narration requires Part 32 for the vehicle type description and Part 15 for the travel speed and cost. Infrastructure tiers I–IX (Part 15 Area 16) are the travel infrastructure network within which Part 32's vehicles operate; Part 32 Area 13 cross-references all nine tiers as the physical deployment context for each vehicle class |
| Canon-Part 33 | Part 15 informs Part 33 — Weapon delivery vehicle travel mechanics | Part 33 (Technology — Weapon Law) governs weapons deployed from or against moving vehicles; Part 15 governs the travel mechanics of those vehicles. Intercept weapons (weapons designed to destroy or deflect a moving vehicle), pursuit weapon systems (weapons deployed during vehicle chase sequences), and FTL-range weapons with delivery vehicle payloads all require simultaneous consultation of Part 15 (for vehicle/target velocity and transit mechanics) and Part 33 (for weapon WS class, damage vectors, and deployment authorization). Part 15 Area 14 (FTL Interdiction) governs whether FTL-speed weapons can be deployed or intercepted — this is the Part 15 side of what Part 33 classifies as IW-Band and GW-Band weapons with FTL delivery systems. Part 15's travel paradigm bands define the operational theaters within which Part 33's TW/OW/IW/GW/CW weapon paradigm bands operate |
| Canon-Part 17 | Part 15 informs Part 17 | Hero travel capability is a power dimension tracked in the Hero Ledger — a hero's travel paradigm (whether they are bound to Surface-paradigm travel or have achieved FTL personal transit via Heaven Treading at Core Solidification) is a Hero Ledger Section 3 capability parameter; achieving FTL personal travel capability (Heaven Treading prerequisite at Core Solidification — Part 15 Area 14) is a mandatory Hero Ledger update trigger because it represents a qualitative expansion of the hero's operational freedom and threat projection range; formation teleportation array access and personal formation gate knowledge are Hero Ledger Section 8 (Resources and Possessions) assets — they define which locations the hero can reach and in what timeframe; travel consequence events (failed FTL transit stranding the hero, gate collapse trapping the hero mid-transit, depletion in deep space) are simultaneously Part 15 mechanics, Part 27 CSS events, and Part 26 PIS events requiring Hero Ledger crisis entries; a hero crossing a travel paradigm threshold (e.g., first FTL transit, first cross-star-system gate transit) is a Hero Arc Status event logged in Hero Ledger Section 9; the travel time required for the hero to reach a combat or crisis location is a tactical constraint governed by Part 15 tables in combination with Part 14 distance values |
| Canon-Part 18 | Part 15 informs Part 18 | Villain travel capability and transportation infrastructure are power base elements tracked in Villain Ledger Section 3; a villain who controls gate networks (Part 15 Area 13 FTAs) effectively controls who can approach or escape their territory — this is simultaneously a Part 15 infrastructure asset and a Part 22 faction power asset; villain FTL interdiction (Part 15 Area 14) is a legitimate villain tactical capability that can trap heroes in villain territory; villain Heaven Treading FTL personal combat mobility (Core Solidification+ cultivation) is a Part 15-governed capability that the villain's combat confrontation scenes depend on; villain travel asset destruction (disrupting gate networks as a strategy against the villain) is a Part 15 / Part 27 consequence event chain; villain exile or containment defeat type (Part 18 Rule 18.1.3.3) specifically involves travel law — exile requires ensuring the villain cannot return, which is a Part 15 / Part 22 joint governance matter |
| Canon-Part 19 | Part 15 informs Part 19 | Side character travel capabilities and geographic accessibility directly shape their narrative function; mentor side characters who provide the hero with FTA access (gate keys, formation teleportation knowledge) are performing the Ally function (Part 19) with a Part 15 infrastructure dimension — this is one of the most practically transformative things an ally can do; rival side characters who have achieved FTL personal travel (Heaven Treading) while the hero has not creates a specific power gap and tactical disadvantage that is Part 15 / Part 19 joint governance; travel time constraints govern whether side characters can reach heroes in distress — the tragedy of a side character who dies before the hero can travel to their location is a Part 15 + Part 14 + Part 26 joint narrative event |
| Canon-Part 24 | Part 24 governs information security of travel intelligence and courier systems | ITC-3 Courier transmission channels (Part 24 Area 3.3) depend on Part 15's travel law for courier speed benchmarks — Cultivator Courier speed follows Part 15 cultivation travel tables (Core Solidification courier = ~0.33c; FTL-capable courier = spatial fold speed per Part 15 Area 14); Beast Carrier speed follows Part 15 Tier 1–5 paradigm speeds; Physical Package transport speed is the slowest ITC-3 subtype and follows civilian surface/space travel paradigm speeds. Cross-Gate Message Protocol (Part 24 Area 3.2 ITC-2 subtype CGMP) are ITC-2 transmissions that specifically route through Formation Teleportation Array networks (Part 15 Area 13) — gate network availability determines whether CGMP is accessible in a given region; gate destruction or control (a Part 15 infrastructure event) constitutes a communications interdiction event in Part 24 terms. The five Part 15 technology travel paradigm bands directly determine what ITC-4 technology communication infrastructure exists: a Surface-paradigm civilization (Tiers 1–5) has no faster-than-courier technology communication at interstellar scale; a civilization with star gate networks (Tier 10) has Cross-Gate Message Protocol available as an ITC-2 channel. FTA network carrier capacity (Part 15 Area 13.3 throughput table) is the limiting factor for intelligence distribution when using Part 24's Cross-Gate Message Protocol for wide dissemination campaigns |
| Canon-Part 29 | Part 15 → Part 29 | Travel context shapes dialogue register — long-distance travel parties develop speech intimacy (F4); vehicle/vessel crew hierarchies produce authority speech patterns per Part 29 Area 5; travel danger triggers combat/high-stakes dialogue per Part 29 Area 7. |
