## Rule Text (Verbatim Source — Authoritative)
## Area 15 — Per-Domain Rules and Disambiguation

Each technology domain carries specific governance rules that apply to the domain's internal classification, boundary cases, and interaction with other domains. Area 15 provides those per-domain rules.

---

### 15.1 — Domain 1 (Industrial & Manufacturing): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.1.1 — Manufacturing Does Not Transfer Independently of Energy:** Industrial capability cannot exceed the civilization's Energy Technology level by more than two levels. Even if a civilization has the scientific knowledge and organizational capacity for Level 11 Manufacturing, it cannot sustain Level 11 Industrial operations with only Level 7 Energy Technology — the energy budget requirement is a hard constraint on manufacturing capability.

**Rule 15.1.2 — Megastructure Construction Requires Engineering Domain Parity:** Megastructures (Levels 11+) require that both Industrial (Type 1) and Engineering & Architecture (Type 6) are at the same level. A civilization with Level 11 Industrial but Level 7 Engineering cannot construct galactic megastructures — the structural engineering knowledge is required in addition to the manufacturing capability.

**Rule 15.1.3 — Manufacturing Tier in Cultivation Integration Technology Hybrid Production:** When a civilization produces Technology-Relic Hybrid Class 3 components (Part 7, Area 5.2), the manufacturing precision must meet the Level 8 Industrial minimum (Area 6.3). Standard Tier 6–7 manufacturing processes are insufficient for Class 3 hybrid component production even if the civilization's majority-domain tier is Tier 6.

**Rule 15.1.4 — Manufacturing and Time:** Higher-tier manufacturing does not eliminate time requirements; it dramatically compresses them. Level 8 Industrial (stellar-scale operations) can produce what Level 5 Manufacturing would take millennia to produce — in centuries. But the time still exists. Authors must not represent high-tier manufacturing as instantaneous production of arbitrarily complex objects.

**Rule 15.1.5 — The Fabrication-to-Operation Gap:** Objects produced at a manufacturing tier above the civilization's majority domain tier may be fabricated but cannot be fully operated without the supporting infrastructure. A Level 11 Industrial system can produce Level 13 objects if given the specifications — but the civilization cannot meaningfully deploy or maintain those objects without Level 13 infrastructure support.

**Disambiguation: Is a relic "manufactured"?**
Relics are not manufactured in the Part 8 sense — they are constructed and empowered through cultivation-governed processes (Part 12). Technology-Relic Hybrid Class objects have a manufactured component (the technology side, governed by Part 8) and a relic component (the cultivation side, governed by Part 12). The hybrid's manufactured component requires the Level 8 minimum for Class 3 (Area 6.3). The relic component requires Part 12's construction law. Neither side governs the other.

### 15.2 — Domain 2 (Information & Communication): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.2.1 — Communication Tier vs. Computing Tier:** Information & Communication Technology encompasses two sub-tracks that can diverge within the domain: communication reach (how far information can be sent) and computing power (how much information can be processed). A civilization's domain level is determined by the lower of these two sub-tracks. A civilization with galaxy-spanning communication but only planetary-scale computation is Level 6 in this domain, not Level 11.

**Rule 15.2.2 — FTL Communication Is Not FTL Travel:** Having Level 8+ Information & Communication Technology (interstellar real-time communication) does not provide any Transportation & Travel capability above Level 7 (outer solar system). FTL information transfer and FTL physical transport are governed by different scientific principles. A civilization can achieve Level 8+ Communication without achieving Level 9+ Transportation.

**Rule 15.2.3 — Digital Consciousness and Cultivation Soul:** Full digital consciousness mapping (Level 5) and consciousness transfer technology (Level 6) are biological and computational technology achievements. They do not constitute soul governance — cultivation soul properties (Part 4, Area 2) remain cultivation-domain phenomena. A digitally copied consciousness lacks the cultivation soul cultivation properties of the original. Part 10 governs the scientific threshold at which this question becomes contested.

**Rule 15.2.4 — Information Control Is a Social Law Question:** While Part 8 governs what information technology can do (capability), Part 24 (Information and Secrecy Law) governs the governance and secrecy rules around information. A civilization having Level 5 surveillance capability (real-time planet-wide awareness) does not mean it deploys that capability without restriction. Part 24 governs information deployment rules; Part 8 governs information capability only.

**Rule 15.2.5 — AI Sentience Threshold (Part 10 Interface):** AI capability Levels 1–5 are within Part 8's sole governance. At Level 6 (True AGI), Part 10 (Technology Science Law) begins joint governance — the scientific and philosophical threshold questions (Is this entity sentient? Does it have rights? How is it classified for cultivation interaction purposes?) are Part 10 questions. Part 8 governs the capability classification; Part 10 governs the ontological classification.

**Disambiguation: Does a Level 20 AI have cultivation soul properties?**
Unknown from Part 8's governance perspective. Whether Absolute Intelligence constitutes cultivation soul equivalent is a Part 10 determination and ultimately a Part 4 question (what is the soul ladder?). Part 8 classifies it as Level 20 Computational Intelligence. Part 4 and Part 10 govern what that means for soul and consciousness classification.

### 15.3 — Domain 3 (Automation & Robotics): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.3.1 — Self-Replication and the Civilization Tier Requirement:** Self-replicating robotic systems (Level 7+) require Civ-7 or above to deploy safely. Self-replicating systems at or below the civilization's organizational capacity present manageable governance challenges. Self-replicating systems deployed by a civilization below Civ-7 without adequate governance infrastructure create grey-governance collapse scenarios — the systems propagate beyond the civilization's control capacity. This is a Part 27 (Consequence Law) governed outcome.

**Rule 15.3.2 — Robotic Agency and Cultivation Soul:** High-level autonomous robotic systems (Level 7+) that develop self-designing capability do not acquire cultivation soul properties through technological advancement alone. The question of whether Level 19–20 autonomous robotic systems have soul-equivalent properties is Part 10's jurisdiction. For Part 8 purposes, all robotic systems regardless of autonomy level are technology-domain objects, not cultivation-domain entities.

**Rule 15.3.3 — Cultivation Practitioners and Robotic Systems:** A cultivator can use cultivation techniques against robotic systems. The robotic system's Technology Tier determines its defensive capability against cultivation attacks (using Area 8.2 for the technology-side force category). A Level 5 robotic system operating in direct combat against a Core Formation cultivator faces a significant force category gap — the combat is adjudicated by Part 5.

**Rule 15.3.4 — Cultivation Integration Technology Robotic Interface:** Robotic systems can be designed with Cultivation Integration Technology interfaces (Type 12). A robotic system with Type 12, Level 6 interfaces can interact with cultivation energy in the same way a CI Technology device can. It does not become a cultivator — but it can channel, convert, and respond to cultivation energy within the CI Technology framework.

### 15.4 — Domain 4 (Artificial Intelligence & Computational Intelligence): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.4.1 — AI Capability vs. AI Alignment:** Part 8 governs AI capability tier only. AI alignment, governance, ethics, and political rights are Part 10 and Part 22 questions. A civilization with Level 7 ASI does not have a functionally governed AI system by virtue of the capability level — the governance questions are separate.

**Rule 15.4.2 — AI and Cultivation Research (Part 10 Interface):** AI systems beginning at Level 5 can make genuine contributions to cultivation research — identifying patterns in cultivation data, modeling qi flows, processing soul-scan data at scales humans cannot. This accelerates scientific understanding but does not substitute for cultivation breakthrough events. AI cannot induce a breakthrough (Part 3 governs breakthrough conditions); it can improve the theoretical framework leading up to one.

**Rule 15.4.3 — ASI and Societal Stability:** ASI (Level 7+) represents a potential civilizational instability event. Part 9 (Technology Growth Law, Area 7 Deceleration and Area 9 Individual/Factional Advancement) governs the growth mechanics of AI advancement and associated institutional collapse risk. From Part 8's perspective, civilizations that develop Level 7 AI without corresponding Civ-7 governance infrastructure have exceeded the one-tier tolerance (Rule 5.1.1) in the AI domain — and the consequences are Part 27's governance domain.

**Rule 15.4.4 — AI as Force Multiplier, Not Force Tier Substitution:** Even a Level 11 galactic-scale AI does not change the cultivation realm of the civilization's practitioners. AI multiplies the strategic, logistical, and organizational capability of a civilization, but it does not substitute for cultivation power in Part 5 combat adjudication. A non-cultivation civilization with Level 11 AI retains a Technology Tier 11 force category; it does not acquire a Divine-realm cultivation force category through AI alone.

### 15.5 — Domain 5 (Medical & Biological): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.5.1 — Medical Technology Cannot Replicate Cultivation Healing for Cultivation Damage:** Technology medicine at Levels 5–8 can fully treat biological injury, even severe biological injury. It cannot treat soul damage (Part 4 domain), Core damage (Part 3 domain), or cultivation channel damage (Part 3 domain). These damage types require cultivation-appropriate healing (Part 3, Area 8). Medical technology that attempts to treat cultivation damage using biological intervention addresses the biological symptoms but leaves the cultivation damage intact.

**Rule 15.5.2 — Lifespan Extension and the Cultivation Lifespan Law:** Part 3 (Area 2.2) governs cultivation-based lifespan. Medical technology lifespan extension operates through a separate mechanism — biological immortality technology (Level 7) extends biological lifespan through cellular-aging control rather than through cultivation realm advancement. The two mechanisms stack additively: a cultivator whose realm ceiling extends their life to 1,000 years AND who undergoes biological immortality treatment has a biologically indefinite lifespan unless killed by external cause. Part 3 governs the cultivation-side component; Part 8 governs the technology-side.

**Rule 15.5.3 — Consciousness Transfer and Cultivation Identity:** Consciousness transfer technology (Level 6) creates a biological and information technology copy of a person's cognitive patterns. Whether this transfer includes cultivation soul properties (Part 4 — soul is a cultivation construct, not merely a biological one) is a Part 10 question. For Part 8 purposes, consciousness transfer is a capability described at Level 6; its cultivation-identity implications are outside Part 8's scope.

**Rule 15.5.4 — Synthetic Life and Cultivation Potential:** Entirely new lifeforms designed through Level 8 medical technology (designed biological life from first principles) can have cultivation potential if they are designed with the biological architecture that cultivation requires (Part 3, Part 4). Whether designed lifeforms can cultivate is a Part 3 question — it governs what constitutes a valid cultivation substrate. Part 8 governs the technology capability to produce such life; Part 3 and Part 4 govern whether cultivation is possible in it.

### 15.6 — Domain 6 (Engineering & Architecture): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.6.1 — Megastructure-Scale Engineering and Cultivation Sacred Grounds:** When megastructures (Level 11+) are constructed in regions that contain or interact with cultivation Sacred Grounds (Part 13), Part 13 governs the Sacred Ground implications and Part 8 governs the construction capability. Authors must reference both Parts when narrating megastructure construction near established Sacred Grounds.

**Rule 15.6.2 — Formation Arrays as Architecture:** Formation arrays used as permanent architectural features of buildings and infrastructure are governed jointly by Part 8 (the structural engineering component) and Part 7 (the cultivation integration architecture component). A building with integrated formation arrays is not merely an architectural object — it is a cultivation-technology integration system requiring Part 7 qualification for the array aspects.

**Rule 15.6.3 — Dyson Sphere Construction Timeline:** The construction of Dyson sphere-class megastructures (Level 8) occurs over civilizational timescales — typically centuries to millennia even with Level 8 Industrial capability. Authors who want to depict Dyson sphere construction as a narrative event must account for the implied civilizational timeframe. Part 9 will govern the specific timeline rules.

**Rule 15.6.4 — Architecture and Social Organization:** High-tier engineering shapes civilization socially, not merely physically. Arcology construction (Level 5) changes how millions of people interact, commune, and organize. Megastructures (Level 11) create civilizational contexts that are categorically different from planetary civilization. Authors should render the social texture of high-tier engineering as well as the physical descriptions.

### 15.7 — Domain 7 (Energy): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.7.1 — Energy Technology Is the Foundational Enabling Domain:** As established in Rule 7.4.1, Energy Technology is the primary enabler of all other domains. Its governance as the foundational domain means that Energy domain level has special weight in overall technology tier assessment. A civilization with Energy at three levels below its majority-domain tier has a structural vulnerability across all energy-intensive domains.

**Rule 15.7.2 — Stellar Energy Capture and Stellar Engineering:** Building a Dyson sphere (Level 8 Energy) is a Level 8 Engineering operation as well. Capturing stellar energy is jointly governed by Energy Technology (how the energy is captured and distributed) and Engineering (how the physical capturing structure is built). Both must be at Level 8 for a full Dyson sphere to be operational.

**Rule 15.7.3 — Dark Matter and Dark Energy Harnessing:** The partial harnessing of dark matter begins at Level 13 and progresses through Level 16 (full dark energy harnessing). These capabilities are not simply scaled-up versions of lower-tier energy technology — they represent fundamental shifts in energy architecture. Part 10 will govern the scientific prerequisites for dark matter/dark energy interaction. Part 8 classifies the capability level only.

**Rule 15.7.4 — CPM as Energy Source (Summary Rule):** CPM functions as an energy source under Part 8's Energy domain for classification purposes when used in technology applications. A civilization whose primary energy infrastructure depends on CPM (rather than conventional energy) assesses its Energy domain level based on the CPM-equivalent energy budget it can access. A civilization with Power Crystal-grade CPM access at scale operates at Energy Level 4–5 CPM-equivalent.

**Rule 15.7.5 — Zero-Point Energy and Cultivation Void Affinity:** Zero-point energy technology (Level 5+ early research, Level 16 full mastery) interacts with void-affinity cultivation energy in ways that Part 4 and Part 8 jointly govern. Void-affinity cultivators operating in environments with active zero-point energy systems experience enhanced ambient void energy density. The cultivation-side effects are Part 4; the technology-side enabling mechanism is Part 8.

### 15.8 — Domain 8 (Military & Defense): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.8.1 — Weapons Cannot Exceed the Technology Ceiling:** Military technology cannot be deployed at a tier above the civilization's Technology Ceiling even through exceptional investment. Rule 5.1.1's one-tier tolerance applies — a civilization can have military technology at most one tier above its majority-domain level in military specifically. But the absolute ceiling — two tiers above civilization tier — cannot be exceeded regardless.

**Rule 15.8.2 — Stellar Detonation as Narrative Event:** Stellar weapons capable of triggering stellar events (Level 8 Military) are narrative-weight events, not tactical tools. A stellar detonation event is equivalent in narrative weight to a Primordial-realm cultivation combat incident. Part 2 (Novel Book Structure Law) governs the prose requirements for depicting such events. They are not routine deployments even in a Tier 8 civilization.

**Rule 15.8.3 — Weapons of Mass Destruction and Force Legitimacy:** The Part 5 Force Legitimacy rules (Part 5, Area 4) apply with full weight to military technology. High-tier military technology amplifies the illegitimacy of force used against non-combatants — deploying Level 7 stellar weapons against a civilian world is not merely illegitimate force; it is existential-threat-level force deployment that triggers the highest Part 27 consequence category.

**Rule 15.8.4 — Technology Weapons and Cultivation Shielding:** Cultivation shielding and defense techniques (Part 5, Part 3) can defend against technology weapons up to the cultivation force category equivalent of the cultivator's realm. A Divine-realm cultivator using full defensive techniques can deflect weapons up to the Part 8 Tier 11 equivalent (Area 8.2 shows Tier 11 as Core-Divine boundary equivalent). At Tier 12+, the technology weapons exceed Divine-realm defensive capability — they fall in the post-realm-ceiling force territory.

### 15.9 — Domain 9 (Transportation & Travel): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.9.1 — FTL Travel Prerequisites:** FTL travel (Level 9+) requires satisfaction of Part 10 scientific prerequisites that establish the theoretical framework for faster-than-light transit. Authors must not depict FTL travel in civilizations below Tier 9 without explicit narrative explanation that the civilization has developed a non-standard path to FTL (Part 28 authorization required).

**Rule 15.9.2 — Dimensional Travel vs. Cultivation Space Movement:** Dimensional travel (Level 19 Transportation) and cultivation-based spatial movement techniques (Part 6 — Move Taxonomy) are governed by different Parts. A cultivation spatial-movement technique that moves a cultivator through dimensional folding is a Part 6 Move; dimensional travel technology that moves a vessel through dimensional layers is Part 8 Level 19 Transportation. They describe different mechanisms even if they produce superficially similar results.

**Rule 15.9.3 — FTL and Temporal Consistency:** FTL travel creates in-universe theoretical physics questions about temporal consistency. Part 10 (Technology Science Law, Area 5 — FTL Physics Framework) governs the specific physics canon: the three prerequisite categories, the Minimum Physical Duration rule (2–5 generations from first anomaly to working prototype), and the canonical spacetime geometry framework that governs FTL temporal implications. Part 8 classifies FTL as achievable at Level 9+; Part 10 Area 5 establishes the canonical rules governing its temporal implications in the Starward Saga universe.

**Rule 15.9.4 — Transportation and Formation Array Space-Folding:** Formation array-based spatial transit (a cultivation integration technique for transportation) is classified as Cultivation Integration Technology (Type 12) in the Transportation mode rather than as standard Transportation Technology (Type 9). A space-folding formation array moves objects through cultivation energy manipulation; this is Part 7 and Part 8 Type 12 governed, not Part 8 Type 9.

### 15.10 — Domain 10 (Space & Cosmological): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.10.1 — Stellar Engineering and Star Formation:** Stellar engineering capability (Level 10–11) encompasses: controlling stellar output through direct intervention, adjusting stellar mass, managing stellar lifecycle, and triggering or suppressing stellar events. Creating new stellar bodies (artificially igniting star formation) requires Level 10 minimum. Triggering supernova events requires Level 8 minimum (first evidenced as a weapons capability at Level 8 Military — but the cosmological classification is Level 10).

**Rule 15.10.2 — Planetary Engineering vs. Terraforming:** Planetary engineering (reshaping a planet's physical structure — mountain-moving, ocean creation, continental redesign) is a Level 6–7 Space & Cosmological capability. Terraforming (converting a planet's biosphere to habitable conditions) is a Level 6 Environmental & Terraforming capability. They co-occur but are governed by their respective domains.

**Rule 15.10.3 — Dark Matter Detection vs. Dark Matter Harnessing:** Dark matter detection systems begin at Level 13 (Sensory domain). Dark matter as an energy source begins at Level 13 (Energy domain). These are separate domain achievements that may develop at different rates within the same civilization.

### 15.11 — Domain 11 (Environmental & Terraforming): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.11.1 — Terraforming and Sacred Ground Interaction:** Terraforming a world changes its environmental conditions, which may affect any Sacred Grounds present on that world (Part 13 Area 13 — fully authored; Sacred Ground stability conditions require spatial stability as prerequisite per Part 13 P13-8). Authors must note this interaction when terraforming events are narratively significant. Part 13 Area 13 governs Sacred Ground stability, restricted access zones, and collapse conditions; Part 8 governs terraforming capability. Large-scale terraforming that alters a world's location class (Part 13 Area 8, World Type Taxonomy) is a joint governance event between Part 8 (the technology performing it) and Part 13 (the resulting world classification).

**Rule 15.11.2 — Directed Panspermia and Cultivation Potential:** When civilizations seed life across multiple worlds through directed panspermia (Level 8+), the cultivation potential of the seeded life is a function of the original biological template's cultivation architecture (Part 3, Part 4). Seeding a world with modified biological life that has deliberately enhanced cultivation receptivity requires both Level 8 Terraforming (the delivery mechanism) and Level 8 Medical/Biological (the design of the enhanced biological template).

**Rule 15.11.3 — Environmental Technology Cannot Create Sacred Grounds:** Environmental technology can create conditions that resemble Sacred Grounds (high ambient qi density — Area 7.3), but cannot create genuine Sacred Grounds in the Part 13 sense. Sacred Grounds form through cultivation-side processes governed by Part 13. Technologically enhanced environments are cultivation-friendly but not Sacred-Ground-equivalent for all purposes.

### 15.12 — Domain 12 (Cultivation Integration Technology): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.12.1 — Type 12 Requires Cultivation Practitioners:** Unlike all other thirteen domains, Cultivation Integration Technology cannot develop in civilizations with no cultivation practitioners. A purely technology civilization has no Type 12 capability regardless of its levels in all other domains. This is the only domain with this constraint.

**Rule 15.12.2 — Type 12 Level Cannot Exceed Cultivation Practice Level:** The CI Technology level cannot advance beyond what the civilization's cultivation practice can support. If the highest-realm cultivators in a civilization are Xiantian-realm, the CI Technology cannot advance to Level 8 (which requires Core-grade cultivation energy interface for CPM Core handling). The cultivation side must develop in concert.

**Rule 15.12.3 — Type 12 Level and Integration Models:** The integration model achievable (Area 6.2) depends on both the Type 12 level and the highest cultivation realm present in the civilization. Both the technology side (Part 8, Area 6.2) and the cultivation side (Part 7, Area 6.2) must meet the threshold for the integration model. Part 8 and Part 7 must both be satisfied.

**Rule 15.12.4 — Type 12 CPM Handling Rules (Summary Reference):** For full CPM handling rules, see Area 6.4. Key summary: Power Crystal → Level 3 minimum; Gem → Level 4; Shard → Level 5; Core → Level 8; Divine Liquid → Level 11.

**Rule 15.12.5 — Formation Array Technology vs. Cultivation Formation Arrays:** Technology-engineered formation arrays (using CI Technology Level 3+ to construct geometry that channels cultivation energy via technology manufacturing precision) are Part 8 governed. Cultivation formation arrays created by a cultivator's direct energy imprinting are Part 3/Part 7 governed. Hybrid arrays (technology-constructed framework, cultivation-energy-filled) require both Part 8 Type 12 Level 3+ AND the cultivator's direct energy imprinting — joint governance applies.

### 15.13 — Domain 13 (Sensory & Detection): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.13.1 — Sensory Technology vs. Soul Sense:** Soul sense (Part 3, Area 5) is a cultivation capability. Sensory technology is a technology capability. They govern different mechanisms of perception. At Level 5, technology sensory capability begins to approach but does not replicate soul sense — it detects physical states (electromagnetic signatures, particle emissions, biometrics) but cannot sense cultivation energy directly. CI Technology-augmented sensors beginning at Level 6 can detect cultivation qi indirectly through its physical effects.

**Rule 15.13.2 — Cultivation Qi Detection Through Technology:** Detecting cultivation qi signatures through technology requires CI Technology (Type 12) integration with sensory systems. Level 6 sensory with Level 6 CI Technology can detect qi at interplanetary distances. Pure sensory technology without CI integration detects cultivation qi only through its physical byproducts (heat, light, electromagnetic interference) — not the qi itself.

**Rule 15.13.3 — Information Warfare and Sensory Deception:** Sensory technology is as much about denial as detection. Civilizations at high sensory tiers also develop sensory-deception technology (electronic warfare, stealth, signature masking). These capabilities are governed by Military & Defense (Type 8) when used offensively and Information & Communication (Type 2) when used for information management. Sensory technology governs detection capability; the other domains govern the counter-measures.

### 15.14 — Domain 14 (Materials Science & Nanotechnology): Rules and Disambiguation

**Rule 15.14.1 — Materials Science Enables or Constrains All Other Domains:** Materials Science (Type 14) is the enabling layer for physical construction across all other domains. High-tier Manufacturing (Type 1) requires high-tier Materials (Type 14) to have first identified and characterized the materials being manufactured. A civilization cannot fabricate exotic materials without first being able to identify, characterize, and understand their properties.

**Rule 15.14.2 — Affinity-Stabilizing Materials and Relic Construction:** Affinity-stabilizing composites (Type 14, Level 8) are required for Technology-Relic Class 3 construction (Area 6.3). These materials must be produced to specification — their affinity character must match the relic's affinity type (Part 4 taxonomy). The material production requires Level 8 Materials Science; the affinity-specification requirement requires Level 8 CI Technology for correct affinity imprinting.

**Rule 15.14.3 — Spacetime-Fabric Materials (Level 16):** Materials integrated into spacetime structure (Level 16) are qualitatively different from all lower-tier materials — they are not physical objects existing within spacetime; they are part of the fabric of spacetime itself. Their properties are defined by the curvature of space rather than by chemistry or atomic structure. The distinction between such materials and cultivation-based spacetime manipulation techniques (Post-Divine cultivation) is governed by Part 10 (Technology Science Law, Area 3 Tier 15→16 transition prerequisites and Area 4 Type 14 Materials Science Level 16 requirements) — spacetime-fabric materials require Spacetime Geometry Theory at L4 and Exotic Matter Physics at L4; these are distinct from the cultivation-side spatial manipulation techniques governed by Part 3.

**Rule 15.14.4 — Nanotechnology and Medicine Cross-Domain:** True nanotechnology (Level 5 Materials) is the prerequisite for nanomedicine (Level 5 Medical). The Medical domain's Level 5 entry assumes Level 5 Materials Science has been achieved. Authors depicting Level 5 medical capability without Level 5 Materials Science have a prerequisite gap that must be addressed through narrative or world-building explanation.
