## Area 4 — The Advancement Prerequisite System

### 4.1 — Three Categories of Advancement Prerequisites

Every tier transition in every technology domain requires satisfying prerequisites in three categories:

**Category I — Scientific Knowledge Prerequisites:**
The theoretical understanding required to design, build, and explain technology at the target tier. Governed jointly by Part 9 (the advancement mechanism) and Part 10 (the specific scientific knowledge content). Part 10 is fully authored (Areas 1–16); Part 10 Area 3 provides the Tier-by-Tier Scientific Prerequisites table for all 20 tier transitions and Part 10 Area 4 provides Domain-Specific Science Tables for all 14 domains. Category I prerequisites are the most domain-specific: each of the fourteen domains has its own scientific knowledge prerequisites for each tier transition. The Four-Layer Knowledge Stack (L1–L4) defined in Part 10 Area 2 is the framework for classifying what level of scientific understanding is required.

**Category II — Resource Prerequisites:**
The material, energy, and infrastructure inputs required to attempt and sustain advancement. Resource prerequisites are denominated in the existing tier's resource frameworks — a Tier 5 civilization attempting Tier 6 advancement needs Tier 5-accessible resources at a scale and quality that can support Tier 6 experiments.

**Category III — Institutional Prerequisites:**
The organizational and population-scale capacity required to execute the transition. Institutional prerequisites include: minimum practitioner populations in the relevant domains, minimum institutional stability duration (the civilization must have been at its current tier for long enough to build institutional memory — measured in the Standard Advancement Timeline for that tier), governance structures that can protect research programs from short-term political disruption, and knowledge recording and transmission systems sufficient to preserve advancement gains.

### 4.1.1 — Prototype-State Technology Governance

When a civilization is actively researching the next tier but has not yet achieved stable deployment, it exists in a **Prototype State** — it has working examples of higher-tier technology but cannot yet produce, maintain, or deploy them at civilizational scale.

**Prototype-State Rules:**
- **Prototype does not equal tier advancement:** A civilization with one functional FTL research vessel is still a Tier 8 civilization — it has a Tier 9 prototype. Tier advancement requires capability deployment at sustainable civilizational scale (minimum 10% of relevant operations using the new tier's capabilities).
- **Prototype existence accelerates timeline:** A working prototype reduces the Standard Advancement Timeline by 20–40% for that transition, because the most difficult prerequisite (proving the concept works) is satisfied. The remaining barrier is scaling and sustainability, not discovery.
- **Prototype degradation risk:** A prototype built above the civilization's current Technology Ceiling (Part 8, P8-2) is subject to degradation. If the advancement fails or stalls, the prototype cannot be maintained indefinitely — degradation follows Part 8, Area 8.2 timelines.
- **Prototype as narrative catalyst:** The existence of a functional prototype of the next tier's technology is among the highest-stakes information objects in the civilization. Rivals who know a prototype exists have intelligence about imminent tier advancement — this is a Tier I Strategic Position Secret under Part 24 Area 4.1 Category B.
- **FTL Prototype case (Tier 8 → Tier 9):** Late Tier 8 FTL research vessels are the canonical example of prototype-state technology. They exist, they function, but they are not civilization-scale FTL infrastructure. The civilization is still Tier 8. Standard FTL travel does not begin until Tier 9 is achieved at the Space/Cosmological domain. (See Part 8 Area 3.9 governance note; Part 15 Section 11.1.)

### 4.3 — Domain-Specific Advancement Notes

Not all fourteen technology domains have the same advancement difficulty at each tier transition. The following notes describe the characteristic advancement difficulty profile of each domain:

**Type 1 — Industrial & Manufacturing:**
Industrial advancement is primarily resource and infrastructure-gated. Scientific prerequisites are relatively lower than for Energy or AI domains — the primary barriers are access to raw materials, sufficient energy to power industrial processes, and a large enough manufacturing workforce. Industrial advancement tends to follow Energy advancement with a lag of 0–2 tiers. Civilizations that develop Energy fast and Industrial slowly produce energy they cannot efficiently deploy; civilizations that develop Industrial fast and Energy slowly produce manufacturing capacity that is energy-starved.

**Type 2 — Information & Communication:**
Information advancement is primarily institutional and practitioner-gated at low tiers (recording and transmission require organizational investment). At higher tiers (Level 5+), Information advancement becomes scientifically gated: quantum computing, neural interfaces, and consciousness mapping require specific scientific breakthroughs. Information advancement is the domain where accidental discovery (Mechanism 2) most frequently produces disproportionate leaps, because anomalous information behaviors — unexpected transmission phenomena, unexplained computational outcomes — are among the most observable and documentable anomalies.

**Type 3 — Automation & Robotics:**
Automation advancement strongly depends on AI advancement (Type 4) at mid-to-high tiers. Low-tier automation (Levels 1–4) is mechanically driven and relatively straightforward. High-tier automation (Levels 5+) requires AI at or near the same level. Automation also depends heavily on Manufacturing (Type 1) for the physical components of robotic systems. Three-domain dependency (AI + Manufacturing + Energy) makes Automation one of the harder domains to advance quickly.

**Type 4 — Artificial Intelligence:**
AI advancement is the most scientifically gated domain at high tiers. The jump from narrow AI (Level 3–4) to broad AGI (Level 5–6) is one of the most difficult tier transitions in the Information-AI cluster because it requires scientific breakthroughs in understanding cognition, consciousness, and emergent intelligence that are not producible on a reliable timeline. After AGI is achieved, AI advancement follows more rapidly because AI systems contribute to their own advancement research. AI advancement in cultivation-technology civilizations can be assisted by soul sense research (Part 31, Area 3.3) providing empirical models of consciousness that inform AI architecture design.

**Type 5 — Medical & Biological:**
Medical advancement depends on Materials (Type 14), AI (Type 4), and Energy (Type 7) at high tiers. Low-tier medical advancement (Levels 1–3) is primarily experiential — empirical accumulation of what works. Mid-tier advancement (Levels 4–7) becomes scientifically driven as molecular biology and biochemistry enable designed medical interventions. High-tier advancement (Levels 8+) requires nano-fabrication, AI, and energy systems. In cultivation-technology civilizations, medical advancement is strongly assisted by cultivation-assisted discovery (Mechanism 3) — cultivators' biological self-awareness and qi manipulation produce medical insights that are difficult to derive from external observation alone.

**Type 6 — Engineering & Architecture:**
Engineering advancement is primarily Materials (Type 14) and Energy (Type 7) gated. Engineering is one of the most steadily advancing domains — it benefits strongly from repetition (every structure built is a data point) and rarely experiences the paradigm-gap-requiring-scientific-breakthrough dynamic that afflicts AI and Energy. The major advancement constraint at high tiers is the scale problem: engineering at stellar system scale (Level 7+) requires not just better materials and energy, but entirely new design philosophies that approach structures as dynamic systems rather than static objects.

**Type 7 — Energy Technology:**
Energy is the foundational domain. It has the most significant scientific prerequisites of any domain (the physics of energy extraction, conversion, and transmission at each new tier require genuine scientific understanding). Energy advancement unlocks advancement in almost every other domain. Energy is also the domain most frequently catalyzed by accidental discovery (Mechanism 2) — unexpected energy phenomena (new physics behaviors at extreme conditions) are the seeds of most major Energy tier transitions. Energy advancement is also the domain most strongly affected by CPM integration (Part 31, Area 3.1) in cultivation-technology civilizations: CPM as an energy source provides a high-value energy input that can effectively advance the functional Energy domain level by 1–2 tiers in CI-capable civilizations.

**Type 8 — Military & Defense:**
Military advancement is Energy (Type 7) and Materials (Type 14) gated at all tiers, with additional Information (Type 2) and AI (Type 4) dependency at mid-to-high tiers. Military advancement often advances faster than other domains in civilizations facing existential threats — the acceleration mechanism of Crisis-Driven Innovation (Area 6.4) most commonly activates for Military technology. Military advancement is also the domain most prone to intentional suppression: civilizations concerned about arms races may deliberately restrict military technology sharing, creating a strategic asymmetry where the overall technology tier of civilization is higher than its visible military technology would suggest.

**Type 9 — Transportation & Travel:**
Transportation advancement is Energy (Type 7) and Engineering (Type 6) gated. The most significant transit points are: the development of powered flight (Tier 3–4), space launch capability (Tier 4–5), and FTL capability (Tier 8–9). The FTL transition is the highest-prerequisite Transportation advancement and requires a scientific breakthrough in physics (Part 10) that cannot be rushed by resource investment alone. All other Transportation advancements are primarily resource and engineering gated.

**Type 10 — Space & Cosmological:**
Space advancement is Transportation (Type 9) and Energy (Type 7) gated. It is also the domain that most depends on direct observational access — knowledge about space accumulates through actually going there. Cultivation-assisted discovery (Mechanism 3) is particularly valuable in Space advancement because high-realm cultivators can operate in space environments that technology cannot yet reach safely, providing observational data about conditions at higher tiers than the civilization's current level.

**Type 11 — Environmental & Terraforming:**
Environmental advancement is Space (Type 10), Energy (Type 7), and Engineering (Type 6) gated. It is also the domain with the longest feedback timescales: terraforming operations at high tiers run on geological timescales, meaning advancement data accumulates slowly. Civilizations advancing in Environmental technology must accept that they will be operating on multi-generational investment horizons with limited short-term feedback. This makes Environmental technology advancement the most institutionally demanding — it requires the longest institutional continuity of any domain.

**Type 12 — Cultivation Integration Technology:**
CI Technology is unique among the fourteen domains: it requires cultivation practitioners as an operational prerequisite. Advancement in CI Technology depends on both technology-side prerequisites (Materials, Energy, Engineering) and cultivation-side prerequisites (the availability of cultivators at specific realms to interface with the technology being developed). CI Technology advancement is also strongly gated by the Cultivation-Technology Synergy dynamics in Part 31: civilizations that invest in synergy infrastructure advance CI Technology faster than civilizations that treat cultivation and technology as parallel-but-separate tracks. Accidental discovery (Mechanism 2) in CI Technology most commonly occurs at the boundary of cultivation-technology interaction — cultivators working with technology systems or technologists working with cultivation artifacts notice unexpected interaction behaviors that seed new CI Technology research programs.

**Type 13 — Sensory & Detection:**
Sensory advancement is Information (Type 2) and Energy (Type 7) gated. Sensory advancement is among the domains where cultivation-assisted discovery (Mechanism 3) most directly contributes — soul sense observational capability provides Sensory Technology with a phenomenological reference point that purely instrument-based research cannot reach on the same timeline. Civilizations with high-realm cultivators in their research institutions frequently advance Sensory Technology significantly faster than their overall technology tier would predict.

**Type 14 — Materials Science & Nanotechnology:**
Materials advancement is Energy (Type 7) and Industrial (Type 1) gated. It is also among the most scientifically prerequisite-heavy domains at high tiers — designing exotic materials (metamaterials, topological materials, negative-mass materials) requires theoretical physics beyond ordinary chemistry and engineering. Materials advancement is the domain where systematic research (Mechanism 1) produces the most reliable return on investment — materials research is highly tractable through hypothesis-experiment-integrate cycles, and the accumulated knowledge base compounds well across generations.
