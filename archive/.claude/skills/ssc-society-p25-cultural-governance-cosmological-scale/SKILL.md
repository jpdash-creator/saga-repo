## Area 27 — Cultural Governance at Cosmological Scale

### 27.1 — Galaxy-Scale Cultural Dynamics

At CCT-11+ (Galactic civilization and above), cultural governance operates at scales that require different analytical frameworks:

**The Cultural Archive Problem:** No CCT-11 civilization can hold all of its internal cultural diversity in active cultural memory. Cultures that flourish at local scale (village, town, city) become statistical noise at galactic scale. Part 24 Institutional Amnesia laws (IA-1 through IA-3) apply to cultural memory as much as to information: civilizations above CCT-10 inevitably suffer civilizational-scale cultural amnesia regarding their sub-system cultural diversity.

**The Cultural Preservation Imperative:** In response to the Cultural Archive Problem, CCT-11+ civilizations that prioritize cultural health establish Cultural Preservation Institutions (CPIs): archive factions, living-culture reserves, cultural sanctuary treaties, and active revival programs. CPIs are typically governed by Academic/Research (ACR) or Cultural/Arts (CAF) factions with civilizational mandate.

**The Cultural Gravity Problem:** At CCT-11+, the dominant cultural form of the civilization (usually the founding civilization's culture) exerts gravitational pull on all subsidiary cultures, slowly drawing them toward Cultural Assimilation (CE-1). The cultural diversity of a CCT-11 civilization is on a long, slow decline unless active CPI programs resist it. This is a civilizational tragedy-in-slow-motion that provides deep narrative material.

### 27.2 — The Primordial Cultural Horizon

At CCT-15+ and approaching The Primordium (Part 13), cultural forms become incomprehensible to lower-tier civilizations. The cultural practices, values, and social conventions of an Absolute civilization (CCT-20) cannot be meaningfully analyzed through the cultural frameworks of lower-tier civilizations. Part 28 authorization is required for any narrative depiction of CCT-16+ cultural forms. Authors writing characters who encounter CCT-16+ cultural forms should portray them as experiencing awe, incomprehension, or existential challenge — not cultural understanding.
