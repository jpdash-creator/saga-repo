## Area 14 — Vehicle Narration Standards

### 14.1 — Vehicle Introduction Standards

**N32-1 — First Appearance Consistency:** When a vehicle type appears for the first time in a narrative, it must be presented with sensory detail proportional to its significance: a Tier 2 carriage earns a sentence of visual description; a Tier 9 FTL warship earns a paragraph of spatial presence — its scale, the sound of its drives, the light it reflects or emits, the environmental effect of its arrival or departure. Vehicle introduction follows Part 2's deep-POV immersion requirements.

**N32-2 — Vehicle Scale Consistency:** Once a vehicle's scale class is established, all subsequent narrative references must maintain consistency with that scale. An SC-V vessel cannot be "a small ship" in one scene and then "the enormous flagship" in another without in-universe explanation (such as observational perspective shift).

**N32-3 — Technology Tier Specificity:** Authors do not narrate vehicles with technology anachronisms. A Tier 3 civilization's vehicles reflect Tier 3 capability — not Tier 5 or Tier 7. If a character encounters a vehicle inconsistent with the local civilization's tier, that inconsistency is a narrative signal (Remnant technology, contraband, alien origin) that must be acknowledged in the character's perception.

### 14.2 — Vehicle Transit Narration

**N32-4 — Transit Experience Has Phenomenology:** Vehicle travel is not invisible. A Warpstream FTL transit creates the visual phenomenon of starfield compression; a Riftjump creates the subjective experience of a brief dimensional transition (described per the Part 15 phenomenology standards for that drive type). Surface vehicle transit has weather, terrain sound, the sensation of motion. Vehicle travel is always sensory.

**N32-5 — FTL Transit Renders Through Characters:** FTL transit in a vehicle is experienced by the characters aboard, not narrated from outside. A passenger aboard an LLV-2 Lightleaper Clipper entering warp experiences the transition — the hum of the Warpstream Drive, the slight visual compression as the drive activates, the transition to the characteristic starfield distortion of warp transit. Authors use Part 15's FTL phenomenology guidance (Area 19) and Part 32's propulsion taxonomy together.

**N32-6 — Vehicle Damage Renders Truthfully:** Vehicle damage in combat must reflect the actual scale and capability of the vehicle. An SC-V Dreadnought does not "shudder" and carry on after taking a weapons hit that would be classified as an E5-scale event. Damage is proportional; structural failure is catastrophic at scale.

### 14.3 — Cultural Vehicle Narration

**N32-7 — Vehicle as Cultural Artifact:** Part 25's culture governance applies to vehicles. A Tier 2 noble's express carriage carries social status (CPT-1/CPT-4 prestige object). A Tier 9 diplomatic vessel embodies the civilization's design aesthetic, material culture, and technological self-image. Vehicles are not neutral objects — they carry cultural meaning that authors render through the POV character's perception.

**N32-8 — Vehicle Name Conventions by Culture:** Cultivation civilizations name their vehicles differently from purely technological civilizations. CS-Type civilizations (Cultivation-Sovereign) name vessels with cultivation-resonant terminology; TS-Type (Technology-Sovereign) use technical/engineering nomenclature; BP-Type (Balanced-Path) blend both. Authors follow the cultural conventions of the civilization building the vehicle when naming specific vessels in narrative.
