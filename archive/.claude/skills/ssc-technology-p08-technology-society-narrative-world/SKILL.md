## Area 11 — Technology in Society and the Narrative World

### 11.1 — Technology Stratification Within Civilizations

Technology is not uniformly distributed within civilizations. The civilization's overall tier represents the most advanced technology available within it — not the technology available to every member.

**Rule 11.1.1 — Access Stratification by Social Structure:** Technology access is stratified by wealth, faction standing, geographical location, political structure, and social organization (Part 22 — Faction Law; Part 25 — Culture Law). High-tier technology within a civilization is not uniformly accessible. This stratification is a primary source of social tension and narrative conflict.

**Rule 11.1.2 — The Operational Gradient:** Within any civilization, the technology tier available at civilization centers (major cities, technological hubs, faction capitals) is typically 0.5–1 tier-equivalent above what is available at the civilization's periphery. A Tier 7 civilization may have frontier regions effectively operating at Tier 5–6 conditions. This gradient produces real differences in daily life, medical access, communication capability, and military power.

**Rule 11.1.3 — Technology as Social Stratifier:** In civilizations where high-tier technology is accessible only to elites, possessing a one-tier advantage in personal equipment is socially equivalent to having a cultivation realm advantage. Access to Tier 5 personal technology in a Tier 4 civilization puts an individual in the position of a local Xiantian in a Mortal-dominated society — outstanding individual capability that others cannot easily challenge.

### 11.3 — Coexistence of Multiple Civilization Technology Tiers

It is common for a narrative world to contain civilizations at different technology tiers simultaneously. This creates:

**Technology Advantage and Disadvantage:** A Tier 8 civilization encountering a Tier 5 civilization has overwhelming technological military advantage, but the Tier 5 civilization may compensate through high-level cultivation power, knowledge of local terrain, Sacred Ground access, and non-technology factors.

**Technology Transfer Dynamics:** Higher-tier technology does not automatically benefit lower-tier civilizations that acquire it. Operation is possible; reproduction is not without independent scientific development. Acquired technology can be operated but not reproduced, repaired at specification, or fundamentally modified without the underlying scientific and civilizational infrastructure.

**The Technology Gap as Narrative Driver:** The gap between technology tiers is a consistent source of inter-civilizational conflict. The emotional resonance of confronting civilizations whose technology is three or more tiers above your own — where the capability gap is so large that the technology appears as sorcery — is one of the Starward Saga's primary narrative dynamics. Part 22 and Part 9 govern the political and growth dynamics that technology tier gaps produce.
