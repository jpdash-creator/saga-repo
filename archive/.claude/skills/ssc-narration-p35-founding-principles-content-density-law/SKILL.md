## Area 1 — Founding Principles of the Content Density Law

### 1.1 — The Ten Governing Principles of CDL-2

**CDL2-P1 — The Starward Saga is a Cultivation-Technology-Fight Novel Series.**
The series' primary identity — as established in Part 30, Laws P30-3 and P30-14 — is cultivation progression, technology mastery, Named Move development, power-system mechanics, combat strategy, and dual-track synergy. Content Density Law CDL-2 does not dilute this identity. It governs it with precision: every category exists in service of the story's core axis, and the weight given to each category in each Section reflects what that Section's story requires to make the cultivation-technology-fight axis land with maximum force. Lore earns the stakes. Dialogue earns the bonds. Consequence earns the weight of the fight.

**CDL2-P2 — Ten Categories, Not One.**
CDL-1's single combat percentage is replaced by ten independent content categories, each measured on its own 0–100% scale. A Prose unit that is 80% Combat AND 60% Lore is not contradicting itself — it is a combat scene rich with world-context. The categories are lenses, not buckets. A prose unit is scored on all applicable categories simultaneously; totals per prose unit routinely exceed 100%. The ten categories collectively map the full narrative DNA of every prose unit and every Section.

**CDL2-P3 — Individual Prose Units Are Free; Sections Are Governed.**
No individual Prose unit is required to carry any specific content percentage in any category. A Prose unit may be 100% combat with zero lore. A Prose unit may be 100% dialogue with zero combat. This freedom enables authentic storytelling: the story's moments are what they need to be. The governance constraint operates at the **Section aggregate level** only. Over the span of a full Section, the weighted aggregate of all Prose units in that Section must arrive within the Low–High acceptable range of each category's Section target.

**CDL2-P4 — Four Universal Anchor Floors.**
Four categories are designated as Universal Anchor Floors — they must achieve at least their Anchor percentage at Section-close in **every Section, regardless of arc type or story context**. These four are: COMBAT, CULTIVATION GROWTH, DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION, and LORE & WORLD-BUILDING. A Section that falls below 20% Anchor on any of these four categories at Section-close is a **Hard FAIL** regardless of all other category performance. These floors exist because these four categories are the structural pillars of the Starward Saga's identity — a Section without meaningful combat, cultivation growth, dialogue, and world-building has failed its core identity as a Starward Saga narrative unit.

**CDL2-P5 — Independent Lenses, Not Compositional Splits.**
Each category is scored independently on the full 0–100% scale. The categories do not divide a Prose unit's word count into mutually exclusive slices. Instead, each category represents a **dimension of narrative content** that may be simultaneously present with any other. A scene containing combat, named-move deployment, cultivation pressure, and tactical dialogue is scored at meaningful percentages in all four simultaneously. This architecture prevents the false choice between "combat scene" and "dialogue scene" — the best scenes in the Starward Saga are both at once.

**CDL2-P6 — Scoring is AI Self-Assessment by Word-Count Proportion.**
At Gate 2, after a Prose unit is written, the AI assesses each of the ten categories by estimating the proportion of the Prose unit's word count that is meaningfully engaged in that category's content type. A combat exchange that occupies 800 of a 1,200-word prose unit is scored at approximately 65–70% for COMBAT. A lore-heavy dialogue that runs 600 words of a 1,000-word unit is scored at approximately 60% for both LORE & WORLD-BUILDING and DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION. The AI reports its scores transparently in the Completeness Indicator. Scores are honest assessments, not targets to hit — the Section target is what governs the aggregate, not the individual Prose score.

**CDL2-P7 — Multi-Level Tracking with Tiered Enforcement.**
CDL-2 tracks content distribution at four levels of the narrative hierarchy: Prose-unit level (record and display — no enforcement), Area level (record and display — no enforcement), Chapter level (WARN if aggregate trends suggest Section-close will miss an Anchor floor), Section level (Hard FAIL if Section aggregate falls outside the Low–High acceptable range for any of the four Universal Anchor Floor categories; Advisory WARN for all other categories). The enforcement pyramid concentrates force at the Section level where it belongs, while giving the AI and author visibility at lower levels to course-correct before Section-close.

**CDL2-P8 — Section Targets Are Locked Guidance.**
The Section × Category target table in Area 3 of this Part is **Locked Guidance** (Part 28 §2.3). The targets are canonical defaults derived from Part 30's Section arc definitions and approved by the user at Gate 1 (Canon v4.26 approval session). They may be revised for a specific Section through a **CDL-2 Section Override** — a documented decision recorded in the SSC Canon event log, requiring the user's explicit approval. A CDL-2 Section Override does not require a full Canon Amendment; it requires documentation. Overrides that reduce a Universal Anchor Floor below 20% Anchor require explicit written justification.

**CDL2-P9 — No Dead Weight at Any Content Level.**
Part 30, Law P30-3 (no dead weight) and Law P30-14 (progression axis centrality) remain in force and operate jointly with CDL-2. A Prose unit that scores 0% in all ten CDL-2 categories has no content of any recognized narrative type and is by definition dead weight. CDL-2 does not create new dead-weight tolerance by spreading content across ten categories — every Prose unit must carry meaningful content in at least two categories, and the primary category must be scored at 40% or above. A Prose unit that carries nothing decisive is a Part 2 pacing failure under P30-3, regardless of CDL-2 category compliance.

**CDL2-P10 — The Pacing-Appropriate Depth Mandate Applies Uniformly.**
Part 28's Pacing-Appropriate Depth Mandate (Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10; 3500%–5000% range per Part 2 Area 6.2.1) and the pacing-determined word count range per Prose unit remain in force and are not modified by CDL-2. CDL-2 governs *what* is in each Prose unit at the category level; Part 28 governs *how deeply* each element is rendered. These two frameworks are complementary and simultaneously in force. A fast-paced 1400–1800-word combat scene scored at 90% COMBAT must meet both the CDL-2 category target contribution AND the Part 28 depth standard for every rendered beat. **Pacing and CDL-2 interact as follows:** Fast-paced units naturally spike single categories (COMBAT, TRAVEL); medium-paced units distribute more evenly; slow-paced units spread depth across LORE, DIALOGUE, and CONSEQUENCE. The Section aggregate across all pacing-varied units achieves the balanced distribution the Section targets require.

### 1.2 — The Ten Content Categories

Each category is defined below with its governing Part dependencies, its narrative scope, and its classification criteria. Categories are non-exclusive — any single Prose unit may carry content across multiple categories simultaneously.

---

**Category 1 — COMBAT**
*Governing Parts: Part 5 (Combat Law), Part 6 (Cultivation Move Taxonomy Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the direct or indirect expression of cultivation combat, physical conflict, tactical warfare, or power-system confrontation. COMBAT content is scored when the prose is materially engaged with: active fight scenes between cultivators, armies, or factions; sparring and training with combat intent and cultivation-energy expression; tactical planning sessions directly serving combat preparation; post-combat recovery and wound assessment; combat-driven power-state analysis; pre-battle strategic decisions; threat intelligence gathering whose direct purpose is combat readiness; weapon and combat-gear acquisition in a combat-readiness context; enemy power-level assessment.

**Scoring Guidance:** A full combat exchange (Named Move deployment, energy collision, injury assessment) scores 80–100% COMBAT. A tactical council with maps and formation decisions scores 50–70% COMBAT. A post-battle wound treatment scene with tactical review scores 40–60% COMBAT. A training spar between two protagonists with technique correction scores 60–80% COMBAT. Pure travel between combat zones without combat content scores 0% COMBAT.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — COMBAT must achieve ≥20% Anchor at Section-close in every Section.

---

**Category 2 — NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES**
*Governing Parts: Part 6 (Cultivation Move Taxonomy Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the introduction, development, deployment, refinement, or formal naming of cultivation techniques, Named Moves, technology weapon systems, formation arrays, or combat-applicable abilities governed by Part 6. This category is distinct from COMBAT: COMBAT measures the presence of conflict; NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES measures the presence of the named technique architecture that is the Starward Saga's most distinctive power-system feature. A combat scene can score high COMBAT and low NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES (if the combat uses unnamed force); a technique-creation scene can score high NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES and low COMBAT (if it is a pure creation/naming moment without active conflict).

**Scoring Guidance:** A scene in which a protagonist formally creates and names a new cultivation technique scores 70–100% NAMED MOVES. A combat scene in which three Named Moves are deployed and described in full Part 6 nomenclature scores 50–80% NAMED MOVES. A training scene focused on refining an existing Named Move's mechanics scores 40–70% NAMED MOVES. A combat scene using only raw cultivation force with no named technique reference scores 0–15% NAMED MOVES.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — this category has no universal floor. Some Sections will naturally have lower Named Move density than others.

---

**Category 3 — CULTIVATION GROWTH**
*Governing Parts: Part 3 (Cultivation Power Law), Part 4 (Cultivation Affinity Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the advancement, measurement, or experiential rendering of cultivation realm progression, breakthrough events, affinity development, dantian growth, meridian network expansion, CPM absorption, soul-stage transitions, or any cultivation-system growth event governed by Parts 3 and 4. CULTIVATION GROWTH is distinct from COMBAT: training combat scores COMBAT; the breakthrough event that training accumulation produces scores CULTIVATION GROWTH. Both may score simultaneously in a scene that features a breakthrough during combat.

**Scoring Guidance:** A full cultivation breakthrough scene (energy surge, meridian expansion, realm transition) scores 80–100% CULTIVATION GROWTH. An extended training session building toward a future breakthrough scores 40–60% CULTIVATION GROWTH. A cultivation assessment conversation between protagonist and instructor scores 30–50% CULTIVATION GROWTH. A combat scene in which a breakthrough occurs mid-battle scores both high COMBAT and 50–70% CULTIVATION GROWTH simultaneously. A pure political scene with no cultivation content scores 0% CULTIVATION GROWTH.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — CULTIVATION GROWTH must achieve ≥20% Anchor at Section-close in every Section.

---

**Category 4 — TECHNOLOGY**
*Governing Parts: Part 8 (Technology Scale Law), Part 31 (Cultivation-Technology Synergy Law), Part 32 (Vehicle and Transport Law), Part 33 (Technology Implementation Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the deployment, description, development, or integration of technology — Tier 1 through Tier 6 per Part 8 — in any narrative context. TECHNOLOGY content includes: combat-technology integration (Valia's dual-track capability); vehicle and transport technology (Part 32 airships, ground vehicles); communication technology in diplomatic or military context; medical technology in post-combat recovery; artifact detection and analysis equipment; construction and infrastructure technology; technology-cultivation fusion events (Part 31 dual-track synergy moments); technology weaponry and its tactical deployment.

**Scoring Guidance:** A scene in which Valia deploys technology-integrated cultivation techniques in combat scores 60–80% TECHNOLOGY and 70–90% COMBAT simultaneously. An airship travel scene whose prose focuses on the vessel's systems and capabilities scores 50–70% TECHNOLOGY and 70–90% TRAVEL & DISCOVERY simultaneously. A diplomatic communication via technology relay scores 30–50% TECHNOLOGY. A cultivation-only combat scene with no technology content scores 0–10% TECHNOLOGY.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Technology density appropriately varies by Section arc and available technology infrastructure.

---

**Category 5 — LORE & WORLD-BUILDING**
*Governing Parts: Part 13 (World Systems Location Law), Part 14 (World Systems Distance Law), Part 21 (People, Society, and Civilization Law), Part 25 (Social World Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the introduction, expansion, or deepening of the Starward Saga's world — its geography, history, cultivation-system architecture, social structures, civilizations, ancient forces, cosmological context, faction histories, kingdom cultures, beast ecosystems, relic lore, and all other elements of the world's texture and depth. LORE & WORLD-BUILDING is scored whenever the prose materially advances the reader's understanding of the world the story inhabits — not through exposition dumps, but through rendered experience: a protagonist encountering the World Tree and sensing its ancient energy; a diplomatic summit at which the Nine Suns Continent's political ecology becomes visible; a border village scene that establishes the texture of daily life at cultivation-world margins.

**Scoring Guidance:** A World Tree discovery sequence (first approach, first sensing, first contact) scores 80–100% LORE. An ensemble scene at a diplomatic summit where the continent's political factions become visible scores 60–80% LORE. A cultivation breakthrough scene whose context includes detailed description of the Sacred Ground's energy type and historical function scores 40–60% LORE alongside high CULTIVATION GROWTH. A pure combat scene in a location with no environmental or historical rendering scores 0–15% LORE.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — LORE & WORLD-BUILDING must achieve ≥20% Anchor at Section-close in every Section.

---

**Category 6 — DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION**
*Governing Parts: Part 29 (Dialogue and Social Interaction Law), Part 25 (Social World Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the direct or rendered interaction between characters — through dialogue, social exchange, relationship development, ideological conversation, familial bonding, ensemble cohesion moments, cultural exchange between characters of different origins, and the social texture of the cultivation world's interpersonal dynamics. DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION is the category that carries the ensemble's emotional life: the friendship between Varya and Aegon, the alliance between Varys and Aeries, the deep bond between Valia and Aalia, the cultural navigation of Hercules and Bison — all rendered through actual interaction, not description of interaction.

**Scoring Guidance:** A full relationship scene between two protagonists (extended dialogue, emotional revelation, ideological exchange) scores 80–100% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL. A tournament banquet scene with political small-talk and relationship undercurrents scores 50–70% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL alongside moderate POLITICAL & FACTION. A combat scene in which characters call to each other, strategize in motion, and reveal relationship through action scores 25–45% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL alongside high COMBAT. A pure solo cultivation breakthrough scene with no social content scores 0–10% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION must achieve ≥20% Anchor at Section-close in every Section.

---

**Category 7 — INFORMATION & INTRIGUE**
*Governing Part: Part 24 (Information and Secrecy Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the gathering, withholding, misrepresentation, revelation, or strategic deployment of information — in any context. INFORMATION & INTRIGUE is the category of secrets, intelligence, espionage, dramatic irony, information asymmetry, faction intelligence operations, reconnaissance, the Third Faction's hidden movements, the World Tree's communicated knowledge, the Olympus suppression backstory's gradual revelation, and all other moments in which *what is known by whom* is the scene's primary axis. This category is distinct from POLITICAL & FACTION (which governs the political stakes of information) and from LORE (which governs world-knowledge delivery): INFORMATION & INTRIGUE governs the *strategic dynamics* of information in the narrative.

**Scoring Guidance:** A scene in which Valia perceives a hidden political signal that others miss scores 70–90% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE. A scouting mission reporting on an enemy faction's troop movements scores 60–80% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE alongside moderate COMBAT (if the scout encounters resistance). A diplomatic summit scene in which hidden agendas are present beneath formal courtesies scores 40–60% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE alongside high DIALOGUE & SOCIAL. A full combat scene between two explicitly identified combatants scores 0–15% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE (unless a hidden combatant or hidden capability is revealed).

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Information & Intrigue density appropriately varies by Section arc.

---

**Category 8 — CONSEQUENCE & PSYCHOLOGY**
*Governing Parts: Part 26 (Psychological Arc Law), Part 27 (Consequence Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the rendering of psychological interior — grief, joy, fear, pride, shame, determination, identity conflict, relationship reckoning — and/or the narrative consequences of prior events as they land on characters and the story's world. CONSEQUENCE & PSYCHOLOGY is the category that ensures the Starward Saga's events *cost* something, that its characters are changed by what happens to them, and that the cultivation and combat progression is anchored in genuine human (and Demigod) emotional reality. This category includes: post-battle emotional reckoning; breakthrough moments' inner experience beyond the physical; Hercules and Bison's displacement psychology; Varya and Aegon's emotional growth alongside cultivation growth; the political and personal consequences of diplomatic failures; the war's toll on the ensemble.

**Scoring Guidance:** A post-battle scene in which a protagonist processes a loss and its meaning scores 80–100% CONSEQUENCE & PSYCHOLOGY. The Olympus Suppression Seal's suppressed-power-glimpse moment (Hercules feeling Demigod power pressing against the cap) scores 70–90% CONSEQUENCE & PSYCHOLOGY alongside moderate COMBAT. A First Awakening scene that renders Varya's inner experience of cultivation energy for the first time scores 60–80% CONSEQUENCE & PSYCHOLOGY alongside high CULTIVATION GROWTH. A pure combat scene between unfamiliar combatants with no interiority rendered scores 0–15% CONSEQUENCE & PSYCHOLOGY.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Consequence & Psychology density appropriately peaks in certain Sections (S4, S7, S8) and is lower in others (S2's high-action tournament arc).

---

**Category 9 — TRAVEL & DISCOVERY**
*Governing Parts: Part 13 (World Systems Location Law), Part 14 (World Systems Distance Law), Part 32 (Vehicle and Transport Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the act and experience of travel, exploration, and first-encounter discovery — the physical movement through the world of Saha and the Nine Suns Continent, the approach to new locations, the first sensory rendering of places the protagonists (and reader) have never visited, and the discovery of phenomena, beings, or territories that expand the known world. TRAVEL & DISCOVERY is the category of geographical immersion, exploration momentum, and world-expansion. It is highest in Section 5 (quest-world exploration), Section 6 (World Tree approach and discovery), and Section 8 (airship journey and Snow Town discovery).

**Scoring Guidance:** The airship departure and journey through unknown skies (Section 8) scores 80–100% TRAVEL & DISCOVERY. An approach sequence through new terrain toward a quest objective scores 50–70% TRAVEL & DISCOVERY. A combat scene in a fully established location with no discovery content scores 0–15% TRAVEL & DISCOVERY. A quest scene in a new region combining active exploration, first encounter with new cultivator phenomena, and environmental rendering scores 60–80% TRAVEL & DISCOVERY alongside moderate LORE.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Travel & Discovery density varies enormously by Section arc; some Sections are predominantly stationary.

---

**Category 10 — POLITICAL & FACTION**
*Governing Parts: Part 22 (Faction Classification Law), Part 23 (Political Systems Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the operation, conflict, negotiation, alliance formation, or consequence dynamics of political and factional power structures — kingdoms, guilds, cultivation sects, empire-level governance, inter-kingdom diplomacy, faction military operations, political betrayals, alliance stress, continental political ecosystems, and the Starward Saga's broader political canvas. POLITICAL & FACTION is the category of statecraft, faction strategy, and political consequence. It is highest in Section 3 (diplomatic summit, faction introduction, Third Faction emergence) and Section 6 (political race for World Tree access, continental wars).

**Scoring Guidance:** A diplomatic summit scene with multiple kingdoms negotiating alliance terms scores 80–100% POLITICAL & FACTION. A war council in which kingdom military commanders plan a campaign scores 60–80% POLITICAL & FACTION alongside high COMBAT (tactical planning). A guild negotiation scene with economic and political stakes scores 50–70% POLITICAL & FACTION. A pure training scene between two protagonists of the same kingdom scores 0–15% POLITICAL & FACTION (unless the training itself has political implications).

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Political & Faction density appropriately peaks in diplomatic and war sections and is lower in personal-arc and exploration sections.
