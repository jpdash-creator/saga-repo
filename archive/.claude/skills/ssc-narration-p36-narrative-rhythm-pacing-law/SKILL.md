## Area 4 — Narrative Rhythm and Pacing Law

### 4.1 — The Prose-Level Rhythm Mandate

Every prose unit must demonstrate a **detectable narrative rhythm** across its full length. Narrative rhythm is the pattern of mode variation within the prose — the alternation between action and reflection, dialogue and sensation, explanation and experience, tension and release. A prose unit with rhythm feels alive and varied. A prose unit without rhythm — where the same mode dominates for extended consecutive runs — feels monotonous regardless of how technically correct its content is.

This mandate operates at the prose-unit level (not the paragraph level in isolation): the AI must assess the macro shape of the entire prose unit and confirm that mode variation is present and deliberate.

### 4.2 — Narrative Mode Taxonomy

For the purposes of Part 36 rhythm governance, narrative modes are classified as follows:

**(M-1) Action Mode:** Physical events are happening. Characters are moving, fighting, fleeing, building, performing techniques, traveling. The world is in motion.

**(M-2) Interiority Mode:** The POV character's internal landscape is foregrounded — thought, analysis, memory, feeling, decision-making, psychological response to events. Time may slow or pause in the external world while the interior world is rendered.

**(M-3) Dialogue Mode:** Characters are speaking to each other. The exchange carries the scene's forward motion through speech, silence, subtext, and the physical behavior surrounding the words.

**(M-4) Sensation Mode:** Sensory experience is foregrounded — the physical reality of the scene rendered through the POV character's sensory apparatus. Not what is happening in plot terms, but what it feels, looks, sounds, tastes, or smells like to be in this moment.

**(M-5) Explanation Mode:** Technical content is being delivered — cultivation mechanics, technology function, world history, power system rules. This is the mode that must never run consecutively for three or more paragraphs (per Area 3 Exposition Ceiling).

### 4.3 — The Three-Consecutive-Mode Rule

**No three consecutive paragraphs in a prose unit may be in the same narrative mode.** This is the Three-Consecutive-Mode Rule.

The rule applies to all five modes. Three consecutive Action Mode paragraphs without any interiority, sensation, or dialogue is a rhythm violation. Three consecutive Interiority Mode paragraphs without any action or dialogue anchor is a rhythm violation. Three consecutive Dialogue Mode paragraphs without action beats, sensation, or interiority — pure speech exchange with no physical world — is a rhythm violation.

*Combat Exception:* Extended combat sequences (Part 5 / Part 28 DP-1) may sustain Action Mode across more than three consecutive paragraphs when each paragraph carries the exchange-by-exchange resolution required by DP-1. However, even within extended combat, Part 36 requires at least one interiority beat, one sensation beat, or one consequence-echo beat per five paragraphs of sustained action (per Area 6 — Combat Scene Thematic Overlay). The exception is narrow: it permits action continuation, not rhythm abandonment.

### 4.4 — Scene-Open and Scene-Close Requirements

**(a) Scene-Open:** The first paragraph of every prose unit must establish the scene's physical and emotional reality simultaneously. It must not open with pure explanation. It must not open with a ledger-style inventory. It must open in the world — a sensory detail, a character state, an action already in motion, or a dialogue line that presupposes the scene's reality. The reader must land in the scene, not outside it looking in.

**(b) Scene-Close:** The last paragraph of every prose unit must carry the scene's emotional current across the close. It must not end with a technical summary. It must not close on an expository note. It must close on something that matters — a thought, a sensation, a decision, a consequence beginning to unfold — that makes the next prose unit feel necessary rather than optional.
