## Area 12 — Information Flow Through Civilizations and Factions

### 12.1 — Civilizational Information Ecology

Every civilization has a characteristic information ecology — the sum of all information transmission channels active within it, the access barriers between different societal layers, and the governance philosophy that determines who knows what.

**Five Canonical Information Ecology Types:**

**Ecology Type IE-1 — Open Flow:**
Information circulates freely throughout the civilization. There are no significant access barriers between societal layers. Secrets are held individually but not institutionally. This ecology produces rapid innovation (knowledge diffuses quickly), but also rapid destabilization (crises are immediately public) and high enemy intelligence penetration (what your citizens know, spies can find out).

**Ecology Type IE-2 — Hierarchical Flow:**
Information access is tied to social status, cultivation rank, or administrative position. Higher tiers have access to lower-tier information plus their own tier's classified data; lower tiers are systematically excluded from upper-tier information. Classic structure of autocratic civilizations and strict cultivation hierarchies. Advantages: Tier I information is protected from casual leakage. Disadvantages: lower tiers make decisions based on incomplete pictures; inefficiency accumulates; lower-tier discontent cannot be accurately monitored by upper tiers who are not receiving accurate feedback from below.

**Ecology Type IE-3 — Faction-Siloed Flow:**
Information circulates freely within factions but is treated as classified when moving between factions. Inter-faction information is traded, purchased, stolen, and weaponized. This is the dominant ecology of middle-CCT civilizations with multiple powerful factions. Most information battles in the narrative occur in IE-3 ecologies.

**Ecology Type IE-4 — Institutional Controlled Flow:**
A central intelligence institution controls all significant information flows. All information channels pass through the institution, which processes, classifies, and distributes as it decides. Total information control ecologies are functionally impossible above CCT-7 due to information volume, but approximations exist in theocratic and autocratic civilizations with highly centralized control apparatus.

**Ecology Type IE-5 — Chaotic Flow:**
No coherent information control exists. Information moves through every available channel without governance. This is the ecology of war zones, collapsed civilizations, and territories where multiple competing powers have destroyed each other's information infrastructure. Extremely difficult to operate in because no information can be trusted — disinformation runs unchecked.

**Secrecy Culture Type → Information Ecology Mapping (cross-reference with Part 25 Area 15.1):**

The Five Secrecy Culture Types (Part 25 Area 15.1) map to these canonical information ecology types:

| Secrecy Culture Type | Part 25 Name | Default IE Type | Notes |
|---------------------|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| OSC | Open-Share | IE-1 Open Flow | CPT-5 civilizations; information hoarding taboo |
| ETC | Earned-Trust | IE-2 Hierarchical Flow | CPT-1 civilizations; access stratified by relationship depth and rank |
| CCC | Commercial-Confidential | IE-3 Faction-Siloed Flow | CPT-3 civilizations; information weaponized commercially between entities |
| SSC | Sovereign-Secret | IE-4 Institutional Controlled Flow | CPT-4 civilizations; sovereign institution controls all channels |
| CPC | Covenant-Protected | IE-4 Institutional Controlled Flow | CPT-6 civilizations; theocratic institution as control authority |

CPT-7 (Anarchic-Sovereign) civilizations fluctuate between IE-1 and IE-5 depending on conflict level. IE-5 Chaotic Flow is not a stable baseline ecology — it represents collapsed or war-zone conditions. Civilization type (Part 21 Area 2.6) determines the starting IE type; faction competition and political structure determine actual IE type in practice.

### 12.2 — Faction-Level Information Architecture

Within a faction, information architecture is governed by the faction's secrecy doctrine (Area 6.2) and its faction type (Part 22):

| Faction Type | Characteristic Information Architecture |
|-------------|----------------------------------------|
| CS-F (Cultivator Sects) | Hierarchical by rank; core technique sealed to leadership; technique lineages classified by seniority; outer disciples operate on need-to-know |
| IEF (Intelligence Factions) | Cell structure; no member knows full organizational map; Tier I information restricted to director layer |
| GOV (Government Bodies) | Function-compartmentalized; political information restricted by position; public information maintained as governance tool |
| RIF (Religious/Ideological) | Doctrinally controlled; the theological justification for hierarchy determines who has access to what |
| NAH (Noble Houses) | Lineage-based; house secrets transmitted only within bloodline; marriage creates controlled information sharing events |
| CRF (Criminal Factions) | Need-to-know; operational security through fear; betrayal punished by death (creates extreme silence culture) |
| AHF (Assassination/Hunting) | Target intelligence classified as Tier I for operation duration; clients know nothing about methodology; operators know nothing about other clients |
