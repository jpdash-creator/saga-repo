## Area 7 — Move Learning, Inheritance, and Transmission Law

### 7.1 — The Five Learning Methods

**Method L-1 — Direct Transmission:**
A technique is taught explicitly by a person who possesses it — through verbal instruction, physical demonstration, manual transmission, or qi-imprint teaching. Direct Transmission is the most reliable learning method and produces the fastest mastery development. The teacher's understanding of the technique's principle is transmitted alongside its form. A student who learns through Direct Transmission starts at Novice mastery with access to the teacher's conceptual framing, which accelerates advancement to Practiced and beyond.

**Method L-2 — Manual Study:**
Learning from a written transmission manual, recorded formation, jade slip, or equivalent encoded record. Manual Study is slower than Direct Transmission because the student must interpret the principle from textual or energetic encoding without the teacher's living guidance. Errors in interpretation are common. However, Manual Study allows techniques to be transmitted across generations and distances. Students learning through manuals typically begin at Novice but advance more slowly due to interpretive uncertainty.

**Method L-3 — Combat Observation:**
Observing a technique in use during combat and reverse-engineering its principle through analysis. This is the most common way cultivators develop partial understanding of opponent techniques. Combat Observation typically produces an approximate version of the original — functionally similar but missing the principle depth that Direct Transmission provides. A technique learned through Combat Observation is classified as a new Named Move (the observer's interpretation) rather than the original, unless it is developed to the point where the original's principle is correctly replicated.

**Method L-4 — Experiential Discovery:**
The cultivator independently develops a technique through intuition, experimental practice, and breakthrough insight. Experiential Discovery is the source of all original Named Moves. It is the slowest and most difficult method but produces the deepest personal understanding — a technique discovered rather than learned is often more profoundly mastered. Signature moves are almost exclusively produced through Experiential Discovery.

**Method L-5 — Absorption (Void Root Specific):**
The Void Root's ability to absorb and convert hostile qi (Part 3, Area 5.3) allows a Void Root cultivator to, over time, internalize the principles of techniques they have experienced. This is not instantaneous technique theft — it is an accelerated form of Combat Observation learning that draws directly on energy-level interaction with the original technique. The resulting Named Move is the cultivator's own interpretation, not a copy of the original. Full rules for Void Root absorption capabilities are governed by Part 3.

### 7.2 — Lineage Techniques

**What Is a Lineage Technique:**
A Lineage Technique is a Named Move that is formally registered as the intellectual and spiritual property of a sect, clan, family, or cultivation lineage (Part 22 — Faction Law). It can only be officially transmitted to members of that lineage by recognized authorities within it. Teaching a Lineage Technique to non-members without authorization is a violation of Part 23 (Affiliation Law) and carries significant cultural and political consequences.

**Bloodline Resonance:**
Some Named Moves are specifically developed using bloodline energy — cultivation qi derived from or amplified by a specific genetic heritage. These techniques may have reduced energy cost or amplified output when used by blood descendants of the creator. They cannot achieve the same depth in non-blood practitioners even if the mechanics are identical — the bloodline resonance is part of the technique's principle. Bloodline techniques are the most tightly held lineage secrets in cultivation society.

**Sect Exclusive Techniques:**
Techniques developed by a sect for its members are Sect Exclusive Techniques. Unlike bloodline techniques, they can be taught to any member (non-blood) and may achieve full depth in any practitioner — but they remain sect property. A cultivator who leaves a sect retains personal mastery of its techniques but loses the right to transmit them.

### 7.3 — Technique Conflict and Move Ownership

A Named Move has one creator of record. If two cultivators develop techniques that are mechanically similar but independently named, both are valid Named Moves — the similar mechanics are governed by separate names and separate lineages. If one was clearly derived from the other (determined by creation date and cultivation history), Part 23 governs the affiliation and ownership implications.
