## Area 2 — The Negotiable Canon Framework

### 2.1 — What Negotiable Canon Is

Negotiable Canon is the category of content that is: (a) original to the author's creative act; (b) not pre-specified by any SSC Canon Part; (c) fully consistent with all SSC Canon Parts; and (d) established as fixed within the novel once written.

Negotiable Canon is the living body of the Starward Saga's specific world — the particular names, histories, customs, techniques, rivalries, and textures that make the governed universe feel inhabited. Without Negotiable Canon, the Starward Saga would be a governance document. With it, the governance document becomes a world.

### 2.2 — Locked Canon vs. Negotiable Canon

- The Phase 1 Section × Category target table (Low–Anchor–High ranges for all 8 Sections × 10 categories) (Part 35)
- The Completeness Indicator ten-category block format (Part 35)

**Negotiable Canon** (governed by Part 28 as free creative territory) includes:
- The *specific names* of every location, road, tower, gate, district, mountain, sea, temple, and wilderness area
- The *specific names* of every faction, sub-sect, alliance, syndicate, organization, and political body
- The *specific names* of every Named Move and cultivation technique within the Part 6 taxonomy
- The *specific names* of every character, including minor characters, historical figures, and background population
- The *specific names* of every cultural event, tournament, festival, ceremony, custom, taboo, tradition, and governance institution within the Part 25 CPT framework
- The *specific names* of every vehicle, weapon, relic, and artifact that is not already named by the governing Part
- The *specific history* of every location, faction, and civilization that is not required by Canon law
- The *specific political conflicts*, internal faction tensions, governance controversies, and ideological debates within and between factions and civilizations
- The *specific tactical choices* made in combat, negotiation, and strategy, within Part 5's escalation framework
- The *specific emotional responses* of characters to events in the prose, within Part 26's psychological framework
- The *specific dialogue content* of all conversations, within Part 29's voice architecture
- The *specific behavior* of any character, faction, or civilization in any given moment, within their governed identity

### 2.3 — The Negotiable Canon Establishment Rule

Once Negotiable Canon content is established in the prose — a name is given, a custom is described, a history is told — that content is fixed. It becomes a reference point for all subsequent prose and may not be contradicted. The author who names a faction's headquarters in Chapter 3 cannot rename it in Chapter 11. The author who describes a cultural marriage ceremony in one volume cannot describe the same culture as having no marriage ceremony in a later volume. Negotiable Canon, once established, acquires the same inviolability as Locked Canon.

**The Establishment Threshold:** Content is established when: (a) it is given a proper name, (b) it is described with specific detail that creates expectation of consistency, or (c) a character-level consequence (IE event, CSS event, PIS event) is attached to it. Content that is vague, unnamed, or consequentially unattached is not yet established and may be refined.

### 2.4 — The Retroactive Enrichment Rule

Later prose may enrich, deepen, and expand upon earlier Negotiable Canon content without contradiction. A faction named in Volume 1 may have its full internal governance structure, cultural traditions, and political history revealed in Volume 3 — provided the revelation does not contradict anything established earlier. A Named Move first used in Volume 1 may have its cultivation technique architecture, affinity requirements, and historical origin fully elaborated in Volume 2 — provided the elaboration is consistent with the move's established effects. Retroactive enrichment is a powerful narrative tool: the world deepens as the reader progresses, without becoming inconsistent.
