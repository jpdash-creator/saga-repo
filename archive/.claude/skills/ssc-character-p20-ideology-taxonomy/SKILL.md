## Rule Text (Verbatim Source — Authoritative)
## Area 2 — The Ideology Taxonomy

### 2.1 — Foundational Ideology Categories

All character ideologies in the Starward Saga belong to one or more of twelve foundational categories. These categories are not mutually exclusive — most characters hold compound ideologies drawn from two or three foundational categories.

**Category I — Strength-Dominant Ideology (SDI)**

The foundational belief that power (cultivation power, military power, political power, informational power) is the real currency of all existence. Everything that appears to operate on different terms — compassion, justice, loyalty — is ultimately a power calculation in disguise. The SDI character does not respect morality; they respect what morality is able to accomplish. When morality can be leveraged to accumulate power, they use it. When it cannot, it is discarded.

*SDI Variations:*
- **Pure-Force SDI:** Raw combat power is the only measure. Tactical intelligence matters only insofar as it amplifies physical force output.
- **Network-Power SDI:** Social and informational power are the real currencies. Cultivation strength is a tool; the real power is in who owes you.
- **Structural-Power SDI:** Institutional power (sect leadership, governmental authority, legal authority) is the highest form. The SDI practitioner who controls the rules controls the outcome of every contest conducted by those rules.
- **Potential-Power SDI:** Future power is real power. The SDI practitioner who correctly reads cultivation potential invests in future power even when present power is negligible.

*SDI characters frequently:* justify exploitation through productivity ("the strong must use the weak or the weak are wasted"), maintain surface ideologies of loyalty while calculating defection windows, experience ideological crisis when they encounter an opponent whose power exceeds their own without exploiting them (which their ideology cannot account for).

---

**Category II — Justice-Centered Ideology (JCI)**

The foundational belief that reality operates according to principles of fairness, desert, and accountability — and that one of the character's primary responsibilities is to enforce or restore that alignment. JCI characters do not experience harm as pain first; they experience it as *wrong* first. Their primary question when observing the world is not "what happened?" but "was this right?"

*JCI Variations:*
- **Retributive-JCI:** Those who do harm must suffer equivalent harm. Justice is balance. The scales must be balanced personally, regardless of institutional authority.
- **Restorative-JCI:** Justice is achieved when those harmed are made whole and those who harmed are accountable. Punishment matters less than repair.
- **Structural-JCI:** Individual actions are less important than structural conditions. Justice requires changing the institutions that produce unjust outcomes.
- **Cultivator-JCI:** The strong have a special obligation to protect the weak — because their power creates an asymmetry, they bear disproportionate moral responsibility. Strength without service is corruption.
- **Cosmic-JCI:** The Dao or cosmos has a justice built into it. What is truly wrong will be destroyed eventually. The practitioner's role is to accelerate that justice or align with it.

*JCI characters frequently:* experience profound conflict when the path of justice requires actions that appear unjust (torturing one to save many); accumulate personal enemies by applying their justice standard consistently without regard for power differentials; experience ideological crisis when they are confronted with their own unjust actions.

---

**Category III — Loyalty-Bound Ideology (LBI)**

The foundational belief that relationships — to persons, to lineages, to institutions, to principles — create binding obligations that supersede abstract reasoning. The LBI character does not ask "is this action right?" first; they ask "does this honor or betray the bond?" Loyalty is the organizing principle from which all other values are evaluated.

*LBI Variations:*
- **Personal-Loyalty LBI:** Specific relationships (master, sworn brother, beloved) are the binding units. The LBI character will violate any principle to protect those specific persons.
- **Lineage-Loyalty LBI:** Blood and cultivation lineage are the binding units. Sect, clan, family — these come before any individual. Individuals are expressions of lineage; the lineage is what matters.
- **Institutional-Loyalty LBI:** The institution (sect, government, organization) is the primary loyalty object regardless of who leads it. The institution's survival is worth more than any member's principles.
- **Principled-Loyalty LBI:** Loyalty is owed to the principle the group embodies — and withdrawn when the group betrays the principle. "I am loyal to what this sect stands for; if it stops standing for that, my loyalty ends."
- **Code-Loyalty LBI:** A personal code (honor code, warrior's oath, merchant's pledge) is the organizing loyalty unit. The code may mandate loyalty to enemies when they follow the same code.

*LBI characters frequently:* discover that their multiple loyalties are in irreconcilable conflict; experience profound identity crisis when a loyalty object betrays them (because their identity is organized around the loyalty, not the self); take actions that appear irrational to outsiders because loyalty logic is opaque to those who don't share the structure.

---

**Category IV — Freedom-Sovereign Ideology (FSI)**

The foundational belief that self-determination — the right to choose one's own path without constraint from external authority — is the highest value. FSI characters resist all institutional authority, social expectation, and conventional moral framework that they did not choose. This is not nihilism; the FSI character has values. Their values simply originate entirely from within rather than being inherited or imposed.

*FSI Variations:*
- **Personal-Sovereignty FSI:** No one has authority over me. I will determine my cultivation path, my alliances, my moral code, my life's purpose — and no sect, government, or cosmic force has standing to override that.
- **Anarchic-Political FSI:** No institution has legitimate authority over any individual. The FSI practitioner opposes all hierarchy actively, not just personally. This can generate revolutionary politics.
- **Dao-Sovereignty FSI:** The Dao itself is freedom — the ultimate state is cultivation beyond all constraints, including the constraints of self, relationship, and identity. This FSI variant often becomes transcendent or ascetic.
- **Selective-Authority FSI:** Authority is legitimate only when chosen. The FSI practitioner consents to specific hierarchical relationships (master, guild, temporary alliance) while rejecting imposed ones.

*FSI characters frequently:* make powerful allies uncomfortable because the alliance is always conditional; have difficulty maintaining long-term cultivation relationships because relationships carry mutual obligation that FSI experience as constraint; occasionally discover that their commitment to freedom was itself a constraint.

---

**Category V — Transcendence-Seeking Ideology (TSI)**

The foundational belief that ordinary existence — personal survival, social standing, material accumulation, even cultivation advancement — is not the real point. There is something beyond. The TSI character is organized around reaching, understanding, or becoming that something beyond — and ordinary concerns are evaluated entirely by how much they advance or impede that goal.

*TSI Variations:*
- **Cultivation-Transcendence TSI:** The highest objective is the cultivation ceiling — whatever lies beyond the current known limit of the Heavenly Scale. Nothing else genuinely matters by comparison.
- **Dao-Comprehension TSI:** The real point is understanding the Dao itself — not accumulating power derived from it, but comprehending its nature. Power may or may not be a byproduct.
- **Cosmic-Role TSI:** The character believes they have a cosmically significant function. Finding and fulfilling that function is the transcendent goal. Personal desire, social approval, even survival are secondary to role fulfillment.
- **Enlightenment-TSI:** The transcendent goal is a state of being — freedom from suffering, complete understanding, liberation from the cycle of karma or cultivation striving. This TSI variant often appears in elder cultivators who have exhausted ordinary ambitions.
- **Void-TSI:** The transcendent goal is dissolution of self into the void, the absolute, or the undifferentiated. This is rare and unsettling to most other characters — it is indistinguishable from suicidal ideology at first glance but operates according to a completely different metaphysical logic.

*TSI characters frequently:* seem detached or indifferent to events that galvanize other characters; perform startling acts of self-sacrifice that appear irrational without the transcendent framework; occasionally reveal that their transcendence framework is a defense mechanism against ordinary human vulnerability.

---

**Category VI — Order-Maintaining Ideology (OMI)**

The foundational belief that stability, structure, hierarchy, and institutional continuity are primary goods. Chaos is the enemy. Disruption — even disruptive change aimed at improvement — must be evaluated carefully against the value of what it may destroy. The OMI character may be a powerful force for stability or a rigid enforcer of unjust structures — both are possible within this ideology.

*OMI Variations:*
- **Cultivation-Hierarchy OMI:** The cultivation hierarchy is the natural order. Realm determines worth. All social structures that reflect this hierarchy are legitimate; those that challenge it are dangerous.
- **Institutional-Continuity OMI:** Existing institutions (sects, governments, guilds) are themselves valuable because they represent accumulated wisdom and stability. Reform should be incremental and internal, not revolutionary.
- **Cosmic-Order OMI:** The cosmos has a designed order — cultivation tiers, faction hierarchies, species relationships — and human attempts to reshape that order produce catastrophe. The practitioner serves the cosmic order.
- **Pragmatic-Stability OMI:** The OMI practitioner supports order not from belief in its intrinsic rightness but from conviction that chaos costs more than structure costs. Even an unjust order may be preferable to disorder.
- **Meritocratic-Hierarchy OMI:** Hierarchy is right, but it must be based on actual merit (cultivation ability, skill, service), not birth or lineage. This variant actively polices the hierarchy to ensure it reflects true worth.

*OMI characters frequently:* become villains when the order they maintain is revealed to be built on exploitation; experience profound crisis when a figure they consider their legitimate superior acts in ways the OMI character cannot reconcile with order-values; defend positions that from outside appear as complicity with oppression.

---

**Category VII — Ambition-Driven Ideology (ADI)**

The foundational belief that advancement — in cultivation, in social standing, in power, in wealth, in legacy — is the organizing purpose of existence. The ADI character's guiding question is always: *does this advance my position?* Not advancement for what purpose — the advancement itself is the purpose. This distinguishes ADI from SDI: SDI treats power as a means of existing safely; ADI treats advancement as the meaning of existing at all.

*ADI Variations:*
- **Cultivation-Advancement ADI:** Higher realm is the goal. Every resource, relationship, and decision is evaluated by its contribution to cultivation speed and depth.
- **Legacy-Advancement ADI:** What I leave behind is the real ambition — sect founding, knowledge preservation, bloodline continuation, the name that lasts beyond my lifespan.
- **Dominion-Advancement ADI:** The goal is the largest domain under control — territory, followers, influence. This ADI variant produces empire builders and sect patriarchs who cannot stop expanding.
- **Knowledge-Advancement ADI:** The goal is the widest and deepest knowledge — techniques, relics, universal mechanics, historical secrets. The accumulation of understanding is the meaning of the ADI practitioner's existence.
- **Status-Advancement ADI:** Social recognition and prestige are the core measurement. The ADI practitioner wants to be *acknowledged* as great, not merely to be great.

*ADI characters frequently:* sacrifice relationships that cannot be converted into advancement; experience the ADI plateau — reaching a goal only to find it immediately unsatisfying and requiring a new, larger goal; have profound difficulty with characters who do not share the advancement framework (those who are *content*).

---

**Category VIII — Vengeance-Anchored Ideology (VAI)**

The foundational belief that a specific wrong — a harm done to self, to loved ones, to a group — must be rectified through the suffering of the responsible party. VAI is not justice (JCI) because it is personal and specific rather than principled and universal. The VAI character does not seek justice; they seek this specific person or group to suffer *this* specific consequence. Everything else is provisional — relationships, cultivation path, survival itself — organized around the central drive.

*VAI Variations:*
- **Targeted-Personal VAI:** A specific individual or small group is the target. All other concerns are subordinated to reaching, defeating, and destroying this target.
- **Collective-VAI:** An entire faction, civilization, or category of person bears the guilt. The VAI practitioner cannot distinguish between the original perpetrators and all members of the responsible group.
- **Proxy-VAI:** The original perpetrators are dead, inaccessible, or already destroyed. The VAI practitioner redirects to proxies — successors, inheritors, symbolic representatives of the original harm.
- **Expanding-VAI:** What began as specific vengeance metastasizes into a broader ideological program. The original wound becomes a lens through which all perceived injustice toward the VAI practitioner's group is filtered, making the target list perpetually expand.
- **Self-Directed VAI:** The VAI practitioner holds themselves as the object of vengeance — for failures, betrayals, or acts they cannot forgive. This variant drives self-destruction patterns that appear externally directed.

*VAI characters frequently:* experience the VAI completion crisis — when the target is destroyed, the organizing purpose disappears, leaving a void; discover that their VAI has expanded to include their own allies or even themselves; struggle with the moment the target shows vulnerability or genuine remorse, which threatens the VAI's moral foundation.

---

**Category IX — Truth-Seeking Ideology (TRI)**

The foundational belief that reality has a structure that can be understood, and that understanding it correctly is a primary obligation. The TRI character does not experience comfort of false belief as a trade-off worth making — truth, even devastating truth, is preferable to comforting falsehood. This ideology can produce scientists, philosophers, archaeologists of the Dao, or characters so committed to accuracy that they become socially destructive.

*TRI Variations:*
- **Empirical-TRI:** Truth is found through observation, systematic investigation, and experimentation. The cultivator who approaches the Dao scientifically. Technology-aligned characters frequently hold Empirical-TRI.
- **Philosophical-TRI:** Truth is found through rigorous reasoning and conceptual analysis. The TRI practitioner who debates cultivation principles for decades before touching qi.
- **Dao-Navigation TRI:** The Dao itself is the truth. The TRI practitioner reads the Dao directly as a navigational act — the Dao shows what is real; all social truths are provisional by comparison.
- **Historical-TRI:** What actually happened — recovered from falsification, legend inflation, and motivated distortion — is the truth that matters most. This TRI variant produces historians who are dangerous to establishments built on false history.
- **Self-Knowing TRI:** The truth most worth finding is the truth about the self. The TRI practitioner's entire project is radical self-knowledge, including knowledge of their own ideological contradictions.

*TRI characters frequently:* cause harm by telling the truth at the wrong moment; experience profound conflicts with LBI characters (who prioritize loyalty over accuracy) and Surface-ideology performers (who experience TRI revelation as attack); occasionally discover that their commitment to truth-seeking was itself organized around an unexamined assumption.

---

**Category X — Protection-Centered Ideology (PCI)**

The foundational belief that those one is responsible for — loved ones, the weak, one's sect members, one's civilization, life in general — must be protected. The PCI character's primary motivation is not their own advancement but the shielding of their protection domain. This ideology is among the most powerful in the series because it is both deeply sympathetic and capable of producing atrocities when the protection domain is defined broadly and the means used become extreme.

*PCI Variations:*
- **Family-Anchored PCI:** The protection domain is specific loved ones. Everything else is instrumental to their safety.
- **Sect-Protective PCI:** The sect and its members are the protection domain. The elder who will do anything — including terrible things — to protect the younger generation.
- **Universal-Protective PCI:** All sentient life (or all human life, or all cultivation-capable life) is the protection domain. This variant resembles altruistic-JCI but differs because the PCI practitioner is not primarily concerned with justice — they are primarily concerned with shielding.
- **Civilization-Protective PCI:** A civilization or cultural tradition is the protection domain. Its survival justifies compromises that would otherwise be unconscionable.
- **Future-Protective PCI:** The protection domain is those who do not yet exist — future generations, potential cultivators who will be born, the civilization a thousand years hence. This variant produces characters willing to sacrifice the present for an imagined future.

*PCI characters frequently:* discover that protecting one group requires harming another; experience the PCI paradox — the actions taken in the name of protection become the thing that most harms the protection domain; have difficulty distinguishing protection from control.

---

**Category XI — Hedonistic-Libertine Ideology (HLI)**

The foundational belief that pleasure, beauty, sensation, and the full experience of existence are primary goods. Not merely the pleasure of the flesh — this ideology encompasses the pleasure of cultivation itself, of beautiful technique, of aesthetic experience in combat, of the sensory richness of a world full of cultivators and technology. The HLI practitioner is not irresponsible — they may be disciplined and strategic — but what they are disciplined *toward* is experience, not advancement or justice.

*HLI Variations:*
- **Sensory-HLI:** Physical pleasure and aesthetic experience are the primary goods. The cultivator who cares more about how a technique *feels* to execute than what it achieves.
- **Aesthetic-Discipline HLI:** The cultivation of beauty and refinement in all things — technique, appearance, social performance, speech — is the organizing principle. This variant produces master artists, legendary courtesans, and the most dangerous social manipulators.
- **Experience-Maximizing HLI:** The goal is to experience as many different states of existence as possible. This variant drives exploration, experimentation, and a tendency to take risks that purely advancement-focused cultivators consider irrational.
- **Dao-of-Joy HLI:** The deepest insight into the Dao is available through uninhibited experience rather than ascetic restriction. This philosophical HLI produces controversial cultivation methods that may be more or less genuine.
- **Epicurean-Moderate HLI:** Pleasure is the primary good, but excess destroys pleasure's value. The sophisticated HLI practitioner cultivates the conditions that enable sustained pleasure: health, social warmth, aesthetic environment, moderate indulgence.

*HLI characters frequently:* appear lightweight until the moment their genuine capability is revealed; collect enemies among ascetic and discipline-obsessed characters who mistake their pleasure orientation for weakness; experience the HLI crisis when the depth of their hedonism comes into direct conflict with loss, suffering, or catastrophic consequence.

---

**Category XII — Sacred-Covenant Ideology (SCI)**

The foundational belief that a sacred relationship — to a deity, to a cosmic principle, to an ancestor, to a cultivated lineage — is binding and constitutes the highest authority. The SCI character does not evaluate actions by self-interest, by loyalty to persons, or by abstract principle — they evaluate them by the covenant's terms. What does the covenant require? That is the question. The covenant's authority transcends all else.

*SCI Variations:*
- **Deity-Covenant SCI:** A specific divine entity (or what the character takes to be divine) has issued commands or established terms. The SCI practitioner fulfills those terms regardless of personal cost.
- **Ancestor-Covenant SCI:** The cultivation lineage's founders made promises and established standards. The SCI practitioner is their living embodiment — to deviate is to betray not just the past but the covenant itself.
- **Dao-Covenant SCI:** The practitioner has entered into a relationship with the Dao itself — a personal covenant of alignment. Specific behaviors violate that alignment; the SCI practitioner avoids them categorically.
- **Oath-Covenant SCI:** A specific oath taken at a specific moment constitutes the covenant. The SCI practitioner may or may not believe in the cosmic framework into which the oath was embedded — but the oath itself is irrevocable.
- **Destiny-Covenant SCI:** The practitioner believes they are cosmically assigned to a specific role or outcome. The covenant is not with a person or deity but with the structure of fate itself. This variant produces characters who appear fearless because they believe their survival is guaranteed until their destined purpose is fulfilled.

*SCI characters frequently:* experience devastating ideological rupture when the covenant's source is revealed to be morally compromised (the deity is cruel, the ancestor was a murderer, the Dao-alignment was a manipulation); produce intractable conflicts because they cannot be argued or bought — only the covenant terms can motivate them; occasionally find their covenant-following produces outcomes that appear miraculously aligned.

### 2.2 — Compound Ideology Profiles

Most significant characters hold compound ideologies — two or three categories in a specific hierarchy. The hierarchy determines which ideology governs when the categories come into conflict.

**Compound Ideology Profile Structure:**
- **Primary Category** — governs by default; provides the foundational framework for all evaluation
- **Secondary Category** — governs when the primary category does not directly address the situation, or modifies how the primary category is expressed
- **Tertiary Category** — provides the aesthetic or emotional coloring of ideology without directly governing decisions

**Example Compound Profiles:**

| Profile Name | Primary | Secondary | Tertiary | Character Type |
|-------------|---------|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Righteous Warrior | JCI | LBI | PCI | A cultivator who pursues justice within the framework of personal loyalty; will ultimately choose justice when the two conflict |
| Calculating Patriarch | SDI | OMI | LBI | A sect leader who maintains order through power while using loyalty language — power is real; order is the method; loyalty is the performance |
| Driven Avenger | VAI | ADI | SDI | A revenge-seeker who has organized their entire cultivation advancement around the VAI goal; strength is the tool |
| Transcendent Scholar | TSI | TRI | HLI | A cultivator who pursues cosmic truth with intellectual discipline but takes genuine pleasure in the process |
| Loyal Guardian | LBI | PCI | OMI | An elder whose loyalty to their master organizes their protective drive; they will protect their ward even against their ward's explicit wishes |
| Revolutionary Truth-Seeker | TRI | FSI | JCI | A character who finds truth regardless of institutional authority; their freedom is in service to accuracy |
| Holy Avenger | SCI | VAI | JCI | A covenant-bound character whose sacred terms include vengeance against specific enemies; they experience their violence as divine service |

### 2.3 — Ideological Polarity

Every ideological position has a **polarity**: the degree to which it is held as absolute versus provisional.

| Polarity Level | Description | Narrative Characteristic |
|---------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Polarity-1 (Fluid)** | The ideology is held as the best current framework, consciously provisional | Character frequently questions and examines their own positions |
| **Polarity-2 (Settled)** | The ideology is held with conviction but can be updated through sufficient evidence or experience | Character holds their ground but is genuinely persuadable |
| **Polarity-3 (Anchored)** | The ideology is held as fundamental; updating it requires significant narrative events (PIS-6+) | Character resists ideological change; it takes major experiences to shift them |
| **Polarity-4 (Crystallized)** | The ideology is held as absolute within specific domains; those domains cannot be entered by contrary argument | Character has ideological no-go zones that produce categorical responses regardless of argument quality |
| **Polarity-5 (Absolutist)** | The entire ideology is held as categorically true; no evidence or argument can enter | Character is doctrinaire; ideological challenge produces hostility or violence |

High polarity is not inherently villainous. A Polarity-5 SCI character who has covenanted with a genuinely moral cosmic principle may be the most reliably virtuous character in the narrative. A Polarity-1 SDI character may be the most adaptable and survivable. Polarity describes *rigidity of belief*, not quality of belief.
