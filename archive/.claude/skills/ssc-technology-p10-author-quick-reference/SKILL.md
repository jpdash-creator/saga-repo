## Area 15 — Author Quick Reference

### 15.1 — The Five Science Questions

Before writing any technology advancement, discovery, or scientific milestone in the narrative, answer these five questions:

**Question 1: What tier is this technology, and what are its scientific prerequisites?**
Consult Part 10, Areas 3 and 4. Identify the specific scientific understanding required for the technology being depicted.

**Question 2: Does the civilization possess the required scientific prerequisites?**
If yes: technology is buildable (pending Part 9 resource and institutional prerequisites). If no: the technology cannot be reproduced, repaired, or independently advanced by this civilization — only operated if transferred from elsewhere.

**Question 3: How was the scientific prerequisite achieved (or how is it being achieved)?**
Which growth mechanism (Part 9, Area 2)? Over what timeline? With what cultivation contributions (Part 10, Area 7)? A scientific discovery with no plausible backstory is a canonical violation.

**Question 4: What scientific discoveries are currently in progress?**
Every civilization of narrative significance should have ongoing scientific investigations. What anomalies are they accumulating? What paradigm tension exists? What breakthrough is approaching? Science is never static.

**Question 5: For AI or digital consciousness — what sentience classification applies?**
Consult Part 10, Area 6. Identify the AI's S-classification. Check joint Part 4 governance requirements if S3+.

### 15.2 — Four Common Science Scenarios

**Scenario A: Civilization acquires FTL technology through transfer**
Governance: Part 9, Area 2.4 Type 4a applies. The civilization can OPERATE the FTL technology. It cannot reproduce it (Part 8, P8-5.2). It cannot repair it to full specification. It cannot advance beyond it. To achieve independent FTL capability, the civilization must independently satisfy Part 10, Area 5 prerequisites. Timeline for independent FTL prerequisite from the point of acquired-technology inspiration: typically 20–50% compressed from standard timeline because the existence proof reduces the anomaly-detection phase and the theoretical framework phase — they know FTL is possible; they must derive the why independently.

**Scenario B: Cultivation-assisted science acceleration**
Governance: Part 10, Area 7 (what the cultivation contribution produces); Part 9, Area 2.3 and 6.6 (timeline effects); Part 31, Area 3.5 (synergy output). The author must specify: which domain of science is being accelerated, what realm of practitioner is contributing, and what specific observation is being made. A Xiantian practitioner providing soul-sense data for atomic-scale materials science is appropriate; claiming a Xiantian practitioner is accelerating FTL physics is not (Xiantian soul-sense does not resolve at the spacetime geometry level required for FTL contribution).

**Scenario C: AI approaches S3 threshold**
Governance: Part 10, Area 6.2 S2→S3 transition. The narrative must show: the specific Level 5 behaviors that trigger S2 classification; the scientific community's formal assessment protocols; the debate about whether S3 threshold has been crossed; the societal response when S3 is confirmed. This is a civilization-level narrative event; it cannot be treated as background lore or a single-scene discovery.

**Scenario D: Civilization attempts to build technology above its scientific tier**
Governance: Part 8, P8-5.2; Part 10, Area 3. The result: the technology cannot be built. The civilization may attempt it — resource investment may be made, research programs launched — but without the scientific prerequisites, the attempts will fail. The failures will (at best) accelerate the accumulation of anomalies in Phase 2 of the Anomaly-Paradigm Cycle (Area 2.3), eventually contributing to the paradigm shift needed. At worst, the failures are simply wasted resources. The narrative value of a civilization straining against its scientific ceiling is significant — it is a productive tension that drives character and plot.
