Sacred Ground Divine Liquid formation (Part 11 Area 2.5) is a major EA advancement trigger |
| Canon-Part 12 | Part 12 informs Part 21 EA and SA | Part 12 Formation Arrays of Tier 4 contribute to civilization EA by creating Sacred Ground-approximation ambient energy environments; Part 12 Technology-Relic Hybrid Classes are SA advancement infrastructure; Part 12 governs the objects; Part 21 uses the civilizational infrastructure they constitute as EA and SA inputs |
| Canon-Part 13 | Part 21 receives LA from Part 13 — Active | Part 13 v3.0 cosmological hierarchy (Village through The Primordium / Absolute Chaos) and classification tables are the reference frame for LA axis values. Part 13 World Type Taxonomy (Area 9) contributes to civilizational type context. Sacred Ground governance (Part 13 Area 14) drives CA and EA advancement. Part 21 LA axis table (Area 2.4) directly references Part 13 v3.0 location classifications: Village, Town, City, Capital, Kingdom, Empire, World, Star System, Star Cluster, Star Quadrant, Galaxy, Galaxy Cluster, Galaxy Supercluster, Universe, Multiverse, The Primordium / Absolute Chaos |
| Canon-Part 14 | Part 21 receives distance-scale from Part 14 — Active | Part 14 v3.0's Cosmic Measurement Scale (CMS) provides the canonical distance vocabulary for LA axis descriptions. FTL distance governance (Part 14 P14-FTL-1 through P14-FTL-4) creates the hard LA-8 gate (Rule 18.2.1). Communication lag governance (Part 14 Area 5.2) shapes LA administrative reality — CCT-9+ civilizations must design governance around FTL communication latency. CMS units referenced: meter (m), kilometer (km), Megameter (Mm), Gigameter (Gm), Astronomical Unit (AU), light-second, light-minute, light-hour, light-day, light-week, light-year (ly), kilolight-year (kly), megalight-year (Mly), gigalight-year (Gly), trans-universal / Primordial scale |
| Canon-Part 22 | Bidirectional — Active | Part 22 governs factions; Part 21 governs civilizations. The boundary between powerful faction and independent civilization (Area 20) is jointly governed. Faction Power Tier (CTD as proxy) uses Part 21 CTDs but is governed contextually by Part 22. Inter-civilization diplomatic and military governance (Part 22's faction-to-faction framework applied at civilizational scale) |
| Canon-Part 23 | Part 21 governs ceiling for Part 23 — Active | CCT constraints on faction affiliation (Part 23 Area 20) use Part 21's CCT framework as the civilizational ceiling — factions cannot maintain affiliation structures that exceed the maximum complexity supportable by the civilization hosting them; a CCT-3 civilization cannot host AFF-1 Absolute Alliance structures between galaxy-scale factions. Civilizational war (Part 21 Area 16) forces mass affiliation declarations — when two civilizations enter open conflict, all factions within them are compelled toward affiliation spectrum positions that match or exceed the civilizational conflict level. The Three-Tier Gap Rule (Part 21 Area 17) governs the asymmetric power dynamics in trans-scale affiliation — a faction affiliated with a civilization 3+ CCT tiers above it is structurally incapable of equal-footing diplomacy, making all such affiliations tributary, vassal, or puppet structures by default. Part 21 CTD progression creates the civilizational context within which affiliation networks evolve — a civilization advancing from CCT-5 to CCT-6 restructures the faction affiliation web within it |
| Canon-Part 25 | Bidirectional — Active | Part 21 governs civilizational structure; Part 25 governs cultural expression of that structure. CCT tier directly determines Cultural Paradigm Class (CPC-1 through CPC-15): CTD-1 Subsistence generates CPC-1 survival-collective culture; CTD-5 Multi-World generates CPC-5 through CPC-7 expansionist-pluralist culture; CTD-10 Clusteral generates CPC-10 through CPC-12 cosmopolitan-synthetic culture; CTD-15+ Liminal-Universal generates CPC-13 through CPC-15 cosmos-philosophical culture. Civilization Type determines canonical cultural temperament: CS (Cultivation Specialist) civilizations trend CPT-1 Hierarchical-Sacral or CPT-6 Theocratic-Covenant; TS (Technology Specialist) civilizations trend CPT-2 Meritocratic-Martial or CPT-3 Mercantile-Exchange; BP (Balanced Practitioner) civilizations trend CPT-5 Syncretic-Adaptive; ES (Exploration Specialist) civilizations generate CPT-5 hybrid cultures at contact zones; LS (Legacy Specialist) civilizations trend CPT-4 Isolationist-Purity or CPT-6 Theocratic-Covenant; CP (Conquest/Predatory) civilizations trend CPT-2 Meritocratic-Martial or CPT-7 Anarchic-Sovereign. Five Axes profile shapes cultural value priorities: CA axis dominance produces cosmopolitan cultural breadth (higher CE tolerance); TA axis dominance produces innovation-permissive culture; EA axis dominance produces resource-custodian culture; LA axis expansiveness produces multicultural governance; SA axis dominance produces techno-cultural hybrid identity. CTD advancement transitions are Part 25 Cultural Inflection Events — each CTD threshold crossed triggers cultural recalibration of social class axes (CC-Class, W-Class, L-Class, I-Class, A-Class, Ac-Class) and CPT stability review. Civilizational regression (Part 21 Area 11) forces Cultural Mourning (Part 25 Area 10) at civilizational scale |
| Canon-Part 16 | Part 21 informs Part 16 | CCT tier and Civilization Type determine the character classification landscape in Part 16 Area 9 — the ceiling on character advancement (cultivation realm, technology tier, military rank, sect hierarchy position) is determined by the civilization's CCT and axis ratings; CS-Type civilizations generate cultivation-primary character profiles; TS-Type civilizations generate technology-primary character profiles; BP-Type civilizations generate hybrid character profiles. Part 16 informs Part 21: the character classification system provides narrative texture for each CTD tier's population — the character types that a CCT-5 Multi-World civilization produces are qualitatively different from those a CCT-2 Formative civilization produces, and Part 16's character taxonomy is the reference for that population description |
| Canon-Part 17 | Part 21 informs Part 17 | The CCT tier of a hero's home civilization is tracked in the Hero Ledger and shapes three structural conditions of the hero arc: (1) the available power ceiling (realm and tech tier) accessible within that civilization; (2) the type of factions the hero can join and the maximum rank achievable; (3) the scale of civilizational events that can function as Hero Ledger milestones; civilizational collapse, transition, and major advancement events experienced by the hero are Hero Ledger Section 10 Canon Events; a hero whose home civilization is destroyed faces CSS-9 to CSS-10 consequence events and IE-7 ideology collapse — both Part 27 and Part 20 governance events that feed back into the Hero Ledger |
| Canon-Part 20 | Part 21 informs Part 20 | Civilizational context shapes local ideological formation (Part 20 Area 4.1 — local ideology shapers); CCT tier determines the ideological environment characters are formed in; civilizational collapse events are IE-7 (Ideology Collapse) potential events for characters whose ideology was organized around the civilization's continued existence; civilizational-scale figures (government leaders, civilization founders) require full Part 20 Ideology Ledger entries because their ideology directly shapes civilizational governance |
| Canon-Part 26 | Part 21 feeds Part 26 | Civilizational-scale psychological events — witnessing civilizational collapse (PIS-9), Hold regression as a character's lived experience, surviving a civilization-extinction event — are Part 26 psychological impacts triggered by Part 21 civilizational state events; the psychological burden of leadership at civilizational scale (Part 26 Area 5.3) is a joint governance domain; Part 21 governs the civilizational event; Part 26 governs the character's psychological experience of it |
| Canon-Part 27 | Part 21 feeds Part 27 | CCT regression events, civilization-extinction events, Sacred Ground destruction, and civilizational collapse are consequence events governed by Part 27; Part 21 classifies the civilizational state; Part 27 governs consequences |
| Canon-Part 28 | Part 28 governs exceptions | Apex Civilizations (CCT-15+, Liminal-Universal and above) as direct narrative participants, CCT gaps exceeding 5 in direct interaction, and any CCT claim above CCT-15 as a story-active entity require Part 28 authorization |
| Canon-Part 30 | Part 30 informs Part 21 | Part 30 (Novel Macro Story Law) provides story rhythm guidance on civilizational scale escalation; Part 21 governs the in-world structural reality of civilization tiers; Part 30 guides when CCT-level revelations are narratively appropriate |
| Canon-Part 31 | Bidirectional — Active | Part 31's civilization tier synergy access table (Area 2.1) uses Part 21 CTDs as primary reference (Area 15, Rule 2.1.3 deference now resolved). SA axis (Area 2.5) is calibrated to Part 31 integration models. Part 21 CCT is Part 31's primary civilization tier input for synergy category gating. Part 31 governs what integration produces; Part 21 governs what tier the civilization has achieved |
| Canon-Part 32 | Part 32 ↔ Part 21 — Active | Part 32 (Technology — Vehicle Law — fully authored, Areas 1–17) connects to Part 21 through the TA (Technology Axis) relationship: CCT tier determines vehicle production capacity ceiling per the Technology Ceiling law (Part 8, P8-2). A civilization's vehicle fleet tier distribution (the proportion of ST-Band, OS-Band, IS-Band, GT-Band, and CT-Band vehicles in its total fleet) is a downstream expression of its TA axis — an IS-Band (Tier 8+) fleet is only achievable by a civilization with TA axis at Tier 8+ (CTD-8 Interstellar). Part 21 SA (Synergy Axis) is calibrated to Part 31 integration models; CHV vehicle class availability (CHV-A through CHV-E) is an observable indicator of SA axis level — the appearance of CHV-D Synergy-Class vehicles in a fleet indicates Model C/D synergy infrastructure, which corresponds to Part 21 SA-15+ range. Vehicle fleet quality as a civilization metric is a Part 21 TA-axis reading instrument: a civilization whose most advanced vehicles are OS-Band (Tier 5–7) is at CTD-6/7 Solarian/Stellar range on the TA axis; a civilization with GT-Band vehicles (Tier 11–14) is at CTD-11/12 Galactic/Intergalactic range. Three-Tier Gap Rule in Part 21 (Rule 10.1.1) applies to vehicle fleet asymmetries: a CTD-11 civilization's GT-Band fleet against a CTD-7 civilization's IS-Band fleet creates a vehicle technology asymmetry that Part 21 frames as a Three-Tier gap with all its inter-civilization interaction consequences |
| Canon-Part 18 | Part 21 informs Part 18 | Villain power base is always calibrated against the civilization tier of their operational context; a villain controlling a CTD-10 Clusteral civilization represents a structurally different threat than one operating at CTD-5 Multi-World; villain CCT context governs what technology tiers (Part 8), weapon classes (Part 33), and military ranks (Part 16) are available to them; civilizational-scale villains (those who threaten entire CCT tiers) require Part 21 structural analysis of what the villain controls and what collective security response (Part 21 Area 12 collective security protocols) they would face; villain WS-VI+ weapon deployment requires Part 21 civilization-level Absolute Authorization just as any actor does — villain status does not grant exception; villain defeat at civilizational scale produces Part 21 CCT-recalibration events for the affected regions |
| Canon-Part 19 | Part 21 informs Part 19 | Side characters are products of their civilization tier context; a Tier A mentor from a CTD-10 Clusteral civilization has access to cultivation, technology, and social resources qualitatively unavailable to a mentor from CTD-4; side character classification (Part 16 / Part 3 / Part 8) is calibrated against the civilization tier they were formed in; side characters who represent their civilization to the hero (World Carrier and Information Carrier narrative functions from Part 19) embody Part 21 civilization-tier knowledge and culture; civilizational collapse events (CSS-9 to CSS-10) that destroy or transform a Tier A side character's home civilization are Part 21 events that produce Part 26 identity crisis and Part 19 narrative disruption simultaneously |
| Canon-Part 24 | Part 24 ↔ Part 21 — Bidirectional Active | CCT tier determines information infrastructure capability: a CTD-1 Subsistence civilization has access to ITC-3 courier and ITC-7 ambient leakage only; a CTD-5 Multi-World civilization develops ITC-2 Formation Relay Networks; a CTD-8 Interstellar civilization deploys ITC-4 technology communication channels at interstellar range; a CTD-10 Clusteral civilization operates ICR synergy communication systems. Part 24 Area 9 Technology Communication tier table (Tiers 1–5) maps directly to Part 21 CTD levels — the communication infrastructure tier a civilization operates is a CCT-dependent capability. Civilization type determines information ecology (canonical IE definitions in Part 24 Area 12.1): Part 21 Technology Specialist (TS) civilizations tend to IE-4 Institutional Controlled Flow (central tech institution controls info channels); Cultivation Specialist (CS) civilizations tend to IE-2 Hierarchical Flow (cultivation rank = information access tier); Balanced Practitioners (BP) civilizations tend to IE-3 Faction-Siloed Flow (information freely circulates within factions but restricted between them); ES and CP civilizations trend toward IE-3; LS civilizations trend toward IE-4 or IE-2. At CCT regression (Part 21 Area 11), a civilization's information infrastructure degrades with the TA and SA axes — lost communication technology creates a Part 24 forced lag category increase, producing governance fragmentation as a civilizational consequence. Civilizational intelligence apparatus scale and capability (Part 24 Area 14 Six-Phase Intelligence Cycle operational depth) is directly proportional to CCT tier — a CTD-3 Industrial civilization cannot maintain a multi-star intelligence apparatus; a CTD-10 Clusteral civilization maintains integrated civilization-wide intelligence operations |
| Canon-Part 29 | Part 21 → Part 29 | Civilization advancement axis (TA) determines cultural speech ceiling — low-TA civilizations may lack vocabulary for high-tier concepts, producing dialogue gaps; civilization-level political speech follows F1 Ceremonial register for inter-civilization address; Absolute Authorization events (WS-VI+) require formal declaration dialogue per Part 29 Area 5. |
