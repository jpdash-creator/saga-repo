## Area 14 — Interface with Part 21, 22, and 25 (Society and Culture)

### 14.1 — Science and Civilization Tier

Part 21 (Civilization Law — fully authored, Areas 1–22) governs overall civilization tier classification via the CCT synthesis formula. Part 10 provides the science-axis input to Part 21's classification: a civilization's scientific sophistication (what tier of scientific prerequisites it has satisfied) is one indicator of its advancement, contributing to the technology-axis input (TA) alongside the physical infrastructure indicators governed by Part 8 and Part 9.

### 14.2 — Science and Faction Politics

Part 22 (Faction Law — fully authored, Areas 1–30) governs the political structures within civilizations. Part 10 informs Part 22: scientific knowledge is politically contested at every tier. The control of high-tier scientific knowledge — the theoretical framework for FTL, the understanding of AI sentience thresholds, the cultivation-science interface knowledge from high-realm practitioners — constitutes political power. Factions that control access to scientific training, research funding, and knowledge transmission systems have structural advantage over factions that do not. Part 10 establishes what scientific knowledge is significant; Part 22 governs the political governance of that significance.

### 14.3 — Science and Cultural Values

Part 25 (Culture and Social Law — fully authored, Areas 1–28) governs cultural attitudes. Part 10 informs Part 25: civilizations' cultural relationships to science vary enormously. Some civilizations treat science as the primary cultural value — the highest expression of civilization achievement. Others treat science as a tool subordinate to cultivation, religion, or tradition. These cultural attitudes directly affect the institutional investment and incentive structures (Area 2.2 Factor 4) that determine scientific advancement rate. Part 25 governs the cultural attitudes; Part 10 governs their scientific consequences.
