## Area 9 — Side Character Lifecycle Law

### 9.1 — Side Character Introduction Protocol

**Rule 19.9.1.1 — Side Characters Are Introduced With Sufficient Context:** When a Tier A side character is introduced, the narrative establishes: who they are (Part 16 classification), what they want (ideology summary), and why they are in this scene at this moment (faction/location context). This does not require an info-dump — it requires that the character's specificity is communicated through their behavior, speech, and context within the first two or three scenes of their introduction.

**Rule 19.9.1.2 — Side Characters Are Not Introduced and Forgotten:** A Tier A or Tier B side character who is introduced with narrative investment and then does not appear for extended stretches of the narrative without explanation is a canon continuity issue. If a significant side character disappears from the story, the narrative must account for their absence.

### 9.2 — Side Character Evolution

**Rule 19.9.2.1 — Tier Transitions Are Permitted:** A character who begins as Tier C may become Tier A if the story organically develops them. A character who began as a named soldier and has, through narrative events, become a significant relationship to the hero, must be upgraded to the appropriate tier with retroactive governance applied (their ideology and psychology must be established consistently with their past behavior).

**Rule 19.9.2.2 — Narrative Function Evolution Is Permitted:** A Tier A side character may shift primary narrative function. A character who begins as a Mentor may become a Consequence Witness when their teaching is done and their own story becomes about the costs of their past. A character who begins as an Ally may become a Mirror when their diverging choices force the hero to examine the difference between them.

### 9.3 — Side Character Removal Law

**Rule 19.9.3.1 — Side Character Removal Must Be Governed:** A Tier A side character is not removed from the story without governance. Removal may occur through: death (Part 27 CSS event, Part 26 PIS event for connected characters), exile or departure (CSS event for the side character, relational consequence for all connected), transformation (the character is fundamentally altered in a way that ends their previous narrative identity), or natural arc completion (their story thread resolves).

**Rule 19.9.3.2 — Unresolved Side Character Threads Are Prohibited:** A Tier A side character whose narrative thread is introduced but never resolved — a mentor who is established and then forgotten, an ally relationship that is built and then dropped without explanation — is an unresolved thread that violates canon continuity.

**Rule 19.9.3.3 — Side Character Deaths Must Have Appropriate Narrative Weight:** A Tier A side character cannot die off-screen or be killed in passing narrative. Their death requires a scene given sufficient weight to honor what was built in the character. The weight of the scene must match the depth of governance invested in the character.
