## Area 24 — Narration Standards for Cultural Scenes

### 24.1 — N25-1: Cultural Detail Is Mandatory in Every Social Scene

Every scene involving two or more characters interacting socially must include at minimum one culturally specific detail: an honorific, a gesture, a taboo navigated or violated, a dress code element, or a social convention reference. Cultural scenes that contain no cultural specificity are in violation of this standard.

### 24.2 — N25-2: Cultural Conflict Must Be Accurately Attributed

When two characters from different cultural backgrounds misunderstand each other, the narrative must accurately portray which specific cultural difference produced the misunderstanding. "Cultural difference" as a vague narrative device is insufficient. Authors must identify which CPT, which Class Axis, or which specific social convention is the source of the conflict.

### 24.3 — N25-3: Cultural Change Must Be Earned

When a character changes their cultural orientation — adopts a new cultural paradigm, abandons a previous one, achieves genuine cross-cultural understanding — this change must be earned through narrative experience, not asserted by authorial decree. Cultural transformation requires: exposure to the new cultural form (in depth, not surface contact); challenge to existing cultural framework (a significant experience that cannot be accommodated by the old framework); negotiation period (the character working through the conflict between old and new frameworks); and resolution (genuine synthesis, rejection of one framework, or productive holding of the tension).

### 24.4 — N25-4: Portraying Cultural Hierarchy Without Endorsing It

Authors portraying CPT-1 or CPT-6 cultural hierarchies must accurately render the hierarchy as it functions within the culture — including its power to harm as well as its power to organize — without either endorsing the hierarchy as just or dismissing it as simply oppressive. Cultural hierarchies are functionally coherent even when morally problematic; both truths must be present in the narrative.

### 24.5 — N25-5: Oral Tradition as Primary Source

When a scene includes an oral tradition practitioner (bard, elder storyteller, griot, cultural keeper), the narrative must treat their transmission as a primary cultural authority within their community — not as a curiosity or decorative element. What the oral tradition says IS what the community believes to be true; the author may know otherwise, but the community does not.

### 24.6 — N25-6: Death of Culture Is Not Neutral

Any narrative event that results in cultural death — the destruction of the last oral tradition carrier, the burning of a Chronicle, the forced assimilation of a cultural community — must be treated with narrative gravity equivalent to physical death at the same scale. Cultural death of a village-scale tradition is equivalent to significant individual death; cultural death of a civilizational tradition is a civilizational catastrophe.

### 24.7 — N25-7: Honorifics Must Be Consistent

Once an honorific system is established for a faction or civilization, it must be applied consistently throughout all subsequent scenes involving that faction. Authors cannot retroactively change honorific systems; changes must be narratively justified (political change, cultural revolution, character development).

### 24.8 — N25-8: Hybrid Cultures Have Their Own Integrity

When writing a hybrid cultural character (CPT-2 + CPT-4; Location Axis dominant + Cultivation Axis dominant), the hybrid is not a contradiction to be resolved but an authentic cultural identity to be rendered with internal coherence. Hybrid characters are not "confused" — they are navigating a legitimately complex cultural inheritance.

### 24.9 — N25-9: Integration Culture Requires Specificity

When writing characters from civilizations that have integrated cultivation and technology (Part 31), the cultural specificities of their integration context must be portrayed accurately: which CPT governs their response to integration; which Integration Model level they or their faction has achieved; whether they are in cultural acceptance or cultural resistance regarding integration. "Integrated civilization" is not a monolithic cultural form.
