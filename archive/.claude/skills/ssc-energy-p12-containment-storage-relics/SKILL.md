## Area 9 — Containment and Storage Relics

### 9.1 — CPM Containment Requirements

High-grade CPM substances impose specific containment requirements because their energy density exceeds what ordinary materials can sustain without beginning to absorb and degrade the substance (or be degraded themselves).

**Containment by Substance State:**

| Substance State (Part 11) | Containment Requirement | Reason |
|--------------------------|------------------------|--------|
| Power Crystal (Grades 1–5) | Standard material (metal box, stone vault) | Stable solid; low energy pressure |
| Gem (Grades 6–10) | Qi-neutral material or Tier 1 containment object | Energy field interacts with ordinary metal over time |
| Shard (Grades 11–14) | Tier 2 containment object | Energy pressure begins to affect structural materials |
| Core substance (Grades 15–19) | Tier 3 containment object minimum | Core-density energy will break down ordinary materials within days |
| Divine Liquid (Grade 20) | Tier 3–4 containment relic specifically designed for Divine Liquid | As defined in Part 11, Area 5.5 — ordinary containers degrade immediately |

**Rule 9.1.1 — Divine Liquid Containment Vessels Are Tier-Classified Artifacts.**
Divine Liquid containment vessels are among the most precisely crafted of all utility relics. The vessel must be constructed from materials that do not absorb Divine Liquid energy — which eliminates most naturally occurring metals and stones. The confirmed material classes are:
- Core substance-grade base materials (Tier 3 minimum)
- Naturally Sacred-Ground-formed crystal (historically imprinted Class I Relic repurposed as vessel)
- Specific rare materials whose origin is Divine Realm beast organ or bone

The vessel must also be sealed with inscription that prevents leakage (Divine Liquid will escape through any insufficiently sealed boundary over time) and that prevents the vessel from becoming destabilized by the enclosed energy.

### 9.2 — Spatial Storage Artifacts (Deep Rules)

Spatial storage artifacts (storage rings, spatial bags, dimensional pouches) are governed by the following additional rules beyond the Tier classification in Area 5.1:

**Time in Storage:**
Objects stored in Tier 1 spatial artifacts experience real time. A living being placed in a Tier 1 storage artifact will suffocate; food will spoil; CPM will gradually interact with the stored environment.

Tier 2 spatial artifacts can maintain stable, suspended conditions — time effectively stops within the dimensional pocket in terms of biological processes. Living beings cannot survive long storage in most Tier 2 artifacts (the environment requires active energy management for life-support), but CPM, objects, and preserved specimens can be stored indefinitely.

Tier 3 and above spatial artifacts contain genuine extradimensional space that can support independent life. The interior may have its own ambient energy class, its own atmospheric conditions, and — in the most sophisticated constructs — its own formation arrays.

**Rule 9.2.1 — Spatial Artifacts Cannot Store Other Spatial Artifacts of Equal or Higher Tier.**
Attempting to place a Tier 2 spatial artifact inside another Tier 2 spatial artifact creates a dimensional resonance conflict. The inner artifact destabilizes, potentially releasing its contents into uncontrolled dimensional space — a potentially lethal event for any stored living beings. Spatial artifacts may contain lower-tier spatial artifacts without conflict.

**Rule 9.2.2 — Spatial Artifacts and Living Beings.**
Storing a living being in a spatial artifact without their awareness and consent is one of the gravest violations in cultivation social law (Part 25) — equivalent to imprisonment. The physical mechanics do not prevent it; the legal and social consequences are severe (Part 27).

---

### 9.3 — Narration of Storage and Retrieval

Objects retrieved from spatial artifacts appear in prose with the same narrative weight as any other element — they are not narrated as "appearing from nowhere." The cultivator reaches into the artifact (touches the ring, opens the pouch, makes a gesture indicating access), and the object emerges. Cultivation readers understand the spatial storage mechanism; what matters is that the action is shown, not glossed over.
