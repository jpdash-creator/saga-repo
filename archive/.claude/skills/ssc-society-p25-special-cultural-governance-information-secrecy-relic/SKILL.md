## Area 26 — Special Cultural Governance: Information, Secrecy, and Relic Intersections

### 26.1 — Cultural Norms Around Information Rights

Part 24 governs the mechanics of information; this Part governs the cultural values that determine what information belongs to whom by right:

| Cultural Frame | Information Ownership Model | Right to Information | Violation Category |
|---------------|---------------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| CPT-1 Hierarchical-Sacral | Hierarchy determines access | Superiors have right to know subordinates' affairs; inferiors have no right to superiors' | Inappropriate inquiry is insolence |
| CPT-2 Meritocratic-Martial | Earned intelligence is owned | Those who acquired intelligence through capability own it | Information theft equals combat theft |
| CPT-3 Mercantile-Exchange | Information is purchasable property | Whoever paid for information owns it | Unauthorized use is contract violation |
| CPT-4 Isolationist-Purity | Community information is community property | Outsiders have no right to any internal information | External inquiry is invasion |
| CPT-5 Syncretic-Adaptive | Information flows toward utility | Information belongs to those who can best use it | Hoarding valuable information without sharing it |
| CPT-6 Theocratic-Covenant | Sacred information is covenant-protected | Only covenant members have right to sacred information | Unauthorized disclosure is blasphemy |
| CPT-7 Anarchic-Sovereign | Information is sovereign individual property | Each sovereign owns their own information | Forced disclosure is sovereignty violation |

### 26.2 — Cultural Governance of Relic Inheritance

The cultural rules governing relic inheritance by CPT:

**CPT-1:** Relics transmit through master-disciple lineage. The senior surviving disciple of the deceased cultivator inherits cultivation relics. Family relics transmit through eldest lineage member. Conflict between sect lineage and family lineage is a primary CPT-1 narrative conflict.

**CPT-2:** Relics go to the strongest — combat determines inheritance. The most capable surviving practitioner earns the right to challenge for the relic. Inheritance-by-challenge must be announced and conducted formally.

**CPT-3:** Relics are commercial property. They may be sold, gifted for value, or inherited through legal will. Cultural respect for market-price valuation of relics; no intrinsic "belonging" to a lineage.

**CPT-4:** Relics are community property regardless of who holds them. No individual "owns" a community relic; they are custodians. Relic sale or removal from the community is cultural theft.

**CPT-5:** Relics follow the person who can learn most from them. A relic's cultural value is in its ability to teach. Relics should move to whoever will advance the tradition furthest.

**CPT-6:** Relics are covenant gifts. They belong to the institution (the temple, the order) not the individual. Individual ownership of a community relic is heresy in extreme CPT-6 cultures.

**CPT-7:** Relic ownership is sovereign — whoever holds it, owns it. The challenge is the only legitimate transfer mechanism outside of voluntary gift.
