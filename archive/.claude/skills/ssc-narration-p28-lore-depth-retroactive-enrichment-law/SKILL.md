## Area 11 — Lore Depth and Retroactive Enrichment Law

### 11.1 — Lore Depth as Creative Mandate

The world of the Starward Saga has a depth that no single novel volume will exhaust. Every location has a history. Every faction has a founding story, a defining crisis, a lineage of leaders. Every Named Move has an origin — who developed it, under what circumstances, what its failure cost, who inherited it and how they changed it. Every cultural custom has a reason — the historical event it commemorates, the fear it channels, the aspiration it expresses. Part 28 mandates that the author engage this depth wherever the prose benefits from it, and resists the temptation of shallow world-building in which the world only exists as far as the plot currently requires.

### 11.2 — Retroactive Enrichment Standards

When later prose enriches earlier established content, the enrichment must satisfy:

**Consistency:** The enrichment must not contradict anything established earlier. The history revealed in Volume 3 must be consistent with all implications of the content established in Volume 1.

**Organic Integration:** The enrichment must feel discovered rather than retrofitted. The reader must feel that this depth was always present in the world — the author was simply revealing what was already there. Retroactive enrichment that feels like a retcon is a narrative failure even when it does not constitute a Canon violation.

**Proportionality:** The depth of enrichment must be proportional to the narrative weight of the element being enriched. A minor location that appears in one scene does not require the same depth of historical enrichment as the hero's home sect. The author allocates enrichment resources proportionally.

**Consequence Preservation:** If earlier prose established consequences from an event, the retroactive enrichment of that event must be consistent with those consequences. If Volume 1 established that the founding of a sect involved a betrayal that still shapes the sect's internal culture, the Volume 3 history of that founding must be consistent with a betrayal having occurred.

### 11.3 — Ambiguity Clarification

Canon ambiguity — moments where the governing Parts do not fully specify behavior in a particular edge case — may be clarified by creative choice, and that clarification becomes Negotiable Canon once established. When a situation arises that no Part explicitly governs, the author applies the spirit of the most directly relevant Parts, makes a determination consistent with the full Canon, documents the determination as Negotiable Canon, and is thereafter bound by that determination in all similar situations.
