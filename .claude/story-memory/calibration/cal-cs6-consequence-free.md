On the first morning of the thaw Orley and his sister Fenna stood in the boatshed and argued about whether to sell the ferry. Orley wanted to sell it. Fenna wanted to keep it. They had argued about it every week since autumn.

Orley said the ferry lost money. Fenna said the ferry was their father's. Orley said their father was dead. Fenna said that was not the point. A gull walked along the rafter above them and looked down, and neither of them looked up.

At noon they stopped. Orley went out to mend the nets, as he did every day. Fenna swept the shed, as she did every day. Neither had changed a mind, nothing new had been said, and the ferry sat where it had sat that morning, unsold and unpatched. They would argue again next week.
