In the signal service of the northern coast, it was well established that a relay lamp could be read at four stations' distance in clear weather and at two in fog. The First Principle of the Lamp Code states that every message begins with a long flash, continues in groups of short flashes, and ends with a long flash and a pause. The groups stand for the letters of the coast alphabet, which has twenty-two letters, and each group is separated from the next by a pause of two breaths.

The service had adopted the code because earlier systems of flags failed in poor light. A relay station was kept by three lamp-keepers working in turns, and the lamp burned whale oil behind a lens of ground glass, so that the beam could be narrowed to carry farther. A keeper who misread a group was expected to flash the repeat signal, and a station that repeated more than it should was reviewed by the inspector.

Kess climbed the stair to the lamp room and took the night watch.
