The harvest came early that year, and the whole village turned out to bring it in. Men cut and women bound, and by dusk the fields lay bare. A field that is emptied is a field made ready.

By the second night the granary was full. Old Hesk counted the sacks twice and found the same number both times. A counted harvest is a promise, and a promise is only as good as its keeper.

On the third day the rain arrived, too late to spoil anything and too early to be welcome. The children ran out into it and the elders stayed under the eaves. Those who run toward weather are young, and those who wait for it are wise.

The storm moved on by evening, and the roads were mud, and the cart wheels cut deep. A road that is hard to leave is a road that is easy to return to.

Hesk locked the granary and walked home slowly, with his knees aching. What ends one season begins the next.
