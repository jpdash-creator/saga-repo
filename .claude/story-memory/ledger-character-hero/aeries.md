> ### THE ROLL AND THE BREAK — how a long baseline and a total-fragment register coexist. Drafting rule, binding. `[DERIVED]`
> Does the long unhurried baseline of element 6 make the humor fragments of element 2 **harder to spot**? The answer is **no. It makes them more conspicuous, and the two elements are load-bearing for each other.**
>
> **Why.** A fragment is not detected in the absolute. It is detected as a **departure from the shape the ear has already been given.** A baseline of short sentences supplies a weak contrast, because a fragment there differs from its neighbours by a word or two and reads as more of the same clipped texture. A baseline of long, rolling, fully governed clauses supplies the **strongest possible contrast**: the ear has been taught, sentence by sentence, that this speaker finishes everything he starts, and then a half-line lands with nothing holding it up. **The roll is what makes the break audible.** Element 6 is therefore the instrument by which element 2 is heard, and the containment that element 2 exists to make countable is only countable because element 6 has established what containment sounds like.
>
> **The usable drafting rule, in three clauses, all countable at Check #19:**
> 1. **Establish before you break.** In any scene where the humor fires, **at least one full rolling unqualified Aeries sentence lands before the first fragment.** Never open his page-time in a scene with the joke. The reader needs the shape in ear first, or the fragment reads as an author's shortcut rather than as a man setting something down.
> 2. **Break clean, not short.** The fragment must be **actually incomplete grammar**, not merely a brief sentence. A short finished sentence is not a fragment and does not fire element 2; it is just element 6 at its floor. If the line can be read aloud as a whole sentence, it has not broken.
> 3. **Close it again.** The roll returns immediately afterward, in the same speech turn where the scene allows. **The picking-up is the containment resuming**, and it is the half of the beat that carries the meaning. A humor register that never closes is a different character.
>
> **Failure modes an auditor should catch.** (i) A fragment with no rolling baseline anywhere near it: element 2 unearned, element 6 unwritten. (ii) A scene in which the roll never returns after the joke: the containment has been deleted rather than set down. (iii) Fragments arriving outside the humor: element 2 broken outright, and the drafting rule at element 2 applies. (iv) A short-but-complete sentence counted by a drafting agent as its Friction Floor beat: it is not one, and the Friction Floor material is registered separately in the note below.
