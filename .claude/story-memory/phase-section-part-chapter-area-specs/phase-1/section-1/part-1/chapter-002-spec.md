### Area 01 — Two Men at the Well
**Purpose:** The second line opens at first light on Day 1, the same first light the capital line has just had, in a village at the border of the Azure Sky Kingdom and at the edge of a great forest. Hercules's registered mind carries it: it leads with hands and loads and finds first who is struggling and what is being done. He is already at the Hedge Well, drawing water for the village's early needs, and Bison is already there on the open side of the well, a very large, quiet man in a long coat with a man's face, waiting to begin until Hercules has. Bison keeps well back from the well's mouth and its still water, one small care with no word on why, and Hercules does not say. The village (named plainly, Greenhollow Cross), the dark wall of the forest at its back (named plainly, Skyvein Forest) and the two men reach the reader through that work: the rope furred with frost, the weight of the bucket, the white light touching the forest crowns first and coming down the trunks slowly to the well's stones. The cover holds although only the two are there, and the two talk as they work, in plain words and freely, with no old word and no word of where they come from. The Area opens the scene that Area 02 finishes, and it ends on a small turn: the first villager, greeted and answered, asks for the heavy end of something that waits at the crossing, the cover takes over from the quiet, Hercules shoulders the load and goes toward the crossing, and something stays unsaid between the two men.
**Want, obstacle, stake:** Hercules wants the first heavy work of the day done clean and early, before the village wakes, because heavy work is how he gets through the early hours. The cover stands in the way: he may draw only what a very strong man could, and he keeps to it even with no one near. The stake is the cover itself, the one ordinary morning the two men have made, and Bison's place beside him. Planned turn: the first villager comes to the well, the cover takes over from the quiet, and her request for the heavy end of something sends him toward the crossing. Planned outcome: win, with something left unsaid.
