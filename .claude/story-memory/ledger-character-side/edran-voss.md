**3. HE CANNOT ASK — and he states the repayment as he accepts.** `[ASKED]`. **(a) THE MISSING GRAMMAR — a countable absence, not a tendency.** `[ASKED]`
- **THE VALUE.** No *could you*. No *would you mind*. **No request of any kind, ever, from anyone.** **The entire grammar of asking is absent from him**, and people who have known him for years have never once heard him make a request. `B39-L31-AWARE-99` registers why nobody has ever mentioned it to him: *aware of the giving and unaware of the refusing — he could describe every coat in the store and could not tell you that he has never once asked anybody for anything.*
- **THE COUNTABLE RULE. An auditor scans his lines for request constructions in one pass and the count is ZERO.** `[ASKED]` for the absolute; `[DERIVED]` for the word list, which is illustrative rather than exhaustive and is flagged. Barred in any sentence of his whose effect would be to solicit something for himself: *could you*, *can you*, *would you*, *will you*, *would you mind*, *would it be possible*, *if you could*, *if you would*, *I wonder whether you might*, *I was hoping*, *do you think you might*, *might I ask*, *may I ask*, *I would be grateful if*, *I need*, *I would like*, *I require*, *do me a favour*, *lend me*, *put in a word*, *keep a place for me*, *help me*, *please*, and every other construction that would leave him owing the listener.
- **⚠️ THE ONE REAL COLLISION IN THE FILE, AND THE TEST THAT RESOLVES IT.** `B39-L15-ORDER-99` registers **requests that are understood to be orders: the grammar of courtesy with the force of command.** That is courtesy grammar, and an auditor reading the word list above will find it there. **The two do not collide, and the test is not the words.**
  - **THE TEST, applied per sentence: DOES A YES CREATE A DEBT?** If the listener's agreement would leave **him** owing **them** something personally, the construction is **barred absolutely** — that is a request, whatever its politeness. If the sentence merely disposes of work the office already owns, and the listener is conferring nothing on him, it is **his ordinary command form and is lawful**, however courteously it is dressed.
  - **Worked.** *If you would have the Ember Reach returns on my desk before the session* (an audit example of a lawful order form, which establishes no scene, event, figure or fact) is an order in courtesy grammar: the clerk confers nothing, the office owns the work, no debt is created, lawful. The same courtesy frame turned toward a governor who would be doing him a personal service is **barred**, and no degree of politeness rescues it.
  - **⚠️ AND THE FAILURE A DRAFTING AGENT WILL ACTUALLY COMMIT is smuggling the second inside the first**: an office-shaped courtesy frame whose real object is a favour. **The test is what the sentence would create, not what mood or manners it wears.**
- **⚠️ THE SECOND COLLISION, REPORTED HONESTLY AND FLAGGED RATHER THAN SMOOTHED.** `B39-L18-COLLIDE-99` states that colliding oaths go to his principal and that *"it is the one place in his whole register where he asks for something"*; 11.6 row 6 repeats the phrasing. **The author's element 3(a) is absolute — no request of any kind, ever, from anyone — so the two texts must be read together.** `[DERIVED]`, flagged for author confirmation at Check #21.
  - **The reading this field adopts:** what `L18-COLLIDE-99` describes is **escalation, not solicitation.** He carries a matter that is **hers by office** to its owner. She confers nothing on him, he is left owing her nothing, and the debt architecture never engages — which is exactly why that conduct can sit in the same register as an immutable refusal to borrow. Under the test above, a yes creates no debt, so no request has occurred.
  - **What is barred even there:** the referral may not be written as *would you decide this for me* or any cousin of it. It is the placing of a matter on the desk that owns it.
  - **⚠️ Section 11's wording stands exactly as registered.** This is the Part 29 grammatical reading only, it is flagged, and if the author rules the other way the affected material is element 3(a)'s absolute and this bullet alone.

**(b) THE ACCOUNT CLOSED BEFORE IT OPENS.** `[ASKED]`
- **THE VALUE.** On the rare occasion he takes something, **he names what he will give back and when, in the same breath, before the other person has finished offering.** `B39-L12-GIFT-99` registers the acceptance rule beneath it — *accepts the perishable, refuses the durable, and repays the perishable the same day, without exception* — and `B39-L08-GUILT-99` registers the same reflex in the adjacent case: *restitution arrives before the apology and frequently instead of it.*
- **IT IS NEITHER GRATITUDE NOR RUDENESS.** `[ASKED]` It is **a man closing an account before it can open.** A drafting agent must not soften it into thanks and must not sharpen it into a snub; the person offering is usually pleased, slightly wrong-footed, and unable to say why.
- **THE COUNTABLE FORM.** `[ASKED]` for the content; `[DERIVED]` for the turn-level mechanics, flagged. **Take any Voss acceptance. Two things are in the same speech turn as the acceptance: what he will give back, and when.** A repayment term stated in a later turn is the element broken. A repayment term left unstated is the element broken. **The when is specific**, because `B39-L12-GIFT-99` fixes it at *the same day* for anything he accepts at all.
- **⚠️ HOW "BEFORE THE OTHER PERSON HAS FINISHED OFFERING" IS RENDERED, WITHOUT BREAKING A REGISTERED DIMENSION.** `[DERIVED]` `B39-L10-INTERRUPT-99` registers that he **does not interrupt upward** and interrupts a clerk **for exactly one reason, which is to stop them apologizing.** A literal syntactic interruption for this purpose would break that dimension in both directions. **The correct rendering is turn-level pre-emption, not a cut-across:** his reply lands in the very next turn with no gap and with the terms already in it, so that the offer never finishes being an **offer** — the elaboration, the generosity, the *think nothing of it* never get to happen, because his terms are already on the table. **He does not cut across the sentence. ### 11.3 — Contradiction and Cost (B)

**Registered theme:** ***the twice-made man — everything he has, he built; and the pride that came with it breaks before it borrows.***

**Contradiction Pair CP-1 — THE GIVING AND THE REFUSING** `[DERIVED]` *(flagged for author confirmation at Check #21)*

**Collides:** he has built a world in which everybody may be helped except him, and he cannot see the shape of it from inside. It fires every time somebody who owes him tries to repay it, and every time the office needs him to accept something.
**Cost:** the people he has carried have no way to carry him, and eventually stop offering; the generosity that should have built him a network has built him a queue of grateful people who cannot reach him.
**Sub-optimality:** he carries loads that should be shared, and the office pays for the pride in bottlenecks that nobody is permitted to route around.

**Contradiction Pair CP-2 — THE POLISH AND THE PLATE** `[DERIVED]` *(flagged for author confirmation at Check #21)*

**Cost:** he is legible to nobody — the highborn read a self-made man, the quarter reads a court official, and neither reading lets anybody hand him anything.
