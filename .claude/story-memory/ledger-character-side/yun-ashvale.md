- **THE COUNTABLE RULE, IN TWO PARTS, BECAUSE A GRANDMASTER GIVES INSTRUCTIONS CONSTANTLY AND ONE PART IS ABSOLUTE WHILE THE OTHER IS SCOPED.** `[ASKED]` for part (i); `[DERIVED]` for part (ii)'s boundary, reasoned from `L08-LINE-99`'s registered scope and flagged for author confirmation.
  - **(i) PERMISSION AND PROHIBITION MODALS — BARRED ABSOLUTELY, IN EVERY REGISTER, TO EVERY LISTENER, ON EVERY SUBJECT.** In any sentence of hers whose subject is the listener or the listener's action, the count of the following is **zero**: *may*, *may not*, *must*, *must not*, *shall*, *shall not*, *cannot* used as prohibition rather than as physical fact, *you are not to*, *you are to*, *I forbid*, *I permit*, *I allow*, *I will not have you*, *you have my leave*, *you are cleared*, *you are not cleared*, *not until I say*, *you are ready* and *you are not ready* used as a ruling. **This half has no exceptions and no scope**, because she genuinely does not own the vocabulary. - **⚠️ THE BOUNDARY, WORKED OUT AND STATED EXPLICITLY, WITH ITS REASONING SHOWN SO THE AUTHOR CAN CHECK IT.** `[DERIVED]`
  1. **The registered scope of `L08-LINE-99` is an ownership scope, not a subject-matter scope.** Its wording is *"the crossing belongs to the crosser; **she will not take the choice from its owner**."* What the immutable forbids is therefore **taking a decision away from the person whose decision it is** — not speaking in the imperative, and not giving instructions.
  2. **Her own register already draws the line in the ledger's own words, which is why this boundary is stated rather than invented.** `B39-L06-DILEM-99`: ***"where the choice belongs to someone else, she returns it to them with everything she sees; where it is truly hers, she decides alone and lets no one learn it was close."*** That single dimension is the whole test, and Part 29 only has to make it audible.
  3. **THE ONE-QUESTION SEAM TEST, countable, applied per sentence: WHOSE BODY PAYS IF THE ANSWER IS WRONG?** If the cost of a wrong answer lands on **the listener's own foundation, body, cultivation or life**, the decision is **theirs** — Class A — and part (ii) bars the imperative absolutely. If the cost lands on **the halls, the schedule, the roster or the equipment**, the decision is **hers by office** — Class B — and the imperative is ordinary and lawful.
  4. **Class B may never be used to smuggle Class A, and this is the failure a drafting agent will actually commit.** *"Come at dawn"* is Class B. *"Come at dawn instead of crossing tonight"* is a crossing decision wearing operational clothes, and it is barred. **The test is what the sentence decides, not what mood the sentence is in.** A Class B imperative with a Class A purpose is a Class A sentence.
  5. **The pruning case, which looks like a contradiction and is not.** `B39-L03-ANGER-99` registers that a person, practice or condition that endangered the rest **is out of her halls**, without heat and without theater. That is an **institutional act that is hers by office**, announced in the indicative as a fact about the institution. **It is never a ruling on anybody's cultivation:** she does not tell a cultivator they may not cross; she declines to keep them inside her walls, and **the crossing remains theirs to make, elsewhere.** Class B, and it stays Class B precisely because it does not reach the choice.
  6. **The unlucky-day case, already registered, and it settles the boundary from the other direction.** `B39-L23-SUPER-99`: she treats certain days as unfit for crossings, calls it accumulated observation, dares anyone to argue — ***"and even then she only describes; the day is never forbidden."*** The strongest opinion she holds about a crossing still arrives as description. **That line is the boundary's own proof text.**
  7. **The combat case.** `B39-L25-OPEN-99` and `B39-L25-ESCAL-99` register that she names terms, escalations and the likely end before anything moves, each step stated before it comes. **That is the same mood doing the same work against an opponent**: *if this continues, the following happens.* It is a forecast, not an ultimatum, and it must not be written as one.
  8. **Negative description is not prohibition, and an auditor must not miscount it.** *The foundation does not set in one season* is indicative and lawful. *You may not cross* is prohibition and is barred. **The test is the grammatical subject and mood, never the sentiment**, and a sentence that is discouraging in effect is not thereby forbidding in form.
  9. **Physical necessity has no need of a modal in her mouth.** Where an ordinary speaker would reach for a deontic *must*, the future indicative already says it better and shorter: not *the foundation must be set first*, but *the foundation does not hold at that speed.* This is why the flat ban at part (i) costs her nothing expressively.
