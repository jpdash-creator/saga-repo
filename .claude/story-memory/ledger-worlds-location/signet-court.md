## Section 5 — Lore (Part 28 Area 17)

**Schema:** Part 28 Area 17.2 — Location Domain (palace audience hall / sub-location tier)

### 5.1 — Function and Jurisdiction

The Signet Court is the technology-petitions hall of the Royal Palace of Twin Courts and the seat of the Queen's half of Paired Legitimacy (cross-ref: ledger-people-society-faction/azure-sky-kingdom-state.md §7.2, §7.10). It is the signet side of the dual-signature machine: the counter-signature of sword-side orders is held in `azure-sky-kingdom-state.md` §7.10 and the metrology and certificate law in §7.11, and this file does not duplicate either. A short covered span joins it to the Sword Court; the span and the central reconciliation chamber are one structure, the Twin Courts Bridge (azure-sky-palace.md §1).

**Shown at Chapter 003 (Ref: 1.1.1.003; §5.2):** a working morning of this hall, with its furniture, its doors, its record-keeping and its staff of a clerk of record and two orderlies. **OPEN-AREA:** the hall's dimensions, archive and the rest of its procedures are not established here; no Area may treat any of them as already shown.

### 5.2 — The Hall in Use (axis 12; Chapter 003)

Shown on the morning of 1247.1.2 / PD 2 (Ref: 1.1.1.003). The text calls the hall "the Court" and "the hall", sets it across the covered span from the Sword Court, and puts the Queen at its table; it never gives the ledger name, and the identification rests on those three facts. Every item is a first-use fact and an instance, never a custom. The page's own plain words are given, with the ledger's term beside them where the ledger holds one.

- **Warmth.** The hall "was heated for the sake of its ink and wax and paper". The yard door lets the cold in with "a smell of wet horse", and the warmth takes it "a few paces in and no farther".
- **The yard door.** It has a doorsill, with the yard on its outside. Road and dispatch traffic come in through it (a rider from the east road, yard workers) and crates go out by it. The queue of the hurt stands along the wall inside it, and a stool stands by it where the Queen treats.
- **The span door.** The hall's door onto the covered span from the Sword Court (the text says "the span door" and "the far door"). The span's timber "answered boots", and the span is "colder than it looks from this side" (Queen Valia to the runner). A runner from the Sword Court crosses it at an even pace; the Grand Marshal's party comes over it at the end and stops together at the door.
- **The strips over the door.** Two strips run one above the other over "the door" (the text does not say which door): "the strip of writing" above and "the strip of plans" beneath (the palace's paired inscription bands, cultivation side above and signet side below; `azure-sky-palace.md` §5.1).
- **The stove.** On the north wall, with a shelf on which the cups stand; the straw from the crates is burned in it a handful at a time.
- **The working table.** A long table, with boards "scrubbed pale and sound" beneath it. The Queen sits at its head, "where the dais stood bare behind her". The dais is "a low platform made for a chair of state" and holds "nothing on it but a basket of lamp wicks". A petitioner perches at the table's corner nearest the stove and is given a cup. The cups are plain, "because the Court's cups were never anything else".
- **The record desk.** At the table's foot. The clerk of record, Tamsin Holt, sits at it, studies a paper between entries, pours the Queen's tea at the first bell, and makes the Court's record in "the Court's book", which has a clasp with "a small key". The clerk lays a sealed outgoing note in "the courier-flight tray" (the text does not place the tray); the Queen writes at the desk's corner "where the light was best"; her worn leather case lies at its far end; the slips that come in are squared on it.
- **The dispatch table.** At the yard door. Marshal-Lieutenant Isolde Farrow stands there, hand flat on an unopened packet in oilcloth; an orderly carries slips from it to the bench.
- **The shared bench.** By the north windows: "a long slab of scarred oak with a rack of tools along the back", where Grand Artificer Bramwell Cog has been at work "since before the yard door opened". The Court's sealed weights, "the first to the ninth", are kept on a shelf; a wire joint is tested by hanging them from it.
- **Light.** The high windows give light "without a direction". By mid-morning "the one patch of sun the high windows allowed" has crept to the foot of the working table, and the sun has reached the north windows.
- **Bells.** The hall keeps the first bell (tea poured), the second bell (a packet reaches the yard door; the last hoists are fitted before it) and the noon bell (the order for a brake is to be under ink and wax before it).
- **The Court's greeting.** Grand Marshal Kade gives it at the door: "the right hand open to the strip of writing over the door and to the strip of plans beneath it, then the shoulders, the left rising and staying, the right rising to meet it, both dropping"; "The room gave it back." The Sword Court's wording at the first morning is "the left rising and pausing at the top" (`azure-sky-palace.md` §5.6); the two wordings are recorded as the page gave them.
- **The queue.** The Queen "read the queue along the wall before she reached it, the worst-hurt first": a porter standing on one leg, a woman breathing through her mouth, a man nursing a wrist, two yard workers with fingers gone white to the knuckle. She takes a young man's hand ahead of the rest and tells the porter that he is next.
- **A finding sealed.** The king's finding of the day before arrives from the Sword Court in a flat leather folder, "with the king's ink dry at its foot"; the Queen warms "a stick of blue wax at the table lamp until it ran, set the drop beside the ink, and pressed her signet ring into it", and says "It is done in law ... now that the king's ink and my wax stand on one sheet and the clerk has put it in the book" (the doctrine and the mark: `azure-sky-kingdom-state.md` §7.10).
- **The round.** At mid-morning each day, "each of the queen's officers" says "what had changed since yesterday" and "the pot" goes from hand to hand (the custom: `azure-sky-kingdom-state.md` §7.12).

**Not established:** the hall's dimensions and the number of its windows; which door the two strips run over; the end of the hall at which each door lies; the hall's archive; who tends the stove; the staff beyond the clerk of record and the two orderlies; whether the greeting is given at every opening of the Court (OPEN-AREA).

**Narrative summary (as of Chapter 003).** The Signet Court is a heated hall with a yard door at one end, where the cold and a smell of wet horse come in and the queue of the hurt waits along the wall, and a door onto the covered span from the Sword Court at the other. A long working table runs between them, with a bare dais behind the Queen's seat at its head, a record desk at its foot where the clerk keeps the Court's book under a small key, a stove on the north wall, and a shared bench by the north windows where the Grand Artificer tests his work against the Court's sealed weights. On the morning of 1247.1.2 / PD 2 the Queen treats the worst-hurt first, hears a petition at the table, seals the king's finding beside his ink, and sits at the mid-morning round; the Grand Marshal comes in over the span at the end and the hall gives back his greeting (Ref: 1.1.1.003).
