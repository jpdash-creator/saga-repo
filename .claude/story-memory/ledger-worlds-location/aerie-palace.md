### 5.1 — Geography

The Aerie Palace stands on the western rise of the Aerie Plateau, where the high ground lifts out of the Stormbreak Foothills. It is built into and around one natural formation: a long spine of pale stone, carved and pierced by ages of wind, that stands clear of the plateau floor and takes the weather from every side. The palace has no walls. Its edge is set by the wind-cultivation wards, by the height of the stone and by the sworn Dragon-lineage cultivators who garrison it, and a visitor cannot say where the palace ends and the plateau begins. Below the spine lie terraced fields and herb-drying yards, and below the rise, on the plateau floor, the capital city, Highgale (named by the author's ruling of 2026-10-18, `[ASKED]`; about 12 km east-south-east of the palace, DIST-011, SKETCH; cross-ref: highgale.md). To the north the Windward Range stands over the palace and gives it its steady wind. To the east the plateau runs away toward the Tempest Valleys and the Long Coast. The plateau road ends at a wind gap in front of the stone; the way across the gap is the Wind Crossing (5.2). Anyone who can fly may come in over the stone and land on its terraces, so the Crossing is the petitioner's way and not the only way in. Distances from the palace to Azorion, the Twinpost, the plateau's edge and the Long Coast are held in the Distance Ledger (Registry B) and are not repeated here.

### 5.3 — Sensory Signature

The first thing a visitor meets is sound. The wind does not stop, and the palace is built so that it does not stop indoors: it whistles and hums through wind-apertures cut into the halls, a low steady note under a thin high one, so that no room is ever silent. The colors are storm-grey stone, wind-silver metal on lintels and banners, and dragon-green cloth, with a green lichen where the stone stays wet. The air is cold, thin and clean, with a mineral smell from the scoured rock and, when the wind comes off the plateau, the scent of storm-tempered herbs drying in the markets below. Banners and kite-tails crack. The one silence is the Silent Vigil, kept after a sovereign's death or a great national loss, when the bells are stilled and only the natural wind is allowed to carry sound. A cultivator who is not of the wind feels the Class 5 air as a faint wrongness, like standing with the back to an open door.

At the open edge "the air came up the cliff from the valleys in gusts loud enough that a man had to raise his voice at arm's length", and the sky goes "gold and flat over the valleys"; the watch stations of the ward-line are lit one by one for someone walking (§5.5). **Not established:** the palace's weather beyond this hour, and any danger from the edge to a visitor (OPEN-AREA).

### 5.5 — The Terrace, the Court and the Ward-Line as the Page Shows Them (Chapter 005; Ref: 1.1.1.005)

**The terrace and its open edge (Area blocks 03 to 06).** The palace's terrace is paved with flagstones and has a stair, a row of pillars where birds roost (they go out "all together" as the king walks up to the last petitioners, and the row is bare of birds at dusk), an open edge, the Stone Ring (§5.2) and the Court's furniture. The open edge is the king's place: "the high air was where he put the day down". He sets the point of his greatsword on the flags there, and the east wind stands across the whole terrace. A glider comes in from the west over the terraced fields and runs out its speed along the flags at the terrace's edge. The king's table stands "at the open side of the terrace, a plain plank table on trestles where the wind could reach it" (Area block 06).

**The house.** The queen places her house as "a table laid for supper": the king's place at the open edge, the Vigil's at the Stone, the fire in the east wing, and at the far end one place set for nobody. The doors of the house "opened onto the flags as every door in it did". The family wing lies along the ward-line beyond Elbow; the last door before the terrace stair is Prince Aegon's, which stands open on a dark room with the bed made flat, the shelf in order and a wooden sword with a worn point lying along it, and his other boot stands by the door, toe to the wall. The queen's rooms lie "at the near end of the family wing, with the window on the terrace": a table with a sand-box, a second chair, a lamp and a row of pens, a blue bowl by the window with Valia's note under it, and a curtain that the air moves. A latch, a step on the flags, a door and a stool scraping on the terrace carry to her room (Area block 06).

**The Court (Area blocks 03, 05 and 06).** The Court "sat in the open at the Stone Ring". The steward, Ranulf Skeld, keeps the order of the Court's day (a woman of the Host had asked him "for a place in the Court's order, and been given the last"). On 1247.1.14 / PD 14 the king says "The Court is risen", the steward says it after him, naming the day, and the Stone Vigil's two sentries say it after the steward (Area block 06). On 1247.1.12 / PD 12 the Court has risen "because the light is going", with petitioners having waited since the noon bell. "Nobody crossed the boards unasked": the pilot of 1247.1.14 / PD 14 waits where he stopped until the king crosses the terrace to him. Nothing the Court says is written: the record is the people who stood by (Section 4 rows; the Code block). Counts are written in Gale and promises are not; the clerks' table copies the Host's roll out for the families (Area block 06).

**The ward-line (Area blocks 03 and 04).** "Its only edge was the ward-line, a ring of stone marks and watch stations where old wards of the wind-cultivators were carved into the pale stone of the rise"; the king reads the wards "as he read his own pulse, whole and steady, from the Landing Stone ... round to the Wind Crossing" (Area block 03). At each station a **mark** stands, "a pillar of stone worn smooth on its windward side by the hands of the keepers, who touched it in passing as people touch a doorframe", and against each mark "a low stone hut like a loaf on a shelf". A glass is fixed on the mark beside the hut's door, with a **dial** under it and a **slate** below where the keeper chalks the day's readings in a square hand; the needle shows how hard the wind leans on the ward. Each station has a lamp, its chimney on a collar, and a keeper; the third station's keeper is Ragna Holm, an unawakened woman with chalk on both cuffs. The keepers light their lamps for the queen's hour and not for the sun, "so that each station woke as she reached it and slept again behind her", and anyone on the terrace who followed the lights could say where the queen was walking. On 1247.1.12 / PD 12 the needle at the second station sat "a hair high"; on the shoulder of that station's mark is a smooth place where, in the queen's memory, Prince Aegon at five would not go on until she told him what the stone was for (she said it was the edge, and "the edge of here" when he asked the edge of what; recorded as a past moment and nothing more: Variance G6). At the third station the needle "has stood a little high since the wind came round", the keeper did not touch the glass, and the queen said to open it after the lamps are lit. Past the third station the line runs a long way over open ground without a hut to **Elbow**, the keepers' name for the mark "where it bent toward the house"; crushed thyme grows underfoot and a thorn bush stands on the way. Beyond Elbow the line runs along the family wing to the terrace stair. When the dark comes the stations show "one by one as small yellow windows, each at its mark". At least three stations stand before Elbow; their total is not stated.

**The Stone Vigil (Area blocks 03 to 06).** The Vigil keeps the Landing Stone: it lights its lamps there at dusk, changes its watch there ("two in grey going and two coming and none of the four looking her way, which was how the Vigil greeted her") and stands watch with its faces to the plateau. Its watch is a witness of the king's hearings: "Nothing of it was written. Ranulf had stood by, and the Stone Vigil's watch at the Landing Stone, and those were the record" (Area block 03). Two sentries are named: Gudrun Nyland, a Senior of the middle Levels, and Leif Bakke, a Senior of the upper Levels (Area block 05); they say the Court's rising after the steward (Area block 06). The garrison's size is not stated on the page (`ledger-people-society-faction/gale-kingdom-military.md` §6.5).

**The corridor and the letters.** The old wire stops at "the corridor" (Area blocks 03 and 05). Prince Aegon's letter "lay two days waiting for a wind" at the corridor post and came the rest of the way by glider; Varys's letter came to the corridor post by the king of Azure Sky's own rider and from there by glider, with the post-master's word standing behind it. Both reach the palace in a Flightcraft pilot's hands on the terrace. None of the four letters of the Chapter carries a proper name.

**The bedroom of the family wing (Chapter 008, Ref: 1.1.1.008; Area blocks 03 and 04).** The bedroom lies in the family wing, and its door opens, like every door of the house, "on the flags" and is shut tonight, with the king and queen in it. Beyond it "the wing ran away both ways, her own rooms at the near end and the boy's door at the far, and the doors between them shut". It is "a plain one": "a bed against the inner wall, a chest, two chairs, and a low table under the window with the lamp on it and two cups, and a curtain that the air moved"; the stone floor is "cool and dry", and a draught comes from the window "to her ankles before it came to her face". A slot is cut in the wall above the window, and the wind finds its way through it as "a thin whistle" that "rose a half-step, and held, and fell back" (the wind-apertures of §5.3, which the page gives as a whistle and never as a hum). The king's greatsword hangs "flat on its pegs" beside the door, in its harness. The Range stands in the window as "a dark mass ... under a darker edge of cloud". The room smells of lamp oil and the wool of the bed's cover, and the queen's boots by the door smell of crushed thyme from the ward-line. In the room the king's stillness is gone: his hands "went to the harness of the greatsword and undid the buckles in the wrong order, hung the sword on its pegs and set it crooked and set it straight, turned the lamp's wick up and down, rubbed the back of his neck, and drummed on the table"; the queen sits at the table with her feet bare, and the king sits on the end of the bed. The wing beyond is empty: "the household gone to the east wing and its fire", and "no step on the flags since the lamp in this room was lit". At the far end the boy's door is open "on a dark room and a bed made flat", and "the other boot stood with its toe to the wall". The queen's own rooms, at the wing's near end, hold Valia's four lines under the blue bowl. What is said in the room is said in private and binds nothing: "Nothing said in this room binds anything, and the sentence that does is said standing, in the open, with the witnesses named, and this is the end of a bed" (the king; the OPEN EDGE code below, which the page shows kept, with no deviation). *Not established:* the room's place in the wing beyond what the page says, its size, whether the two always share it, and whether the wind-slot is one of several (OPEN-AREA).

**The pause.** The king takes his written answer in to "the long pause that Gale's custom put between the reading of a thing and its seal", and the queen does not follow him; she calls it "the one silence in the house that she had never needed to place" (Area block 06). The custom is recorded at `ledger-people-society-faction/gale-kingdom-state.md`, Section 6.

**The road's end, the Crossing, the Court and the open edge (Chapter 009; Ref: 1.1.1.009, Area blocks 07 and 08; 1247.3.28 / PD 87).** *Seen from the road.* "The Aerie Palace showed first as a long spine of pale stone standing clear of the plateau, carved and pierced by ages of wind, and the wind in its holes made a thin whistle that came down the road ahead of it. It had no wall about it. Its edge was set by the wards, which he could feel on his skin as a coolness, and the road ran up to a gap in front of the stone and ended there." *The Crossing.* "The party stopped where the road stopped." The escort stays with the horses, and "The Wind Crossing ran out across the gap, a causeway of fitted stone laid open on both sides with nothing to hold, and the wind of the gap, which was said to measure the intent of any man who walked it, came round his boots before he had taken a step." *The terrace at the last of the sun.* "At the far end of the stones the air of the terrace took over": the easterly "lay dry and steady across the flagstones with the last of the sun in it", and the pillars along the terrace give back one low note while the birds on them go up together and come down again. *The Court.* "Ahead rose the tall stones of the Stone Ring, and inside them lay the Court's boards, the plank floor where whoever speaks to the Court must stand. King Aeries sat on his block of pale stone at the far side, hearing a man who was a long way from finishing. The clerks' table was at the near side with two clerks bent over it and the steward, Ranulf Skeld, at his place beside it. Past the Ring the two sentries of the Stone Vigil waited at the Landing Stone, where the kingdom had been sworn, a Senior of the middle Levels and a Senior of the upper, their lamps not yet lit." The steward, "a man with no qi in him to read", sees Stavik first and his eyes go to the stiff black sleeve; the king "heard the man on the boards out to his last sentence and answered it with one as long, and only when the man had gone down off the planks did the king rise"; his wind "crossed the terrace flat and soft, went up the stiff sleeve, and rested there as a hand rests on a shoulder. Anyone born in Highgale knew that touch." *The report to the Court.* Stavik says it standing at the planks' edge ("I report in the hearing of the Court"); the steward says each of the nine names after him "in a flat voice, with his eyes on the planks", and "at the clerks' table two pens set it down"; the king says the nine back to Stavik, "every name, once, without a glance at the clerks' table", and says "The clerks will carry the count to their families." *The open edge.* "The flagstones ended at the open edge, a lip of pale stone with nothing beyond it. The air came up the cliff from the valleys in gusts loud enough that a man had to raise his voice at arm's length, and nothing said there would reach the Court." The king takes Stavik and Magnus Dalsen there ("the rest of it is not for the boards"), and after the report he takes the pause: "He stood at the edge with the sky going gold and flat over the valleys, and pressed his hands together until the small tremor in them stopped, and drew one breath that carried over the wind. After that he did not move. It ran long." Stavik reads the silence as the kingdom does: "a short one meant the word was easy, and a long one meant it would never be taken back." *At the close.* "Out on the high ground a mast of the *Kite Line* took up its thin note, and round the palace the watch stations of the ward-line woke one by one, small and yellow at each mark, as the keepers lit them for someone walking. The king did not turn to follow them." The queen is not on the page; the king says she "walks the ward-line until the lamps are lit" and will "hear this from me behind our door".
