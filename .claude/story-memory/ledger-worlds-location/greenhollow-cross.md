### 5.1 — Function and Jurisdiction

The village works both timber and farmland and trades what it grows and makes (author, 2026-10-06; Section 1). It is on Azure Sky ground at the border of the kingdom. Its governance is not set beyond a meeting hall where disputes are settled (§5.7); who rules or speaks for the village, whether it holds a headman, a council or a garrison, and what the toll collector's authority rests on are not established (OPEN-AREA).

### 5.2 — Fields and Farming (author-authored, 2026-10-06)

- Wheat, barley and rye grow in the cleared fields just beyond the tree line.
- Root crops and beans grow in the kitchen plots behind every house.
- Pigs forage for acorns under the first trees of the forest, and sheep and goats graze the open pasture.
- Every household keeps chickens, and an orchard supplies apples and pears for cider and dried fruit.
- Honey, nuts, berries and mushrooms are gathered from the forest edge.
- A hedge and ditch along the tree line keep deer and boar out of the fields.

### 5.3 — Forest Work (author-authored, 2026-10-06)

- Woodcutters fell and trim timber, working the forest in rotation so it regrows.
- Sawyers cut logs into planks and beams at the sawpit and the sawmill.
- Charcoal burners tend slow kilns in the clearings and supply fuel to the forge.
- Hunters and trappers bring in game and pelts.
- Foresters mark which trees may be cut and replant what is felled.
- Log slides and a timber track carry felled logs from the forest down to the mill yard.

### 5.4 — Mills and Machines (author-authored, 2026-10-06; technology profile)

- The waterwheel grist mill grinds the village's grain, and nearby hamlets pay a share of their grain to use it.
- The water-powered sawmill drives a saw blade, so planks cost a fraction of what hand-sawing does.
- A small oil press works flax and rapeseed from the same stream.
- A lathe and a trip-hammer, both turned by the stream, serve the carpenter and the smith.
- Pulley cranes move heavy timber in the yard.

*Technology profile (axis 6): the machines are water-driven craft technology in the author's own words; no Part 8 tier is set (OPEN-AREA).*

### 5.5 — Crafts and Trades (author-authored, 2026-10-06)

- The blacksmith makes axes, saws, nails, hinges and plough blades, and mends tools for the whole district.
- The carpenter and joiner build houses, furniture and carts.
- The wheelwright and carter keep the cart road moving.
- The cooper makes barrels and buckets.
- The tanner works hides from the village's livestock and the hunters' catch.
- Weavers and a dyer turn wool into cloth.
- The potter and basket-weavers supply household goods.
- The baker, brewer and cider-presser work from the mill's grain and the orchard's fruit.

### 5.6 — Trade and Market (author-authored, 2026-10-06; economy)

- Planks, beams, barrels, charcoal, furs, flour, ale, cider and wool cloth go out by cart to the larger towns.
- Salt, lamp oil, spices, glass and fine cloth come in from passing caravans.
- A weekly market is held every seventh day in the square beside the sawmill.
- A timber and harvest fair each autumn draws buyers from several days' travel away.
- A toll collector at the bridge records the carts and log-carts that cross.

### 5.7 — Daily Life (author-authored, 2026-10-06; population)

- About two hundred households live here, along with an inn and stables, a small shrine and a meeting hall where disputes are settled.
- Most families farm or cut timber, and one or two members of each household work a trade.
- The forest edge is the village's boundary, so everyone knows where the safe paths end and the deep woods begin.

### 5.9 — Distinctive Sensory Signature (axis 12)

A visitor at the top of the village slope at first light (1247.1.1 / PD 1; Ref: 1.1.1.002) meets the cold first, with frost white along the length of the well rope, and a white light with no gold in it, white as a peeled rod, that has reached the treetops and nothing below them until the sun comes down the trunks; a thin wind comes down along the hedge from the forest. The sounds are small and near: a bucket knocking once on the wall of the well, a rope let down a pull at a time, boots quick and flat on the ruts, the grass hissing beside a fence. Below the slope the roofs lie low and close together, every shutter shut and one chimney smoking. Behind the hedge and the ditch the forest wall rises (cross-ref: skyvein-forest.md §5.9).

At the crossing a little later the sounds are those of waking work: a pencil squeaking on a slate, stable doors dragged back against a wall, the waterwheel of Longsaw Mill taking the stream with a long wet groan and then its saw, a thin note that climbs and falls and holds at the top, and a cart drumming onto the planks of Alder Bridge. The smells are new bread from the bakery door and warm straw from the inn yard, and the sun finds the walls so that sleeves go up. In the mill yard the next day the sounds are the waterwheel thudding behind the wall of the grist mill, the saw whining through the fence, a sack dropped on the scale and seed pouring in a dry brown stream, with the talk of the night's frost and of the sowing; in the lane there is shouting, and the saw goes on far off with its one note. On the farmland on the third day the ground is dry on top and wet below, the wind comes steady out of the trees smelling of moss, pigs grunt across the ditch, and an axe rings among the trunks from no side a man could name (cross-ref: skyvein-forest.md §5.9).

**Narrative summary (as of Chapter 002).** Greenhollow Cross is a village of about two hundred households at the very edge of a great forest, where the last trees give way to open fields. The forest wall rises at its back and the farmland spreads in front; a stream comes out of the forest and meets the cart road at the village crossing, and the village works both timber and farmland and trades what it grows and makes, with water-driven mills, a sawmill and an oil press on the stream. At first light on 1247.1.1 / PD 1, the first morning of spring, the village is seen from the Hedge Well at the top of its slope: a rope-and-bucket well on worn paving beside the hedge, where a frosted rope comes up a pull at a time while Hercules draws water for the houses and Bison carries it to the head of the path. Below, the roofs lie low and close together, every shutter shut and one chimney smoking, and the bakery's hand, Lowen Wray, comes up the path with a mug of barley water and sends Hercules on to the baker's tub and then to Kitto Hale's cask. A little later the crossing wakes in the sun: the toll collector, Pasco Cobb, pulls down the short end of the bar of Alder Bridge, a bridge of grey planks on stone legs, to raise the long pole clear of the road for a charcoal cart, and counts the load onto a slate that hangs from the post; the waterwheel of Longsaw Mill takes the stream and its saw starts; the stable doors of Rowantree Inn are dragged back; the road fills with people and their loads; Hercules lifts the cooper's full cask onto his cart in the inn yard; and Bison stands on the bare bank at the far end of the bridge in the wind off the forest. On 1247.1.2 / PD 2 the mill yard bags the seed for the sowing at its scale and its crane, where Bison mends the crane's pulley with a nail and the miller explains it; a loaded cart then slides down the soft ruts of the lane toward a child at the ditch and is stopped, and the wheelwright reads a worn axle end that clears the carter's lad. On 1247.1.3 / PD 3 the seed goes out on backs across the farmland to the strips against the hedge and the ditch at the forest's edge, where a forester marks a tree to come down in autumn, the log slide and the timber track run beside the trees, and three red posts stand before a curtain of *Thornweft*; the cart with the worn axle waits in the wheelwright's yard with its near wheel off. The villagers use the two men's bare first names throughout, and the village keeps no code of conduct by the author's ruling (Ref: 1.1.1.002). Hercules's sense finds nearly everyone in the village Unawakened, with a faint trace of qi in two of the twelve villagers the Chapter names, and nobody practises (Ref: 1.1.1.002, text revised in place 2026-10-17).
