## Section 1 — Identity and Classification (B)

**Full Name:** Olympus
**Common Name:** Olympus / Mount Olympus (used in Part 30 text) / The Empire of Gods / The Divine Seat
**Location Tier (Part 13):** Not yet fully established — classified as a major location of extraordinary significance; likely World-tier or higher; the home civilization of the gods suggests a power scale far above the Sol Star System
**Cosmological Area (Part 13):** Not yet established — Olympus may or may not be within the Sol Star System; given the Firmament Lock restricts the Sol Star System, and that Hercules and Bison were "banished from Olympus and are now lost in the Sol Star System," Olympus is most likely located outside the Sol Star System — in another Star System, Star Cluster, or higher cosmological region. Exact location to be established in prose.
**Part 13 Classification:** Classification pending — likely Sacred World (W-8 or W-9) given it is the permanent seat of divine-realm entities (Zeus, Nandi, and the broader Empire of Gods). Sacred Ground status almost certainly present at apex grade (S7–S8).
**Sacred Ground Status:** Probable apex — not yet confirmed in prose; the home of gods implies Sacred Ground conditions of the highest order
**Containing Star System / Region:** Unknown — not yet established in prose; likely outside the Sol Star System
**Physical Description:**
Olympus is the home of the Empire of Gods — the civilization to which Zeus, Nandi, Hercules, and Bison belong (or belonged). It is described as an empire of gods, indicating the population are divine-realm or equivalent beings. Hercules and Bison were banished from Olympus on false charges before the start of the Starward Saga. The exact physical nature of Olympus — whether it is a planet, a dimensional realm, a celestial body, or something beyond standard spatial classification — is not yet established in prose.

**Cultural Association (Part 25):** Divine-tier culture — the cultural norms, governance structure, and social organization of the Empire of Gods are not yet established in prose; they are expected to be of extraordinarily high cultivation and paradigm character

At dusk on the village high ground, after Hercules has told Bison in order that they were betrayed, accused of a thing they did not do, not believed, and exiled ("Back there we were betrayed..."), he says one word more, aloud, in the voice in which he asks for water: "Olympus."
