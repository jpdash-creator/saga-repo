## Section 5 — Lore (Part 28 Area 17)

**Schema:** Part 28 Area 17.2 — Location Domain (Sub-World road)
**Depth Standard:** Proportionality Exception (Part 28 Area 17.3): populated to what the page shows; every other axis reads not established.
**Audit Hook:** ALCA dimension AL-10 (`guardrails/audit/guardrail-alca.md`)

### 5.1 — The Road (axis 12)

Seen at first light on 1247.1.9 / PD 9 (Ref: 1.1.1.005), from the captain's halt on a shale ledge above the ford. The air smells of cold stone and wet wool. The road comes down through a cutting where a ditch runs out of the cutting at the edge of the mist, and drops to the water. Four wagons stand nose to tail on the ledge with their mules in harness and their breath hanging over them. The wheels hiss on the gravel and then in the water. Beyond the ford the road's far side is a guess in the mist. The road is the Whistledeep caravan's alone that morning, but for one man in a good brown coat walking away along the ditch (Section 3).

### 5.2 — The Mule Stair

Named twice. In the first scene the captain's horn is not answered because "the nearest ear was the Ridgewatch outpost at the foot of the Mule Stair, and no horn carried so far through a morning like this one" (Area block 01). In the lieutenant's count the patrol found the caravan "on our first ride up the Mule Stair and the Seam Road since the Windward Trail opened to horses" (Area block 05). So the stair has a foot, the foot is a long way from the ford (no horn carried so far), the outpost of the Ridgewatch detachment stands there, and the patrol's ride to the ford goes up the stair and the road (the page names the stair first). Nothing else is shown: its steps, its length, its rise and what lies above or below it are not established. The Chapter Spec's plan reads it as a cut-step stair that takes the road down from the ford toward the north edge of the plateau; that is the planner's pick (Chapter Spec 1.1.1.005, Skipped; "the author may swap any") and is not a fact of the page.

### 5.3 — What the Road Carries

The page shows one kind of traffic, the Whistledeep caravan (`whistledeep-mine.md`): four wagons, mules in harness, four carters and a clerk, and an escort of a captain of the Windward Host with five of the Host. It also says that traders will crowd the road "by summer", once they come down it after the Windward Trail has opened; none is on it yet.

### 5.4 — The Windward Trail

The text ties the road to the Trail: the Trail "had opened to horses only days before" 1247.1.9 / PD 9, and the traders who will use the Seam Road come down "it" after the Trail opens (Area block 01). Whether the Trail runs into the road, runs through Shalegate Pass, or meets the road at all is not stated (`ledger-worlds-location/aerie-plateau.md` §5.1 holds that whether the Trail runs through the Windward Pass is OPEN-AUTHOR; the same open question is held for Shalegate Pass at `windward-range.md` §5.5).

### 5.5 — Hazards, Climate (axes 13 and 14)

**Established:** ground mist at first light that hides the far bank from the eye; a gravel bar across the ford that "has crept east with the thaw"; soft ground under the willows on the far bank; cold water to the shins (`stirrup-ford.md`); early spring, with snow still going off the slopes below Shalegate Pass; a raid on 1247.1.9 / PD 9; and a distance to help that no horn crosses on a misty morning. **Not established:** any other danger, and the road's climate and seasons (OPEN-AREA).

2. Every distance on the road (mine to ford, ford to stair, stair to outpost) and the road's length.

**Narrative summary (as of Chapter 005).** The Seam Road is the mountain road down which the Whistledeep mine sends its ore in wagons and mules to the Aerie Plateau. At first light on 1247.1.9 / PD 9 it dropped through a cutting to the water at Stirrup Ford under ground mist, empty of other traffic, with snow only now going off the slopes below Shalegate Pass; the Windward Trail had just opened to horses and the traders had not yet come down. The Ridgewatch outpost stands at the foot of the Mule Stair, too far for a horn to carry. The caravan was taken at the ford; the Ridgewatch patrol rode up the Mule Stair and the road two days later and found it (Ref: 1.1.1.005).
