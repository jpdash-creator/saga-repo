### 5.3 — The Eight Districts and the Stair (Chapter 005; Ref: 1.1.1.005, Area block 03)

*The king reads the city as the wind goes through it, from the open edge of the palace terrace on the evening of 1247.1.12 / PD 12; nobody is in the city on the page. Each picture below is the page's own one clause, and nothing is added. The eight names are the author's own (Chapter Spec 1.1.1.005, Names), accepted 2026-10-18; each becomes a MAJOR name if it recurs at Section scale (Part 28 Rule 20.4). A whole-word, case-insensitive scan of 2026-10-19 over `.claude` (the `_archive`, session-log, temp and agent-memory folders excluded) and the written Chapters found each name only in the spec chain, the Chapter 005 text and this ledger; no culture register was applied; originality against published works is not cleared and stays flagged to the author for information.*

| District | What the page shows of it |
|---|---|
| Frame Row | The glue pots cooling beneath the storm-kite frames "bound and mended there from dawn"; the air carries resin and wet cord. |
| Rack Market | Herbs come off their drying racks one rack at a time; their scent climbs the hill on the last warm air of the day. |
| Courier Green | The glider pilots fold their wings; a late glider comes in down the thinning wind "with a sound like a sheet shaken once". The pilot who brought Prince Aegon's letter is sent down to it from the palace terrace. |
| Muster Field | Loud with the Windward Host and the Wind-Sworn Militia drilling; the drill ends, "hundreds of heels coming down together and then not". |
| Leeside | "The sheltered streets that lay out of the easterly"; the air between its walls stands still, so the king crosses it fast. |
| Weighhouse Row | Shutting its scales on the day's ore and grain, "weighed and sealed". |
| Elders' Rise | The wind-academy monasteries stand with their shutters open to the east, "quiet like people listening". |
| Ropewalk | Along the plateau's edge, "a long bare strip where cord and kite-string were laid and twisted" (the text writes "the Ropewalk"). |

**The stair to Rack Market.** The old woman Hallgerd Vorn's business at the Court on 1247.1.12 / PD 12 is "a stair": "the long one down to Rack Market", which has no rope and on which she has fallen twice (Area block 03). The king says his steward will walk it in the morning "and see how much rope it needs". Who keeps the stair is not said. *Chapter 008 (Ref: 1.1.1.008):* on 1247.2.21 / PD 49 the king tells the queen that Hallgerd Vorn's stair "was seen to be mended", and that nine of that day's thirteen petitioners therefore asked for a stair of their own to be mended; how it was mended, and by whom, is not said. *Chapter 009 (Ref: 1.1.1.009):* on 1247.3.28 / PD 87 Gunnar Stavik, riding by, sees that "the long stair down to the market, which had had no rope in the spring, had one now, hemp grey and worn pale by hands" (§5.4); who fitted it is still not said.

**What the page does not show.** Any named street, gate or city wall, the city's size, who governs it, and any scene set in it. *(Updated 2026-10-23: Chapter 009 shows that on the south-west road the city has no wall and no gate, and it rides through the city (§5.4); it still shows no named street, no gate or wall on any other side, no size, no governor and no scene set in it beyond the ride.)*

### 5.4 — The Ride Through the City (Chapter 009; Ref: 1.1.1.009, Area block 07)

*Gunnar Stavik rides in on 1247.3.28 / PD 87 (Year 1247, Sorevany, Yevil, Day 28), from the south-west road to the road that climbs to the palace, with four Warriors of his escort; the page gives the end of the morning at the plateau's lip and the noon sun at Rack Market. Each picture below is the page's own, and nothing is added. The eight districts are those of §5.3, met a second time and in this order. Every item is an instance and not a custom.*

| Order | District | What the page shows of it |
|---|---|---|
| (approach) | none | "Highgale did not begin. There was no wall to pass and no gate to pass it by; the terraces thinned into strips and the strips into yards, and the first he knew of being in the city was that the road had a house on either hand." |
| 1 | The Ropewalk | Along the plateau's edge on his right, "a long bare strip where men walked backward twisting cord into rope with a dry hiss of hemp"; one of them "lifted a hand to five riders and let it fall". |
| 2 | Weighhouse Row | The scales "were being shut on the morning's ore and grain", and "a clerk pressed his wax to a lid as they went by". |
| 3 | Leeside | "The streets turned out of the easterly, and for the first time in four days the air stood still between walls", so that he hears his own horse breathe; "the street smelled of old stone and baking bread". |
| 4 | Rack Market | "The herbs lay out on their racks in the noon sun, bitter-root and thyme"; "the long stair down to the market, which had had no rope in the spring, had one now, hemp grey and worn pale by hands". |
| 5 | Frame Row | "Smelled of resin and wet cord and hot glue, and the storm-kite frames hung bound and mended in their racks." |
| 6 | Courier Green | "The glider pilots were folding their wings, and a late glider came down the thinning wind with a sound like a sheet shaken once"; Stavik thinks of "two seats on the Green that would stay empty" and keeps the two names "behind his teeth". |
| 7 | Muster Field | "The Host and the Wind-Sworn Militia were at drill, and the heels came down together and stopped, and the drill ended and began again, and not one man of them yet knew." |
| 8 | Elders' Rise | "The wind-academy monasteries kept their shutters open to the east, and nobody in them made a sound." |

**Out of the city.** "Out of Elders' Rise the road bent west and climbed, and the masts of the *Kite Line* rose along it on the high ground one after another, each on its chains, each giving the one thin note. It was an hour at a rider's pace, and he did not hurry the horse." The road ends at a gap in front of the palace's stone (`aerie-palace.md` §5.5).

**What the page does not show.** The city's size; a wall or a gate on any other side; a named street; who governs it; any institution but the monasteries and the drill; and anyone in it who speaks. Stavik "had been born here and he rode through it by the ear and the nose as much as the eye"; nobody in the city speaks to him on the page.
