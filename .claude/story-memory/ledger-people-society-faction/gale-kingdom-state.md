### 7.1 — Founding Event

The ruling state was founded by **Aerion the First-Winged** (first sovereign, same generational era as the Azure Sky Twin Oath — see gale-kingdom.md §5.2 for the continent-scale founding narrative). The founding event of the ruling state specifically is the **Gale Oath-Binding**: following the Gale Ascent circumnavigation, Aerion gathered the mountain communities, Aerie Plateau clans, and Long Coast harbor-lords on the stone that became **Aerion's Landing Stone** and bound them by a single oath sworn under the wind. The founding oath text is transmitted orally rather than written, a deliberate choice that has canonically survived seven generations — Gale law treats the wind itself as the Compact's medium of record.

### 7.2 — Doctrine

The Gale ruling state's foundational doctrine is the **Oath-Under-Wind**: a single, absolute fealty sworn to the wind as sovereign element, with the monarch as the wind's human-bearing vessel. Doctrinal corollaries:
- **Singular Legitimacy** — sovereign authority is held by whichever of the ruling pair is the primary wind-bearer; Aeries is currently the primary wind-bearer, Aalia the primary storm-keeper, and edicts are issued by Aeries with Aalia's counter-sign as storm-keeper only for military-force matters.
- **Dragon-Lineage Precedence** — canonically, full-blooded Dragon-Human cultivators of Aerion's descent hold automatic noble standing; partial-lineage standing is graded by cultivation realm and wind-oath performance.
- **Single-Affinity Depth Preference** — the state doctrinally favors deep single-affinity mastery over breadth.
- **Honored Restraint** — the doctrine commits the kingdom to defensive posture with formal respect for the Windward Range neighbors (the Northern Accord is the expression); offensive war requires public wind-sworn declaration at the Wind Crossing.

### 7.3 — Governance Structure

The state is a single-sworn monarchy with a complementary regent-pair in the reigning couple; three principal organs:
- **The Wind-Crown** — the reigning sovereign (Aeries) as primary wind-bearer; the executive authority.
- **The Storm-Counsel** — the storm-keeper sovereign (Aalia) as counter-sign authority for military-force matters, plus the **Wind-Council** (a body of named wind-academy elders, Dragon-lineage elders, and Long Coast harbor-lords that advises the Wind-Crown).
- **The Stone Tribunal** — the judicial body, canonically held on a named ring of stones at the Aerie Palace precinct; adjudicates wind-oath violations, dragon-lineage disputes, and inter-clan matters. *(Author ruling, 2026-10-18, applied 2026-10-19: the ring is named the Stone Ring; `ledger-worlds-location/aerie-palace.md` §5.2. The Tribunal does not appear on the page of Chapter 005, where the Court sits at the same ring.)*

Administrative infrastructure below the Wind-Council is a **governor-per-zone** structure: the Lyraen Frontier Governor, the Aerie Plateau Governor (a direct appointee of the Wind-Crown), the Tempest Valleys Governor, and the Long Coast Warden (a non-governor title acknowledging the maritime character of the eastern zone).

### 7.5 — Oath Network

The kingdom is bound by the following canonical oaths (Part 23 instruments):
- **The Gale Oath-Binding** (internal foundational — §7.1).
- **The Northern Accord** (external; with Azure Sky Kingdom; co-signatory of record: King Aeries). Key provisions: (a) Amendment Protocol — supplementary records touching on patrol jurisdiction require sovereign co-signatory signature no less than two hours before any session in which patrol jurisdiction is discussed; (b) Session Format Categories — the Accord distinguishes a "review session" from a "full sovereign-to-sovereign treaty event"; (c) **Windward Protocol** — sub-instrument governing bilateral monitoring data exchange; both sides commit to full dataset exchange from their respective monitoring stations; both sides maintain midpoint array access for real-time verification; Protocol relay infrastructure built under the Second Bilateral Infrastructure Accord in the same construction period as the Northern Accord corridor infrastructure.
- **The Lyraen River Compact** (external; multi-party).
- **The Wind-Naming Rite Oath** (internal; sworn by every wind-named citizen at age 14; binds the named wind to the citizen's civic identity).
- **The Sovereign Ascension Oath** (sworn at every accession, at the Wind Crossing, witnessed by palace-wind).

### 7.6 — Economic Base

The ruling state's fiscal base draws from: a wind-affinity cultivation ingredient export duty (principal revenue); Long Coast maritime service levies; a distinctive **Flight-fee** charged to non-Gale citizens requiring Aerie Flightcraft transit of the kingdom, set at 1 Gem per passenger per governance zone crossed (Part 11 Area 12.3); Aerie Plateau agricultural tithe (modest — the plateau is cultivation-terraced and low-yield); Lyraen River Compact customs share; academy and guild service fees. The kingdom maintains a smaller treasury reserve than Azure Sky, reflecting its lower technology tier and cultivation-dominant resource allocation.

### 7.7 — Reputation and Myth

Continental reputation: Gale is regarded as the exemplar of wind-affinity cultivation; the Dragon-Human royal lineage is continental prestige; the kingdom's word-is-wind reputation makes Gale diplomatic commitments highly valued. In folk speech, "wind-sworn" is a continental idiom for a commitment beyond ordinary guarantee; "to leave a silence" is the idiom for breaking a wind-oath.

Internal mythology: the **Returning Wing** — a popular belief that Aerion did not die in the last storm but became a wind-spirit that still circles the Windward Range, and that certain rare ambient-wind conditions are Aerion's direct presence. The crown neither affirms nor refutes; the belief is broadly held on the Aerie Plateau and near the Long Coast.

### 7.8 — Open Obligations and Legacy Weight

- **The named wind-order captaincies** — Stormwing Grand Captain, Windward Host Marshal, Long Coast Warden; positions held by named-but-not-yet-prose-established officers.
- **The royal dragon-lineage purity question** — §5.13 of gale-kingdom.md — canonically sensitive and politically live; Storm-Counsel is the body with jurisdiction.
- **The oral Founding Oath transmission chain** — the oath text is held by a lineage of wind-academy masters (the **Oath-Voices**); the current chain's continuity is a state-critical condition.
- **The Silent Tower** (named Long Coast landmark with canonical "no wind inside" status — gale-kingdom.md §5.13) — its jurisdiction and custodianship are state-level open questions.

**Shared conduct — what every member displays:**

- **FEW RULES, ABSOLUTE — the wall-less palace as a whole culture.** `[ASKED]` The seat of the kingdom has no walls; wind-cultivation wards define its perimeter and its shelter (`ledger-worlds-location/aerie-palace.md` §1; `ledger-worlds-kingdom-state/gale-kingdom.md`). **Wards, not walls** is not only how the palace stands — it is how the kingdom conducts itself. Gale holds very few regulations, and **the ones it holds cannot be bent by anyone, for any reason, in any circumstance.** Members therefore **do not ask permission for most things**: there is no permission apparatus to ask, and the absence is genuine rather than winked at. And members **never test the few lines, not even jokingly** — no probing, no hypothetical, no "what if I did." The lines are not treated as edges to be found; they are treated as facts about the world. The doctrinal root is the **Oath-Under-Wind** (§7.2): a **single, absolute fealty** sworn to the wind as sovereign element. A fealty that is single and absolute does not produce a body of graduated rules; it produces very few, and admits no degree in any of them.

- **SPOKEN INTO THE WIND — nothing binding is written.** `[ASKED]` A promise is made **standing, outdoors, before witnesses.** The trail of an agreement is **the people who heard it**, and that is the whole of the record. Members **go outside to say the thing that matters** — the important sentence is not said under a roof, and a member who is about to make a commitment moves before they speak. **A paper promise is distrusted**, on its face and regardless of what it says. **Witnesses are named aloud** as part of the act, so that the record is constituted at the moment of speaking rather than assembled afterwards. And **asking a Gale member to put it in writing is a small insult** — small, because it is usually taken as ignorance rather than as an accusation, and an insult nonetheless. The doctrinal root is again the Oath-Under-Wind, whose own founding text is canonically **transmitted orally rather than written**, a deliberate choice that has survived seven generations, with **Gale law treating the wind itself as the Compact's medium of record** (§7.1). The largest instrument in the kingdom is built the same way: offensive war requires a **public wind-sworn declaration at the Wind Crossing** (§7.2, Honored Restraint) — spoken, outdoors, witnessed, at the exposed stone causeway where the ambient wind is said to *"measure the petitioner's intent"* (`gale-kingdom.md` §5, verbatim; the phrase is canon's, about petitioners specifically, and is cited here for the causeway, not extended to declarants). Zonal militia is raised by **wind-sworn muster** (§7.4), and every citizen is bound at fourteen by the spoken **Wind-Naming Rite Oath** (§7.5).

**How a counter-sign works in a kingdom that writes nothing — resolved from FEW RULES, ABSOLUTE and SPOKEN INTO THE WIND above.** `[DERIVED]` Canon requires a counter-sign: edicts issue from the Wind-Crown with the storm-keeper's counter-sign **for military-force matters** (§7.3), and the Wind-Sworn Militia is mobilized **by Storm-Counsel counter-sign** (§7.4). Those two elements answer it between them, and nothing beyond them is invented to explain it. **A Gale counter-sign is not a second mark; it is a second voice.** The order is spoken; the counter-sign is the storm-keeper standing and speaking it too — outdoors, before witnesses, witnesses named — so that the order's trail is **two people who were heard**, exactly as SPOKEN INTO THE WIND constitutes every other binding thing in the kingdom. The requirement is one of the kingdom's very few lines, and it is therefore absolute in the FEW RULES sense: an order for military force that the storm-keeper has not spoken is **not a weak order, it is not an order** — there is no provisional form, no retroactive form, and no form that can be cured afterwards. That absoluteness is what makes the office the institutionalized restraint that `aalia.md` §11.5's build note names it. Two consequences follow and are registered: **(i)** the counter-sign cannot be given by messenger, seal, token or delegate, because none of those is a voice; **(ii)** because the record is the hearers, the counter-sign is verified by asking the people who were there, which is why the militia clause and the Wind Crossing declaration are both **public** acts rather than internal ones. **Open, and flagged rather than invented:** the word *counter-sign* is the ledger's own term (§7.3, §7.4) and reads as a written-instrument word; whether Gale uses that word for the act itself, or whether the record here is using an outsider's term for a spoken counter-voice, is **not established.** No in-world name is coined for it (Part 28 Area 20 — Entity Naming Law).

**Greeting and deference ritual:** `[DERIVED]` Meeting is unceremonious and unpermissioned — members approach without requesting leave to approach, and the absence of an approach protocol is the visible fact, not an oversight. Where something is to be committed to, the greeting is cut short and the parties **go outside**; a member who begins to say a binding thing indoors is stopped and moved, and being moved is not a rebuke. **Rank modulates almost nothing in the forms** and everything in the two places where it does: the Wind-Crown's word, and the storm-keeper's counter-voice on force (§7.3). Deference is otherwise owed to the wind rather than to persons — at rites it is the wind that halts the room, not the sovereign. **Not established, deliberately:** whether Gale carries a named greeting form of its own. None exists in canon and none is invented.

Standing exactly as ruled: §G16.1.4 (no wind-name under fourteen; assigning one is CRITICAL); §G16.2.5 condition 1 (a hearer of an acknowledgment, or of any sworn or binding matter, is a person who holds a wind-name, past the Rite, who can afterwards be named and produced); the speaker of an acknowledgment, which is an entry act, holds one too; §G16.2.6 Cases A, B and C; §G16.2.10 (the wind witnesses at two rites only); §G16.2.11 (a pre-rite child is spoken for, before a Gale body, by an adult who holds a wind-name); and every CRITICAL row of §G16.8 about a wind-name under fourteen, a child in a witness clause, a child entering hearing, or the wind as witness. The one loosening is §G16.1.8.
- **(v) The slot rule.** Every [wind-name] in this block, and in every ledger and table that applies it, is a slot and stays one until the author sets a name. Where §G16.1.7 does not require the wind-name, read [wind-name] as [name]: the speaker's birth name, an office-noun, or their wind-name if they choose it. No wind-name is coined here.

The habit is conduct; an address TERM simply need not be the wind-name outside a court ruling.

**G16.1.7 — OFFICIAL COURT RULING, AND THE THREE WIND-NAME RITES: WHERE THE WIND-NAME IS REQUIRED. ADDED BY AUTHOR RULING.** `[ASKED]` as to the two settings and the exclusions named by the author, and `[ASKED]` as to the edges, which the author confirmed (open item 23, settled). An official court ruling is **(a)** the Stone Tribunal's proceedings and rulings at its ring, and **(b)** a ruling the sovereign gives, sitting in formal court, on a petition or a dispute; in both, with wind-names for every party from each party's entry to the ruling, the sovereign counting as a party when appearing before the Stone Tribunal. `[ASKED]` **NOT an official court ruling:** a promise, a sworn undertaking, a witnessing, an edict or a counter-sign, a muster, a pay or water claim, a charge being spoken. `[ASKED]` The same holds, `[DERIVED]` from the author's answer that the four military constituting acts follow the same rule (`gale-kingdom-military.md` §6.11.3), for a commission, a handover and a courier's delivery, and for an entry acknowledgment or a completion spoken outside such a proceeding. A charge reaches the Stone Tribunal only by being heard there: the speaking of the charge is outside a ruling or a named rite, its hearing is inside one and keeps wind-names. The Wind-Naming Rite Oath, the Sovereign Ascension Oath and the war declaration at the Wind Crossing are not official court rulings, but they KEEP the spoken wind-name: it is REQUIRED there `[ASKED]` (author ruling; open item 21, settled). A petition heard in formal court that ends with no ruling still needs the wind-name from entry, because the proceeding runs from entry to the ruling `[ASKED]` (open item 23, settled).

**G16.1.8 — WHO MAY WITNESS WHAT: HOLDING STAYS FOR SERIOUS MATTERS, AND ONE LOOSENING FOR ORDINARY EVERYDAY WORDS. ADDED BY AUTHOR RULING.** `[ASKED]` on the split, the author's answer being *"Split by context"*, and `[ASKED]` on the plain-words definition in (iii), its single test question and its reading that a small promise nobody stood to, went outside for or named hearers to is an ordinary word a child may witness, the Collective Behavior Code untouched; the author confirmed these (open item 23, settled). The definition was worked out from what this ledger already treats as small.
- **(i) Serious matters: holding a wind-name stays required.** For a sworn or binding matter (anything the speaker means to be bound by, or that anyone may afterwards rely on), for entering a hearing, and for anything in an official court ruling, the speaker of the acknowledgment and every hearer who is to count must hold a wind-name (§G16.1.6(iv)). The rulings stand for these exactly as ruled.
- **(ii) The loosening: an ORDINARY EVERYDAY WORD may be witnessed by anyone, a child under fourteen included.** Such a witnessing counts as WITNESSED and not as BINDING: the hearer heard it and can say so, and that is all. It makes no record, creates nothing anyone can rely on afterwards, and gives the Stone Tribunal nothing to hear.
- **(iii) What an ordinary everyday word is, in plain words.** A word of the ordinary register of §G16.1.1 (the daily, working, domestic, market, household and academy-corridor speech that is the overwhelming majority of all Gale speech) that nobody means to be bound by and nobody could afterwards be held to: a greeting; a petition of courtesy (a small favour asked, a small kindness thanked); a remark or an answer; a small promise between household members or friends that has no sworn register, which is to say nobody stood to it, went outside for it or named hearers to it. The test is one question: **could anyone afterwards hold the speaker to it, before a zone head's office or the Stone Tribunal?** If yes, it is not an ordinary everyday word.
- **(iv) Never an ordinary everyday word, whoever is in the room:** a sworn or binding promise or undertaking; an acknowledgment, a witness clause or a speaking-for; the entering completion; any entry into hearing; a claim on a zone head's office (pay or water); a commission, a muster, a handover, a courier's delivery or a charge; an edict or a counter-sign; a rite oath; anything in an official court ruling. A word becomes one of these the moment the speaker stands to it, goes outside for it and names hearers to it, and that change of act is the signal, as it was before.
- **(v) The fence, stated because the loosening is small and must stay small.** A child under fourteen still cannot enter hearing, cannot make or be named in an acknowledgment or a witness clause (CRITICAL, §G16.8), cannot count as a hearer of a sworn or binding matter, cannot be the person afterwards produced for one, and cannot move a matter from ordinary to sworn weight. A child who is in the room when a sworn matter is made is not a hearer of it and does not count (Case B stands). A pre-rite child, Prince Aegon included, can hear and so witness a greeting, a household promise to fetch water or a neighbour's thanks; he cannot enter a hearing, witness a promise made standing and outdoors with hearers named, or be named as a hearer.

### §G16.2 — The Spoken Acknowledgment: The Gale CAT-5 Form

**Author ruling, verbatim and load-bearing:** `[ASKED]` *"Address in Gale is something you say rather than a rank you cite — the subject speaks their acknowledgment aloud and it counts because it was heard. Grows straight out of nothing binding being written."* **This is the kingdom's Rule P29-13.7.1 vocabulary and it is Locked Canon on the point that it is Gale's own.** The specific words below are `[DERIVED]` and flagged for author confirmation before first Area use.

**G16.2.1 — What the form is, stated as a category before it is stated as words.** `[DERIVED]` In Gale the lower-rank-to-sovereign address is **not a title attached to the addressee. It is an utterance the speaker makes about themselves**, and its function is to **put the speaker onto the record** — because in this kingdom the record is the hearing. The doctrinal root is the Collective Behavior Code's **SPOKEN INTO THE WIND** provision above: nothing binding is written, *"the trail of an agreement is the people who heard it, and that is the whole of the record,"* witnesses are named aloud *"so that the record is constituted at the moment of speaking rather than assembled afterwards,"* and *"a promise is made on the feet, so a member rises before they commit."* An address system built on that premise cannot work by citing rank, because a cited rank is a written-instrument device: it labels a person against a register that exists somewhere else. **Gale has no somewhere else.** So the subject says the thing, and the saying is the address.

**The single most important structural consequence, and the one a writer must hold: the form does not scale with the addressee's rank. It scales with how much of the speaker is not yet on the record.** The same construction is spoken to the Wind-Crown, to a zone governor and to the Stone Tribunal; what changes across the three formality depths of **Rule P29-13.7.3** is how much the *speaker* must say about *themselves*.

**G16.2.2 — The three constituents.** `[DERIVED]` The acknowledgment is one short spoken act of at most three constituents, in fixed order:

| # | Constituent | Words | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The vocative** + **the standing clause** | ***"[Office], I stand in hearing."*** | Locates the hearer by **office-noun** (never by rank-honorific — the office-noun points, it does not elevate; see §G16.4) and declares the speaker **audible, present on their feet, and answerable for what follows.** The standing is literal: §7.1's oath was sworn standing, and the code's in-group marker is that *a member rises before they commit.* |
| 2 | **The self-naming clause** | ***"I am [wind-name]."*** | Enters the speaker **personally**, by the term that is their true name in wind-sworn contexts (§G16.1.1). Without it the speaker has entered only their function; with it they have entered themselves, and can be called on afterwards. Where §G16.1.7 does not require the wind-name, the name here may be the speaker's birth name, or their wind-name if they choose it. The speaker must still HOLD a wind-name (§G16.1.6(iv)) |
| 3 | **The witness clause** | ***"Heard by [wind-name], and by [wind-name]."*** | Constitutes the record at the moment of speaking, naming aloud who else can afterwards be produced. This is the code's `[ASKED]` in-group marker — *naming the witnesses aloud, in full, at the moment of speaking* — performed inside the address act. Where §G16.1.7 does not require the wind-name, each hearer is named by birth name, or by wind-name if the speaker chooses. Each hearer must still HOLD a wind-name and be producible (§G16.2.5 condition 1) |

**G16.2.3 — Order is fixed and its violation is legible.** `[DERIVED]` Vocative, standing, self-naming, witnesses. The order runs outward from the room to the speaker to the record, and a speaker who inverts it has said the record exists before saying who is in it. Out-of-order constituents are a MINOR error class (§G16.8) unless the prose flags the disorder as characterization.

**G16.2.4 — THE THREE GRADUATED DEPTHS (Rule P29-13.7.3), expressed in a system whose unit is an utterance rather than a title.** `[DERIVED]` **Rule P29-13.7.3** sets three floors — senior official **moderate**, new staff **high**, commoner petitioner in formal court **highest**. Gale expresses them by **constituent count**, because in a said-not-cited system the only thing that can deepen is how much is said:

| Rule P29-13.7.3 floor | Speaker | Constituents | The line, in full | Why this depth and no other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Moderate** | A **senior official** of the court or of a state organ, addressing the sovereign | **1 only** | ***"Wind-Crown, I stand in hearing."*** | Their wind-name is already in the room's standing record and every person present could produce it. Saying it again would assert that it was in doubt. **Brevity here is not familiarity — it is the proof of standing.** |
| **High** | **New palace staff**, a newly seated official, anyone whose person the room does not yet hold | **1 + 2** | ***"Wind-Crown, I stand in hearing. I am [wind-name]."*** | The office is known; the person is not. They must enter themselves before anything they say can be relied on. The self-naming stops being required on the day the room can produce the name without them. |
| **Highest** | A **commoner petitioner in formal court** | **1 + 2 + 3** | ***"Wind-Crown, I stand in hearing. I am [wind-name]. Heard by [wind-name], and by [wind-name]."*** | Nothing about them is on any record. They must constitute the whole of it themselves — standing, person, and the hearers who can afterwards be produced. **The witness clause at this depth is the petitioner's own protection and not a courtesy to the crown**, and that is the correct way to write the beat: a petitioner who omits it has protected nobody. |

Reading note for this table and the one above. Where §G16.1.7 does not require the wind-name, read every [wind-name] as [name]: the speaker's birth name, or the wind-name if they choose it. A commoner petitioner heard in formal court on a petition is inside an official court ruling from entry (§G16.1.7), so for that speaker the wind-name is REQUIRED. The constituent counts and the depths are unchanged, and no cell is rewritten.

**Two derived corollaries of the petition case, flagged.** `[DERIVED]` **(i)** A petitioner reaching the Aerie Palace has already undergone the **Wind Crossing**, *"a ritual passage along an exposed stone causeway where the ambient wind is said to measure the petitioner's intent"* (`gale-kingdom.md` §5.3, canon's phrase, cited for the causeway and not extended); the acknowledgment is spoken at the causeway's end and again in the hall, because the hearers differ. **(ii)** Depth is a **floor**, not a ceiling: a speaker may go deeper than their standing requires, and doing so is legible — it claims the matter is graver than the room has understood, or it is a self-demotion. Going shallower is not rudeness; see §G16.2.6.

**G16.2.5 — WHAT MAKES AN ACKNOWLEDGMENT COUNT.** `[DERIVED]` except where marked `[ASKED]`. Four conditions, all necessary, and none of them is the addressee's assent:

1. **It must be HEARD by at least one wind-named PERSON other than the speaker, who can afterwards be named and produced.** `[ASKED]` **AUTHOR-CONFIRMED by the ruling closing open item 1 — this condition is no longer derived. It is the system's engine and it is ruled.** This is the whole of the first ruling's *"it counts because it was heard."* **The sovereign's own hearing is sufficient but is not necessary** — what the system requires is a hearer who can be called on, and a sovereign is one such person among others. A person under fourteen cannot serve (§G16.1.4). **The wind itself cannot serve either, outside the two wind-witness rites — see §G16.2.10.** "wind-named" means a person who HOLDS a wind-name, past the Rite, fourteen or over; it does not require that the name be spoken (§G16.1.6(iv)). This condition governs an acknowledgment and every sworn or binding matter. An ORDINARY EVERYDAY WORD needs no such hearer: anyone may witness it, and that is witnessed and not binding (§G16.1.8)
2. **It must be spoken in the open.** The code's provision is that *the important sentence is not said under a roof.* **The wall-less palace is what makes court address possible without leaving the room**: the Aerie Palace *"has no walls in the ordinary sense"* and its halls are open colonnades and wind-aperture halls that treat the wind as an intentional interior element (`gale-kingdom.md` §5.3, §5.12). The perimeter is wards, not walls — so the seat of government is already outdoors, and an acknowledgment spoken in the throne hall satisfies the condition on its face. **This is the single most useful fact in the system for a writer**, and it is why Gale court address needs no special staging.
3. **It must be in the speaker's own voice.** No herald, no messenger, no delegate, no token, no seal. The code has already settled this for the counter-sign — *"the counter-sign cannot be given by messenger, seal, token or delegate, because none of those is a voice"* — and the same reason governs here: a relayed acknowledgment makes the trail the relayer's, not the speaker's.
4. **It must be made on the speaker's feet.** *A promise is made on the feet, so a member rises before they commit* (in-group markers, above). The standing clause is a description of what the speaker is physically doing, not a metaphor.

**G16.2.6 — WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT IS NOT HEARD. Three distinct cases, and they are not the same case.** `[DERIVED]` This is the question the system exists to answer and it must not be collapsed into one answer:

- **Case A — spoken, and genuinely not heard.** The wind took it, the distance was too great, the hall was loud, the storm was up. **It did not happen.** It is not an insult, not a failure and not a fault; it is a non-event, and **it is simply said again.** Nobody is embarrassed and nobody is expected to be, because a culture that locates the record in the hearing rather than the saying has no reason to treat an unheard utterance as anything at all. **A Gale speaker repeats without being prompted and keeps repeating until someone acknowledges hearing** — and an outsider reading that repetition as slow-wittedness or as insistence has misread it. Prose may show a line said two and three times with no rise in temperature.
- **Case B — heard, but by nobody who counts.** Someone heard it, but no wind-named person who can afterwards be named — only children, only strangers, only the speaker's own people, nobody producible. The exchange **proceeds**: the sovereign may answer, business may be done, and nothing is void. But **nothing said in it can afterwards be relied on**, and the **Stone Tribunal** will not adjudicate on it, because there is no record for it to adjudicate against (§7.3 — the Tribunal adjudicates wind-oath violations). This is the practical engine of the whole system: it is why the petitioner names witnesses, why the counter-sign is a public act, and why a Gale official quietly counts the room before speaking. **CASE B IS UNTOUCHED BY THE AUTHOR RULING ON THE WIND, AND IS STATED AGAIN HERE BECAUSE THE RULING IS EXACTLY WHAT PRESERVES IT.** `[ASKED]` The wind is **not** a hearer of last resort standing behind an empty room. An acknowledgment heard by nobody who counts **still leaves nothing that can afterwards be relied on**, and that remains the reason a petitioner names witnesses — **for their own protection, not as a courtesy to the crown.** Had the wind been able to constitute the record, Case B would have collapsed into Case A and the system would have had no engine; the ruling is that it does not.
- **Case C — WITHHELD. The speaker does not make the acknowledgment at all, in a place where one was due.** This is the real breach and it is a different order of thing. Because there is no title to omit, **the failure cannot be read as coldness, stiffness or bad manners — there is no scale of politeness for it to sit on. What is present is an absence**, and the kingdom already owns the word for an absence where a sworn thing should have been: **"to leave a silence"** is the canonical continental idiom for breaking a wind-oath (§7.7). `[AUTHOR-PENDING]` **This block records that a withheld acknowledgment lands in that class and reads to the room as that kind of act, and it stops there:** it sets no penalty, because the code's **Enforcement** clause above expressly leaves the response to a crossed line unspecified in kind and degree, and this block invents none. Whether a withheld acknowledgment is in fact one of the **few absolute lines** is routed to the author (open item 3, §G16.9).

**G16.2.7 — The acknowledgment is an ENTRY ACT, not a per-utterance honorific.** `[DERIVED]` It is made **once, on entering hearing**, and is not repeated line by line. After it, the speaker uses ordinary name-terms and office-nouns as the sentence needs them, or nothing at all. **This is why Gale has no repeated per-line honorific** — the record is constituted once and does not need re-constituting every time the speaker opens their mouth. Writing the acknowledgment as a recurring tag is a MINOR error class (§G16.8). **Re-acknowledgment is required in exactly two situations:** the speaker left hearing and returned; or the matter moved from ordinary weight to sworn weight, in which case the acknowledgment is made again at the depth the sworn matter requires.

**G16.2.8 — The completion: how the addressee closes the act (this is CAT-6, and it is the sovereign's whole instrument).** `[DERIVED]` The acknowledgment is completed by the addressee, and there are exactly two completions:

- **Naming the speaker back** — the sovereign, governor or tribunal answers using the speaker's name. **This enters them.**  in an official court ruling or at a named rite the wind-name is used. Elsewhere either name enters the speaker: if the sovereign, governor or tribunal chooses the wind-name it moves the matter to sworn weight in front of everyone, and if not, the birth name enters the speaker and the act sets the weight. The name-back is needed to ENTER the speaker; it is not needed to rely on a matter sworn by the act (author ruling)
- **Answering without a name** — *"Speak."* **This hears them without entering them.** It is not a rebuke and carries no heat; it is a decision about the record, taken in public, in one word, and everyone in the hall understands what has been decided.

**That binary is the entire discretionary power the system gives a Gale sovereign over address**, and it is consistent with **Rule P29-13.8.4**, which makes sovereign-to-petitioner address *"dignified and impersonal unless the petitioner is known to the sovereign."*

**G16.2.9 — Scope of the form: civil, not universal.** `[DERIVED]` The spoken acknowledgment is the **civil and court** form — it governs address to the sovereign, to the organs of state and to the offices at §G16.4. It does **not** govern cultivation-context address, which is CAT-7 (§G16.3, CAT-7 row) under **Rule P29-13.2.1**; and it does **not** govern military address, which is expressly outside this block (§G16.5). In a mixed setting, **Rule P29-13.2.1's primary-purpose test decides** and no Gale-specific override is created.

**G16.2.10 — THE WIND AS WITNESS: RITE-SPECIFIC ONLY. CLOSED BY AUTHOR RULING.** `[ASKED]` The **Sovereign Ascension Oath** is *"sworn at every accession, at the Wind Crossing, witnessed by palace-wind"* (§7.5) — a rite in which **the wind itself is the named witness** — and the Wind Crossing is canonically where ambient wind *measures a petitioner's intent* (`gale-kingdom.md` §5.3). §G16.2.5's first condition requires a wind-named **person**. **The question of whether those two canon facts generalise was routed to the author as open item 1 and has been ruled. THE ANSWER IS NO.**

> **Author ruling, verbatim:** *"No — only at the named rites. The wind witnesses at the Ascension Oath and the Wind Crossing because those specific rites say so, and nowhere else. Ordinary acknowledgment needs a person who can afterwards be produced. Keeps the system's engine intact."*

**What is settled, and it is Locked Canon on the point:** `[ASKED]` **(i)** The palace-wind's witnessing is **a property of two wind-witness rites and of nothing else** — it holds at the **Sovereign Ascension Oath** and at the **Wind Crossing**, because those rites say so, and **it does not extend by analogy to any other occasion.** **(ii)** An **ordinary acknowledgment requires a wind-named person who can afterwards be produced**, without exception (§G16.2.5 condition 1, `[ASKED]`). **(iii)** **A speaker alone under the open sky has made no record.** The wind carried it; the wind cannot be called on to say so. **(iv)** Consequently **Case B at §G16.2.6 stands entirely unchanged** and does not collapse into Case A — the ruling's own stated reason is that it *"keeps the system's engine intact,"* and the engine is Case B. **An Area in which a solitary Gale speaker's words bind because the wind heard them is a CRITICAL error** (§G16.8).

**One distinction preserved rather than erased.** `[DERIVED]` The Crossing's ambient wind **measures a petitioner's intent** (`gale-kingdom.md` §5.3, canon's phrase); measuring is not recording, and the ruling does not turn the Crossing into a hearer for anything other than the rites named. A petitioner who has crossed still speaks their acknowledgment to people at the causeway's end and again in the hall (§G16.2.4).

**G16.2.11 — SPOKEN FOR: HOW A PRE-RITE CHILD ENTERS HEARING BEFORE AN ORGAN OF STATE. CLOSED BY AUTHOR RULING.** `[ASKED]` **§G16.1.4** establishes that a Gale child under fourteen has **no wind-name**; **§G16.2.2** makes the speaker's **own wind-name** the acknowledgment's second constituent. The form is therefore **unavailable to such a child at any depth** — not shortened, not reduced, unavailable. The reason is now HOLDING and not the second constituent. Where §G16.1.7 does not require the wind-name, constituent 2 may carry a birth name, but the speaker of an acknowledgment must hold a wind-name, and a child under fourteen holds none, so the form is still unavailable to him at any depth. Nothing in the rule lets a child enter hearing **What a pre-rite child does on entering hearing before an organ of state that is NOT a parent** — the **Wind-Council**, a **Governor**, the **Long Coast Warden**, the **Stone Tribunal** — **was established nowhere**, and was flagged as a drafting blocker rather than invented. **It has been ruled.**

> **Author ruling, verbatim:** *"He is spoken for."* — *"An adult with a wind-name makes the acknowledgment and names him. Needs no new form, and it is consistent with his locked 'the absence is the signature.' It also gives an eight-year-old prince a real constraint: he cannot speak for himself before his own state."*

**(a) WHO SPEAKS — an adult with a wind-name.** `[ASKED]` That is the class the ruling supplies, and **it is sufficient to stage the room**: any wind-named adult present may make the act, and the act is theirs. Two things are certain in both directions and follow from the system rather than from taste: the speaker **must be wind-named**,  because the speaker of an entry acknowledgment must HOLD a wind-name, whether or not the name is spoken (§G16.1.6(iv)); and the speaker **must be an adult**, because a person under fourteen cannot serve anywhere in this system (§G16.1.4). `[AUTHOR-PENDING]` **What canon does not settle is whether that class NARROWS.** No guardian, escort, presenting office, tutor or attendant exists in Gale canon; **no Rule P29-13.7.4 long-service relationship is established for this court** (open item 5); and **none is coined here** (Part 28 Area 20). **Whether a particular relationship is required is routed as open item 17 and is not decided.**

**(b) WHAT IS ACTUALLY SAID — the speaker's own acknowledgment, at the speaker's own depth, with the child named INSIDE ITS SECOND CONSTITUENT.** `[DERIVED]` **No new form is created; the ruling's own point is that none is needed.** Where the naming falls is worked out and not chosen:

- **Constituent 1 cannot carry it.** The vocative and standing clause locate the hearer and declare **the speaker** audible, on their feet and answerable; it is about the speaker's body in the room (§G16.2.2).
- **Constituent 3 must NOT carry it.** The witness clause names those who can afterwards be produced, and a hearer must be **a wind-named PERSON** — a pre-rite child cannot serve (§G16.2.5 condition 1; §G16.1.4). **Naming a pre-rite child in a witness clause is a CRITICAL error** (§G16.8).
- **Constituent 2 carries it, because constituent 2 is the constituent that says who is entering**, and the whole of *"he is spoken for"* is that the speaker enters the child along with themselves: ***"I am [wind-name]. I speak for [birth name]."*** The wording is `[DERIVED]` and is flagged with the rest of the block's wording at **open item 9**. Read [wind-name] as [name] wherever §G16.1.7 does not require the wind-name: the speaker's birth name, or their wind-name if they choose it. The speaker still holds a wind-name, the child is still named by birth name, and the two-constituent floor is unchanged (§G16.1.6(v))
- **The child is named by BIRTH NAME**, because it is the only term that exists for him. `[DERIVED]` **This is NOT the §G16.1.3 refusal-to-be-bound.** That rule presupposes a speaker choosing between two terms; here **there is no choice**, and a room written as reading it as a declining has been written wrong.
- **THE DEPTH IS THE SPEAKER'S, NEVER THE CHILD'S.** `[DERIVED]` The Gale form does not scale with the addressee or with anyone's rank — it scales with **how much of the speaker is not yet on the record** (§G16.2.1). **A prince's presence raises and lowers not one constituent.**
- **BUT SPEAKING FOR A CHILD RAISES THE FLOOR TO TWO CONSTITUENTS.** `[DERIVED]` The naming lives inside constituent 2, so **a speaker whose own standing sets the floor at constituent 1 alone must speak constituent 2 anyway in order to carry the child** — there is nowhere else for the naming to go. Depth is a floor and not a ceiling (§G16.2.4), so the deepening is permitted; what is derived is that here it is **required**. Flagged with open item 9.
- **In every other respect the act is ordinary and is the speaker's.** §G16.2.5's four conditions apply **to the speaker**, unchanged: heard by a producible wind-named person other than the speaker, spoken in the open, in the speaker's own voice, on the speaker's feet.

**(c) WHAT THE CHILD DOES WHILE IT HAPPENS — he says nothing.** `[DERIVED]` He speaks **no constituent**, speaks nothing alongside it, and confirms nothing after it. **There is no reduced, partial, junior or child form of the acknowledgment and this ruling creates none** — its whole economy is that an existing form in another mouth does the work. **His posture, his placement and what he does with himself are conduct and are not this block's property**: nothing here requires or forbids anything of them, and **the standing clause is a statement about the speaker that says nothing whatever about the child.**

**(d) THE COMPLETION ANSWERS THE SPEAKER.** `[DERIVED]` §G16.2.8's binary — **the name back**, which enters, or ***"Speak,"*** which hears without entering — closes the act, and **the act was the speaker's.** `[AUTHOR-PENDING]` **Whether an addressee may in addition name the child** — a naming that could carry no register consequence, the child's only name being the ordinary-register one — **is not established and is not invented** (open item 19).

**(e) WHAT HE MAY SAY AFTERWARDS, ONCE HEARING IS OPEN.** `[DERIVED]` The act complete, hearing is open and **he may speak as anyone speaks after the entry act** — answer, ask, be questioned, use ordinary name-terms and office-nouns as the sentence needs (§G16.2.7). **Three limits, none of which may be softened:** **(i)** everything he says is **in the ordinary register and constitutes no record**, whatever he intends by it (§G16.1.4); **(ii)** **he cannot move a matter from ordinary weight to sworn weight**, because the re-acknowledgment §G16.2.7 requires for that is the very form unavailable to him; **(iii)** **his hearing makes no record**, so he cannot be the person afterwards produced for anything said in that room.

### §G16.7 — Writing a Correct Line: The Four Questions

`[DERIVED]` A writer needs no further ruling to produce a correct Gale address line. Four questions, in order:

1. **Which hierarchy is active?** Rule P29-13.2.1's primary-purpose test. Political or court → the civil system below. Cultivation or academy → CAT-7 (§G16.3). Military → **`gale-kingdom-military.md` §6.11, which is where it is established; §G16.5 is the pointer and supplies nothing itself.**
2.  is the matter an official court ruling or one of the three wind-name rites (§G16.1.7)? Yes: the wind-name. No: the birth name, or the wind-name if the speaker chooses it to escalate (§G16.1.3). Whether the matter is sworn is decided by the act (§G16.1.6(iii)), not by the name Under fourteen → birth name, always, and no sworn matter is available (§G16.1.4).
3. **Is this an entry into hearing?** If the speaker is entering the presence of the sovereign, an organ of state or an office, **and is lower-rank**, the acknowledgment is made **once**, at the depth **Rule P29-13.7.3** assigns to that speaker (§G16.2.4). If the speaker has already entered, **no acknowledgment is written** — use the office-noun or the name as the sentence needs. **➤ If the person entering is UNDER FOURTEEN, stop and go to §G16.2.11: the form is unavailable to them at any depth, a wind-named ADULT speaks it and names them inside constituent 2, and the child says nothing while it happens.** **➤ If the matter is PAY or WATER ADMINISTRATION, the office being entered is the zone head's — §G16.4.9, and *"Governor"* for three zones, *"Warden"* for the Long Coast.**
4. **Who is the completion?** The addressee closes the act with **the name back** or with ***"Speak"*** (§G16.2.8). One or the other is written; leaving the act unclosed is itself a beat and should be deliberate.

**Two standing traps.** `[DERIVED]` **(i)** The acknowledgment is **not a per-line tag** — writing it at every turn is the commonest way to get this system wrong (§G16.2.7). **(ii)** A Gale official does **not** say *"Your Majesty"* — that is the royal child's ruled public form (§G16.3, CAT-1) and it is a category error in an official's mouth. *(Superseded 2026-10-19 by the author's ruling of 2026-10-18, head note of this block: Gale people say "Your Majesty" in a public setting.
