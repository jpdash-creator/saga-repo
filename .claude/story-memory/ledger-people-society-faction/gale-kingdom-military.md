## Section 1 — Identity (B)

**Full Name:** **None established, and none is coined.** The kingdom's armed forces carry **no collective proper name** in canon. What canon names are the **four bodies** — the Stormwing Order, the Windward Host, the Long Coast Fleet, the Wind-Sworn Militia — and two standing formations, the Stone Vigil and the Northern Accord Expeditionary Wing. This file's title is a **descriptive ledger heading, not an in-world name** (Part 28 Area 20). Open item 2 at §6.10.
**Common Name / Aliases:** Canon's own collective phrase is **"wind-orders"** (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §7.4; `ledger-worlds-kingdom-state/gale-kingdom.md` §3), used of the four bodies together. It is retained here as canon's word and is **not** treated as a rung, a rank or a formal title.
**Faction Type (Part 22):** **MSF — Kingdom Army** (header). A **crown establishment**, tracked as an entity through the parent state's §7.3 governance organs and §7.4 command structure. Not a sect, not a guild, not an order of chivalry, and not an independent political body.
**Founding / Origin:** The forces descend from the founding muster of the **Gale Oath-Binding** — the mountain communities, Aerie Plateau clans and Long Coast harbor-lords bound by a single spoken oath at **Aerion's Landing Stone** (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §7.1). **No founding date, founding instrument or order-of-battle from any earlier reign is established**, and none is invented. Two named sovereigns of the intervening line touch military history and are cited, not extended: **Aerthos the Cliff-Warden**, who *fortified the Long Coast*, and **Aerek the Still-Winged**, whose reign is recorded as *"a controversial reign of deliberate military restraint"* (`ledger-worlds-location/gale-kingdom.md` §5.2).
**Command Authority:** The **Wind-Crown** (King Aeries) — executive authority and the source of every order; the civil authority from which the military chain descends. The **Windward Host Marshal** — **the Part 22 Area 5.4 Supreme Commander tier** (Section 3, §6.7). The **Storm-Counsel** (Queen Aalia as storm-keeper) — **counter-sign authority for military-force matters**, and canonically the office whose *"storm-keeper role governs the kingdom's military wind-cultivation orders"* (`ledger-worlds-location/gale-kingdom.md` §5.3); **an external gate on the chain, not a tier in it** (§6.7).
**Primary Purpose / Mission:** Defence of the kingdom under the doctrine of **Honored Restraint** — defensive posture with formal respect for the Windward Range neighbours, with **offensive war requiring a public wind-sworn declaration at the Wind Crossing** (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §7.2). §6.2 records the structural consequence: **the kingdom's largest formation cannot be assembled until that declaration is spoken.**
**Establishment Strength:** **Not established, and no total is invented.** Canon fixes no figure for any Gale formation's establishment. What this file fixes is the **ladder** — rung names, rung strengths, and the rank and Part 22 command tier at each rung — not how many of each rung the kingdom fields. Every total is left open at §6.10.

Personal custody of orders bounds it again, differently: **the number of standing orders a formation can hold is the number one person can carry accurately in their head and say again correctly at a handover.** That is a hard per-commander ceiling, and it does not rise when the kingdom grows richer or when the formation grows larger — **a brigade's orders and a squad's orders are held in exactly the same amount of one person's memory.** Two things in the table follow from it and are visible now the reason is stated: the ladder is **shallow** (seven Part 22 tiers and no more, so an order crosses few custodians between the Marshal and a squad), and the standing establishment stops at **brigade** — because the tier above a brigade is the tier that would have to re-speak the brigade's orders after a death, and the kingdom keeps that tier close.
- **Nothing crosses arms by seniority; it crosses by tier.** Part 22's chain is linear, so a Colonel of the Host does not command a Stormwing company because he is a Colonel — he commands what his appointment gives him, and cross-arm authority meets at **Strategic Command** and above (§6.7).

### 6.6 — The Stormwing Order: Inner and Outer Cadre, Structurally

**The canon fact, unaltered:** *"full-blooded Dragon-Human cultivators form the inner cadre; partial-lineage and ordinary wind-cultivators form the outer cadre"* (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §7.4).

**⚠️ TWO SEPARATE AXES, AND NEITHER SILENTLY DETERMINES THE OTHER.** `[DERIVED]` *(structural reading; flagged)*

- **AXIS TWO — COMMAND TIER, WHICH IS PART 22 AREA 5.4 AND HAS A REALM FLOOR.** The seven tiers each carry a cultivation floor relative to the faction's peak (§6.1). That floor is **canon's, is about realm, and says nothing about lineage.**
- **⚠️ THE TWO ARE HELD APART DELIBERATELY.** Lineage does not set a command tier, and a command tier does not confer or imply lineage. **Nothing in this file lets one axis silently decide the other**, and **the royal dragon-lineage purity question — an open §7.8 obligation under Storm-Counsel jurisdiction, canonically sensitive and politically live — is not touched, advanced or resolved anywhere.**

**What the cadre distinction does, structurally.** `[DERIVED]` **It decides what a formation is composed of, and therefore what it is given to do.** The Order's **guard companies** at the royal seat are canonically manned from the sworn dragon-lineage cultivators — an inner-cadre manning. The **response companies** draw on the whole Order. **Both stand on the one ladder and both sit inside the same seven Part 22 tiers.**

**Cadre is not noble standing either.** §7.2's **Dragon-Lineage Precedence** confers **automatic noble standing** on full-blooded Dragon-Human cultivators of Aerion's descent, and grades partial-lineage standing by cultivation realm and wind-oath performance. **Noble standing is a civil status; it commands no soldier.** A rank commands soldiers. The two run alongside each other in the same Order and **canon nowhere reconciles them.**

**⚠️ THE QUESTION THIS STRUCTURE RAISES AND MAY NOT ANSWER.** **May an outer-cadre officer command inner-cadre soldiers?** The Part 22 answer is that **the tier governs and the cadre does not** — that is what a linear chain and one ladder mean together. But the answer runs directly into §7.2's precedence doctrine, and **that doctrine is the politically live lineage question the Storm-Counsel holds jurisdiction over.** **This file does not answer it.** Registered as **open item 9** and routed to the author.

**What is deliberately NOT established and NOT invented anywhere in this subsection:** any test, rite, register, mark or address form by which cadre membership is determined, conferred, recorded or withdrawn; **any realm requirement for either cadre**; any quota, ratio or establishment for either; and any consequence of lineage for pay, precedence or promotion. **Canon fixes the two cadres and nothing about their machinery, and none is coined** (Part 28 Area 20).

### 6.7 — The Command Chain, and the Authorisation Gate on Top of It

**⚠️ PART 22 AREA 5.4 GOVERNS THE CHAIN: *"linear authority from supreme commander to smallest unit."* GALE'S ONE VARIANT IS AN EXTERNAL AUTHORISATION GATE, AND IT SITS ON TOP OF THE CHAIN RATHER THAN INSIDE IT.** Area 5.4 permits variants in terms — *"a variant of the command chain hierarchy"* — and this is Gale's.

**THE LINEAR CHAIN — canon's seven tiers, filled.**

**Wind-Crown** *(King Aeries — civil executive authority; the source from which orders descend; **not a military tier**)*
**↓**
**SUPREME COMMANDER — the Windward Host Marshal**
**↓**
**STRATEGIC COMMAND — the Stormwing Grand Captain · the Long Coast Warden (Admiral) · a General, when a division stands**
**↓**
**OPERATIONAL COMMAND — Colonel** *(brigade)* **· Lieutenant-Colonel** *(regiment; militia regiment on muster; Fleet shore establishment)* **· Commodore** *(flotilla)*
**↓**
**TACTICAL COMMAND — Major** *(battalion; the Flightcraft arm)* **· Captain** *(company; cutter squadron; Flightcraft squadron; battalion second)*
**↓**
**UNIT COMMAND — Lieutenant** *(platoon; cutter; flight)*
**↓**
**SPECIALIST GRADE — Sergeant Major · Staff Sergeant · Sergeant · Petty Officer** *(squad)*
**↓**
**ENLISTED — Corporal · Lance-Corporal · Private / Seaman / Airman · Recruit**

**One carve-out is flagged rather than asserted:** the Stormwing's **guard companies** discharge a Part 22 Area 5.3 **Royal Guard** function, which Area 5.3 places outside the MSF military mandate. **Whether they sit in the Marshal's linear chain or answer directly to the Wind-Crown is NOT established** — open item 8.

**THE AUTHORISATION GATE — ON TOP OF THE CHAIN, NOT A STEP IN IT.**

**The Storm-Counsel** *(Queen Aalia as storm-keeper)* holds the **counter-sign authority for military-force matters** (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §7.3, §7.4) and canonically *"governs the kingdom's military wind-cultivation orders"* (`ledger-worlds-location/gale-kingdom.md` §5.3). **Registered position** `[DERIVED]`**: the Storm-Counsel holds NO Part 22 Area 5.4 tier, stands nowhere between two rungs of the chain above, and commands no soldier.** She is a **constitutional check on the linearity Part 22 supplies** — the chain is complete and unbroken from the Marshal down, **and it may not move to force until a voice outside it has spoken.** Whether her canonical governance of the wind-cultivation orders reaches further than authorisation — into manning, doctrine or appointment inside the Stormwing Order and the Host's wind-cultivation formations — is **NOT established.** Open item 11.

**WHAT THE COUNTER-SIGN IS, AND WHAT IT DOES TO A COMMANDER IN THE FIELD.** The parent file's **Collective Behavior Code** already resolved the mechanism from two author-locked elements, and this file **cites it and adds nothing to it**: **a Gale counter-sign is not a second mark; it is a second voice.** The order is spoken; the counter-sign is the storm-keeper standing and speaking it too — outdoors, before witnesses, witnesses named. **An order for military force the storm-keeper has not spoken is not a weak order, it is not an order** — no provisional form, no retroactive form, and no form that can be cured afterwards. And the code's own registered consequence: **the counter-sign cannot be given by messenger, seal, token or delegate, because none of those is a voice.**

**⚠️ THIS IS WHERE THE LINEAR CHAIN AND THE UNWRITTEN KINGDOM COLLIDE, AND IT IS THE HARDEST POINT IN THE STRUCTURE.** Part 22 gives Gale an unbroken line of authority from the Marshal to a squad. **The gate above that line cannot travel, cannot be delegated, and cannot be written down** — and since the ruling the orders inside that line **cannot be written or copied either** (§6.8 point 9). **Six consequences follow, all `[DERIVED]` and flagged.**

1. **FORCE CANNOT BE AUTHORISED AT A DISTANCE.** A commander three hundred twenty kilometers from the storm-keeper cannot obtain a counter-sign, because the thing he needs cannot travel. **The chain reaches him; the gate does not.**
2. **THEREFORE GALE AUTHORISES IN ADVANCE, IN THE OPEN, OVER A NAMED FORMATION, FOR A NAMED THING.** A formation departs with its authorisation **already spoken over it at its muster**, before named witnesses, covering what it may do. This is consistent with canon's own insistence that the militia clause and the Wind Crossing declaration are **public** acts rather than internal ones.
3. **A FIELD COMMANDER'S AUTHORITY ENDS EXACTLY WHERE THE WORDS SPOKEN OVER HIS MUSTER ENDED.** He cannot send for more. He cannot be granted more by a superior who is also in the field — **a superior's tier commands, and no tier authorises.** He cannot act and be forgiven afterwards; the code forecloses the retroactive form in terms. **What he was given at the muster is the whole of what he has, for the whole of the posting.**
4. **THE KINGDOM'S ORDERS ARE THEREFORE FEW AND BROAD RATHER THAN MANY AND NARROW.** A narrow authorisation would strand the formation the first time the situation moved. This is a force-structure reason — not a temperamental one — that the kingdom's posture is **defensive**: a military whose authority cannot be widened in the field is a military that holds ground.
5. **MOBILISATION SPEED IS A TRAVEL TIME.** The militia is mobilised by counter-sign, the counter-sign cannot be sent, and there are four zonal musters. **The kingdom's mobilisation rate is the time it takes to carry the storm-keeper to four muster grounds** — which the Aerie Flightcraft can do, canon recording a **Flight-fee** *charged to non-Gale citizens requiring Aerie Flightcraft transit of the kingdom* (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §7.6), so the craft carry passengers. **Whether the counter-sign may be carried to a muster by any other voice is NOT established**, and the code's own line that a **delegate** is not a voice reads against it. Open item 11.

6. **⚠️ AND SINCE THE AUTHOR'S RULING THE GATE IS NOT THE ONLY UNWRITABLE THING IN THE CHAIN.** `[ASKED]` *(**"The commander holds them personally"** — §6.8 point 9; the working below is `[DERIVED]` and flagged.)* **The counter-sign authorises force and the commander's standing orders direct it, and neither can be written, relayed or delegated.** They are **two authorities of the same kind and of different shapes**: the counter-sign is spoken **over a formation in public** and so survives any one death, while the orders are spoken **to one person** and die with him. **The full interaction — what a decapitated formation retains, why a campaign must be planned backwards from its muster, and why the two independent single points of failure can be lost together — is worked at §6.8 point 9 (e) and is not duplicated here.** The one line that belongs in this section: **the gate cannot travel and the orders cannot be reissued at a distance, so a Gale formation in the field is limited both in what it may do and in what it can be told, and neither limit can be lifted by anything sent to it.**

**⚠️ AND ON THE LONG COAST THE GATE REACHES A CIVIL AUTHORITY, BECAUSE ONE PERSON IS BOTH.** `[ASKED]` *(the dual office is author-ruled, — "One post, both hats"; the consequence below is `[DERIVED]` and flagged.)* **The Long Coast Warden commands the Fleet and governs the zone in one office** (Section 3). The counter-sign gate therefore **falls on a person who is simultaneously the territory's civil head** — and it falls on them **in one capacity only.** Three consequences, stated because they are structural rather than decorative:
- **The Warden may act as the zone's civil authority without any counter-sign at all.** Governance is not military force, the gate is on force, and no part of the zone's civil administration passes through the Storm-Counsel.
- **The Warden may NOT move the Fleet to force on their own authority as the zone's head.** Being the civil authority for the water does not authorise fighting on it. **The gate is unchanged by the second hat**, and a Warden who moved the Fleet to force on a spoken order of their own has not issued a weak order — they have issued no order (§6.7's opening).
- **The two therefore have to be told apart in the moment, by the same person, out loud, in front of the same witnesses.** In the kingdom's other three zones a Governor and a commander are two people and the line between civil and military authority is drawn between them. **On the Long Coast the line runs through one office**, and the kingdom's only instrument for marking which side of it a decision falls on is the one it uses for everything else: **who was standing there, and what was said** (§6.8).

**⚠️ ONE QUESTION THE STRUCTURE CANNOT LEAVE UNASKED AND MUST NOT ANSWER.** **Does a soldier under attack need a counter-sign to defend himself?** Canon says *military force requires the Storm-Counsel counter-sign* and carves out nothing. A defensive kingdom whose soldiers cannot fight back without a gate that cannot reach them is not coherent, which reads strongly toward **no** — but that is a reading, and the counter-sign is one of the few absolute lines, where readings are exactly what the culture refuses to make. **Not decided here.** **Open item 11a**, routed to the author.

**WAR-SCALE AUTHORISATION.** `[DERIVED]` Offensive war requires a **public wind-sworn declaration at the Wind Crossing** (§7.2). Structurally, **the declaration is the authorisation for war-scale force, and the division — Part 22's Strategic Command scale — is the formation it authorises** (§6.2). One fact seen from two sides.

**THE JUDICIAL LINE.** The **Stone Tribunal** adjudicates wind-oath violations (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §7.3). A soldier's oath is a wind-oath, so **a soldier's breach goes to the Stone Tribunal.** **No military judicial forum distinct from it is established anywhere in canon, and none is invented** — recommendation at open item 8. Canon's idiom for the failure stands: **"to leave a silence"** (§7.7).

### 6.8 — How an Unwritten Military Works

**⚠️ THIS IS A DESIGN PROBLEM, NOT A DECORATION — AND PART 22's LINEARITY IS WHAT MAKES IT ONE.** A linear chain from supreme commander to smallest unit is an instrument for moving orders down seven tiers reliably. **Gale must run that instrument with no documents anywhere in it.** Nothing binding in Gale is written down. The kingdom's registered collective conduct is **SPOKEN INTO THE WIND** — *a promise is made standing, outdoors, before witnesses; the trail of an agreement is the people who heard it, and that is the whole of the record; a paper promise is distrusted on its face; witnesses are named aloud; asking a Gale member to put it in writing is a small insult* — and **FEW RULES, ABSOLUTE** — *very few regulations, and the ones held cannot be bent by anyone, for any reason, in any circumstance; no warning, no graduated penalty.* Both are author-locked provisions of the parent file's Collective Behavior Code, **cited here and not restated into anything new.** Below is how a seven-tier chain runs on voices. **All of it is `[DERIVED]` and flagged for Check #21, with ONE EXCEPTION: point 9's custodian is `[ASKED]` — the author ruled that the commander holds his formation's standing orders personally, and only the consequences worked out from that ruling are `[DERIVED]`.**

**1 — THE DISTINCTION THAT MAKES THE WHOLE THING POSSIBLE: A COUNT IS NOT A PROMISE.** What the code forbids is **binding** writing. **Rolls, stores ledgers, pay lists, ration quantities, hull inventories and casualty counts are counts, not commitments** — they record what is, and they bind nobody. **Gale writes those freely.** What is never written is anything that **binds a person**: an order, a commission, a muster obligation, an authorisation, an oath, a charge. The kingdom therefore has clerks, stores, a pay office and a quartermaster's arithmetic, and **not one binding instrument anywhere in the establishment.** The corollary, and it matters: **the muster roll is evidence of a count and never evidence of a commission.** Being written on the roll does not make a man a soldier. **The speaking did.**

**2 — ORDERS TRAVEL IN PEOPLE, WHICH IS WHY THE AIR ARM EXISTS.** A kingdom that cannot send paper must send a voice. **The Aerie Flightcraft messenger arm is therefore not a convenience of the Windward Host — it is the kingdom's command-and-control system entire** (§6.4), and it is what makes Part 22's seven-tier linearity physically possible over a kingdom that runs from a river frontier to a sea coast. The courier's function is precise: **a courier does not carry an order; the courier IS the order.** He stood there. He heard it spoken. He speaks it again at the far end, in the first person of the man who gave it, **and he names the witnesses who stood with him.**

**3 — THE RELAY SUMMONS; IT DOES NOT ORDER.** The Storm-Kite Relays are inscribed frames, and inscription is writing. **A kite can say *come*, *stand to*, *a thing has happened*; it cannot say *do*.** The relay is how Gale reaches a garrison quickly. The Flightcraft is how Gale tells it anything that binds.

**4 — VERIFICATION IS BY WITNESS, WHICH IS WHY ORDERS ARE FEW.** Every spoken order carries **its witness list with it**, because the code constitutes the record *at the moment of speaking* by naming the hearers aloud. A receiving officer who doubts a courier does not examine a seal — **he asks who was standing there, and he can send and ask them.** That is a real verification, and it is slow, and slowness is why the kingdom's orders are **few, broad and long-lived** rather than many, narrow and constantly revised. **FEW RULES, ABSOLUTE is not only the kingdom's culture; it is its signals doctrine.**

**5 — STANDING ORDERS ARE RE-SPOKEN, NOT POSTED. THE PARADE IS THE ARCHIVE.** A standing order in Gale survives by **being said again** — at muster, at relief, at the change of watch. There is no orders board, no standing-orders book, nothing pinned anywhere. What another army does with a noticeboard, this one does with a formation of men standing outdoors while the order is spoken over them again. **An order nobody has re-spoken in living memory has not been rescinded; it has been forgotten, which in this kingdom is the same thing** — a genuine institutional hazard, registered as one.

**6 — COMMISSIONS ARE SPOKEN, AND A COMMISSION IS THE PEOPLE WHO HEARD IT.** An officer is commissioned by **being spoken into the rank — standing, outdoors, before named witnesses.** No parchment, no seal, no warrant. Consequences, all structural:
- **A commission is verified the way everything else is: by asking who was there.**
- **A commission cannot be revoked by paper either.** It takes another speaking, in the same form, before witnesses.
- **A commission cannot be given by a messenger's mark or a token** — the reasoning the code applies to the counter-sign. Whether a *courier's voice* may carry a commission, as it carries an order, is **NOT established** (open item 11b).
- **This is why the kingdom carries no warrant-officer class** (§6.1) — and **Part 22 Area 5.4's own table carries no warrant tier**, so nothing is lost by it. A third class of commission would need a third class of witnesses, and a kingdom that commissions by voice cannot afford the memory.
- **And it is why the officer corps has a ceiling.** It can be no larger than the number of commissions the kingdom can personally attest — a real bound on force structure, and one reason brigade is the largest standing formation (§6.2).
- **The Part 22 tier a man holds is therefore constituted by a speaking, not by an establishment table.** Meeting a tier's cultivation floor (§6.1) qualifies nobody; **the words did.**

**7 — MUSTER IS AN EVENT, NOT A LIST.** The Wind-Sworn Militia is *raised by wind-sworn muster* (§7.4). A muster is a **public spoken occasion in the zone**: the obligation is called, the men come, the counter-sign is spoken over them, and the record of who is bound is the people standing in the field looking at each other. A man's militia obligation is not on a document that can be produced against him; **it is on the memory of his neighbours**, which in a kingdom with this code is the heavier instrument.

**8 — DISCIPLINE HAS NO CHARGE SHEET.** A charge is spoken before witnesses; a wind-oath breach goes to the **Stone Tribunal** (§6.7). The code's own enforcement grammar governs and is not softened: **there are no graduated penalties and there is no warning.** For a military that is stark and is stated rather than smoothed — **the kingdom's few military absolutes have no correction stage, so small soldiering faults are left entirely alone, and the one line that is touched is not survived.**

**9 — THERE IS NO ARCHIVE, AND THE AUTHOR HAS RULED THE CUSTODIAN: THE COMMANDER HOLDS THE ORDERS PERSONALLY.** `[ASKED]` *(author ruling, verbatim: **"The commander holds them personally"** — *"Each formation's orders live with whoever commands it, and pass at handover by being spoken. Makes a change of command genuinely dangerous."*)*

There is no orders archive to consult, because there were never any orders on paper to file. **The custodian is not an institution — it is a person, and it is a different person for every formation in the kingdom.** *Each formation's orders live with whoever commands it.* A squad's orders live in its **Sergeant**; a company's in its **Captain**; a brigade's in its **Colonel**; the armed forces' entire in the **Windward Host Marshal**. **There is no master set, no second holder, no deputy, no register and no copy.**

> **THE OATH-VOICES' CANON FUNCTION IS UNTOUCHED AND UNEXTENDED.** They keep the **state's Founding Oath** — that is what canon gives them and it is all they have here. **No military order, commission, muster obligation or authorisation passes through them, and no military role of any kind is created for them.**

**⚠️ THE AUTHOR CHOSE THE HARDER MECHANISM AND ITS CONSEQUENCES ARE WRITTEN OUT IN FULL RATHER THAN SOFTENED. THE RULING'S OWN WORDS ARE *"makes a change of command genuinely dangerous",* AND THIS IS WHAT THAT COSTS.** Everything from **(a)** to **(i)** is `[DERIVED]` and flagged for **Check #21**. **Where the structure has a failure mode canon does not answer, the failure mode is registered as live and routed to the author — no rescue mechanism is invented anywhere below.**

**(a) WHAT "PERSONALLY" MEANS ON A SEVEN-TIER LADDER: THE ORDERS NEST, AND NOBODY HOLDS THE WHOLE.** Every formation from squad to the establishment entire has one commander, and each commander holds **his own formation's orders and no one else's.** What a subordinate holds is **not a copy of his superior's orders** — it is the narrower thing that was spoken to him, out of the wider thing his superior was told. **The kingdom's standing orders are therefore not a body held anywhere; they are a set of non-overlapping fragments, one per commander, distributed across the whole officer corps, and no single person and no single place holds them all.** An enemy cannot capture Gale's orders, because there is nothing to capture. **Neither can Gale.**

**(b) A COMMANDER'S DEATH TAKES HIS FORMATION'S STANDING ORDERS WITH HIM. THERE IS NO CUSTODIAN AND NO COPY, AND THIS IS THE CENTRAL FACT.** Two things survive the death and one does not, and the distinction is the most consequential single line in this file:
- **The formation's parts keep their own orders.** Its subordinate commanders were each spoken to separately, so a decapitated brigade does not go blank — **it degrades to the sum of its regiments, each doing the narrower thing it was told, with nothing left that coordinates them.** The formation stops being a formation and becomes its own subordinate units standing next to each other.
- **The authorisation survives, because the authorisation was never held privately.** The Storm-Counsel counter-sign is spoken **over the whole formation at its muster, outdoors, before named witnesses** (§6.7 point 2) — so the permission is distributed across everyone who was standing there, and **the death of the commander does not touch it.**
- **⚠️ THEREFORE: THE PERMISSION SURVIVES AND THE PURPOSE DOES NOT.** A decapitated Gale formation is a body of soldiers who **remain lawfully authorised to use force and no longer have anything they have been told to do with it.** That is the exact shape of the danger the ruling names, and it is stated here rather than smoothed.

**ON WHOSE AUTHORITY DOES IT THEN ACT?** **Command succeeds automatically and the orders do not.** Part 22's chain is linear, so the next man by tier — the battalion's **Captain** as second, the formation's senior appointment, the surviving subordinate of highest tier — holds the post within the hour, and §6.8 point 6 governs how he is confirmed in it. **But succession transfers a post and cannot transfer a memory.** The successor commands the formation while holding **only the narrower orders he already had**, which are the orders of the formation he used to command. **The only route back to a full set is the ordinary one — the tier above speaks new ones — and that is a courier journey each way** (point 2), on Flightcraft, weather-limited. **That is not a remedy. It is the absence of one, written out as a duration.** *(Shown on the page at Chapter 005, Ref: 1.1.1.005, as one plain line in a lieutenant's count: a captain's death takes her company's standing orders with her, and no one has yet spoken the company new ones. The Chapter leaves open who gives them and when; §6.12 holds the line and the queen's understanding of it, which is hers alone.)*

**(c) HANDOVER IS THE MOMENT OF MAXIMUM INSTITUTIONAL VULNERABILITY, AND THE RITUAL HAS TO CARRY THE WHOLE WEIGHT OF IT.** The orders exist in exactly one head and pass by being spoken from one person to another. The code's own form governs and is not extended: **standing, outdoors, before named witnesses, the trail being the people who heard.** Six things follow:
- **It is a speaking, not a briefing.** The outgoing commander says each standing order aloud, and the incoming commander hears it with the witnesses named. **Whether the incoming commander must say it back — whether the ritual requires a return speaking to prove custody has actually transferred — is NOT established** (open item 20).
- **It is an audit of the formation's entire body of standing orders, performed out loud, and anything omitted is destroyed by the omission.** Point 5 already holds that an order nobody re-speaks has been forgotten and that forgetting is the same as rescinding. **At a handover that rule bites hardest: an order the outgoing commander fails to say is not merely mislaid — it ceases to exist at the moment he stops speaking.** A hurried handover is a rescission of whatever was skipped, and nobody in the kingdom can afterwards tell that anything was.
- **The witnesses must outlive the handover and must be findable**, because §6.8 point 4's verification is *send and ask the people who were standing there.* The natural witnesses are the formation's own — its **Sergeant Major** by appointment and its subordinate commanders. **But witnesses drawn wholly from inside the formation die with the formation**, so a handover witnessed only from within leaves nothing behind if the formation is destroyed entire. **Whether Gale requires a witness from outside the formation is NOT established** (open item 20).
- **It cannot be done by courier.** A courier **is** an order — one order, carried by one person who stood there (point 2). **A body of standing orders is not one order**, and whether a courier's voice may carry even a single commission is already open (item 11b). **So a change of command requires the two commanders in the same place**, which means it requires a journey, which means **a Gale formation cannot have its commander replaced at a distance.**
- **A dead man cannot hand over.** **Gale has a handover procedure and no succession procedure** — the ritual exists only for a planned relief. That asymmetry is the whole of the weakness at **(b)**, restated where it is visible.
- **There is a window in which the post and the orders are in different hands.** A man is commissioned into the post by one speaking and given the orders by another. **Whether the outgoing commander's authority persists until the second speaking is finished is NOT established** (open item 20). Between the two, the formation has a commander who does not yet know what he is commanding it to do, **and canon does not say who commands it in the interval.**

**(d) DECAPITATION IS DISPROPORTIONATELY EFFECTIVE AGAINST THIS KINGDOM. IT IS A GENUINE STRATEGIC WEAKNESS AND IT IS WRITTEN AS ONE.** Killing the commander of a written army removes a decision-maker and leaves the orders standing on paper for his successor. **Killing a Gale commander removes the decision-maker AND deletes the orders, in one act, with no recovery except a journey to the tier above.** The multiplier is structural, not incidental. Four things make it worse and are stated rather than argued away:
- **The system is not a secret and cannot be made one.** Gale musters in public, authorises outdoors before named witnesses, declares war on a causeway in the open, and sends couriers who announce themselves as the order. **Anyone who has watched a single Gale relief understands the entire mechanism.** A kingdom that constitutes its records in public **cannot keep the shape of its command system private**, and any enemy who has served alongside its soldiers, traded at its ports or stood at one of its musters knows exactly which one death is worth the most.
- **The obvious countermeasure is permanently unavailable.** Every other army's answer is a written copy held elsewhere. **That is the one thing the kingdom's code forecloses** — and it forecloses it under **FEW RULES, ABSOLUTE**, which admits no exception, no warning, no graduated form and no emergency variant. **Gale cannot buy its way out of this weakness, cannot reform its way out, and cannot make a wartime exception.**
- **The weakness scales with the value of the target.** The higher the tier killed, the more orders die and the further the courier must go to replace them — so the incentive runs **upward**, toward exactly the commanders the kingdom can least afford to lose, and culminates at **(f)**.
- **What partially offsets it is stated as partial, and it does not close the hole.** Orders are **few and broad** (point 4), so less is destroyed per death than a narrow-order army would lose; the ladder is **shallow** and the establishment **small** (§6.2), so the tier above is usually near; and **Honored Restraint** keeps the kingdom fighting on ground where that journey is short. **None of these prevents the loss. They shorten the interval — and the interval is where this kingdom is beaten.**

**(e) IT INTERACTS WITH THE COUNTER-SIGN, AND THE TWO UNWRITABLE AUTHORITIES FAIL IN DIFFERENT SHAPES.** Gale now runs on **two things that cannot be written and cannot be relayed**: the **Storm-Counsel counter-sign**, which authorises force, and the **commander's standing orders**, which direct it. Both live only in specific people. **They do not fail the same way, and the difference is the whole of Gale's campaign logic:**
- **The counter-sign is spoken over a formation in public and is therefore distributed** — it survives any one death, including the commander's. **The orders are spoken to one person and are therefore point-located** — they survive nothing.
- **Consequence: a decapitated formation keeps the WIDER of its two authorities and loses the NARROWER.** It retains the outer limit of what it may lawfully do and loses the instruction about what to do inside that limit.
- **⚠️ CONSEQUENCE FOR A CAMPAIGN, AND IT IS SEVERE.** A formation that has lost its orders can be re-tasked **only inside the words already spoken over it at its muster.** The tier above can send a new order; **it cannot send a new authorisation**, because the counter-sign cannot travel, cannot be delegated and cannot be carried by a messenger (§6.7 points 1 and 3). **So a new purpose can be given only if that purpose was already covered at the muster — and a campaign is precisely the situation moving past what was said at the muster.** Beyond those words the formation must return to a muster ground, or the storm-keeper must be brought to it.
- **The two single points of failure are independent, and one campaign can lose both.** Each formation's commander is one person; **the Storm-Counsel is one person for the whole kingdom.** Losing a commander costs a formation its purpose. Losing access to the storm-keeper costs **every** formation the ability to be given a new one. **Losing both is not compound bad luck — it is a field army that can neither be re-tasked nor re-authorised, standing on ground it is still permitted to hold and doing nothing else.**
- **Therefore a Gale campaign is planned backwards from the muster, not forwards from the front.** What is spoken over a formation before it leaves determines **every option it will ever have**, because neither of the two things it runs on can be added to afterwards. **That is a doctrine of pre-commitment, and the kingdom has no alternative to it.**

**(f) AT THE VERY TOP OF THE CHAIN THERE IS NO TIER ABOVE TO RE-SPEAK ANYTHING, AND THAT IS A LIVE WEAKNESS WITH NO IN-CANON ANSWER.** The **Windward Host Marshal** holds the Part 22 Area 5.4 **Supreme Commander** tier (Section 3) and therefore holds **the standing orders of the kingdom's armed forces entire.** Every consequence above applies to that post, and the recovery route at **(b)** does not: **the recovery route is "the tier above re-speaks," and above the Supreme Commander there is no military tier.**
- **What stands above is the Wind-Crown — a civil office holding no Area 5.4 tier** (Section 3, §6.7) — and canon makes the Wind-Crown **the source from which orders descend**, not the custodian of the standing body of them.
- **⚠️ WHETHER THE WIND-CROWN HOLDS, OR CAN RECONSTITUTE, THE MARSHAL'S STANDING ORDERS IS NOT ESTABLISHED, AND THE STRUCTURE HAS NO IN-CANON ANSWER.** Two readings, **neither asserted**: the sovereign is the source of every order and so stood there when they were given, which reads toward *yes*; the ruling says the **commander** holds them **personally** and the Wind-Crown is not the commander, which reads toward *no*. **Not decided here, and no mechanism is invented to bridge it.** Open item **19**.
- **Registered plainly, because the ruling requires the consequence rather than a repair:** on the mechanism as ruled, **the death of the Windward Host Marshal destroys the top layer of the kingdom's standing orders and canon supplies no established means of restoring it.** **This is the single largest live weakness in the Gale Kingdom's military structure.**
- **⚠️ AND THE FLOOR-COLUMN RULING BEARS ON EXACTLY THIS POINT: THE SOVEREIGN OUTRANKS HIS OWN SUPREME COMMANDER IN CULTIVATION.** `[ASKED]` *(**"The floor is the faction's military peak, not its absolute peak"** — *"The Marshal is the highest cultivator in the army, which is not the highest in the kingdom. A sovereign who outranks his own supreme commander in cultivation is a fact about this kingdom, not a violation."* §6.1; open item 15 **closed**.)* **The Marshal is the army's peak cultivator; `gale-kingdom-state.md` §2's record of King Aeries as the kingdom's sole apex cultivator stands untouched and is not a contradiction.** **No realm is assigned to the Marshal here or anywhere in this file**, and **no lineage question is touched or presupposed** (§6.6). *(Author ruling, 2026-10-22: the first holder, Magnus Dalsen, is Ruler Level 1 Low; the level is the person's and not the post's. See §6.1 and Section 3.)* The two rulings meet, and the meeting is worked rather than left sitting side by side:
  - **The kingdom's order-custody and its cultivation resilience are arranged in opposite directions.** The custodian of the **highest** military orders — the Marshal — is the army's **best-protected** rung, which blunts the upward incentive at **(d)** partway: an enemy hunting the top layer of Gale's orders has to get through the army's peak cultivator to reach them. **It does not blunt it at the tiers below**, where the orders are just as irrecoverable and the custodian is not the army's peak.
  - **The one person above that peak holds nothing a killing would destroy.** The **Wind-Crown** is the kingdom's apex of capability and **holds no Area 5.4 tier, no formation's standing orders and no counter-sign.** **In a cultivation-dominant kingdom the most survivable person in it is therefore also the one whose death would delete no orders at all** — a fact about how this kingdom is built, stated flatly and not tidied.
  - **⚠️ WHICH MAKES THE WIND-CROWN'S PHYSICAL PRESENCE A COMMAND EVENT AND NOT ONLY A COMBAT ONE.** Canon makes the Wind-Crown **the source from which every order descends** (Section 1). Orders cannot be sent, cannot be written and cannot be reissued at a distance — **but a source that is standing on the ground can speak new ones directly, at the top, into whatever commander is in front of it.** **The sovereign's presence is the only circumstance in which the kingdom's highest orders can be constituted somewhere other than through the Marshal**, and in a cultivation-dominant kingdom the sovereign is also the one person able to reach a contested place and survive being there. **Registered as a structural fact of this establishment, not as a plan, a doctrine or a behaviour** — nothing here states or implies what any sovereign would choose to do.
  - **⚠️ AND THAT PRODUCES A FAILURE MODE WITH NO IN-CANON ANSWER, WHICH IS FLAGGED AND NOT RESCUED.** Part 22 Area 5.4 requires **linear authority from the supreme commander down.** If the Wind-Crown speaks an order directly to a commander in the field, **does that order become that commander's standing orders — held personally, by him, superseding what the Marshal spoke over him — and what then becomes of the Marshal's own custody of the layer above?** Canon does not say. Two readings and **neither asserted**: the sovereign is the source of every order, so an order from the source is simply an order, which reads toward *yes*; the chain is linear and a civil office holds no tier in it, which reads toward *no* — and if *yes*, the kingdom has a lawful way for its chain to be entered other than at the top, which is the one thing Area 5.4's linearity is for. **Not decided here.** Open item **19a**.
  - **What is structural regardless: the kingdom's highest military orders live in the memory of one serving officer who is not the sovereign, and the sovereign holds no tier from which to inherit them** — which is the unanswered question at the head of this point, and the ruling on the floor column does not answer it.
- **Two unwritable authorities sit at the top of this kingdom in two different single office-holders, and neither office has a deputy anywhere in canon:** the **Storm-Counsel's** counter-sign and the **Marshal's** kingdom-level standing orders. **Neither can be written, relayed, delegated or held in reserve.** Recorded as a structural fact of the establishment; **no deputy, successor-designate or reserve custodian is invented for either.**

**(g) WHAT SURVIVES A DEATH IS WHAT WAS OVERHEARD — AND WHETHER A WITNESS MAY RE-SPEAK IS THE HINGE THE WHOLE MECHANISM TURNS ON.** The code constitutes the record by **naming the hearers aloud**, so every order ever given to a commander had witnesses, and those witnesses are the nearest thing this kingdom has to a copy. **But the code gives a witness a role in VERIFICATION, and verification is attestation, not transmission.** A witness can say *the order was given, and these were its words* (point 4). **Whether that is enough to constitute the order in a new commander's hands — or whether only the commander who holds an order may pass it, which is what "the commander holds them personally" most naturally says — is NOT established.**
- **Everything about how badly a decapitation hurts turns on this one unanswered question.** If a witness may **transmit**, a well-witnessed formation loses hours and the weakness at **(d)** is survivable. If a witness may only **attest**, a decapitated formation loses its orders **outright** and must wait on the tier above.
- **No answer is invented, and the question is expressly not settled by inference in either direction.** Open item **18**, registered as **the single most load-bearing unanswered question in this file.**

**(h) WHAT THIS PUTS ON ORDINARY GALE SOLDIERING, BRIEFLY.** **Every relief is a handover and every handover is the dangerous act**, so the kingdom performs its most vulnerable institutional procedure constantly and routinely — the Wing's rotations being the clearest case (§6.9; rotation length and frequency are OPEN-AREA). **A commander who is unreachable is a dead commander for orders purposes**: illness, isolation by weather on a Flightcraft-dependent frontier, or separation from his formation all produce the same effect as death until he returns. **And a captured commander is a worse case than a dead one, because his orders are intact and in someone else's hands** — **what a Gale commander's capture does to the orders he holds is NOT established and no rule is invented for it** (open item 20).

**(i) THE TWO THINGS THE FILE ALREADY ESTABLISHED ARE NOT RESTATED HERE — THEY ARE SHOWN WORKING.** That **orders are few and broad** (point 4) and that **the officer corps is bounded by memory** (point 6, §6.2) were both derived from the *witness* rule before this ruling. **Personal custody re-derives both from a second, independent direction, which is why the structure holds:** orders must be few and broad because **a commander must be able to carry them accurately in his own head and say them all again correctly at a handover** — a cap set by one man's memory, which does not rise with the treasury or with the size of the formation, so **a brigade's orders and a squad's orders occupy exactly the same amount of storage.** And the corps is bounded twice over, because **each officer is now both a rank and an archive**, and the kingdom loses both in the same instant. **§6.2's third structural fact carries the force-structure consequence.**

**10 — THE PROBLEM A COMBINED FORMATION CREATES, AND THE CODE'S OWN ANSWER TO IT.** Gale serves alongside a party whose instruments are written: the **Northern Accord** is a treaty with a **co-signatory of record** (§7.5). The code already resolved outsider paper and its resolution is cited unchanged: **a Gale member signs where signing is required and says the thing aloud as well, and it is the saying that they hold themselves to.** Applied to the Wing: **a written combined order that no Gale officer stood for and spoke does not bind Gale soldiers.** See §6.9.

#### 6.11.1 — The four facts this register is built ON, not around

`[DERIVED]` *(each fact is cited from where it already stands; none is created here)*

1. **THE ADDRESS FORM IS A SPOKEN ACKNOWLEDGMENT THE SPEAKER MAKES ABOUT THEMSELVES, NOT A TITLE ATTACHED TO THE ADDRESSEE.** It puts the speaker on the record, **because the record is the hearing**, and **it scales with how much of the speaker is not yet on the record — never with the addressee's rank** (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §G16.2.1). **A military hierarchy therefore does not deepen this form by climbing it.** A Private speaking to the Marshal and a Private speaking to his own Sergeant are not on a scale of two ranks; they are on a scale of **whether the hearer's record already holds the speaker.**
2. **THE OFFICE-NOUN IS THE ADDRESS TERM, UNMODIFIED** — no honorific, no possessive, *"because a state with no permission apparatus has no honorific apparatus"* (§G16.4.1). **In service the office-noun is the rank-noun**, and it is bare: **Sergeant. Captain. Marshal. Admiral.** Never *"my lord Captain,"* never *"Your Marshal."* Area 16 §13.11's own MSF vocabulary — *"Marshal, General, Captain, Lieutenant, Sergeant"* — is therefore satisfied exactly as canon states it, with **no decoration added** and none available.
3. **THE NAMED WIND IS THE ADDRESS-TERM OF THE SWORN REGISTER, AGAINST THE BIRTH NAME AS THE ORDINARY REGISTER** (§G16.1.1), **and nobody under fourteen has one** (§G16.1.4). Every *[wind-name]* below is **a slot**; see §6.11.14. The wind-name is the address-term of an official court ruling and is available, never required, elsewhere; see the block at the head of this section
4. **NOTHING BINDING IS WRITTEN.** Orders, commissions, musters and authorisations are **spoken**; rolls, stores, pay and rations are **counts and are written freely** — ***a count is not a promise*** (§6.8 point 1). **This is what makes a military address register possible at all in this kingdom**, and it is also what draws the line the register turns on.

#### 6.11.2 — ⚠️ TWO AXES, AND THEY ARE INDEPENDENT. CONFLATING THEM IS THE COMMONEST WAY TO GET A GALE MILITARY SCENE WRONG

`[DERIVED]` A writer staging a Gale military exchange is deciding **two separate things**, and neither answers the other:

| | The question | What it decides | Where it is answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AXIS ONE — REGISTER** | **Is something being constituted here?** | **Which name-term is spoken** — birth name (ordinary) or wind-name (sworn) in a constituting act the wind-name is available and not required; the act sets the weight | **§6.11.3** |
| **AXIS TWO — ENTRY** | **Does this hearer's record already hold this speaker?** | **Whether an acknowledgment is owed at all, and at what depth** | **§6.11.4** |

**They cross in all four combinations and every one of them occurs in ordinary service.** A soldier standing at his own change of watch **owes no acknowledgment whatever** (his formation holds him) **and is nonetheless in the sworn register** for as long as his standing orders are being re-spoken over him. A courier landing at a strange fortress **owes the deepest acknowledgment the system has** and, until he has finished making it, **is in no register at all, because he is nobody the room can produce.** **Neither axis is a proxy for the other, and a scene written as though rank set the register, or as though the sworn register required an acknowledgment, has been written wrong.**

#### 6.11.3 — ⚠️ AXIS ONE: THE SWORN / ORDINARY BOUNDARY. THIS IS THE HEART OF THE SECTION

**The problem, stated before the answer.** `[DERIVED]` §6.8 point 1 puts **an order, a commission, a muster obligation, an authorisation, an oath and a charge** on the *never-written* side of the line — the side of things that **bind a person.** §G16.1.1 puts the **sworn register** — the wind-name — at *"any moment a speaker intends to be bound,"* and names **wind-sworn muster** among its listed contexts. **Taken together and taken loosely, that would put nearly all military speech in the sworn register**, since an army is a machine for issuing orders. **That reading is wrong, and the reason it is wrong is already in this file.**

**⚠️ THE BOUNDARY: AN ORDER IS A PROMISE. A DIRECTION IS AN EXECUTION. THE SWORN REGISTER BELONGS TO THE FIRST AND NEVER TO THE SECOND.** `[DERIVED]` §6.8 point 4 establishes that this kingdom's orders are **few, broad and long-lived** rather than many and narrow; §6.7 point 3 establishes that a field commander's authority is **exactly the words spoken over his muster**, and no more. It follows that the thousand small directions a Sergeant gives in a day — *move up · take the left path · fill the skins · you have first watch* — **are not orders in the constitutional sense at all.** They are **the execution of orders already held.** They create nothing that did not exist before he spoke, they bind nobody newly, and nothing about them will ever have to be produced afterwards. **They are to an order exactly what a stores return is to a commission: a count, not a promise.**

**THE TEST, AND IT IS ONE QUESTION.** `[DERIVED]` **Is something being made here that did not exist before someone spoke?**
- **Yes → SWORN REGISTER. ** the act carries the weight and the witnesses are named aloud. Wind-names are available and are REQUIRED only where the act is an official court ruling (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §G16.1.6, §G16.1.7), such as the Stone Tribunal's hearing of a charge
- **No → ORDINARY REGISTER. Birth names, and the register never opened.**

**THE FOUR CONSTITUTING ACTS, AND THERE ARE ONLY FOUR.** `[DERIVED]` *(each is already established in this file; the register consequence is what is new)*

| The act | Where it is established | What is constituted | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A COMMISSION** — a man spoken into a rank before named witnesses | §6.8 point 6 | A Part 22 tier that did not exist in that man a moment earlier | **SWORN** |
| **A MUSTER** — the obligation called, the men come, the counter-sign spoken over them | §6.8 point 7; §6.5; §6.7 point 2 | A formation, its binding on each man, and the outer limit of what it may do | **SWORN** |
| **THE GIVING, RE-SPEAKING OR HANDOVER OF STANDING ORDERS** — including a courier's delivery, the courier **being** the order | §6.8 points 2, 5, 9(c) | A formation's purpose, held in one head, existing nowhere else | **SWORN** |
| **A CHARGE** — a disciplinary accusation spoken before witnesses | §6.8 point 8 | A soldier's answerability, and the matter the Stone Tribunal will hear | **SWORN** |

"SWORN" in this table means constituted by the act, with the hearers named aloud, and not "in wind-names". Wind-names are available at all four acts and required at none, except in the Stone Tribunal's hearing of a charge, which is an official court ruling (`gale-kingdom-state.md` §G16.1.7). The speaking of a charge is not a ruling.

**Everything else in military life is the ordinary register and birth names:** every direction, every report, every question, every count, every roll-call, every stores return, every word spoken on a march, in a camp, at a rail or over a meal. **This is the overwhelming majority of all Gale military speech**, exactly as §G16.1.1 says it is of all Gale speech.

**⚠️ WHEN IT STARTS AND WHEN IT STOPS — THE ANSWER A WRITER ACTUALLY NEEDS.** `[DERIVED]` **The sworn register is not a mood, a setting or a rank; it is a seam inside a scene, and it opens and closes on the act.** It opens at the first word of the constituting act and **closes the instant the act is finished** — and the closing is as legible as the opening, because **the name-term changes back** (§G16.1.2: *"a speaker who has been saying a birth name all scene and then says a wind-name has moved the matter from ordinary to sworn, and everyone in the room knows it in the same instant"*). **The same two men, thirty seconds apart, in two registers, is not a slip. It is the system working, and the seam is audible to everyone standing there.** This is the seam where a speaker chooses the wind-name. Where he does not, the act itself (the standing, the open air, the hearers named aloud) opens and closes it

**⚠️ AND IT IS NOT RARE — IT RECURS ON A SCHEDULE, WHICH IS THE STRUCTURAL FINDING OF THIS SUBSECTION.** `[DERIVED]` §6.8 point 5 already holds that **a standing order survives by being said again — *at muster, at relief, at the change of watch*** — and that **an order nobody re-speaks has been forgotten, which in this kingdom is the same as rescinded.** So the re-speaking is not a recital of something that exists; **it is the act that keeps it existing**, and it therefore constitutes, and it is therefore sworn. **The consequence, stated plainly because it is what makes this kingdom's military speech distinctive: a Gale soldier enters and leaves the sworn register several times a day, briefly, in the same short shape, on a timetable.** §6.8 point 5's line that **the parade is the archive** now has its mechanism: **the archive is maintained by entering the sworn register on a schedule, and a watch that changes without the seam has lost whatever was not said.**

**FOUR CONSEQUENCES, ALL `[DERIVED]` AND FLAGGED.**
1. **A soldier who uses wind-names through an ordinary working exchange has asserted that everything he is saying binds** — an escalation under §G16.1.3, and it will be read as one by everyone present. In a military that is a serious act: it says *hold me to this,* about a thing nobody asked him to be held to.
2.  a soldier who uses birth names at a commission, a muster, a handover or a charge has declined nothing: the act and the hearers named aloud bind him, and the wind-name is the speaker's choice, audible when chosen. The refusal of §G16.1.3 survives only where the wind-name is required (§G16.1.7 of `gale-kingdom-state.md`); among the four acts that is the Stone Tribunal's hearing of a charge, where a birth name used where the wind-name is required is a refusal to be bound
3. **A constituting act in which nobody is named is not a weak act — nothing was made.** §G16.2.5 condition 1 requires **a wind-named person who can afterwards be produced**, and §6.8 point 4's verification is *send and ask the people who were standing there.* **A commission, muster, order-giving or handover written with the witnesses unnamed has produced no record**, and the scene has staged an event the kingdom cannot afterwards find.
4. **Therefore nobody who cannot enter the sworn register can be commissioned, mustered, charged, or given standing orders.** §G16.1.4 puts the sworn register out of reach below fourteen. **This file sets no enlistment age and invents none** — what it records is that **the constituting acts of this military are all sworn acts, and a person with no wind-name cannot be the subject of one.** §6.1's Recruit is *"not yet spoken into service"*; the speaking is the sworn act. **Whether the kingdom's actual entry age is the rite age or later is NOT established** — open item **26**.

#### 6.11.4 — ⚠️ AXIS TWO, UPWARD: IS THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT ON ENTERING A COMMANDER'S HEARING THE SAME ACT AS A CIVILIAN'S? THE CENTRAL QUESTION, WORKED OUT

**The answer is: it is the same act, made on the same conditions, at a depth service almost always sets to nothing — and the reason is the form's own reason, not a military exemption.** `[DERIVED]`

**THE DERIVATION, IN THREE STEPS, BECAUSE THE ANSWER IS ONLY TRUSTWORTHY IF THE STEPS ARE VISIBLE.** `[DERIVED]`
1. **§G16.2.4's moderate depth already carries the whole answer.** A senior official speaks **one constituent only** because *"their wind-name is already in the room's standing record and every person present could produce it. Saying it again would assert that it was in doubt."* **Brevity there is not familiarity — it is the proof of standing.**
2. **A soldier stands in that position permanently with respect to his own formation, and stands in it by construction.** He was **spoken into service** and, if he holds a rank, **spoken into it before named witnesses who are standing in the same formation** (§6.8 point 6); if he is militia he was **bound at a muster where the record of who is bound is *the people standing in the field looking at each other*** (§6.8 point 7). A Gale squad is **8–12 men** and a company **110–130** (§6.2), and a commission the kingdom could not personally attest does not exist (§6.8 point 6). **There is no such thing as a Gale soldier his own formation cannot produce.**
3. **Therefore the depth collapses, and then the act itself does.** The acknowledgment is an **entry act, made once on entering hearing** and never repeated line by line (§G16.2.7). **A soldier inside his own formation has already entered, and did not enter this morning — he entered when he was spoken into it.**

**⚠️ THE RULE, STATED FOR A WRITER: INSIDE HIS OWN FORMATION A SOLDIER MAKES NO ACKNOWLEDGMENT AT ALL — NOT A SHORTENED ONE, NONE.** `[DERIVED]` He addresses his Sergeant, his Lieutenant, his Captain and his Colonel with **the bare rank-noun** (§6.11.1 fact 2) or with nothing, exactly as the sentence needs. **A soldier who acknowledges his own commander on his own parade has said that his commander's record does not hold him**, which is either a claim that he has been forgotten or an outsider's mistake, and in a formation of ten men it is heard as one of the two. **This is the single most useful fact in the section**, and it is what makes ordinary Gale soldiering writable: **the kingdom's most distinctive address form is almost never spoken between soldiers who serve together.**

**⚠️ AND THAT IS EXACTLY WHY IT MATTERS ENORMOUSLY WHEN IT IS.** `[DERIVED]` **The acknowledgment is owed the moment a soldier enters the hearing of a commander whose record does not hold him** — which in a military is a specific, frequent and dramatically loaded set of occasions:

| Speaker | Depth | Constituents | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A soldier of the same formation who left hearing and returned** | **Moderate** | **1** — ***"[Rank-noun], I stand in hearing."*** | §G16.2.7 requires re-acknowledgment where the speaker *left hearing and returned*. The room holds his person; it does not hold his return. **This is the detached party's form, the sentry's, the scout's** |
| **A soldier of another formation of the same arm** | **High** | **1 + 2** | The rank-noun locates a post the room knows; **the person it does not.** He must enter himself before what he says can be relied on |
| **A soldier of another arm, a militiaman reporting to the Host, a Stormwing detachment officer arriving at a fortress** | **High** | **1 + 2** | Same reason. §6.2's third structural fact — *nothing crosses arms by seniority; it crosses by tier* — means a cross-arm arrival is always an arrival among strangers |
| **Anyone the receiving formation cannot produce at all — the courier above all** | **Highest** | **1 + 2 + 3** | Nothing about him is on that formation's record. **He must constitute the whole of it himself** (§6.11.7) |
| **Any soldier entering the hearing of the Windward Host Marshal, having no standing in the kingdom-level record** | **Highest** | **1 + 2 + 3** | See the rank-gap rule immediately below |

**⚠️ HOW RULE P29-13.7.3's RANK-GAP DEPTHS APPLY IN A SYSTEM WHOSE FORM NEVER SCALES WITH THE ADDRESSEE'S RANK — AND THEY DO APPLY, THROUGH A DIFFERENT DOOR.** `[DERIVED]` **Rule P29-13.7.3** provides that *"the greater the rank differential, the more formal the address."* The Gale form cannot obey that literally, because it scales with the **speaker's** standing and never with the **addressee's** rank (§6.11.1 fact 1). **It obeys it exactly all the same, because in this military the rank gap is a near-perfect proxy for the thing the form does scale with.** A Private and the **Marshal** have never stood in the same room and never will in the ordinary course; the Marshal's record does not hold him; the depth is **highest — all three constituents.** A **Colonel** entering the Marshal's hearing was commissioned into a brigade command by a kingdom that can personally attest every commission it holds (§6.8 point 6), and the Marshal is the man who spoke it or stood at it; the depth is **moderate — one constituent.** **The gradation Area 16 asks for arrives intact, and it arrives without the form ever becoming a title.** A **Lieutenant** between them takes **high depth** for the same reason and no other: known by post, not by person.

**A corollary a writer will need, and it is not a softening.** `[DERIVED]` **Depth is a floor, not a ceiling** (§G16.2.4), so a soldier may go deeper than his standing requires — and doing so is **legible**, exactly as it is in the civil system: it claims the matter is graver than the room has understood, or it is a self-demotion. **In a military the first reading is far the commoner**, and it is one of the few instruments a Gale soldier has for saying *this is not routine* without saying so.

#### 6.11.5 — DOWNWARD: OFFICER TO RANKER, NCO TO RANKER

`[DERIVED]` **There is no downward acknowledgment, because the acknowledgment is a CAT-5 lower-to-higher act and Gale has built nothing to answer it with except the completion.** What runs downward is three things:

- **THE NAME, AND WHICH NAME IS THE WHOLE OF THE SIGNAL.** Ordinary register → **birth name**; a constituting act → **wind-name**. §G16.3's CAT-6 already fixes the civil twin of this — *"a sovereign who answers an official by wind-name has moved the matter to sworn weight in front of everyone, and that is a public act, not a warmth."* **The military case is the same act with more force**, because a formation is smaller than a court and everyone in it hears: **a Sergeant who calls a man by his wind-name has told the whole squad, in one word, that what follows will bind him.** That is the kingdom's entire downward apparatus for gravity, and it needs no second device. By choice. A Sergeant who calls a man by his wind-name has told the squad this; one who does not has declined nothing, and the act binds all the same
- **THE RANK-NOUN, WHICH LOCATES A POST AND NOT A PERSON.** §G16.4.1's rule is that the office-noun **points, it does not elevate.** So a commander addressing a **subordinate commander** says the rank-noun — ***"Sergeant"*** — because it is the post he is speaking to and the post is what he means. A commander addressing **an individual soldier who commands nobody** says **the name**, because a rank-noun would locate a grade the formation holds a dozen of. **Neither choice carries deference in either direction, and neither may be written as if it did.**
- **THE COMPLETION, WHERE ONE IS OWED.** §G16.2.8's binary is the addressee's instrument and it belongs to a commander receiving an acknowledgment exactly as it belongs to a sovereign receiving one: **the name back**, which enters the speaker, **or *"Speak,"* which hears him without entering him.** Which name is used sets the register (§G16.1). In an official court ruling; elsewhere the act sets the weight `[AUTHOR-PENDING]` **Whether a commander may decline to enter a courier — and what declining does to a delivery, the courier being the order — is NOT established and is not decided here.** Open item **24**.

**Two things that are NOT downward address in Gale and must not be written as though they were.** `[DERIVED]` **(i)** **There is no downward honorific, diminutive or address-of-familiarity** — the kingdom has closed the diminutive slot even for its royal family by author ruling (§G16.3 CAT-1), and nothing here opens one for a military. **(ii)** **A commander does not confer standing by how he addresses a man.** Standing is conferred by the speaking that commissioned or mustered him and by nothing else (§6.8 points 6, 7); **a warmly addressed soldier is not thereby entered on anything.**

#### 6.11.6 — LATERAL: PEERS OF EQUAL RANK

`[DERIVED]` **Ordinary register, birth names, and no vocative required.** Two Sergeants of the same company are each already on the other's record, so **no entry act is owed in either direction, ever.** The **rank-noun** remains available as a locator where the **post** rather than the person is being addressed — one squad's Sergeant speaking to another's about that squad's business — and it is the same bare, unmodified noun that runs upward and downward.

**⚠️ AND THE STRUCTURAL FINDING OF THIS SUBSECTION IS A NEGATIVE ONE, WHICH IS WHY IT NEEDS SAYING: GALE HAS NO LATERAL FORM AT ALL, AND NEEDS NONE.** `[DERIVED]` A peer honorific is a device for marking equality of **rank**, and **this kingdom's address never marks rank in any direction.** The consequence is genuinely distinctive and a writer should hold it: **two soldiers of different ranks who are both on each other's record and are not constituting anything speak to each other in exactly the same way two soldiers of the same rank do.** A Captain and his Company Sergeant Major, at a rail, at the end of a day, sound like two men — because in address terms, at that moment, they are two men. **Rank returns to the exchange the instant something is being made, and not one moment before.**

**Lateral across a boundary is not lateral.** `[DERIVED]` Two Captains of **different formations** who have not met are not peers for this purpose but **strangers**, and the arrival rules of §6.11.4 govern: the visitor's record is not held by the host's formation, so **high depth**, one and two. **Equality of rank buys no shortening**, because rank was never what was being measured.

#### 6.11.7 — ⚠️ THE COURIER: THE ONE DISTINCTIVE FORM THIS MILITARY HAS, AND THE ONE A WRITER WILL REACH FOR MOST

**The canon fact this is built on, quoted because every line below is an unpacking of it and none of it is new invention.** §6.8 point 2: ***"a courier does not carry an order; the courier IS the order. He stood there. He heard it spoken. He speaks it again at the far end, in the first person of the man who gave it, and he names the witnesses who stood with him."***

**WHAT A COURIER IS CALLED.** `[DERIVED]` **He is called by his own rank-noun, or by his name — because *courier* is what he is doing and not a post he holds.** §6.4's air arm carries **no courier rank and no courier rung**: a Flightcraft is flown by *"its pilot (Airman, Corporal or Sergeant by grade)"*, and §G16.4.1 makes the **office-noun** the address term — an office being a post on the ladder, which a courier's errand is not. **He is a Sergeant who is carrying an order, and he is addressed as a Sergeant.** *Courier* and *messenger* are **canon's own descriptive English words** for the function (`ledger-worlds-location/gale-kingdom.md` §5.6 — *"used by Gale messengers, scouts, and royal couriers"*) and are used here as this record's terms, exactly as *acknowledgment* and *counter-sign* are used as the record's terms rather than the kingdom's (§G16.9 items 8, and the parent code block's own item 1). `[AUTHOR-PENDING]` **Whether the Gale Kingdom has an in-world address term of its own for the function is NOT established, and none is coined** (Part 28 Area 20). Open item **22**.

**⚠️ WHAT HE SAYS ON ARRIVAL: TWO SPEAKINGS, IN FIXED ORDER, AND THE SEAM BETWEEN THEM IS THE WHOLE OF THE FORM.** `[DERIVED]` *(wording flagged at open item 23; the structure is derived from canon's own sentence above and from §G16.2)*

**FIRST — HE ENTERS HIMSELF. The ordinary acknowledgment, at the highest depth, all three constituents:**

> ***"[Rank-noun], I stand in hearing. I am [wind-name]. Heard by [wind-name], and by [wind-name]."***

**Why the highest depth, always, and not as a courtesy.** He has flown in from the tier above and **the receiving formation cannot produce him** — nothing about him is on its record (§G16.2.4's highest-depth reason, applied). And the receiving officer may have to **send and ask** (§6.8 point 4), which requires a person who can afterwards be found. **The witness clause here is the courier's own protection and the receiving officer's**, in the same way §G16.2.4 records it is the petitioner's own and not a courtesy to the crown.

**SECOND — HE BECOMES THE OTHER MAN'S VOICE. The delivery, and it is not an acknowledgment at all:** he names **the giver, by wind-name**, because the giving was a sworn act; he speaks the order **in the giver's own first person**; and he names **the witnesses who stood at the giving.**

**⚠️ THE ORDER OF THE TWO IS NOT STYLISTIC AND MAY NOT BE INVERTED.** `[DERIVED]` **Until the courier is on the record, the thing he says next is being said by nobody.** §G16.2.5 condition 1 requires a hearer who can be produced; the mirror of it is that a speaker who cannot be produced has constituted nothing. **A courier who delivers first and enters himself afterwards has spoken an order into a room that has no one to hold to it.**

**⚠️ THE TWO WITNESS LISTS ARE DIFFERENT LISTS AND MERGING THEM DESTROYS THE VERIFICATION CHAIN.** `[DERIVED]` **This is the sharpest error the form invites and it is a CRITICAL class** (§6.11.13):
- **The courier's own witnesses** are people **in the room he has just landed in** who can afterwards say *he arrived and he spoke.*
- **The order's witnesses** are people **at the far end, where the order was given**, who can afterwards say *it was given, and these were its words.*
- **§6.8 point 4's verification is *send and ask the people who were standing there*, and "there" is the GIVING, not the arrival.** A courier who names one list for both has left the receiving officer with nobody to send to, and **the order he brought cannot be verified by anyone, ever.**

**⚠️ THE FIRST-PERSON SHIFT IS THE DELIVERY, AND A THIRD-PERSON REPORT IS NOT A DELIVERY AT ALL.** `[DERIVED]` Canon's line is that he speaks it *"in the first person of the man who gave it."* So the courier says ***I*** and means his Colonel. **A courier who says *"the Colonel says you are to hold the pass"* has narrated an order rather than delivered one** — he has reported a fact about a conversation, which is a count and binds nobody, instead of being the order, which is what he is. **This is the beat the form exists for and it is worth staging deliberately: a stranger's mouth, in a strange formation, at the end of a flight, becomes a commander three hundred twenty kilometers away for the length of one sentence.**

**WHICH NAME-TERM, AT WHICH MOMENT.** `[DERIVED]` The giver: **wind-name.** The courier naming himself: **wind-name.** The witnesses at either end: **wind-names**, because a witness must be a producible wind-named person (§G16.2.5 condition 1). **The receiving officer afterwards, once the delivery is finished: birth name** — the register closed when the act closed (§6.11.3). This paragraph's "wind-name" is the wind-name by choice; the birth name is right wherever §G16.1.7 does not require the wind-name. The witnesses must still each hold a wind-name and be producible

**THE FOUR CONDITIONS BIND THE DELIVERY, AND ONE OF THEM IS AWKWARD FOR A MILITARY. STATED, NOT SOFTENED.** `[DERIVED]` §G16.2.5's conditions govern any speaking into the record, and a delivery is one: **heard by a producible wind-named person · spoken in the open · in the speaker's own voice · on his feet.** The awkward one is **the open air** — *the important sentence is not said under a roof.* The Aerie Palace is wall-less and needs no staging (§G16.2.5 condition 2), **but a frontier fortress, a Watch-Fortress chamber, a tent and a cutter's cabin are not.** So **a Gale order is delivered outdoors**, on a wall, in a yard, on a deck — and in weather, which is where §G16.2.6 **Case A** does the work canon already gave it: **spoken and genuinely not heard is a non-event, and it is simply said again, with no embarrassment on either side.** On a Flightcraft-dependent, **weather-limited** frontier (§6.4) that is not a curiosity; **it is the ordinary condition of Gale command and control, and a delivery repeated three times into a gale is correctly written with no rise in temperature at all.**

**WHAT A COURIER MAY NOT CARRY — BOUNDARIES CITED, NOT DECIDED.** `[DERIVED]` His competence is **exactly one order.** He may **not** carry the **Storm-Counsel counter-sign** — foreclosed in terms, *a delegate is not a voice* (§6.7). He may **not** carry a **body of standing orders**, so a change of command cannot be done by courier (§6.8 point 9(c)). **Whether he may carry a commission is already open and is not decided here** (open item **11b**). **None of those three is reopened, extended or softened by anything in this subsection.**

#### 6.11.8 — HANDOVER AND MUSTER: THE TWO MOST CONSEQUENTIAL SPEAKING ACTS, IN ADDRESS TERMS ONLY

**⚠️ SCOPE HELD TIGHTLY. §6.8 point 9 and §6.5 own these two acts and everything about what they do; this subsection adds ONLY the address terms, and re-opens, re-decides and softens nothing.**

**HANDOVER.** `[DERIVED]` **Sworn register throughout.** Sworn throughout by the act; wind-names by choice, required only where §G16.1.7 of `gale-kingdom-state.md` says. It is the constituting act at its heaviest: **a body of standing orders is being moved from one head to another, and it exists nowhere else** (§6.8 point 9(a)). The vocatives on both sides are **bare rank-nouns**; neither man acknowledges the other, both being commanders whose formation holds them. Three address-side notes, each pointing at an existing open item without deciding it:
- **The witnesses are named by wind-name, and naming a man the kingdom cannot afterwards produce does not fail loudly — it fails silently, later.** The witnesses are named aloud, by birth name or by wind-name at the speaker's choice, and each must hold a wind-name and be producible; the rest of this bullet stands §G16.2.5 condition 1 is the requirement; §6.8 point 9(c)'s worry is that witnesses drawn wholly from inside the formation **die with the formation.** **Address supplies no remedy** and open item **20(ii)** stands exactly as it stands.
- **The return-speaking question is also a question about how many records the handover makes.** Open item **20(i)** asks whether the incoming commander must say the orders back. **In address terms: if he must, the return is a second sworn act needing its own witness naming; if he need not, the transfer is one act with one list.** **That is a sharpening of item 20(i), not an answer to it, and the item is not decided here.**
- **A person who cannot enter the sworn register cannot witness a handover** (§G16.1.4), which in a military is not a limit that ever bites, since nobody who cannot enter it can serve (§6.11.3 consequence 4). **It is stated because it is the same rule, not because it is a hazard.**

**MUSTER.** `[DERIVED]` **Sworn register throughout — and §G16.1.1 already lists *wind-sworn muster* among the sworn contexts, so this is canon's own placement and not a derivation.** The obligation is called and the men answer; the **Storm-Counsel counter-sign** is spoken over the formation, outdoors, before named witnesses (§6.7 point 2 — **cited unchanged; the counter-sign is a second voice, not an address form, and nothing here touches it**).

**⚠️ THE ONE CONCRETE ADDRESS DETAIL THIS SUBSECTION CONTRIBUTES, AND IT FALLS STRAIGHT OUT OF *A COUNT IS NOT A PROMISE*.** `[DERIVED]` **The muster roll and the muster calling use different name-terms for the same men, and both are correct.** The **roll is written**, and writing in this kingdom is for **counts**, which bind nobody — §6.8 point 1's own line is that *"the muster roll is evidence of a count and never evidence of a commission."* A written roll is not a sworn context, so **the roll is in birth names.** The **calling is the sworn act that binds**, so **a man is called by wind-name and answers by wind-name.** **The clerk's list says one thing and the officer's mouth says another, thirty seconds apart, and the kingdom sees no contradiction in it whatever** — because the list is a count and the calling is a promise, which is the distinction the whole establishment runs on. The calling is the sworn act, so it binds whatever name is called. "Called by wind-name and answers by wind-name" is the muster's escalation and is by choice. The roll in birth names and the calling are both still correct, and a calling in birth names is no longer a refusal to be bound

**And the incidental structural fact worth holding:** `[DERIVED]` a muster is **the kingdom's largest single act of mutual attestation.** Every man's wind-name is in the air at once, in front of everyone, which is precisely why §6.8 point 7 says the record of who is bound is *the people standing in the field looking at each other.* **After a muster, every man there is a person the others can produce** — and that, not the roll, is what the formation actually runs on afterwards.

#### 6.11.9 — ⚠️ A SOLDIER ADDRESSING THE CROWN, IN UNIFORM: ALL THREE CASES

**Rule P29-13.10.1 decides, and it decides by context** — *military rank in a military engagement, social/political rank in a court session, and in mixed cases the PRIMARY PURPOSE of the event.* **The Gale problem is that the mixed case is the normal case**, because the **Wind-Crown is the source from which every order descends** (Section 1) and the **Storm-Counsel holds the counter-sign** (§6.7), so a soldier is constantly on military business in a civil, wall-less court. **All three cases are worked below and none is left to inference.**

**⚠️ THE FACT THAT GOVERNS ALL THREE, AND IT IS ALREADY ON THE RECORD: NEITHER SOVEREIGN HOLDS A PART 22 AREA 5.4 TIER.** Section 3 states it of both — the Wind-Crown is *"a civil office above the military chain, not a rung in it"*, and the Storm-Counsel *"holds no Area 5.4 tier either."* **So military-rank governance produces no military vocative for either of them, because there is no rank-noun to say.** What fills the vocative slot in every case is **the civil office-noun** — ***"Wind-Crown," "Storm-Counsel"*** — supplied by §G16.4.2 and **cited from there, not established here.**

**CASE 1 — MILITARY SETTING, MILITARY MATTER: the sovereign at a muster, a fortress, a field.** `[DERIVED]`
- **Vocative: the civil office-noun, *"Wind-Crown."*** It is the only address term the sovereign has, and §G16.4.1's rule is that the office-noun **points** — it locates the hearer, which is exactly what a vocative is for. **It is not a courtly intrusion into a military scene; it is the locator doing its one job.**
- **The act is the military one, at the depth §6.11.4 sets** — and the sovereign's record does not hold an individual soldier, so for almost every soldier in the kingdom the depth is **highest, all three constituents.**
- **The register is set by the ACT and never by the setting** (§6.11.3). A sovereign speaking orders is constituting, and it is sworn; a sovereign walking a line and asking a man where he is from is not, and it is not. **The presence of a crown does not put a scene in the sworn register, and writing it as though it did is a MAJOR error.**

**CASE 2 — COURT SESSION: social rank governs, and the finding here is the sharpest in the subsection.** `[DERIVED]`
- **The soldier is a subject, and he uses the civil CAT-5 form exactly as §G16.2 supplies it.** Nothing military applies. **A uniform is not an address fact in this kingdom.**
- **⚠️ AND HIS DEPTH IS SET BY WHETHER THAT HALL HOLDS HIM, NOT BY HIS RANK.** This falls straight out of §G16.2.1 — the form scales with how much of the **speaker** is not yet on the record — and **a rank is not a record of a person.** A **Marshal** who sits in the kingdom's councils is a man that hall can produce and speaks at **moderate depth, one constituent.** A **Colonel** brought in from a frontier brigade, whose commission was attested by men on the Lyraen Frontier and by nobody in that hall, is a man that hall **cannot** produce, and speaks **deeper** — **and his rank does not shorten it by one word.** **Writing a senior officer's court acknowledgment as short *because he is senior* is a MAJOR error** (§6.11.13); the shortening is earned by standing in that room, and rank is not standing in that room.

**CASE 3 — MIXED: a military matter in a civil setting. The primary-purpose test, and in Gale the purpose is legible from the act.** `[DERIVED]` **Rule P29-13.10.1's test resolves cleanly here because this kingdom makes purposes visible by what is being made:**

| What the soldier has come for | Primary purpose | Governs | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| To be **given an order**, to be **commissioned**, to seek a **counter-sign**, to **answer a charge** | **Military** — and **something is being constituted** | **Military rank hierarchy** (§13.10.1), vocative the civil office-noun | **SWORN** |
| To **petition** about his pay, his water-share, his family, his land | **Civil** — **he is a subject who happens to serve** | **Social/political rank** — the plain CAT-5 form at his personal depth | **ORDINARY** unless he intends to be bound |
| **Both at once, and it happens** — a commander reporting a matter that is also a grievance | **Rule P29-13.10.1's primary-purpose test decides, and no Gale override is created** | Whichever the test gives | **Set by whether anything is constituted, not by which hierarchy won** |

**⚠️ THE HARDEST MIXED CASE IN THE KINGDOM — A COMMANDER SEEKING THE COUNTER-SIGN.** `[DERIVED]` The **Storm-Counsel** holds **no tier** and commands **no soldier** (§6.7), yet the thing being sought is the authorisation without which force is not a weak order but **no order at all.** So: **vocative *"Storm-Counsel"*** (§G16.4.2, cited); **acknowledgment at the commander's own depth**, by §6.11.4's rules and not lowered by the gravity of the errand; and **sworn register throughout**, the counter-sign being the paradigm sworn act — outdoors, before witnesses, witnesses named. **What closes the act is NOT §G16.2.8's binary.** A counter-sign is completed by **the storm-keeper standing and speaking the order too** (§6.7, cited unchanged). **That is an authorisation and not an address form, nothing here states or implies anything about it, and §6.7 remains its only place in this file.**

**⚠️ AND THE LONG COAST WARDEN — WHERE THE ADDRESS TERM SUPPLIES THE INSTRUMENT §6.7 SAID THE STRUCTURE LACKED.** `[DERIVED]` **One post, both hats** (`[ASKED]`, Section 3). §G16.4.4 fixed the **civil** term — ***"Long Coast Warden," short form "Warden," never "Governor"*** — and §G16.4.5 and §G16.5 expressly refused to supply the military half, leaving it to this file. **This file supplies it, and it is not a coinage: the military vocative is the rank-noun the post already carries, *ADMIRAL***, Part 22 Area 5.4's own naval title at Strategic Command, applied at Section 3 and §6.3. **The consequence is the useful part.** §6.7 recorded that because one person is both, *"the two therefore have to be told apart in the moment, by the same person, out loud, in front of the same witnesses"*, and that the kingdom's only instrument for marking which side of the line a decision falls on is **who was standing there and what was said.** **The address term is that instrument.** ***"Warden"* is the zone. *"Admiral"* is the Fleet.** Which noun is spoken **is** the marking, it is spoken in front of the witnesses §6.7 requires, and **a scene in which the wrong noun is used has moved a decision from one hat to the other in the hearing of everyone present.** **This changes nothing about the office, the counter-sign gate or the reporting line** — §6.7 and Section 3 stand exactly as written.

#### 6.11.10 — ⚠️ THE "SIR" / "MA'AM" QUESTION: DECIDED AGAINST, NOT COINED, AND THE CLOSURE ITSELF ROUTED

**The slot is real and Area 16 names it.** §13.11's **Military and Security Forces** row reads *"Marshal, General, Captain, Lieutenant, Sergeant; **'sir' / 'ma'am' equivalents**"* — and **canon establishes no word for the second half anywhere.**

**THE FIRST HALF IS ALREADY FILLED, EXACTLY AS CANON WROTE IT.** `[DERIVED]` **Marshal, General, Captain, Lieutenant, Sergeant** are Part 22 Area 5.4's own universal titles, already on this file's ladder at §6.1, and §6.11.1 fact 2 makes them **the address terms, bare and unmodified.** **Area 16's MSF row is therefore satisfied on its rank-title half with nothing added and nothing invented.**

**⚠️ THE SECOND HALF CANNOT EXIST IN THIS KINGDOM, AND THE REASON IS TWO THINGS ALREADY LOCKED.** `[DERIVED]`
1. **A "sir"/"ma'am" form is by definition a rank-honorific that attaches to the ADDRESSEE and deepens as his standing rises.** **That is precisely the thing this kingdom's address does not do.** §G16.2.1 is Locked Canon on the point that the Gale form *"does not scale with the addressee's rank"* and *"scales with how much of the speaker is not yet on the record."* **A form that scaled with the addressee would not be a variant of this system; it would be a second system running against it.**
2. **The kingdom has no honorific apparatus to build one out of.** §G16.4.1: *"No honorific is prefixed to a Gale office and no possessive is attached to it… **FEW RULES, ABSOLUTE** produces a state with no protocol apparatus and no permission apparatus… so there is no honorific apparatus either."* **A military inside that state cannot have the one thing the state does not have.** The kingdom has already closed an honorific-adjacent slot by negative establishment once, for its own royal family: **there is no Gale diminutive of Father or Mother, and inventing one is a MAJOR error class** (§G16.3 CAT-1, author ruling). **The pattern is the kingdom's own.**

**⚠️ THE DECISION, AND ITS LIMITS, STATED EXACTLY.** **No "sir"/"ma'am" equivalent exists in the Gale Kingdom's military, none is written here, and none may be written in prose.** `[DERIVED]` **What stands in its place is the bare rank-noun and nothing else** — *"Sergeant," "Captain," "Marshal," "Admiral"* — which is a **locator, not a deference marker**, and carries no more weight upward than downward (§6.11.5).

`[AUTHOR-PENDING]` **AND THE CLOSURE ITSELF IS ROUTED RATHER THAN TAKEN.** **Area 16 §13.11 is Locked Canon on its framework**, and a **negative establishment against a slot canon names by name is exactly the kind of thing this kingdom rules rather than derives** — the diminutive slot above was closed by an author ruling and not by a file's reasoning. **So: the reasoning is `[DERIVED]` and above; the closure is routed to the author as open item 21.** **NO WORD IS COINED IN EITHER DIRECTION.** If the author wishes the slot filled, **filling it would require a new in-world word**, and **Part 28's Establishment Threshold would fix that word permanently at first Area use** — which is precisely why one is not written here (Part 28 Area 20).

## Creation and Threshold Note — why one file, and why this file

**Part 37 Threshold F-1 covers *"any named organization, sect, guild, alliance, **military unit**, political bloc, or formal association."*** On its face that reaches the Stormwing Order, the Windward Host, the Long Coast Fleet, the Wind-Sworn Militia, the Stone Vigil and the Expeditionary Wing — six named formations, and on a literal reading six files. **They are held in ONE file, deliberately, for three reasons.**

**(i) The author directed one taxonomy at one depth.** The ruling is *"Yes, same depth, same pass"* — **a taxonomy**, a single structure with parts, not six independent bodies that happen to be adjacent. Splitting it would put the rank ladder and the Part 22 tier mapping in one file and the formations that use them in five others.

**(ii) None of the six is an independent faction.** Each is a crown establishment answering up one Part 22 chain, with no treasury, territory, membership register or external relations of its own. Part 37's **No-Duplication rule** is the operative constraint: six files would each have to restate the ladder, the tiers, the counter-sign and the chain of command to be usable, and five of those restatements would be duplication.

**(iii) The Expeditionary Wing is bilateral and cannot sit wholly in either kingdom's file.** It is recorded here **from the Gale side**, cross-referenced to the Azure Sky side's own pass, with the Wing's total left unstated.

**How No-Duplication is honoured against the parent file.** **Stays in `gale-kingdom-state.md`:** the doctrine (§7.2), the governance organs (§7.3), the **names** of the four wind-orders and the two frontier formations (§7.4), the oath network (§7.5), the economic base (§7.6), the open obligations (§7.8), and the **Collective Behavior Code** in full — including the counter-sign resolution, which is **cited here and not restated into anything new.** **Lives here:** the rank ladder and its Part 22 Area 5.4 tier mapping, the unit ladders for all three arms, the placement of every named formation, the cadre structure, the command chain and its external authorisation gate, the unwritten-military mechanics, and the Wing's combined structure. **§7.4 of the parent file cross-references this file**, and its Expeditionary Wing description carries the combined bilateral fact.

**What the parent file's own placement note left open, and what stays open.** That file's Collective Behavior Code block recorded, explicitly, that **no code is written for the Wing, the Stormwing Order, the Windward Host, the Long Coast Fleet or the Wind-Sworn Militia**, and that whether any of them carries a distinct code of its own is **not established and not decided.** **This file does not decide it either.** No Collective Behavior Code is authored here, no member is bound to anything, and no Part 39 dimension is created, altered or implied in this file or in any character file. *(Amended 2026-10-18: one Collective Behavior Code is now registered in this file, THE RECITAL, for the Whistledeep mine escort of Chapter Spec 1.1.1.005, in the section "Collective Behavior Code — the Whistledeep mine escort" just above this note, on the author's confirmation. The sentences of this note stand for every other formation named in this file, and that block adds, alters or implies no Part 39 dimension line in any character file. Whether the Stormwing Order, the Windward Host as an order, the Long Coast Fleet or the Wind-Sworn Militia carries a distinct code of its own is still not established and not decided here; the Wing's code stands in its own file.)*
