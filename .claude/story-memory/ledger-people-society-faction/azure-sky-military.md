### MA.7 — ⚠️ A SOLDIER ADDRESSING THE CROWN — the boundary this block exists to draw

**THE PROBLEM, STATED HONESTLY.** **§13.10 puts a military engagement under military rank and a court session under social rank**, and **Grand Marshal Kade and Marshal-Lieutenant Farrow both stand at court in uniform routinely** — he as one of the Four Great Officers, she as a named second at the Queen's table under the deputies' attendance custom (`azure-sky-kingdom-state.md` §7.3). **So the same two people move across this boundary in the ordinary course of their week, and a drafting writer must be able to place any scene without a further ruling.**

#### CASE 1 — AT COURT. The kingdom's CAT-5 governs in full, and this register is not consulted.

`[DERIVED]` **A court session is political; §13.10 assigns it social/political rank outright.** **A soldier at court is therefore a CAT-5 speaker like any other**, and the kingdom's **two-layer system** governs entire:
- **LAYER 1 — the everyday word.** The **author-confirmed Locked palette** at `varys.md` §7 and the **author-settled palette** at `valia.md` §7, **with the term chosen by the FORMALITY OF THE SETTING**, exactly as those entries state. **Cross-referenced and not restated, narrowed, extended, re-assigned or relocated by a word.**
- **LAYER 2 — the ceremonial style.** The graduated full style at **A16.3**, spoken **ONLY at a formal court opening or at a petition**, **once**, at the speaker's depth, closing into the honorific, **then Layer 1 for the remainder of the hearing.**
- **THE BOUNDARY TEST IS THE KINGDOM'S OWN AND IS NOT MODIFIED:** *"Is this scene a formal court opening or a petition?"* **No → Layer 1 and no style at all. Yes → the style once at the opening, then Layer 1.** **The question is about the OCCASION, never about who is speaking.**

**DEPTH, worked out and stated rather than assumed.** `[DERIVED]` **flagged for Check #21.**
- **THE GRAND MARSHAL STANDS AT THE MODERATE FLOOR.** **A16.3.2's moderate row names *"a Great Officer (§7.3)"* in terms**, and the Grand Marshal is one of the Four Great Officers (§7.3; §3). **His portion of the style, on the one occasion that admits it, is ELEMENT 1 ALONE, spoken ONCE at the opening, and nothing further ever.**
- **A MARSHAL-LIEUTENANT STANDS AT THE MODERATE FLOOR — and this is a DERIVATION the register states rather than smuggles.** **A16.3.2's moderate row names *"a Vice office (§7.3)"* and does NOT name the Marshal-Lieutenancy in terms.** The placement rests on **§7.3's deputies' attendance custom**, which names the crown's seconds together as one class — **Vice Chancellor Edran Voss, Marshal-Lieutenant Isolde Farrow, and Vice Artificer Selene Marrow** — making the Marshal-Lieutenancy a **§7.3 named second office of that same standing class.** **The reading is this register's work, is `[DERIVED]`, is ROUTED, and must not be cited as author-locked.** Item **A4**.
- **⚠️ EVERY RANK BELOW THE MARSHAL-LIEUTENANCY IS NOT PLACED, AND IS NOT PLACED BY PREFERENCE.** `[AUTHOR-PENDING]`, item **A4**. **A16.3.2's three populations are the crown's senior office-holders, service that is NEW, and a petitioner WITHOUT office.** **A Captain, a Sword-Sergeant or a Soldier at a formal court opening fits none of those three in terms** — a serving soldier is neither a senior court official, nor necessarily new, nor without office. **This register will not choose a row for them**, and **a drafting agent who puts a line soldier at a court opening stops and says so.**

**⚠️ AND THE MILITARY WORD NEVER TRAVELS INTO A CAT-5 FORM.** `[DERIVED]` `[IMMUTABLE]` **No rank word, no rank-derived honorific and no sir-class form is ever spoken toward a sovereign, in any register, on any occasion.** **The reason is structural rather than decorous: the crown holds no rung on this ladder.** The **Grand Marshal is its top** and he **answers to the Crown-in-Twin**; §4 records that the establishment's ceiling on its own initiative is **constitutional, not structural** — *"the ladder below can move, and only the dual mark can send it to war."* **A hierarchy supplies forms for the persons it ranks, and it ranks no sovereign.**

#### CASE 2 — IN THE FIELD, AT A MUSTER, OR ON ANY MILITARY OCCASION. Layer 1 alone, and no style at all.

`[DERIVED]` **§13.10 puts the room under military rank — and §13.10 selects a HIERARCHY, while a hierarchy can only supply a form for a person it ranks.** **Rule P29-13.6 is decisive and is quoted rather than paraphrased: the universal framework *"is never absent; faction modifiers add to it."*** **The military modifier ADDS rank forms for military addressees. It REMOVES nothing for an addressee the ladder does not carry, and it carries no sovereign.**

**So a soldier addressing the crown outside a court occasion speaks the kingdom's CAT-5, and speaks its EVERYDAY LAYER ONLY:** **the Locked palette's word for the setting, and NO CEREMONIAL STYLE WHATEVER** — because **a field, a muster, a tower line and a relief are not a formal court opening and are not a petition**, and the ceremonial layer is gated on that occasion alone.

**⚠️ THIS IS THE LIKELIEST MAJOR FAILURE IN ANY MILITARY SCENE THAT PUTS A SOVEREIGN IN IT**, and it is the same failure the kingdom block names as the likeliest CAT-5 failure overall: **the graduated full style spoken outside a formal court opening or a petition.** **A soldier declaiming the sovereign's style on a frontier road is that error exactly.**

#### CASE 3 — THE MIXED CASE, which is where Kade and Farrow actually live.

`[DERIVED]` **Rule P29-13.2.1 decides it and nothing else does: the event's PRIMARY PURPOSE.** Worked, so that a writer never has to re-run the reasoning:
