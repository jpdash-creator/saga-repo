# Guardrail: Entity Naming Law

**Source:** Part 28 (Novel Creative Flexibility Law), Area 20 · Canon v10.0 · full detail: `narration-novel-creative-flexibility-law` §Area 20 (§Area 20.8 named items, Rules 20.8.1 to 20.8.7; §Area 20.4(g) and Part 40 Area 13 monster names)
**Applies to:** Area Maker, Outline Planner (naming decisions); Continuity Checker (audits it — Gate 1 Check #17; reads the named-item form at Check #12 and keeps the named-item register at Gate 2)

## The rule

**Narrative-Significance Test.** An entity introduced in an Area — person, region, location,
faction, guild, event, institution, or any other entity type — **MUST be named in that same
Area** if ANY of the 5 prongs holds:

1. it **speaks**;
2. it **acts with story-state consequence**;
3. it **recurs** (the naming obligation triggers at the recurrence);
4. an **IE/CSS/PIS event, relationship bond, or ledger obligation attaches** to it;
5. the **POV dwells on it with identity-creating detail**.

An entity failing all five prongs is background texture and may remain unnamed. This is a permission and never a recommendation. Below the line the
recommended default is to name; see "Naming is not a cost" below.

**Thematic Naming Methodology (Area 20.3)** — names are crafted in this order, every time:
ledger-index scan for collisions first → Part 25 CPT cultural register → entity-type
convention (family patterns for persons; feature+function for locations; charter nouns for
factions/institutions; commemorative for events) → story resonance without telegraphing
twists → phonetic/visual distinctiveness → originality (no new name may equal a proper name
from a widely known published work; a doubtful or matching name is flagged to the author,
never silently changed). No contemporary-Earth anachronism; names are immutable once
established (Area 3.4).

**Major/minor consultation split (Area 20.4, Area 19 Rule 19.3 category 2).**
**MAJOR entities** are author-consulted via the Decision Consultation Gate with 2 to 3 crafted
candidate names + SKIP (four in all). The MAJOR list is that of Part 28 Rule 20.4(a) to (g), stated once there and not
restated here. For monsters, Rule 20.4(g) makes MAJOR every sentient monster, every Tier A or B monster
Individual, every Tier A or B monster Kind and any monster Kind that recurs at Section scale. **Every other entity is MINOR**
and is AI-named autonomously (Part 28 Rule 20.4 lists them, Tier B, C and D side characters among them); the author's veto path is `[REVISE AREA]` before `[CONFIRM WRITE]`.

## Hard rules

- **Gate 1 Check #17 (Entity Naming) is a blocking check.** Any of these five sub-checks is
  a Gate 1 FAIL, and the Correction Protocol runs until clean:
  - (a) an unnamed entity that passes the Narrative-Significance Test;
  - (b) a name off the Part 25 cultural register (or otherwise violating the Area 20.3
    sequence, including contemporary-Earth anachronism, and, for a Named Move, the Move-Naming Law of
    Part 42 Rule 42.12.2.3: see "Move names" below);
  - (c) a naming that creates an Area 17 ledger obligation left unflagged;
  - (d) a major-entity name assigned without Decision Consultation Gate author consultation;
  - (e) an address/title convention for a Hero/Villain-ledger character invented or altered
    without a prior author-confirmed ledger record (see next bullet).
- **Address/Title Conventions Are Not Free Invention.** Forms of address,
  honorifics, and modes of address directed *at* a Hero/Villain-ledger character (how other
  characters address them in dialogue) are never covered by the Area 20.4 minor-entity
  autonomous-naming allowance, even when the character's ledger file marks the field
  "Negotiable Canon, to be established in prose." Such conventions recur pervasively across
  a character's entire ongoing dialogue presence — satisfying Narrative-Significance prong 3
  many times over on first use alone — so any address/title convention not already recorded
  as author-confirmed in the character's ledger file is an open branch that must be
  escalated via the Decision Consultation Gate (Rule 19.3, major-entity-naming category)
  before drafting, never invented autonomously by Area Maker and never treated as a
  fillable Negotiable Canon custom mid-draft. A ledger field reading "to be established in
  prose" only licenses autonomous invention for genuinely minor, non-recurring textures — it
  never licenses inventing how people address the protagonist.
- **Never rename an established entity** outside the Area 3.4 amendment pathway — names are
  immutable once established, same force as the Establishment Rule
  (`.claude/guardrails/authority/guardrail-locked-vs-negotiable-canon.md`).
- **ALCA AL-10** covers both failure directions: **significant-but-unnamed** AND
  **named-but-unledgered**.
- The naming obligation feeds the ledger Completeness Requirement — a newly named qualifying
  entity is a ledger obligation like any other (`.claude/guardrails/ledger/guardrail-ledger-update-law.md`).

## Naming is not a cost

Home of the rule: Part 28 Rule 20.9. The author's words: "naming people, things, places, moves, etc. should
not be considered cost, that should be a go forward recommended approach."

- **Never a cost.** In every consultation, plan, option set, recommendation, finding and report, naming a
  person, thing, place, move or any other entity is never presented as a cost, a risk, a burden or a
  downside of an option. The ledger work a name creates (an entry, a register row, a Gate 2 obligation, a
  collision scan, a consultation round for a MAJOR name) is routine and is never weighed for or against an
  option, and is never a reason to leave an entity unnamed.
- **Named is the recommended default.** Where an option could name an entity or leave it unnamed, the
  named version is the one marked `(Recommended)`, and a plan or spec writes the entity with its name. An
  unnamed variant is offered only when the author asks for it or the story has a reason of its own.
- **Still required:** the author consultation for MAJOR entity names (2 to 3 crafted candidates plus SKIP),
  the cultural-register check, the collision scan, the originality check, the no-anachronism rule, name
  immutability, the Gate 2 ledger obligation and the Narrative-Significance Test as the line at which
  naming is required (Check #17(a)). "May remain unnamed" is read as a permission: it permits and
  never recommends.

## Named items

A **named item** is a made or used thing that the story gives a proper name of its own, and anything
that is an item gets registered. Its Type is one of: apparatus or device, remedy, gear or tool, weapon,
system or service (the thing that is run, never the body that runs it), material, food, drink, dress or
adornment, vessel or vehicle, relic or artifact, formation array, energy cache, or other item. The full definition and the list of what it does not cover are in
Part 28 Rule 20.8.1. The rules that bind:

- **Italic, every mention.** The name is set in italic in the Area body and in every spec file
  (Section Spec, Novel-Part Spec and Chapter Spec), at its first use and at every use after:
  `*Name*`. The name only: a possessive `'s` stays outside the italic. **Inside a passage that is
  already italic** (speech, an italic thought) the name is set in **roman**, the italic span closing
  at the word before it and opening again at the word after, so it still stands out. One name, one
  form: the same spelling and capitals and the same italic every time (Rule 20.8.2). **The article is
  never part of the name**: the Area text writes
  `the *Hourline*`, and `The *Hourline*` at the start of a sentence. **Emphasis guard:** italic
  emphasis on ordinary words stays free, but a word that is a registered item name is never
  italicised for emphasis (Rule 20.8.2(f)). Headings stay plain.
- **Relics are items** (Part 12 Rule 2.2.3). Named Relics, Named Artifacts, formation
  arrays and energy caches are set in italic like every item and are registered in the named-item
  register; their descriptive entries stay in `ledger-energy-relics-artifacts`.
- **Not named items:** people, places, kingdoms, factions and institutions, and titles;
  Named Moves and cultivation levels (bold-italic); events, research programs, scientific
  frameworks, intelligence operations, routes and distances (each has its own ledger);
  species-language terms; category terms and tier labels from the numbered Parts.
- **Documents and agreements go to the information ledger** (Rule 20.8.7). A named map,
  letter, charter, decree, codex or other document is set in italic and is registered in
  `ledger-society-information/_index.md`, not in the named-item register. An agreement, treaty or
  accord is set in roman (the author: "Roman for agreements, treaties and accords; italic for named
  maps and documents") and is registered there too, with its file in
  `ledger-people-society-affiliation/` as the home entry.
- **The register.** Every named item written into a confirmed and written Area is entered as a row of
  `ledger-named-items/_index.md` at Gate 2 (Part 37 Threshold O-4, schema Area 3.8). There is
  no significance test: a name that appears once counts. The row points at the item's home ledger entry
  and never copies its values. A name that exists only in a spec, or in a draft discarded by
  `[SKIP WRITE]`, enters nothing. The Pre-Write Ledger Check reads the register for every item the Area
  uses, and the Area 20.3 collision scan includes it.
- **Audit.** Gate 1 **Check #12** fails an item name that is not italic, is italic inside an italic span,
  is bold or bold-italic, carries its leading article inside the name or the italic, or is written in a
  form other than the registered one (the author: "Recommended, it is important writer does not make
  writing mistakes"), and reads the emphasis guard and the form of named documents and agreements in
  the same pass; **Check #5** reads the
  roman name inside speech as lawful; **Check #17(c)** treats the register row as the Gate 2 obligation
  to flag; **ALCA AL-10** reads the register both ways (named but not registered; registered but not
  written as registered). That amendment did not change the count of checks (now 23, Part 44). The light audit of each spec reports an
  item name that is not in italic, for the planner to correct before `[CONFIRM SPEC]`.
- **Hand-off.** The Area Maker lists each item that is new in the Area, and each named document and
  agreement, treaty or accord that is new in it, marked for the information ledger; the Gate 1 checker
  reconciles the list with the italic names in the draft; the orchestrating command passes the checked
  list unchanged to the Gate 2 ledger instance.

## Monster names

Home of the rule: Part 40 Area 13. A monster is a living being and sits with persons and species, so:

- **Roman type.** A Kind name and an Individual's name are set in roman. They are not italic (italic is
  for named items, and persons and species are not items, Rule 20.8.1(a)), and not bold or bold-italic.
  A move a monster uses keeps the bold-italic Named Move form. A sentient monster's own-language term
  follows Part 16 N16-14.
- **Made by the 20.3 sequence.** The ledger scan of step one includes `ledger-character-monster/_index.md`.
  A wild beast is named by the local people in the Part 25 register of the region; a sentient monster by
  its own language, or a human-register name it adopted.
- **MAJOR names (Rule 20.4(g)):** every sentient monster, every Tier A and Tier B monster Individual,
  every Tier A and Tier B monster Kind, and any monster Kind that recurs at Section scale. The author chooses
  from 2 to 3 candidates plus SKIP. Every other monster name (a Tier C or D Kind or Individual) is MINOR: the
  assistant names it, and `[REVISE AREA]` is the author's veto.
- **Both names are given and naming is never a cost** (Rule 20.9). The ledger row written at Gate 2
  satisfies Check #17(c).

## Move names

Home of the rule: Part 42 Rule 42.12.2.3, the Move-Naming Law (Canon Amendment 2026-10-08), with Part 6 Area 6.2.
A Named Move is not a named item (it stays bold-italic), but its name is an entity name and passes Check #17.

- **The law in short.** A move name belongs to the move and never to the situation in which it is called. It is one
  or two words (three only as the rule allows: at most 5 percent of a manual's moves, and Catastrophic or Mythic
  moves, which are still mostly two; never four or more). It is tied to the weapon type and to the level and grade of
  the move: simple at Basic and Intermediate, more impactful at Advanced and Elite, highly impactful at Signature and
  Fusion, highest at Catastrophic and Mythic; generic and weapon-based up to Mortal Tier 2 Senior level, and generic,
  weapon-anchored, cultivation-anchored or combined from Warrior level upward. It fits the move's function (attack,
  defence, control, mobility, heal, scout and the rest). It never names a place, a setting, a tactic or an enemy.
- **Where it is checked.** Gate 1 Check #17(b) reads each new move name in a draft against the law as the
  entity-type convention for moves, in the Area 20.3 sequence, and a departure blocks like any naming departure. The
  Part 6 Area 6.2 patterns and their three-word-and-longer examples are illustrations and set no minimum length.
- **Grandfathered.** One name written before the amendment does not meet the law: ***Open Guard Reading Cut***
  (Varys's personal move, written in the frozen Chapter 001). It stays as written and is no precedent. A name already
  written in a confirmed Area is never replaced (Rule 20.3 and Area 3.4).
- **Naming is not a cost** (Rule 20.9) applies to moves as to every entity: the register row, the collision scan and
  the Gate 2 entry are routine and never a reason to leave a move unnamed.

## Escalation

Whether an entity is major or minor, or whether it passes the Narrative-Significance Test
at all, is never silently decided when genuinely unclear — escalate via `AskUserQuestion`,
through the orchestrating command, rather than guessing. The same holds for an edge of Rule 20.8.1
(whether a thing is a named item, a place, or a Named Relic or Artifact).
