# Guardrail: Plain Language and Narrative Impact

**Source:** Canon v10.0 · cross-referenced into
`narration-novel-creative-flexibility-law` Area 13 and `narration-novel-thematic-law`
**Applies to:** all 4 agents, and every command that puts a question or report to the author.
Area Maker writes to it; Continuity Checker measures it at **Gate 1 Check #22**.
**Authority Tier:** 0 — a **fifth Tier 0 layer**, a craft layer alongside Parts 28, 29, 36 and 39.

## Why this exists

The author's words, verbatim: *"Do not use heavy words and sentences in the prose. Needs a
permanent fix — we need to use plain English for readers to understand — why use 'he had done
the arithmetic' when you can say 'He had done the math' — Creating heavy words and sentences
makes it tedious for the reader … Same goes for dialogue as well. Also do not leave out impact
and wonder in narration when needed."*

A novel that is accurate, compliant, and tedious has failed. Every other Part in this canon
governs whether a sentence is *permitted*. This one governs whether it is *readable*.

## The rule

- **Plain words, always.** Where a plain everyday word carries the meaning, the plain word is
  the correct word. `arithmetic` → **`math`**. `remuneration` → **`pay`**. `occupation` →
  **`work`**. `commenced` → **`began`**. `sufficient` → **`enough`**. `endeavoured` →
  **`tried`**. The bar is what a reader takes in without stopping.
- **The standard is plain vocabulary and clarity** (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06). It covers all prose: narration, lore,
  worldbuilding, explanation, fights, thoughts and dialogue. Mystery stays in the story, never in
  the wording. Eight parts, vocabulary and clarity only:
  1. *Everyday words.* If a plainer word exists, use it; heavy, formal, literary or rare words are
     replaced unless the story needs them.
  2. *One word, one meaning.* A word does not carry two meanings in the same Area (failing cases:
     "the forms" for both court manners and sword practice; "walk" as a path and as a verb; "level"
     as height and as voice; "floor" as the room, the crowd and the stone).
  3. *Picture over label.* A special practice, blow, custom or object is shown by what people do
     and see, in plain words, not left as a bare label.
  4. *One-clause explanation at first use.* A special or invented word is explained by the scene in
     one plain clause the first time it appears (what it is, shown by use). One new special term at
     a time. Where a technical or cultivation word is needed, the first use carries its picture;
     later uses may be bare.
  5. *No jargon wall.* Do not stack several special terms in one sentence or one speech.
  6. *Figures clear at first read.* A figure of speech that makes the reader stop is cut. Figures
     still come from the world and the point-of-view person's trade, and a clear sentence gets no
     figure (Part 36 rule 18.2.3).
  7. *Ceremonial speech* stays formal in tone and in the fixed phrases of the occasion, but is built
     of plain words. The full royal style is for royal ceremonies, not petitions.
  8. *Canon terms are never renamed.* Realm names, Named Moves, place names and Part 3 terms stay as
     they are.
  This changes words, not events, names, order, length or structure, and it does not thin the prose.
- **This binds dialogue exactly as it binds narration** (author directive, explicit). A
  character's registered voice governs *how they build a sentence*; it never licenses heavy
  vocabulary inside it.
- **Sentence rhythm is NOT restricted.** Long, accumulating, multi-clause sentences are permitted and are part of this
  novel's voice. **The plainness lives in the vocabulary, not in the sentence length.** No
  agent may shorten or chop a sentence in the name of this guardrail, and sentence length is
  **not a countable element** of Check #22.
- **Sentence variety is guidance, never a count.** Part 36's
  narrator-voice Area asks that sentence and paragraph construction vary, so paragraphs do
  not all take one shape. That guidance sets no length, no ratio and no count, is not an
  element of Check #22, and leaves the ruling above untouched.
- **Ground the plain word in the world.** This is a world of
  kingdoms and empires, forges, musters, crowns and innovation. The plain word must belong to
  it. Modern slang and brand-modern idiom are excluded — *okay*, *guys*, *kids*, *no big
  deal*, *a lot going on*. `math` is plain and belongs; `no worries` is plain and does not.
- **Colloquial register is scaled by relationship, not banned**.
  Informal speech between friends, peers and squadmates is correct and welcome. The limit is
  the reader: *"it is okay between friends, but should not come out harsh to the reader."*
  Contractions are barred in dialogue and in narration (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1c, Canon
  Amendment 2026-10-16); Gate 1 Check #4 FAILs them.
- **Impact and wonder are owed where the scene earns them.** Slow
  Areas in particular carry their weight in narration, and narration that renders a
  genuinely breathtaking thing must reach for it at full strength. **This is a peak, not a
  default** — the author's ruling is explicit that it applies *"only when the scene is
  breath taking, not always."* Wonder applied uniformly is not wonder; it is wallpaper.
- **Wonder may live in the narration without living in the character.** Deep POV narration
  can see more than the viewpoint character would say. A character whose registered profile is
  unromantic, flat, or purely practical does **not** have to feel awe for the prose to deliver
  it. Where a character *is* to be visibly moved, that is a **sanctioned moment**, placed
  deliberately and sparingly, and it must not contradict their Part 39 registration or their
  Part 29 register.
- **Questions and reports to the author use the same plain words.**
  A question or report put to the author is written like the prose: everyday words first. A
  code may follow a plain sentence and never replaces it. Worked example, a Check #21 question:
  *"Near the end of this Area [Character A] shows mercy for the first time, and while they
  wait their hands stay still, though their profile says they always find something to do with
  them. The record of changes already allowed for them says nothing about a first mercy. Is
  this a real change in them? I suggest yes, and for good, because a first mercy would change
  them. You can keep the profile and fix the Area, change the profile for good, change it for
  now only, redraft the beat, or ask again at the next boundary."* The codes come after it, for
  the record: `D-1 and D-2; B39-L29-HANDS-02; [KEEP PROFILE] / [EVOLVE] / [PROVISIONAL] /
  [REVISE AREA] / [DEFER]`.

## Friendly speech (reference examples)

**Illustration, establishes nothing (Part 38 Rule 1.3.2). Author to confirm each exchange.**
The exchanges below show how friendly speech sounds in plain words with no em dash (Part 29 Rule
29.2.1.1b). They are written with every word in full, as Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1c requires
(Canon Amendment 2026-10-16: no contraction in dialogue or narration). They use plain words that belong to this world, no modern
slang, and no names. They are positive references for tone and rhythm, not lines to reuse, and they
set no vocabulary, address form or custom for any character or place. They are short because each
moment is quick, friendly banter. They are not a model for how long or how clipped speech should
run: that follows the speaker and the moment (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.7).

**1. Two friends, one back from a long road**

*"You look thin."*
*"And you look the same, which is worse. Sit down. The bread is still warm."*
*"Then I will forgive you for the letter you never answered."*
*"I answered it in my head. Eat."*

**2. Two squadmates changing the watch**

*"Your turn on the wall. I kept the stone warm for you."*
*"That was kind of the stone. Is there anything hot to drink?"*
*"There is something hot. I would not call it tea."*
*"Then I will call it a mercy and drink it fast."*

**3. A child and a grandmother on a bridge**

*"Grandmother, is this bridge very old?"*
*"Older than my mother, and sturdier than my knees. Hold the rail and count the planks with me."*
*"One, two, three, and here is a gap!"*
*"There is always a gap. That is how you know the bridge is honest."*

**4. An elder and a young person who is sulking**

*"Sit down before you fall down, young one."*
*"I am not going to fall."*
*"No. But you are going to sulk, and that is harder to watch."*
*"I am not sulking."*
*"Good. Then you can carry the water and not sulk at the same time."*

**5. A merchant and a returning customer**

*"Back again. Your usual, and a fair price because you are a friend."*
*"A fair price for a friend is half of what you named last time."*
*"Then it is lucky that I like you, because I do not like losing."*
*"Two thirds, and I will bring my cousins in the spring."*
*"Done. Bring the cousins first."*

**6. Two rivals who like each other**

*"You took the seat by the fire again."*
*"You were slow again. I am only being kind to the fire."*
*"One day I will be early."*
*"One day I will let you win, and then you will never forgive me."*

**7. A brother and a sister at an old well**

*"Do you remember the well behind the old house?"*
*"I remember you slipping in the mud beside it."*
*"And I remember you pulling me out and laughing the whole way home."*
*"Both of those are true. I was laughing because I was afraid."*

**8. A smith and an apprentice at the forge**

*"Is the iron ready?"*
*"Look at the color. What does it tell you?"*
*"That it is the color of a low sun."*
*"Then it is ready. Why do you look so pleased?"*
*"Because you did not tell me I was wrong."*
*"I am saving that for tomorrow."*

## Hard rules

- **Plainness never overrides a higher-order prohibition.** It cannot license a contraction in
  narration or dialogue (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1c, Canon Amendment 2026-10-16), an em-dash (Rule
  29.2.1.1b), a forbidden realm term, a coined
  address form, any P30-L8 entity, or a sentient monster (Law P30-L9; save the one narrow artifact-made exception of Canon Amendment 2026-10-19 (Law P30-L9)). Where plain phrasing would require one, the phrasing
  changes — never the prohibition.
- **Plainness never overrides a registered voice.** A character's Part 29 prosodic
  determinants and Part 39 Layer register are theirs. This guardrail changes the *words
  available*, never the *shape of the sentence they build*. A registered formal speaker stays
  formal in plain words. (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06: the voice's rhythm and habits stay as
  registered; only its WORDS are plain, and no ledger register is touched by this guardrail.) A
  registered voice is a set of traits delivered in natural speech, never a sentence formula
  (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.7, Canon Amendment 2026-10-08).
- **Plainness is not flattening.** Removing a heavy word does not license removing an image, a
  specific detail, a sensory anchor, or a Depth Pillar. Vocabulary gets simpler; the prose does
  not get thinner. An Area that loses density while gaining plainness has failed this guardrail;
  the Part 35 reading is shown as information.
- **Wonder is never manufactured.** A scene that is not breathtaking is not made breathtaking
  by reaching. Straining for awe at an ordinary moment is a Part 36 failure (manufactured
  prose) and this guardrail does not excuse it — it forbids it.
- **A heavy-word count is reported, never asked.** The count, the
  instances and the plain alternatives always appear in the audit report as information. They
  are never put to the author as a question and are never a FAIL (the heavy-word counts of 22-1
  and 22-2 inform only; the blocking elements are named in the Check #22 table), and no pass
  rewrites the prose silently; the author reads the report and decides.

## Worked sample: warm speech between friends

The author's ruling is that informal speech between friends is correct and welcome. This
sample shows it inside every ban. The figures are unnamed and the scene is invented.

> They shared the last of the bread on the wall, boots hanging over the drop.
>
> *"You ate the heel again,"* she said. *"You always eat the heel."*
>
> *"You never want it. I am doing you a kindness."* He wiped his fingers on his knee and
> grinned at the camp below. *"Besides, you owe me. Who carried your pack up the ridge?"*
>
> *"You did, and you complained the whole way."*
>
> *"Every step. I have a name to keep up."* But he pushed the last crust into her hand all
> the same. *"Go on. You look thin."*

Between friends the talk is quick, teasing and direct. It is built from shared history (the
pack, the ridge) and an act of care (the crust), and not from slang. It has no modern slang or
brand-modern idiom (element 22-3), no heavy word (elements 22-1 and 22-2), and nothing that would
come out harsh to the reader (element 22-4); no contraction is lawful here (Part 29 Rule
29.2.1.1c, as amended 2026-10-16). The warmth is in the plain words and the gift.

**Sentence length is deliberately absent from this table.** Its absence is the author's ruling,
not an oversight, and no future pass may add it without a fresh author directive.

**Counting method must be stated.** No tokenizer exists in this repo. An auditor reporting a
22-1, 22-2 or 22-7 figure states how it counted, so two passes can be compared
(`.claude/guardrails/quality/guardrail-threshold-consultation.md`).

**Reporting line.** Every flagged heavy-word instance (elements 22-1 and 22-2) is listed in the
audit report with its plain alternative, and `[CONFIRM WRITE]` accepts the listed ones. The
author is asked only if the list is disputed; the count itself is never put as a question (see
Hard rules).

**Elements 22-8 to 22-11 (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06).** The auditor lists each instance with its
line and a plain fix. A "special word" is any invented, technical, ceremonial or rare word. A term
the canon owns (realm, Named Move, place, Part 3 term) is never renamed, but its first use still
carries one clause of picture (22-8). Words a stranger might stop on, with the heavy-word counts and
seed-table hits, are information only. The area-maker runs a "read it as a stranger" pass before
hand-off, listing such words and tallying double-meaning words.

**Element 22-7 (crutch words).** The watchlist is the author's to extend or cut. The count is a
plain text search of the Area for each listed word or phrase, whole words only, each hit
one instance, with narration and dialogue counted apart. It is printed for every Area. It is vocabulary, so the sentence-length ruling above is untouched.
No guide post is set; the author may set one.

## Escalation

A conflict between plainness and any other Tier-0 rule is surfaced, never silently resolved in
plainness's favour — plainness is the newest authority here, not the highest. Where the plain
word does not exist, or where every plain option would flatten the image, **that conflict is
itself the finding** and it goes to the author with the options named
(`.claude/guardrails/quality/guardrail-threshold-consultation.md`).
