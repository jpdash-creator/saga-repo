# Guardrail: Story Specification Law

**Source:** Part 38 · full detail: `spec-story-specification-law` (Areas 9, 10 and 11 hold the spec chain, the Section Spec and the Novel-Part Spec) · **Canon v10.0** | Last Updated: 2026-10-07
**Applies to:** all 4 agents (Outline Planner builds each level of the chain and plans within specs; Area Maker drafts within them; Continuity Checker audits and syncs them; Area Scorer reads their CDL-2 emphasis targets) and every orchestrator command that opens, plans, drafts, or redrafts a Section, a Novel-Part, a Chapter or an Area (`section-spec`, `part-spec`, `chapter-spec`, `start-story`, `next-section`, `next-part`, `next-chapter`, `next-phase` (closes Section 8; builds no spec above the Chapter), `next-area`, `create-chapter`, `revise-chapter`, `redraft-area`, `redraft-chapter`, `revise-area`, and peers)

## The rule

**The spec chain (no Area is planned or drafted without an author-confirmed plan).**
Every Section, every Novel-Part and every Chapter has a **story spec**. The three kinds are called together the **spec chain** (Part 38 Rule 38.9): the **Section Spec**, the **Novel-Part Spec** and the **Chapter Spec**. The formal name is always "Novel-Part Spec", because "Part Spec" would read as the spec of canon Part N; the command that builds it is `/part-spec`. Their files sit in one tree:

```
.claude/story-memory/phase-section-part-chapter-area-specs/
├── _index.md                       one registry, three tables (Section Specs, Novel-Part Specs, Chapter Specs)
└── phase-N/section-N/
    ├── section-N-spec.md           Section Spec      (ref P.S, for example 1.2)
    └── part-N/
        ├── part-N-spec.md          Novel-Part Spec   (ref P.S.P, for example 1.2.3)
        └── chapter-NNN-spec.md     Chapter Spec      (ref P.S.P.CCC)
```

The novel has five tiers: Phase, Section, Novel-Part, Chapter and Area. The **Area is the written unit** of the by-area path; it may be a complete scene, or a scene may continue across consecutive Areas of a Chapter. *Amended 2026-10-07:* a Chapter may instead be written whole by `/create-chapter` (Build mode `whole`, Part 38 Rule 2.3.8), in which case the Area blocks of the Chapter Spec are plan units, no Area files exist, and the Scene Map (Rule 2.4.12) says which blocks make each scene.

The **Chapter Spec** is the lowest of the three. It holds the Chapter's purpose and name chain (names marked proposed vs. locked-at-Gate-1 for structural names, locked-at-Gate-2 for in-world names, the status kept in the notes), per-Area blocks (each the plan of one written Area and the bible that connects the chapter: purpose, want, obstacle and stake, viewpoint with its Dual-POV mark (Part 2 Rule 4.3.1), pacing (planned), size target of 1,600, 1,800 or 2,000 words, scene anchors, continuation (a complete scene, or a scene that continues in the next Area), planned time skips, opening and closing type, carry-over from the previous Area, thread, story link and change, setups and payoffs, consequences landing here (optional), emotional line, world/setting/people with the entities present and the debuts, novel elements carried, CDL-2 emphasis, an optional fight line, and the optional sensory anchor, tone or voice and content fields), and the Chapter's Tier Scope Bracket range (Areas in this Chapter): *(Amended 2026-10-07)* the Chapter header also carries the **Build mode** (`whole` or `by-area`, default `whole`), the **Chapter pacing** and the **Chapter size target (words)**; section 5.0 is the **Scene Map** (scene ID, Area blocks, POV sequence, scene arc, scene pacing, scene word budget, visible break before, runs in from or out to, join state at each Area seam); and each Area block carries a **Budget stretch** (none, or up to 0.72 of target with a reason). The Chapter Spec is THE authoritative backing store for that range (Part 2 Area 9, Rule 9.12.2). No range is stored above Chapter grain in any spec; the Novel-Part and Section Specs hold lists that guide the plan, and a list's length is read as information only (Part 38 Rule 38.5 and Part 2 Rule 9.13). The Chapter Spec carries every item it inherits from the two specs above it, each recorded with its source in its notes, so that Area time reads the Chapter Spec alone.

The **Novel-Part Spec** (Part 38 Area 11, Rule 38.11) lists every Chapter of its Novel-Part in depth: purpose, calendar window, threads in and out, who is on the page, key events, reveals owed, opening hook, closing beat, a one-line Area outline of descriptors only, and (Amended 2026-10-07) a Chapter pacing and Chapter word budget (guides, each `unset` until the author gives one) with a Scene Map across Chapters (scene IDs `PS1`, `PS2`, ...). The **Section Spec** (Part 38 Area 10, Rule 38.10) is the Section's plan set against what Part 30 expects of it: epigraph (the Section's own title-page epigraph, no agent ever writing it; Section 1's Section Spec carries its own too, and a Phase's first title page prints the Phase epigraph, a line of Part 30, above it: the author alone settles each line), calendar window, the Novel-Parts with a high-level Chapter list, threads, setups and payoffs as a guide, protagonist arcs, the progression plan, opposition and reveals, and the hand-off to the next Section; each Novel-Part entry also carries a pacing and a word budget (guides, `unset` until the author gives one) and the block carries a Scene Map across Novel-Parts (scene IDs `SS1`, `SS2`, ...), Amended 2026-10-07. Part 30 is cited by function name there, never copied. Above Chapter grain, names are working labels: no Establishment status is stored in a Section or Novel-Part Spec, and Establishment is recorded in the Chapter Spec and the delivered files (Part 28 Rules 18.4.1 to 18.4.3 apply to those labels as advice only). Calendar windows nest: Section window, then Novel-Part window, then Chapter window, then Area.

**The chain rule (Part 38 Rule 9.1.1).** A Chapter Spec cannot exist without a Novel-Part Spec, and a Novel-Part Spec cannot exist without a Section Spec. A Chapter Spec is built or revised only while its Novel-Part Spec **and** its Section Spec are CONFIRMED or COMPLETED; a Novel-Part Spec is built or revised only while its Section Spec is CONFIRMED or COMPLETED. A parent that is DRAFT (saved by `[SKIP SPEC]`), AMENDED-PENDING-RECONFIRM or RETIRED refuses the child build, and the refusal names the right command (`/section-spec`, `/part-spec` or `/chapter-spec`). A `[SKIP SPEC]` at any level stops the chain there. The Chapter being built must also have an entry in its Novel-Part Spec; if it has none (the story needed one more), the command stops and offers to add the entry as a light amendment with its own `[CONFIRM SPEC]` prompt, writes it at that confirm, and only then builds the Chapter Spec from it (Rule 9.1.2). A Novel-Part that has no entry in its Section Spec is different: adding a Novel-Part to a Section Spec is a substantial amendment, so the build stops and the author is sent to `/section-spec revise`, with no light add (Rules 9.4.2 and 11.5.1). There is no Phase Spec: Part 30 is the Phase-level plan, and `/section-spec` refuses to build for a Section Part 30 has no entry for, saying Part 30 must be expanded first (Rule 9.1.4).

**The clean body and the Audit and Settlement Notes (Part 38 Rule 38.14).** A spec file carries NO inline `[SETTLED …]`, `[OPEN …]`, `[ASKED …]`, `[SKIPPED …]` or `[FROM …]` tag, and no provenance or audit tag, in its body. The body reads clean, as plain plan text, start to finish. Every settled item, open item, deferral, skip, ruling provenance, variance and audit note is gathered in one final section, **Audit and Settlement Notes**, at the END of the file, each note pointing back to the plan item it concerns by Area, field name or heading. The kinds of note are Settled, Skipped, Open, Deferred, From, Inherited, Name, Variance and Audit (Rule 9.10.2). An open item is found in that section: Check #18, the Area-time Decision Consultation Gate, the light audit and `/status` read open and deferred items there. The light audit checks that the body is clean and the section stands last (Rules 3.3.2(h), 10.5.1(k) and 11.6.1(i)).

**Status and notes.** The spec status set, the same for all three kinds, is `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED-PENDING-RECONFIRM`, `COMPLETED` and `RETIRED`. The **Area** status `PLANNED`/`DRAFTED`/`WRITTEN`/`DROPPED` (Part 38 Rule 2.4.9) is a different thing. A Settled note is an author answer; a Skipped note is an author SKIP, which settles nothing; an Open note is a genuinely open question; a Deferred note is a deferral the author chose (to a later spec, or to Area writing; the planner never defers on its own); a From note records where a pre-filled value came from; an Inherited note carries a settled item of a higher spec down, naming its source. An inherited item never loses its note by being copied. A deferral to Area writing is copied into the Chapter Spec of the Chapter it bears on, so the Area-time gate asks the author, as it does for any open item; the drafter never settles it silently (Rule 9.5.2).

**Authority model (author answers are not asked twice, and a plan is never canon).** A confirmed spec chain is a valid decision input for planning and structural decisions, with **settling power**: items the author explicitly answered at spec-build are settled per DCG Rule 19.4 and are NOT re-asked at Area time. The same power runs downward through the chain: a settled item in a higher spec is not re-asked in a lower build; it is carried into the Chapter Spec as an Inherited note naming its source (Rule 9.5.1). **No pacing question is asked at Area-writing time:** pacing (Fast, Medium or Slow) is decided in the Chapter Spec for every Area and carried (Part 38 Rules 2.3.3 and 4.2.1). Always still asked regardless of the spec: branches the spec records as open (a deferral to Area writing counts as one), anything genuinely new, and anything where proceeding on the spec alone would reduce clarity (Part 38 Rule 4.2.4, at every level, with the stale higher answer shown). A spec is **never** a source of established canon facts, at any level, and a settled item in an upper spec never establishes canon (Rule 1.3.2) — Establishment happens only at a Gate 1 delivery that is confirmed and written or a Gate 2 write (Rule 18.4.4; Area 20.3 for in-world names). On conflict the order is fixed: **Locked Canon > ledger (established) > spec chain (intent) > session-log (never)** — "ledger supersedes spec." Inside the chain, for what a level must do, the order is Section Spec, then Novel-Part Spec, then Chapter Spec; the lower spec governs its own detail. A conflict found in a lower build is a **question** to the author, never a silent pick, and the author chooses, for each departure, to amend the plan or keep the original as the plan baseline (Rules 9.4.1 and 9.4.5).

**Governance after confirm (Rules 9.3.2 and 9.3.3).** After `[CONFIRM SPEC]` the Chapter header governs the Areas and Gate 1, and the Novel-Part Spec entry stays the plan baseline. A departure flows back only for what the Novel-Part entry fixes: purpose, calendar window, threads in and out, who is on the page, key events, reveals owed, opening hook and closing beat. Splitting, merging, re-cutting or renaming Areas is the Chapter Spec's own business and is not a departure. A departure is shown at the confirm step, and the author says whether to amend the plan or leave the original as the plan baseline. **Variance is recorded at every level above (Rule 9.4.5):** a departure at any level leaves a variance note on EVERY spec above the one where it arose, written top-down in one step (the Section Spec first, then the Novel-Part Spec, and then the lower spec is updated), so a Chapter's departure leaves a note on both the Novel-Part Spec and the Section Spec. The note is light and appended only, in the Audit and Settlement Notes: the plan content of a higher spec changes only through that spec's own revise flow. A change to a Novel-Part's list of Chapters (add, split, merge, drop) and any departure of a Novel-Part Spec from what its Section Spec entry fixes are noted on the Section Spec in the same way (Rule 11.4.3).

**Clarity mandate (Part 38 Rule 38.13).** One definition of "done" governs all three builds. A build is done only when: (1) there is no unasked branch at that spec's own level (a question belongs to the level where the author's answer first changes that level's own plan; below-grain matters are not that spec's question); (2) a final sweep by the Outline Planner and the Continuity Checker, covering conflicts with Part 30, the ledgers and the parent spec, and re-listing every SKIP and every deferral for a last chance, returns nothing new; (3) the author gives an explicit "nothing further to steer" answer; and (4) then the confirm token follows. If the sweep returns anything, there are more rounds and then a new sweep. There is no score, no percentage and no round count. The planner never leaves an item open on its own: every field is asked, and only the author may defer one. Questions keep the shape of Part 38 Rule 3.2.2: two or three concrete options plus `SKIP — let AI decide`, up to four questions in one `AskUserQuestion` call, as many rounds as needed. There is no quick build for any spec kind (Rule 3.2.5): a per-question SKIP is the only shortcut, and it is recorded as a Skipped note.

**Lifecycle.**

1. **Build order is top-down: Section Spec, then Novel-Part Spec, then Chapter Spec.** The build happens at the boundary: `/start-story` at the start of the story, `/next-section` for a new Section, `/next-part` for a new Novel-Part, `/next-chapter` for a new Chapter. `/start-story` and `/next-section` build every link the new position needs, from the Section Spec down; `/next-part` builds the Novel-Part Spec and the first Chapter Spec, and `/next-chapter` builds the Chapter Spec only, and each of the two, when a link above what it builds is not in force, stops before anything is written and names the command that puts it right (`/section-spec` or `/part-spec`; Rules 9.1.1 and 9.2.1). `/next-phase` carries a note that the spec chain for Phase 2 waits until Part 30 is expanded for Phase 2 (Rule 9.1.4), and it closes Section 8 (Rule 9.7.3). The author may also run `/section-spec build` or `/part-spec build` for a later Section or Novel-Part once its parent chain exists. A spec built ahead records a built-against stamp (the last completed Chapter or Section and the ledger state it was built against); at the boundary the next-* command checks the stamp against what happened and re-asks only the affected items, showing the original answer and what changed (Rules 9.2.2 and 9.2.3). A build over a Section or Novel-Part that already has written Chapters is a retro-fit: statuses come from the record, unevidenced fields are recorded open or deferred as the author says, and the notes read "as-built reconstructed" (Rule 9.8.2).
2. **Each build is an Outline-Planner-led iterative author consultation, in Plan Mode, until the clarity mandate above is met.** The Section Spec build reads the whole Part 30 SKILL.md every time, prints what Part 30 expects of the Section in chat (Locked and Negotiable marked), and surfaces every Part 30 OPEN-AUTHOR row due in or planted by that Section (Rules 10.4.2 and 10.4.3). Direction and theme questions are asked inside the builds, each at its own level.
3. **Each level then passes a light Continuity Checker audit** (report-only; a blocking finding must be resolved before `[CONFIRM SPEC]` is accepted). The Chapter scope is the Locked-Canon conflicts, forbidden entities (P30-L8, P30-L9 sentient monsters, save the one narrow artifact-made exception of Canon Amendment 2026-10-19 (Law P30-L9), and no-Sapphire-Line), Entity Naming Law flags, Part 30 mandate coverage, established ledger state, schema conformance, Area-block connection (Part 38 Rule 3.3.2(f)), derivation (item (g): the header fields agree with the Novel-Part entry or are noted departures) and the clean body (item (h)). The Novel-Part scope is Rule 11.6.1 and the Section scope is Rule 10.5.1.
4. **Then the author is asked for `[CONFIRM SPEC]` / `[REVISE SPEC]` / `[SKIP SPEC]`, and only after that is the spec active.** The three tokens are shared by all three kinds. The prompt, the session-log line and the registry row name the artifact and its ref ("Section Spec 1.2", "Novel-Part Spec 1.2.3", "Chapter Spec 1.2.3.004"), and only one confirm prompt is open at a time in a chain. Each new spec file is written at its own `[CONFIRM SPEC]`, so it is durable at once. The completion writes of the outgoing specs (Chapter, then Novel-Part, then Section, bottom-up) happen in one write step at the last confirm of the chain, the new Chapter Spec's (Rule 9.4.4). A `[SKIP SPEC]` saves that spec as DRAFT, stops the chain there, leaves every outgoing spec un-completed and leaves the command free to be run again.
5. **The "active" Section, Novel-Part and Chapter** are the first spec in each tier whose status is neither COMPLETED nor RETIRED; a RETIRED spec is skipped (Rule 9.7.4).
6. **Who writes spec files (Rule 1.2.3 as extended, Rule 9.4.3).** The `/section-spec`, `/part-spec` and `/chapter-spec` flows (the orchestrating command writes; the Outline Planner never writes specs); the Gate 2 sync (Chapter Spec only); the reset and rewind reconciliation pass (statuses, stamps and the Coverage cells of the registry rows the rollback changes, and the COMPLETED to CONFIRMED rollbacks; Rules 9.4.3(c) and 1.2.2), where the go-back step of `[REDRAFT AREA]` and `[REDRAFT CHAPTER]` (their Step 2, run as a rewind does) counts as a rewind rollback and refreshes the Coverage cells of the rows it changes too; completion at `/next-chapter`, `/next-part` and `/next-section` (and the `complete` modes of the spec commands); and a lower-level flow may append variance notes to every higher spec it derives from, top-down in one step, but may not change any of their plan content (Rule 9.4.5). No other writer exists. Every spec write also sets its registry row to the file's state (version, status, Last Updated); a reader takes the version and status from the spec header, and a row that reads older than the header is corrected at the next write.
7. **The as-built sync.** A mandatory **as-built sync** happens at every Gate 2, performed by the Post-Write Ledger Update Continuity Checker instance. Its write set is the `ledger-*` files plus the active Chapter Spec file and its registry row; the three-way Gate 2 split stays three-way; after `[SKIP WRITE]` there is no Gate 2 and nothing is written or synced. Novel-Part and Section Spec statuses are derived or written at boundary and completion events, never at Gate 2. The Coverage cell of a Section or Novel-Part registry row is refreshed at that spec's own confirm, at any completion write step (a child's completion included) and at a reset or rewind rollback, and never at Gate 2 (Rules 1.2.2, 10.1.2 and 11.1.2). The Gate 2 sync sets a Chapter Spec to COMPLETED when its last Area is written (Rule 3.6.1 item 1); *(Amended 2026-10-07)* for a `whole` Chapter the one Gate 2 of `/create-chapter` sets every Area row `WRITTEN(P.S.P.CCC)`, flips the Signatures-fired rows per Area block by the hidden Area markers, writes the Completion Record and sets COMPLETED in the same pass (Rule 3.6.1, the whole-Chapter paragraph). `redraft-chapter` re-opens the Chapter Spec first, before any redraft work; a re-opened Chapter returns to CONFIRMED when the plan does not change, and to AMENDED-PENDING-RECONFIRM when it does.
8. **Read sets (Rule 9.9.2).** Agents that BUILD a level read the whole chain above that level in full: the Novel-Part Spec build reads the whole Section Spec, the Chapter Spec build reads the Section Spec and the Novel-Part Spec, and the Section Spec build reads the whole Part 30 SKILL.md. Agents that work PER AREA (Area Maker, Area Scorer, both Gate 1 Continuity Checker instances, and the Outline Planner when it plans one Area) read the Chapter Spec in full, its Audit and Settlement Notes included, plus the Novel-Part Spec's header block and closing-state block only; the Section Spec is not in the per-Area set. This is an explicit exception to "every mandatory read stays in full": nothing is cut down, those reads are simply not mandatory for per-Area agents, because the Chapter Spec carries the inherited items with their source.

## Hard rules

- **Settling power has carve-outs, always (extended down the chain, Rule 9.5.3).** Pacing is decided in the Chapter Spec for every Area and carried: Area pacing is never set above the Chapter Spec, and it is never asked at Area-writing time. *(Amended 2026-10-07:* the Section Spec and the Novel-Part Spec hold only guides for the pacing and word budget of a Novel-Part and of a Chapter; the Chapter Spec settles the Chapter pacing, the Chapter size target, each Area's size and Budget stretch and the Scene Map in the build, asked by `/section-spec`, `/part-spec` and `/chapter-spec`; pacing and size are the author's decision, recorded in the spec, and are never asked at writing time, for a whole-built Chapter as for any other.) Spec-recorded open items (including a deferral to Area writing) and genuinely new branches are always asked. Autonomous Matters (Part 36 Area 13's sensory palette and living-scene territory) are never spec-fixed, and never fixed above Area, except through the author's optional levers: the sensory anchor field, the tone and voice fields and the content field (Part 38 Rules 2.3.5a, 2.3.5b and 2.3.5d), which stay at Area grain. Unset, a lever leaves the call to the drafter at Area time; set, the drafter follows it.
- **A spec never establishes canon facts.** Establishment happens only at a Gate 1 delivery that is confirmed and written or a Gate 2 write (Rule 18.4.4; Area 20.3 for in-world names). An Area that delivers what a spec planned still passes both gates in full; a Gate-accepted deviation makes the spec wrong, not the Area. This holds for all three kinds.
- **Ledger supersedes spec.** A spec records intent; a ledger records established state. Where they disagree, the ledger is right and the spec receives a variance note at the next as-built sync (a Novel-Part or Section Spec: at the next boundary or completion write) — never the other way around.
- **Calendar windows are dates plus Phase Day.** Every calendar window of the chain (Section, Novel-Part and Chapter), and the Area's dates inside the Chapter window, is written as calendar dates (Year, Month and Day) together with the Phase Day span they stand for; a spec cites Part 41 (Area 4, Phase Day conversion and Section windows) and never copies its tables, and the Phase Day stays the stable key. The in-story time a Chapter Spec records as built mirrors the calendar ledger: `ledger-worlds-calendar/_index.md` owns the recorded dates, and where the two differ the ledger stands and the spec takes the variance note, as for any ledger fact above. The ledger records only what is written and never cites spec content (Part 38 Rules 1.3.2 and 1.4.2), so it pre-fills planned windows for the Phase and the Sections only, a conversion of Part 30.
- **The spec folder is NOT a ledger folder.** Part 37 does not govern it — spec schema authority is Part 38's own. No content duplication in either direction: a spec cites ledger entities by name/ref-code only, never restates their content, and a ledger never restates spec intent. Spec-to-spec derivation is a different thing and is allowed, as long as it is recorded with its source (Rule 9.3.4): a lower spec may carry a higher spec's items down, and a Section Spec cites Part 30 by function name and never copies its text (Rule 2.3.2).
- **Specs are written with ordinary Write/Edit tools.** The authorized-scripts law (`guardrails/tools/guardrail-authorized-scripts-only.md`) covers only novel *output* files — spec files, of all three kinds, are story-memory planning files, not novel output, so no authorized script is required (or permitted) for them. No new script may be created inside the repo for them. Stated explicitly to prevent ambiguity.
- **Specs survive resets and rewinds — except `/hard-reset`.** A reset/rewind never deletes a spec of any of the three kinds; statuses roll back to match the on-disk Area state, and a variance note records the rollback. A COMPLETED Chapter Spec whose Areas are removed returns to CONFIRMED; a COMPLETED Novel-Part Spec or Section Spec returns to CONFIRMED when any of its children returns to non-COMPLETED; the completion text is kept as history (Rule 9.8.1). The single exception is `[HARD RESET]` (C-1), which deletes every spec file **of all three kinds** and empties all three registry tables to their headers (Part 38 Rule 7.1.2): C-1 returns the project to a pre-story state, and a plan written against discarded Areas is not intent worth carrying forward. After a hard reset, `/start-story` is a fresh build of the whole chain. A spec is retired only explicitly (RETIRED, never silently), and retiring a Section Spec or Novel-Part Spec that still has children that are not RETIRED is refused until the author rules on each child (Rule 9.8.3).
- **Amending a parent after children exist (Rule 9.4.2).** When a Section Spec or Novel-Part Spec is revised and children exist, the Continuity Checker reports every child entry or spec the change no longer fits, and for each one the author picks: keep it (a variance is noted), amend it, or mark it AMENDED-PENDING-RECONFIRM. Nothing flips on its own. Written Areas are never touched by a spec amendment; a correction there goes through `/revise-area` or a redraft. Changing a Novel-Part's or Section's purpose, closing state or hand-off, calendar window or reveals owed is a substantial amendment, and so is adding, removing or reordering the Novel-Parts of a Section Spec (so a Novel-Part with no Section Spec entry is a stop that sends the author to `/section-spec revise`, Rule 9.4.2); adding, splitting, merging or dropping Chapters (a guide list) and adding variance notes are light.
- **Setups, payoffs and threads are three linked guide registers (Rule 9.5.4).** The Section, Novel-Part and Chapter Specs each carry their own, linked by shared keys (a Part 30 setups-table row number or a short thread name). They are guides and suggestions, never blocks: the story may plant elsewhere, pay earlier or later, or drop one. The Chapter Area fields (Planted here, Carried or paid off here) show where a setup actually lands, and these are what Part 36 reads. `/threads` shows plan against what happened as information only, and an unmet suggestion creates no check, no gate and no question.
- **The Area block is the bible that connects the story.** Each Area block plans one written Area: opening type and closing type (fixed lists), scene anchors, the continuation (a complete scene, or a scene that continues in the next Area; an Area that continues the previous Area's scene opens in motion from the exact state the previous Area left, and an Area whose scene runs on ends on live tension), the planned pacing and the size target (two separate fields, each asked at the build, neither tied to the other), carry-over from the previous Area, thread, viewpoint (a change of viewpoint falls between Areas; a Dual-POV mark is the author's) and planned time skips (catalogued), story link and change, want, obstacle and stake, setups and payoffs, consequences landing here (optional), emotional line, world/setting/people, novel elements carried, an optional fight line, and the optional sensory anchor, tone or voice and content fields. A break in time, place or thread between Areas, a cliffhanger, a cut away and a cut-back are lawful only when the block plans them, and consecutive Areas open differently unless the spec says so (Part 38 section 2.4). The Novel-Part Spec's one-line Area outline holds descriptors only (no anchors, no opening or closing types, no Area size targets, no Area pacing, no names): those stay in the Chapter Spec (Rule 11.3.4). *(Amended 2026-10-07:* a Chapter of Build mode `whole` is measured by the word band instead of the size range: each Area block at 55 to 60 percent of its size target in words, up to 72 percent only where its Budget stretch names it, and the Chapter total inside 0.55 T to 0.72 T; Rules 2.3.10 and 6.1.5.)
- **Completion, not close-out.** The spec status `COMPLETED` marks a unit whose Areas are all written. A Chapter Spec's status becomes COMPLETED at the Gate 2 of its last Area (Rule 3.6.1 item 1) and its completion content is written by `/next-chapter` or `/chapter-spec complete`; a Novel-Part Spec is completed by `/next-part`, a Section Spec by `/next-section`, each on the author's yes to "Has this finished?". Completion writes the final variance summary (planned against as-written, including Chapters or Novel-Parts added or dropped), a Reader-now-knows line, a digest, and the threads and setups carried to the next unit; the author is asked about each unpaid setup or unresolved cliffhanger or thread, and one is marked lapsed only on the author's answer (Rules 9.7.2 and 9.7.3). At the end of Phase 1 no `/next-part` or `/next-section` follows Section 8, so `/next-phase` runs the Section-close reviews and asks the completions once each, in the order Chapter, Novel-Part, Section, and writes them bottom-up in one step (Rules 9.7.2 and 9.7.3); `/part-spec complete` and `/section-spec complete` remain available to the author. A COMPLETED spec stays a living record: `revise` works at any time, and completion pieces are refreshed when it is revised; whether a revision that changes the plan for written Areas is followed by a redraft is the author's call. Reports at the end of a Novel-Part or Section (unmet reveals, threads, Part 30 functions) are informational and never block (Rule 9.7.4).
- **Section close.** The Continuity Checker reports a POV count per protagonist from the written Areas, reading the Section's specs, with the threads off the page past the stretch the author set and the setups still open: informational only, with no target, no order and no warning (Part 38 Rule 3.7.2).
- **Gate 1 Check #18 (Spec Conformance) is a blocking check** (`audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`): every spec-planned element of the drafted Area is compared with the draft and shown as a plan-versus-written list. Core items (beat, scene anchors, the continuation, viewpoint, time skips, Behavioral Plan, opening type, closing type, thread) and every settled item block unless the difference traces to an on-record author answer from this invocation; every other difference is listed (pacing included: a pacing difference is shown and never blocks), `[CONFIRM WRITE]` accepts the listed ones (the spec is updated at Gate 2) and `[REVISE AREA]` rejects them (Part 38 Rules 6.1.1 to 6.1.4). Size is structural and blocks: a count outside 1,280 to 2,400 words is a blocking finding, and the write waits for the author to confirm the Area or send it back (Rule 6.1.4). *(Amended 2026-10-07:* for a whole-built Chapter `/create-chapter` runs the check once on the whole Chapter with the word-band test in place of the size range, and the Scene Map is compared with the scenes as written; Rule 6.1.5.) **Item (a) is "the chain is in force".** The Chapter Spec must be CONFIRMED (or COMPLETED, for an older Area that is revised) **and** its Novel-Part Spec **and** its Section Spec must be CONFIRMED or COMPLETED; a link that is DRAFT, AMENDED-PENDING-RECONFIRM or RETIRED does not count. A missing or unconfirmed link is a FAIL: Critical where no chain stands (a link is missing, DRAFT or RETIRED), Major where a link stands but is AMENDED-PENDING-RECONFIRM (Part 38 Rules 6.1.3 and 9.1.3). The correction path is the command that builds the first missing link (`/section-spec`, `/part-spec` or `/chapter-spec`) and `[CONFIRM SPEC]`, then a re-run. No comparison of the Area against the upper specs is added: slot conformance stays at Chapter grain. The number of Gate 1 checks is unchanged.

## Escalation

A spec/ledger conflict, a spec/Locked-Canon conflict, a conflict found in a lower build against a higher spec, or genuine doubt about whether a spec-build answer actually settled an item is surfaced explicitly — via `AskUserQuestion`, through the orchestrating command, or `/run-canon-audit` — never silently resolved in either direction.
