**Recast (Rule 29.2.1.7a).** Join a clipped pair with a connective the beat already implies (because, though, but, and, so); keep every fact; invent no fact, name or habit.
- Read the speaker first: Tier D `F` and `PSM` tokens sit in the Code cell of the row in `ledger-character-side/_index.md`; heroes have a prosodic section in the hero file. Bison's "word ceiling" (bison.md, element 1(a), property 1) is vocabulary only (village words), not turn length: a longer connected line is lawful with no hedge, vocative, epigram or false statement. A question that hands the matter over ("Will you tell me how it works?") replaces a flat "Tell me".
- Keep, and report, spec-planned one-word beats ("Sweet.", "There,", "Ours,", "Hm,", the bare first-name greeting) and deliberate trail-off pairs.
- A chant is one counted sentence in the order of the calls.
- Ease the word ceiling by cutting a restated image in narration (Single-Statement Standard), outside any passage the command protects; never by clipping speech.
- Report every changed line before and after, and name the ledger cells and spec notes that quote the old wording.

**22-9 sweep (per Area, not per Chapter).** Grep `.{0,40}\b(word)\b.{0,40}` with `-o -n`; list the sense of each hit per Area. Usual words: hold, chest, floor, bench, read, weight, line, post, file, round, row, step, keep, cut, head, hand. Zero-word fixes: keep one sense per Area and swap the others (hold becomes bound, pressed, bear, kept; body "chest" becomes ribs or breastbone; "gave the floor to X" becomes "turned to X"; reading text becomes studying or see, sensing keeps "read"; "pressure" for sensed power and "weight" only for a stroke's crushing force, never both).

**Sameness tallies (count before and after).** Vocative-opening speeches (move the name mid or end; keep commands and shouts), `would sooner|would rather`, `I will (say|tell|give)` (keep registered signatures and squad orders), `Madam`, `breath` as a time unit, `finger's breadth`, `began to`, `all at once`, `a moment`, repeated closers, `as ... as` similes. Count the replacement word too: swapping one word everywhere only moves the repetition. A swap that removes a phrase is net negative; spend the saved words on a plain picture, never on new beats.
