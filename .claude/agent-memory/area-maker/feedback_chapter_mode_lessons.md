**Registered-signature slips to avoid in a hero's POV.** A hero who "sets things right" must never leave a crooked thing crooked on purpose (that is the registered alarm, a drift row the spec says is not engaged); show the world resisting tidiness instead (a letter corner that keeps curling). A hero whose speech register bars weather words (Aeries) may not say wind, cold or storm outside the one alarm line: Grep his quoted lines for them. The pause is only before a promise or, by ruling, between reading and sealing a letter.

**Read the Audit and Settlement Notes (section 7) before drafting each scene, and again after any context break.** Skipped, Settled and Open notes carry choices that override a plan I made early (a different pilot in Area 05; the cost beat deferred; no man in the good coat in Varys's letter). A summary of them is not enough: Grep the note by its key words.

**Do not write a long Area in one stroke and trust it.** The first Area 03 had stray text, an invented detail (a rider's bag), a Level the spec leaves as a range, a rendered cost the author had deferred, and ran 100 words over. Rewrite paragraph by paragraph with Edit, Read the file after an insertion to catch a missing blank line before a marker, then count.

**Lean POVs run short.** Aalia's Areas came out near 1,500 words against a 1,650 floor. Enrich with registered conduct, a place-first memory, sensory anchors and a short exchange, never with restatement. Her speech is brief and whole sentences; Aeries's is long.

**Dialogue format check.** Grep `^\*"[^"]*"\*$` for bare-quote paragraphs and give every speaker change a tag or a beat. A two-person run needs a beat or tag on each turn.

**Counting after edits.** Every edit shifts all later markers. Re-probe each marker offset after the last edit, then compute words = tokens minus 4 per marker, 1 per `---`, 6 per date line, 3 per `* * *`.

**Context breaks lose picks.** Write each Tier D code, the map decisions and the running Area counts to a scratch note the moment they are made (a break once lost every pick and they had to be redone from the catalog; it happened again in Chapter 007, where the summary said only "codes listed in the analysis"). Use the scratchpad directory named in the environment block. The Grep tool omits long lines, so a transcript or a ledger row is read with Read by line number. A returning person's code is on the side-index row; Grep it, never re-pick.

**Count each Area at once.** Hand estimates drift both ways: speech-light action Areas and a villain-viewpoint Area (all interior and one report) came out 20 to 35 percent short (1,016 words against a 1,650 floor), while a run of saves came out 15 percent long. Fill a short Area with registered conduct, room beats, sensory anchors and the spec's own owed beats (never restatement); trim a long one by whole sentences, never speech.

**Tier D codes.** Read the catalog table of each option (Appendix B tables in the synthesis skill) before picking, then match the code to what the prose already shows; put `[SIG]` on the conduct that is on the page; avoid `L24` options that need a realm for an unawakened keeper only where the link rule says so; Role is a trade word, never a rank (use ESCORT, GUARD, RIDER, USHER, PILOT, KEEPER); stratum is S1 for unawakened and S2 for Warrior and Senior.

**Chapter 009 lessons (battle Chapter, two POVs, last Area written after a context break).** (1) After a break, re-read the spec's block for the Area you are about to write and the register lines of every speaker in it before drafting: my first Area 08 glued several speakers into one paragraph, mis-ordered the Marshal's one question and used "storm-keeper" in the king's mouth; the rewrite fixed all three. (2) Dialogue: one speaker per paragraph with a tag or beat on each turn; then Grep `^\*"[^"]*"\*$`. (3) Aeries's register bars weather words even inside a title: have him say the queen's name; the Marshal keeps "storm-keeper". (4) "wound" (injury) and "wound" (wrapped cloth) are a stranger-pass trap: write "wrapped" for the cloth. Also "order" (command vs sequence), "flags" (stones vs banners), "rest/rested", "held": give each one job per Area. (5) Every named person who appears needs a level clause (the Court's steward and sentries, Tamsin Holt, the soldiers in the road Areas), not only the ones the spec table lists; add it in a clause and recount. (6) Count: recount from the last confirmed marker offsets and never subtract an edit that the last recount already contains (I double-counted four `very` cuts). (7) Order of cuts when an Area runs over its cap: scene-setting clauses and the Crossing first, a repeated-fact list second, speech last.

**Chapter 008 lessons (whole Chapter, six hero blocks).** (1) Stage logic slips survive three read passes: after the last edit, walk each scene as a stage manager (where is the jug, whose back is turned and so who can see whose face, who is seated and who has risen, which hand holds the cup). (2) A spec anchor can clash with a ledger trap (Aalia's "finger-width" is to be shown as a position, never a measure): put the measure once as a gap in the placement (chair arm a finger's width short of the table corner) and let the closing act close it; flag it in the hand-off. (3) Before using a name plus event from an earlier Chapter ("Kade read out the lower ford"), Grep the Chapter for place and date: the event was three weeks earlier in a different hall. (4) Registers: no clinical word in Valia's quoted lines (pulse, wound); a hero's narration keeps hers (grep quoted lines for them). (5) Do not echo a block's closing clause at the end of the next block of the same scene. (6) Late additions re-open every grep: "thumb" slipped into a new sentence after the clean banned-substring run; rerun the whole guard search after the final edit. (7) A Chapter-mode block that lands under the floor after cuts needs registered conduct (a residue gesture, a held hand, a beat of the other speaker), not restatement.
