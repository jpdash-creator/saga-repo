## Overview

Part 21 is the Starward Saga Canon's Civilization Law — the governing domain for what a civilization structurally *is*. Where Part 22 defines the factions that operate within and across civilizations, and Part 23 defines the relationships between those factions, Part 21 defines the civilizational architecture itself: the five measurable axes of civilizational capability, the single Composite Civilization Tier (CCT) synthesized from them, the twenty in-world Civilization Tier Designations (CTDs) that name those tiers in prose, the structural Types that cross all tiers, and the governed mechanics by which civilizations advance, interact, regress, and recover.

Ten foundational principles (Area 1) establish that a civilization is the structural expression of collective capability, not political claim; that five measurable axes — Cultivation, Technology, Energy, Location, and Synergy — define civilizational reality; that the Composite Civilization Tier is a structural synthesis rather than an average; that axis asymmetry is narratively significant; that Civilization Tier Designations are the canonical prose vocabulary while axis profiles remain governance references; that the Civilization Type taxonomy crosses all tiers; that civilization boundaries are defined by structural control rather than political claims; that advancement requires all five axes; that regression is governed law; and that the twenty Civilization Tier Designations are in-world terms.

From these foundations, Part 21 builds outward across 22 Areas: the five axes with their full level tables and the Part 21 vs. Part 25 axis-naming disambiguation (Area 2); the twenty Civilization Tier Designations, the CTD × CS%/TS% quick-reference band table, and the Nascent/Established/Apex phase system (Area 3); the CCT Synthesis Formula with its Axis Minimum Rule, worked example profiles, and assessment cadence (Area 4); the CTD minimum axis thresholds (Area 5); the six-type Civilization Type taxonomy (Area 6); the special classifications — Apex Civilizations, Remnant Civilizations, Nascent Civilizations, Fractured Civilizations, and Transcendent Entities (Area 7); the CTD-by-CTD civilizational profiles from Subsistence through the deep tiers (Area 8); axis advancement mechanisms, CCT advancement events, acceleration events, and regression events (Area 9); civilization interaction laws, including the Three-Tier Gap Rule, first-contact governance, and territorial contest (Area 10); cultivation as civilizational infrastructure and the Sacred Ground milestone (Area 11); the technology interface and the 7-of-14 rule (Area 12); regression classification, recovery trajectories, and irreversible regression conditions (Area 13); the five civilization narration standards N21-1 through N21-5 (Area 14); the Part 31 synergy interface and Part 31 Rule 2.1.3 deference to the CTDs (Area 15); the Part 9 technology growth interface (Area 16); the Part 3 cultivation power interface (Area 17); the Parts 13/14 location and distance interface with the LA-8 FTL gate (Area 18); the Parts 11/12 energy resources interface (Area 19); the civilization-versus-faction boundary with Part 22 (Area 20); the Author Quick Reference (Area 21); and the full cross-Part dependency table (Area 22).

## Area 1 — Foundational Principles

### P21-1 — Civilization Is the Structural Expression of Collective Capability

A civilization is not a political entity, a cultural tradition, or a military force. A civilization is the total structural expression of what a collective population has built, sustains, and can draw upon. Cultivation power, technology infrastructure, energy access, and the physical scale of territorial control together constitute the architecture of civilizational existence. A civilization exists to the degree that it functions — not to the degree that it claims.

### P21-2 — Five Axes Define Civilizational Reality

Every civilization possesses five measurable axes:
- **Cultivation Axis (CA):** The depth and distribution of cultivation power within the civilization's population.
- **Technology Axis (TA):** The sophistication, coverage, and sustainability of technology infrastructure.
- **Energy Axis (EA):** Access to, quality of, and command over energy resources — both technological and cultivation-derived.
- **Location Axis (LA):** The physical scale of territory controlled, expressed in cosmological terms (Part 13 × Part 14 joint governance).
- **Synergy Axis (SA):** The degree to which cultivation and technology have been deliberately integrated into unified systems.

No single axis defines a civilization. All five must be assessed.

### P21-3 — Composite Civilization Tier Is a Structural Synthesis

The Composite Civilization Tier (CCT) — expressed as CCT-1 through CCT-20 with Civilization Tier Designations (CTDs) — is not the average of the five axes. It is a structural synthesis: the tier at which a civilization can *sustain and coordinate* the full complexity of all five axes simultaneously. A civilization with CA-17 and TA-3 is not CCT-17 — its technology infrastructure cannot support the coordination complexity that CA-17 produces at civilizational scale. The synthesis formula (Area 4) defines how axis gaps constrain the CCT.

### P21-4 — Axis Asymmetry Is Narratively Significant

Civilizations are rarely balanced across all five axes. Axis asymmetry — where one or more axes are significantly higher or lower than the CCT — is one of the most important sources of civilizational character, conflict, and vulnerability. A civilization with LA-12 but TA-6 controls vast territory it cannot technologically administer. A civilization with EA-15 but SA-3 sits atop energy resources it has not learned to use through integration. These imbalances create the narrative tensions that drive stories.

### P21-5 — Civilization Tier Designation Is the Canonical Civilization Name Tier

When referring to a civilization's tier in narrative prose, the Civilization Tier Designation is used: "a Stellar civilization," "a Clusteral entity," "a Formative settlement." The five-axis profile is a governance reference, not a prose term. Authors use CTDs; governance systems use axis profiles.

### P21-6 — Civilization Type Taxonomy Crosses All Tiers

Civilizations are also categorized by Type — a classification that reflects the dominant axis composition and development philosophy, independent of tier. A CCT-7 (Stellar) civilization may be Cultivation-Sovereign Type or Technology-Sovereign Type or Balanced-Path Type. Types are not better or worse than each other — they are different structural architectures with different strengths, vulnerabilities, and narrative identities.

### P21-7 — Civilization Boundaries Are Defined by Structural Control, Not Political Claims

A civilization's Location Axis is determined by what territory it *structurally administers and defends* — not by what it claims to own. A civilization that claims a galaxy but cannot administer more than a star cluster is a Clusteral civilization by governance, not a Galactic entity. Political claims may exceed structural control; the CCT reflects structural reality.

### P21-8 — Advancement Requires All Five Axes

CCT advancement to a new Tier requires all five axes to have reached or surpassed the minimum threshold for that Tier (SA-0 and SA-1 excepted), with one allowance: CA, TA, EA or LA may lag its threshold by up to two levels (Rule 4.1.1). No civilization can achieve CCT-N status while CA, TA, EA or LA falls more than two levels below the minimum threshold for CCT-N. Extraordinary strength in one axis cannot substitute for structural deficiency in another.

### P21-9 — Regression Is Governed Law

Civilizations can and do regress. CCT regression occurs when the CCT synthesis (Area 4) gives a lower tier, including when CA, TA, EA or LA falls more than two levels below its minimum threshold for the current Tier. Regression events are governed by Part 9 (technology regression), Part 3 (cultivation decline), Part 11 (energy depletion), Part 13 (territorial loss), and Part 31 (synergy collapse). Regression follows specific recovery trajectories (Area 13).

### P21-10 — The Civilization Tier Designations Are In-World Terms

The twenty Civilization Tier Designations are the canonical in-universe terminology used by scholars, historians, diplomats, and cultivators across the Starward Saga universe to classify civilizations. A civilization's inhabitants may resist or dispute their assigned CTD; the designation is a structural assessment, not a political judgement.

> | **EA** | Energy Axis — civilization's CPM substance access tier | *(has no equivalent in Part 25)* |
> | **SA** | Synergy Axis — cultivation-technology integration depth | *(has no equivalent in Part 25)* |

> | **CA% / TA%** | *(not an axis level: CA% and TA% with a percent value are a hero's Cultivation Affinity % and Technology Affinity %, hero-ledger fields (Part 37) used in Parts 17 and 30; they never set a civilization's CA or TA axis level)* | *(does not exist in Part 25)* |

> **Critical collision:** Part 21 uses **CA** for Cultivation Axis. Part 25 uses **CA-C** for Civilization Axis. These are completely different concepts. Always include the -C suffix when referring to Part 25 axes to avoid ambiguity. A percent value changes the meaning: CA% and TA% (for example CA=70%, TA=75%) are a hero's Cultivation Affinity % and Technology Affinity %, never axis levels (see the CA% / TA% row above).

### 2.1 — Cultivation Axis (CA) — Levels 1–20

The Cultivation Axis measures the depth and population-level distribution of cultivation power within a civilization. It is not a measure of the single most powerful individual — it is a measure of what the civilization as a structure can field, sustain, and reproduce in cultivation capability.

**CA Assessment Criteria:**
- **Peak Realm Present:** The highest cultivation realm held by any known individual within the civilization at the time of assessment.
- **Population Distribution:** What fraction of the civilization's population actively cultivates at any level (the percentage in the Area 2.1 table).
- **Institutional Infrastructure:** Whether the civilization has schools, academies, technique libraries, and cultivation support systems that reproduce cultivation capability across generations.
- **CPM Access:** Whether the civilization has reliable access to CPM substances (Part 11) at grades supporting cultivation advancement.

| CA Level | Peak Realm Present | Population Distribution | Institutional Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| CA-1 | Mortal Tier 1–2 scattered individuals | Less than 1% cultivating at any level; no formal cultivation institutions | None — cultivation is informal tradition or accident |
| CA-2 | Mortal Tier 2 (Senior to Warrior) occasional practitioners | 1–5% cultivating; early cultivation families or clans | Oral tradition; no formal cultivation schools |
| CA-3 | Early Xiantian boundary individuals | 5–10% with some cultivation awareness; first formal sects emerging | Small sects; basic technique inheritance |
| CA-4 | Xiantian Grade reached by a known few | 10–20% attempting cultivation; organized sects functioning | Regional sects; Core-building techniques accessible |
| CA-5 | Xiantian Grade reached across a fraction | 20–35% cultivation-capable population; multiple sect generations of inheritance | National sect networks; Xiantian-grade curriculum |
| CA-6 | Core Formation reached by known masters | 30–40% active cultivation; inter-sect exchange networks | Multi-generational cultivation academies; Core Formation accessible |
| CA-7 | Core Formation reached by a recognized class | 40–55% active cultivation; major sect confederations | Major cultivation universities; Core Formation widely achievable |
| CA-8 | Early Divine Realm individuals present | 50–65% actively cultivating; high social prestige for cultivation | Extensive cultivation infrastructure; Divine Realm pathway documented |
| CA-9 | Divine Realm (early stages) accessible | 60–70% cultivation-capable; cultivation as societal organizing principle | Multi-world cultivation academies; Divine Realm reached by regular practitioners |
| CA-10 | Divine Realm broadly reached across elite | 70–80% cultivating at some level; civilization built around cultivation | Cluster-scale cultivation institutions; advanced Divine Realm techniques taught |
| CA-11 | High Divine Realm (Formation and above) | 75–85%; cultivation infrastructure embedded in all societal layers | Galaxy-scale cultivation academies; formation-level practitioners common |
| CA-12 | Sea Formation / Divine Sea Realm present | 80–88%; cultivation as fundamental civilizational resource | Divine Sea cultivation infrastructure; celestial-grade techniques |
| CA-13 | Divine Sea Realm represented in leadership | 82–90%; multi-world cultivation networks spanning cluster to quadrant | Sacred Ground formation capability; cultivation inheritance across star systems |
| CA-14 | Individuals beyond the Divine Sea Realm (not defined) known | 85–92%; civilization with a functional presence at that level | Quadrant-scale cultivation networks; institutions open to that level |
| CA-15 | Cultivation beyond the Divine Sea Realm (not defined) accessible to elites | 88–94%; cultivation infrastructure for that level present | Galactic-scale cultivation governance; paths to that level documented and maintained |
| CA-16 | Cultivation beyond the Divine Sea Realm (not defined) known | 90–96%; cultivation integration in all aspects including technology and governance | Universal-scale cultivation traditions; that level understood theoretically |

| CA-20 | Cultivation presence beyond the Divine Sea Realm (not defined) | 99%+; cultivation and existence are indistinguishable | Cultivation infrastructure at that level; cultivation governs physical reality |

CA-14 to CA-20 lie beyond the Divine Sea Realm, the top of the Part 3 realm ladder. Part 3 defines no realm there, so these rows are told apart by population share and institutional depth, not by a named realm.

### 2.3 — Energy Axis (EA) — Levels 1–20

The Energy Axis measures a civilization's command over energy resources across both technological and cultivation-derived energy forms. It is distinct from TA (which measures technology sophistication) and CA (which measures cultivation power). A civilization with exceptional CPM resources but primitive technology has a high EA and low TA; a civilization with advanced energy technology but no cultivation access has a high TA in Energy domain and a low CA — their EA is scored separately.

**EA Assessment Criteria:**
- **CPM Substance Access:** What grade of CPM (Part 11) the civilization has reliable, civilization-scale access to (not just individual practitioner access).
- **Technological Energy Budget:** The civilization's total energy budget per capita and total, in Part 8 technology-tier terms.
- **Energy Storage and Distribution:** Whether the civilization can store, transport, and distribute energy at civilization scale.
- **Energy Conversion Efficiency:** For civilizations with both CPM and technological energy, the efficiency of converting between them (Part 31 Area 9).

### 2.5 — Synergy Axis (SA) — Levels 0–10

The Synergy Axis measures the degree to which a civilization has deliberately integrated cultivation and technology into unified systems. It is a distinct axis from CA and TA — two civilizations with identical CA and TA profiles may have very different SA values depending on whether they have invested in integration. SA is governed by Part 31 (Cultivation-Technology Synergy Law) in terms of what integration produces; Part 21 governs SA as a civilization classification input.

**SA Assessment Criteria:**
- Integration Model achievable at civilization infrastructure level (Part 31, Area 5).
- Fraction of population operating in synergistic modes (Model A or higher).
- Existence and scale of integration institutions (academies, laboratories, guilds, government bodies).
- Cross-domain synergy outputs at civilization scale.

**Note:** SA-0 is valid — civilizations with zero cultivation or zero technology are SA-0 by definition. Pure-cultivation and pure-technology civilizations are not disadvantaged in CCT by SA-0; SA-0 is offset by higher CA or TA values in the synthesis formula.

| SA Level | Integration Depth | Part 31 Model Equivalent | Civilizational Expression |
|---|---|---|---|

| SA-2 | Coordination synergy | Early Model A | Cultivators applying physical/affinity capabilities to technology construction; CPM used as supplementary energy |
| SA-3 | Basic augmentation | Model A threshold | Power Crystal as CPM fuel; soul-sense research applications; early CI Technology |

| SA-6 | Operational Model B | Model B fully operational | Shard CPM interface; soul-sense research at planetary scale; formation array + technology hybrid infrastructure |
| SA-7 | Model B–C transition | Model B–C | Emerging symbiotic standard; Relic Class 3 prototypes; stellar-scale integration networks |

## Area 3 — The Composite Civilization Tier (CCT) — Civilization Tier Designations

### 3.1 — The Twenty Civilization Tier Designations

The twenty Civilization Tier Designations (CTDs) are the canonical tier names in the Starward Saga universe. Each CTD is a structural descriptor — it tells the reader what kind of civilizational architecture the entity has built and commands, expressed in terms derived from the Kardashev classification tradition extended across the full cosmic scale of the Starward Saga universe. CTDs are scientific in origin: they derive from the civilization's measurable energy command, territorial extent, and structural complexity as classified by universal scholars.

| CCT | Designation | Short Form | Structural Identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCT-1 | **Subsistence** | Subsist | A pre-institutional civilization — a tribe, a clan, a single settlement. No formal governance, no organized cultivation institutions, no systematic technology. Energy at subsistence scale. The raw beginning of civilizational form. |
| CCT-2 | **Formative** | Formative | A civilization taking its first organized shape — city-states, early kingdoms, the first formal cultivation sects and technology guilds. The formative phase of structural emergence. |
| CCT-3 | **Industrial** | Industrial | The civilization has crossed into systematic production. Nation-states, industrial infrastructure, institutional cultivation academies, standardized knowledge transmission. The first large-scale organizational architecture. |
| CCT-4 | **Planetary (Type I)** | Planetary | A true Type I civilization by the Kardashev standard: full planetary energy command, planetary-scale governance, and civilization-wide cultivation and technology infrastructure. The first complete civilizational tier. |
| CCT-5 | **Multi-World (Type I+)** | Multi-World | Ascending beyond planetary scale — multi-world presence, interplanetary governance, early Sacred Ground pursuit as institutional goal. Type I civilization extending its reach across a star system's inner worlds. |
| CCT-6 | **Solarian (Type II−)** | Solarian | The civilization has reached into the void between worlds and taken command of the inner stellar environment. Orbital infrastructure, asteroid exploitation, partial stellar energy capture — a civilization approaching Type II status. Named for the solar scale of energy command it approaches. |
| CCT-7 | **Stellar (Type II)** | Stellar | A true Type II civilization: full stellar system administration, Dyson-scale energy capture, cultivation and technology at deep synergy threshold. The civilization's identity is stellar — it holds and manages its star. |
| CCT-8 | **Interstellar (Type II+)** | Interstellar | The interstellar age has begun. Early interstellar reach achieved (Part 8 Tier 8; Rule 18.2.1); first neighboring star systems administered. The civilization is no longer bound to a single star. Its identity expands with each new stellar system claimed. |
| CCT-9 | **Expansionary** | Expansionary | The civilization is flooding across the interstellar void — 6 to 50 star systems administered, federated or distributed authority emerging. Multi-stellar governance challenges define this tier. |

### 3.3 — CTD Phases: Nascent, Established, and Apex

Each Civilization Tier Designation contains three internal developmental phases. These phases are not separate CCT tiers — they are structural positions *within* a given CCT level. They provide narrative granularity between CCT advances: a civilization at the same nominal tier as another may be significantly further along in its structural development without yet reaching the next tier.

**The Three Phases:**
- **Nascent Phase:** The civilization's final value (Area 4.2) sits low in this CCT's band, below the middle of it. Its institutions are young. Fragile; vulnerable to regression. Narratively: a civilization on the edge of its new tier, not yet comfortable in it.
- **Established Phase:** The civilization has stabilized at this CCT level. Its final value (Area 4.2) sits near the middle of the band. Institutions are mature and reproducing. Narratively: the "typical" expression of a tier — what scholars mean when they describe a civilization by its CTD.
- **Apex Phase:** The civilization's final value (Area 4.2) sits high in this CCT's band — close to the next tier but not yet rounding up to it. Narratively: a civilization on the edge of advancement, powerful relative to its tier peers, often perceived by lower-tier civilizations as already in the next classification.

**Phase Notation:**
| Phase | In-World Scholar Notation | Prose Term |
|---|---|---|
| Nascent | CCT-N (Nascent) / N-CCT-N | "early [Designation]" / "nascent [Designation]" |
| Established | CCT-N (Established) / E-CCT-N | "[Designation]" (default usage; no qualifier needed) |
| Apex | CCT-N (Apex) / A-CCT-N | "deep [Designation]" / "apex [Designation]" / "near-[next Designation]" |

**Phase Words and Class Words:** A phase word (Nascent, Established, Apex) always comes before a designation, as in "Apex Solarian" or "Nascent Stellar". The class words stand alone: "Apex Civilization" means CCT-15 or above only (Area 7.1), and "Nascent Civilization" means CCT-1 or CCT-2 only (Area 7.3). An Apex Solarian civilization is a CCT-6 civilization in its Apex phase and is not an Apex Civilization.

**Phase Examples:**
- A CCT-7 (Nascent) Stellar civilization has just achieved stellar system administration but is still consolidating governance and infrastructure.
- A CCT-7 (Established) Stellar civilization is a fully functioning stellar civilization — the canonical Stellar tier.
- A CCT-7 (Apex) Stellar civilization has a final value near the top of the CCT-7 band and is structurally approaching CCT-8 — first interstellar probes are already reaching neighboring Star Systems; FTL research is near completion.

**Phase Assessment Rule:** The phase follows the final value (Base minus penalty, Area 4.2) for tier N:
- **Nascent:** N−0.49 to N−0.17.
- **Established:** N−0.16 to N+0.16.
- **Apex:** N+0.17 to N+0.50.

A final value up to N+0.50 rounds to N (a half rounds down, Area 4.2 Step 4), so these three bands cover tier N from edge to edge. A final value of 6.8 is CCT-7 (Nascent), 7.0 is CCT-7 (Established) and 7.3 is CCT-7 (Apex).

**Phase and Regression:** Regression from Established to Nascent phase within the same CCT is not a tier regression event — it is an intra-tier weakening. Regression from Nascent phase more than two levels below the minimum threshold (CA, TA, EA, LA; Rule 4.1.1) triggers a full CCT regression.

## Area 4 — CCT Synthesis Formula

### 4.1 — The Axis Minimum Rule

The Composite Civilization Tier is bounded from above by the lowest single axis value, subject to a tolerance:

**Rule 4.1.1 — Hard Floor:** No CCT can exceed the lowest value among CA, TA, EA and LA by more than 2 tiers (SA runs on a shorter scale and is handled by the formula and by Rule 4.1.3). If a civilization's lowest axis is LA-3, its maximum achievable CCT is CCT-5 regardless of all other axis values.

**Rule 4.1.2 — Soft Pull:** If two or more of CA, TA, EA and LA are at the minimum value (tied for lowest), the CCT cannot exceed the lowest by more than 1 tier.

**Rule 4.1.3 — SA Special Case:** SA-0 does not hard-cap the CCT. A civilization with SA-0 (pure cultivation or pure technology) may still achieve high CCT, but with a CCT cap of [average of the four non-SA axes], rounded down. SA-0 civilizations cannot exceed CCT-14 under any circumstances — at extreme cosmic scales, the inability to integrate becomes a structural limitation. An SA-0 or SA-1 civilization is not held to the Min SA column of Area 5 or to the SA part of Area 9.2 item 1; for an SA-0 civilization the CCT-14 cap is its only limit from SA.

### 4.2 — The Synthesis Formula

Let the five axis values be: CA, TA, EA, LA, SA (SA runs 0–10, so it is doubled to sit on the same scale as the other axes; SA counts as zero if SA-0).

**Step 1 — Weighted Average:**

> Base = (CA × 0.25) + (TA × 0.25) + (EA × 0.20) + (LA × 0.20) + (2 × SA × 0.10)

**Step 2 — Axis Gap Penalty:**

Identify the range (highest minus lowest of CA, TA, EA and LA; SA is not part of the range). Apply the following penalty to the Base:
- Range 0–2: No penalty.
- Range 3–4: −0.5 to Base.
- Range 5–6: −1.0 to Base.
- Range 7–9: −2.0 to Base.
- Range 10+: −3.0 to Base.

**Step 3 — Synthesis Floor Check:**

The final CCT = Base − Penalty, subject to Rule 4.1.1 (hard floor cap), Rule 4.1.2 (soft pull), Rule 4.1.3 (SA special case) and the Min SA column of Area 5.

**Step 4 — Rounding and Banding:**

Final CCT is rounded to the nearest integer, with a half rounding down. CCT is always expressed as a whole number from 1 to 20.

### 4.3 — Example Synthesis Profiles

| Example Civilization | CA | TA | EA | LA | SA | Base | Penalty | CCT | Designation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pure-cultivation, early-industrial | 9 | 2 | 6 | 4 | 0 | 4.75 (SA counts as zero) | −2.0 (range 7) | 3 | Industrial |
| Balanced interstellar civilization | 8 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 6 | 8.0 | 0 (range 1) | 8 | Interstellar |
| Technology-dominant, cultivation-poor civilization | 4 | 11 | 10 | 10 | 3 | 8.35 | −2.0 (range 7) | 6 | Solarian |
| Full-synergy expansionary civilization | 9 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 9.1 | 0 (range 2) | 9 | Expansionary |
| Galaxy-spanning balanced tier | 12 | 11 | 11 | 12 | 9 | 12.15 | 0 (range 1) | 12 | Intergalactic |

### 4.4 — CCT Assessment Cadence

CCT is not a fixed value — civilizations change. For narrative purposes:

**Rule 4.4.1 — Assessment Trigger Events:** CCT re-assessment is triggered by any of: major war outcome, first contact with a new civilization, Sacred Ground formation or destruction, technology tier breakthrough, mass cultivation advancement event, territorial conquest or loss exceeding a quarter of the territory counted at the current LA level.

**Rule 4.4.2 — CCT Lag:** Civilizational tier change does not happen overnight. A CCT advancement requires the new axis threshold to have been sustained for no less than two generations (Part 9 timing referenced). An individual breakthrough does not advance the civilization's CA — the distribution must change.

**Rule 4.4.3 — Author Reference Use:** For narrative purposes, authors assign CCT at the time of first depiction and note any significant axis changes within a book's timeline.

## Area 6 — Civilization Type Taxonomy

### 6.1 — Overview

Every civilization has a CCT Designation (tier) and a Civilization Type (structural profile). Types are not value judgements — they are structural descriptions of how a civilization distributes its axis investments and what civilizational philosophy it embodies. Types can change over time; some CCT-level transitions come with characteristic type shifts.

Types are named using the structure: **[Dominant Axis]-[Character]**.

**Order of Types:** Where several Types fit, record the first in this order: CP, ES, LS, CS, TS, BP. Compare SA doubled (Area 4.2).

### 6.2 — Cultivation-Sovereign Type (CS-Type)

**Definition:** CA is the civilization's leading axis, typically 2+ levels above TA. Technology exists but serves cultivation. Institutions are organized around cultivation advancement as the primary social value.

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional cultivation power density; peak cultivators may significantly outclass equivalently-tiered technology civilizations
- Sacred Ground formation capability at comparatively lower CCT levels
- CPM resource access; natural Divine Liquid sites often within territory
- Soul-sense observation, memory, and knowledge preservation advantages

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Technology infrastructure gaps create administrative and logistical vulnerabilities at large territorial scales
- Cultivation reproduction bottlenecks: talent gates limit CA distribution at population scale
- Economic infrastructure often underdeveloped relative to cultivation infrastructure
- Susceptible to technology-based warfare from equivalently-tiered or lower-tiered TA-dominant civilizations

**Characteristic Tiers:** CS-Type is most common at Solarian through Galactic for cultivation-rich regions. Above Galactic, SA requirements force CS-Type civilizations to develop TA or stagnate.

**Example SA Profile:** CS-Type at Stellar (CCT-7) typically has SA-4 to SA-5 — early coordination, CPM as fuel, but not deep integration.

---

### 6.3 — Technology-Sovereign Type (TS-Type)

**Definition:** TA is the civilization's leading axis, typically 2+ levels above CA. Cultivation exists but is a minority practice — perhaps even a cultural curiosity. Institutions are organized around technological advancement and scientific discovery.

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional technology infrastructure density; can sustain more technology domains at peak performance
- Industrial, information, and military technology outputs exceed equivalently-tiered cultivation civilizations
- Large population capacity — TA-dominant civilizations often have significantly larger populations
- Energy conversion efficiency at technological energy scale is unmatched

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Zero or minimal CI Technology — SA-0 or SA-1 by definition; locked out of synergy categories
- Cannot access CPM-based energy, soul-sense research advantages, or Sacred Ground environments
- Absolute upper CCT limit of CCT-14 (SA-0 cap rule, Area 4.1.3) unless cultivation is adopted
- Vulnerable to soul-sense intelligence and perception advantages of CS-Type civilizations at equivalent tier

**Characteristic Tiers:** TS-Type appears across all CCT levels. Many civilizations that have never encountered cultivation remain TS-Type indefinitely. Some TS-Type civilizations transition to Balanced-Path once they achieve first contact with cultivation knowledge.

---

### 6.4 — Balanced-Path Type (BP-Type)

**Definition:** CA and TA are within 2 levels of each other. Neither axis dominates. SA is actively invested in. The civilization pursues cultivation and technology as co-equal development paths and integrates them systematically.

**Strengths:**
- Full access to synergy categories at the highest SA levels; Model D achievable
- No single axis vulnerability — no large gaps exposed
- Economic and institutional flexibility: can leverage both tracks as circumstances demand
- The Starward Saga's primary civilizational model; most protagonists emerge from BP-Type civilizations

**Vulnerabilities:**
- No axis dominance means no axis peak: a BP-Type civilization at CCT-10 has CA-10 and TA-10, while a CS-Type at CCT-10 may have CA-13 with TA-8 — individual cultivation power peaks lower
- Integration investment is expensive: SA development requires simultaneous dual-track infrastructure
- Cultural tension: the coexistence of cultivation and technology values creates ongoing social conflict (Part 31 Area 15.4)

---

### 6.5 — Energy-Sovereign Type (ES-Type)

**Definition:** EA is the civilization's leading axis by 3+ levels. The civilization commands extraordinary energy resources — either through massive CPM deposits, advanced energy technology, or both — while other axes lag behind.

**Strengths:**
- Energy exports as the primary diplomatic and economic lever
- Formation Zone territories of exceptional density; Divine Liquid formation at comparatively low CA
- Technology development in Energy domain (Part 8 Domain 7) at peak levels; energy technology exports

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Resource-dependency — other civilizations covet the energy sources; military vulnerability
- TA and CA may be underdeveloped; energy wealth creates complacency
- Energy surplus does not automatically translate to CCT advancement without corresponding CA/TA/LA growth

**Characteristic Tiers:** ES-Type often appears as a transitional type — civilizations with exceptional resource territories briefly show ES-Type characteristics before developing other axes to match.

---

### 6.6 — Territorial-Sovereign Type (LS-Type)

**Definition:** LA significantly exceeds other axes by 2+ levels. The civilization controls vast territory relative to its cultivation/technology depth — perhaps through conquest, expansion, or inheritance from a greater predecessor civilization.

**Strengths:**
- Strategic depth — vast territory makes total conquest difficult
- Resource access across wide territory; potential energy and cultivation sites within large domain
- Political prestige of territorial scale

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Administrative overreach — LA well above CA and TA means much of the territory cannot be effectively governed
- Regression risk — held territory degrades when infrastructure cannot sustain it
- CCT hard-cap applies: LA-8 with CA-4, TA-5 means CCT is capped at CCT-6 regardless of territorial claims

**Characteristic Tiers:** LS-Type frequently appears post-conquest or in declining civilizations. A civilization that was once Balanced at CCT-8 may become LS-Type at CCT-5 as CA and TA decline while LA is maintained through political inertia.

---

### 6.7 — Convergence-Path Type (CP-Type)

**Definition:** All five axes are within 1 level of each other. Perfect or near-perfect axis balance. The civilization has invested deliberately in uniform development across all dimensions.

**Strengths:**
- Maximum CCT efficiency — no axis gap penalty; CCT equals the weighted average of axes
- Full synergy access; maximum resilience to single-axis degradation events
- Extremely rare; Convergence-Path civilizations at high CCT levels are among the most formidable entities in the universe

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Achieving axis uniformity is extraordinarily resource-intensive; most civilizations cannot sustain it
- No specialist axis advantages; a Convergence-Path CCT-9 cannot match the peak cultivation power of a CS-Type CCT-9

---

## Area 7 — Special Classifications

### 7.1 — Apex Civilizations

An Apex Civilization is a CCT-15 (Liminal-Universal) or above entity. At this scale, civilizations begin to function as environmental forces rather than political entities. Their existence shapes the physics, cultivation conditions, and cultural landscape of vast regions.

**Rule 7.1.1 — Apex Narrative Governance:** Apex Civilizations (CCT-15+) are not standard antagonists, allies, or faction actors in narrative. Their involvement in story events requires Part 28 authorization. Their existence is background cosmic reality, not a direct narrative participant in standard story arcs.

**Rule 7.1.2 — Apex and Absolute Chaos:** At CCT-19 (Dimensional) and CCT-20 (Absolute), civilizational existence begins to touch the boundaries of Absolute Chaos (Part 13 P13-10). Their governance of territory is not entirely describable in standard location classification terms.

### 7.2 — Remnant Civilizations

A Remnant Civilization is one that was once a higher CCT but has experienced sustained regression across multiple axes. Remnant Civilizations are not simply low-tier — they retain cultural memories, infrastructure ruins, and occasional high-axis individuals that do not match their current CCT.

**Rule 7.2.1 — Remnant Narrative Value:** Remnant Civilizations are among the most narratively rich entities in the Starward Saga. Their decayed infrastructure contains knowledge, relics, and cultivation techniques beyond what their current CCT would suggest. Ruins Worlds (Part 13 Area 15.1) are typically the remnants of TS-Type or CS-Type high-tier civilizations.

**Rule 7.2.2 — Remnant Assessment:** A Remnant Civilization's CCT is assessed on its current structural function, not its historical peak. However, narrative notes may indicate "Remnant of CCT-[X]" or "former [Designation] civilization" to signal the historical depth.

### 7.3 — Nascent Civilizations

A Nascent Civilization is CCT-1 (Subsistence) or CCT-2 (Formative) with clear structural development momentum. Nascent Civilizations are typically recently founded or first-generation organized societies.

### 7.4 — Fractured Civilizations

A Fractured Civilization is one where significant internal conflict has created competing CCT assessments — where one faction may be Stellar-tier and another may be Industrial-tier, with no unified governance structure. Fractured Civilizations are assessed by their dominant faction's CCT.

**Rule 7.4.1 — Dominant Faction and Fractured Record:** The dominant faction is the one administering the greatest LA; ties go to the higher CA. Record the Fractured state in the civilization ledger; it ends when one faction holds at least twice the next faction's LA or governance is unified.

### 7.5 — Transcendent Entities

At CCT-18 (Multiversal) and above, some entities cease to function as "civilizations" in any recognizable sense. They become Transcendent Entities — governance structures that operate more like physical laws than political organizations. Part 28 authorization required for narrative depiction of Transcendent Entities as direct story participants.

## Area 8 — CTD-by-CTD Civilizational Profiles

Each Civilization Tier Designation has a detailed civilizational profile — the characteristic social structure, narrative texture, and world-building identity that that tier represents.

**Profile Level:** The profiles below describe a civilization in the Apex phase of its tier (Area 3.3), whose Cultivation Axis and Energy Axis sit above the Area 5 minimum for that tier. For a civilization at the minimum, take the peak realm and the CPM access from the Area 2.1 and Area 2.3 rows for its own CA and EA levels.

---

### CCT-1 — Subsistence

**Social Structure:** Tribal; elder-based; kin-network governance. Population ranges from dozens to low thousands. Every individual is known to every other. Cultivation is instinctual or inherited from elders — no formal training structures. Technology is hand-crafted, pre-industrial, seasonally dependent.

**Narrative Texture:** The world is immediate, personal, and dangerous. The horizon is a mystery. Cultivation power is magic to non-practitioners. Technology is hand-tool scale. Authority is personal reputation and elder status.

**Cultivation Character:** Scattered individuals may have achieved Mortal Tier 1 Soul stage to Mortal Tier 2 Senior; no formal cultivation inheritance. CPM is unknown or treated as sacred found-objects.

**Technology Character:** Stone tools through early bronze/iron equivalents (Tier 1). No systematic knowledge preservation.

**Energy Character:** Fire, wind, water, animal labor. CPM unknown.

---

### CCT-2 — Formative

**Social Structure:** City-states, early kingdoms, organized religion as governance. Formal law beginning. Trade networks between settlements. Population in low millions. Cultivation begins its first institutional expression: small sects, cultivation families.

**Narrative Texture:** The world is growing — cities rise, roads connect settlements, trade brings strangers into contact. Cultivation practitioners are local legends. Technology is increasingly systematic. The first cultivation academies are appearing.

**Cultivation Character:** Mortal Tier 2 (Senior to Champion) practitioners known; Xiantian rare and legendary. First formal sects transmitting cultivation techniques.

**Technology Character:** City-scale equivalents (Tier 2). Written records. Early metallurgy mastered.

**Energy Character:** Early CPM (Power Crystal, Grades 1–4) known as rare resources, possibly not yet understood as cultivation fuel.

### CCT-3 — Industrial

**Social Structure:** Nation-states, empires, established governance hierarchies. Bureaucracy functions. Inter-state conflict and diplomacy at scale. Population in hundreds of millions. Cultivation sects are major social institutions — comparable in influence to religious bodies.

**Narrative Texture:** The world is industrial. Cities have millions. Information moves faster than armies. Cultivation sects compete, form alliances, and influence politics. Technology is disrupting traditional power structures. The first significant CA/TA integration experiments are occurring.

**Cultivation Character:** Xiantian Grade practitioners are common among cultivation families; Core Formation rare but known. Cultivation academies teach structured techniques.

### CCT-4 — Planetary (Type I)

**Social Structure:** Global or continental governance. The first planetary-scale institutions. International law beginning. Cultivation and technology are both established as societal pillars; neither has defeated the other. Population in billions.

**Narrative Texture:** The world is both vast and connected. Space travel is beginning. The planet feels smaller. Cultivation sects operate globally and compete for planetary-scale territories. Technology threatens traditional cultivation power structures — a Tier 4 army can threaten a Core Formation cultivator under the right conditions.

**Cultivation Character:** Core Formation accessible to dedicated practitioners; Divine Realm legendary. Large-scale cultivation academies with multi-generational inheritance.

### CCT-5 — Multi-World (Type I+)

**Social Structure:** Multi-world governance frameworks. The first interplanetary administration. Population spans multiple worlds. Cultivation sects establish satellite academies on new worlds. Technology companies and cultivation sects begin the first systematic partnerships.

**Cultivation Character:** Core Formation widely known; early Divine Realm presence. Planet-scale cultivation academies with interplanetary chapters.

**Technology Character:** Interplanetary transportation, early interstellar probes (Tier 5–6). Automation, life extension beginning.

### CCT-6 — Solarian (Type II−)

**Social Structure:** Inner stellar system governance. Administration of orbital habitats, asteroid belts, inner planets. The void between worlds is routine transit, no longer a frontier. Population of hundreds of billions across inner system.

**Narrative Texture:** Space is a texture of daily life — not a frontier. Sacred Grounds are being approximated (technology-enhanced conditions, Part 31 Area 10.2) and competed over across the inner system. Technology-relic hybrid devices are standard equipment for elite practitioners and high-tier military units.

**Cultivation Character:** Divine Realm practitioners represent an accessible elite. Divine Sea Realm known but rare. Formation-level techniques being pioneered.

**Technology Character:** Inner solar system complete mastery (Tier 6–7). Megastructures beginning. Partial stellar energy capture.

**Energy Character:** Gem CPM civilization-wide; Shard CPM accessible at major faction level.

### CCT-7 — Stellar (Type II)

**Social Structure:** Full stellar system governance. Outer system reaches administered. The civilization is recognizably unified at stellar scale — a single civilization-entity with stellar-scale identity. Cultivation and technology have achieved deep mutual investment; Integrated Practitioners (Part 31, Area 12) are a recognized social class.

**Narrative Texture:** The sun is an owned thing — captured, managed, optimally output. The outer reaches of the stellar system are mapped and governed. Sacred Grounds are approximated on multiple worlds and orbital platforms; natural ones are rare.

**Cultivation Character:** Divine Realm broadly accessible to cultivation-focused practitioners. Early Divine Sea Realm practitioners known. Formation-level cultivation taught at major academies.

**Technology Character:** Full stellar system mastery; Dyson-scale energy capture (Tier 7–8). Megastructures operational.

**Social Structure:** First interstellar reach. The civilization has extended its governance to at least one neighbor star system. The awareness that the universe contains other civilizations is now a lived reality, not a philosophical hypothesis. Diplomatic or military first contact protocols are active.

**Narrative Texture:** The civilization is waking to the scale of the universe. Early interstellar travel is achievable (Tier 8: near-lightspeed travel or a first FTL prototype; standard FTL at Tier 9, per Part 8 and the Part 10 FTL Framework). Communication lag to the new star system is real and politically significant. The expansion frontier is alive with conflict, discovery, and opportunity. This is the dawn of interstellar identity.

**Cultivation Character:** Divine Sea Realm practitioners present and institutionally important. High Divine Realm techniques in active development.

The central authority is distant — weeks or months of FTL travel away. Frontier star systems develop independent identities. The civilization is in the process of becoming vast.

The civilization's total identity is a complex mosaic of sub-civilizations.

### CCT-12 through CCT-20 — Deep Tiers

Tiers 12 through 14 are world-building background; Tiers 15 through 20 represent civilizational scales that exceed what standard narrative can depict without Part 28 authorization for direct participation. These tier levels are world-building context — the background of the universe within which the Starward Saga's stories occur.

**CCT-12 (Intergalactic):** Spans multiple galaxies in the Local Group. Governance architecture that can administer tens of galaxies simultaneously. CPM production at Divine Liquid scale civilization-wide.

**CCT-13 (Cosmological):** Galaxy cluster governance. A repository of civilizational history and diversity. The civilization has absorbed or subsumed multiple former Galactic-class entities.

**CCT-14 (Superclusteral):** Supercluster scale. The distances that once defined civilizational limits are now administrative corridors bridging what was once thought uncrossable.

**CCT-15 (Liminal-Universal):** The observable universe becomes the territory. CCT-15 civilizations stand at the threshold of cosmological governance. Part 21 P21-1 comes into full expression here — civilization is the structural expression of collective capability, and at CCT-15, that capability approaches cosmological scope.

**CCT-16 through CCT-20:** Successive transcension of universal (Type IV), trans-universal, multi-universal (Type V), dimensional, and absolute scales. At CCT-20 (Absolute), civilizational existence is indistinguishable from the substrate of reality. These tiers are named and described; they are not narratively accessible without Part 28 authorization.

## Area 9 — Civilization Advancement Mechanics

### 9.1 — Axis Advancement Mechanisms

Each axis advances through distinct mechanisms:

**CA Advancement (Cultivation Axis):**
- Breakthrough events by recognized cultivation figures (Part 3 breakthrough mechanics)
- Formation of new cultivation academies and cross-academy knowledge exchange
- Discovery of Sacred Grounds (Part 13 Area 9) within territorial control
- CPM resource discoveries enabling higher-grade cultivation support
- Inter-civilizational cultivation knowledge transfer

**TA Advancement (Technology Axis):**
- Governed entirely by Part 9 (Technology Growth Law) — systematic research, accidental discovery, crisis-driven innovation, cross-civilizational transfer, cultivation-assisted research
- Part 21 receives TA values from Part 9's outcome; Part 21 does not govern TA advancement mechanics

**EA Advancement (Energy Axis):**
- Discovery and extraction of higher-grade CPM deposits (Part 11)
- Advancement of Energy Technology domain (Part 8, Domain 7) per Part 9
- Sacred Ground formation within territory (Part 13) — drives EA upward by providing Divine Liquid formation zones
- CPM-technology conversion efficiency improvements (Part 31 Area 9)

**LA Advancement (Location Axis):**
- Territorial conquest or peaceful annexation with functional administrative takeover
- FTL transportation reach expanding to new star systems (Part 14 FTL mechanics, Part 10 science prerequisites)
- Colonial formation with administrative infrastructure reaching new cosmological levels

**SA Advancement (Synergy Axis):**
- CI Technology domain advancement (Part 8 Domain 12, Part 9 growth mechanics)
- Cultivation-technology integration institution formation (academies, research guilds, integration corps)
- Integration Model threshold crossings (Part 31, Area 5)
- Sacred Ground + technology infrastructure investment (Part 31, Area 10)

### 9.2 — CCT Advancement Events

CCT advancement (moving from one Designation to the next) is one of the most significant events in a civilization's history. It requires:

1. All five axis minimums for the target CTD are sustained (Area 5 table), allowing the two-level lag on CA, TA, EA and LA that Rule 4.1.1 permits.
2. The synthesis formula (Area 4.2) produces a result at or above the target CCT level.
3. The new axis state has been sustained for at least two generations (Rule 4.4.2).

**Narrative Significance:** CCT advancement is not announced or celebrated — it is recognized retroactively by historians and scholars. A civilization does not "become" Stellar on a specific date; it is *recognized as* a Stellar civilization by later analysis. This creates narrative opportunities where characters argue over whether their civilization has truly advanced or is merely claiming advancement prematurely.

### 9.3 — Acceleration Events

Certain events accelerate civilizational advancement:

- **Sacred Ground Formation:** Drives CA upward by establishing higher-ambient cultivation zones; drives SA upward through cultivation-technology synergy opportunities.
- **First Contact with Higher-Tier Civilization:** May accelerate TA, SA through technology transfer; may cause CCT disruption if the contact is destabilizing.
- **Mass Breakthrough Event:** A generation of extraordinary cultivators all advancing simultaneously — rare but historically documented.
- **CPM Vein Discovery:** Major new CPM deposits of higher grade than previously accessible drive EA upward.

### 9.4 — Regression Events and CCT Decline

CCT regression follows axis decline:

- **War and Conquest:** LA loss; potential CA loss from practitioner casualties; TA loss from infrastructure destruction.
- **Sacred Ground Loss:** EA and CA decline; SA may decline if integration architecture was Sacred Ground dependent.
- **Cultivation Suppression:** CA decline across generations; SA decline from integration disruption.
- **Technology Infrastructure Collapse (Part 9 regression mechanics):** TA decline; EA and SA follow.
- **CPM Depletion:** EA decline; SA decline from energy-dependent integration loss.

**Regression Rate:** CCT decline can occur faster than advancement. The asymmetry is governed: Part 9 regression mechanics govern TA decline timelines; cultivation decline follows Part 3 generational timelines; LA decline follows territorial loss rate.

## Area 10 — Civilization Interaction Laws

### 10.1 — Interaction Between Different CCT Levels

**Rule 10.1.1 — The Three-Tier Gap Rule:** When two civilizations are separated by 3 or more CCT levels, they do not typically interact as peers. The lower-tier civilization exists as a subject, client, curiosity, or invisible entity to the higher-tier civilization. Narrative depictions of cross-tier-gap relationships require Part 28 authorization when the gap is 5 or more CCT levels.

**Rule 10.1.2 — Technology Transfer Asymmetry:** Higher-tier civilizations can offer TA-advancing technology to lower-tier civilizations; lower-tier civilizations typically cannot offer corresponding value. This asymmetry is a primary driver of civilizational politics — what a lower-tier civilization trades for access to higher-tier technology.

**Rule 10.1.3 — Cultivation Exchange Principle:** CA-level cultivation knowledge can be transferred between civilizations of any CCT level. A CCT-3 cultivation tradition may contain cultivation techniques that are genuinely valuable to a CCT-9 civilization, particularly if the CCT-3 tradition has followed a unique cultivation path. Cultivation knowledge does not obey the same asymmetry as technology.

**Rule 10.1.4 — SA Isolation Principle:** A civilization's SA level is primarily a product of its own internal integration investment. SA cannot be meaningfully transferred between civilizations — the institutional architecture for integration must be built internally.

### 10.2 — First Contact Governance

**Rule 10.2.1 — First Contact Assessment:** Upon first contact between civilizations, Part 21 requires that a CCT assessment be performed (narratively) for both civilizations within the scope of the story event. The relative CCT levels determine the dynamic and appropriate narrative handling.

**Rule 10.2.2 — Contact Below Five-Tier Gap:** When two civilizations make first contact and their CCT difference is 4 or less (a gap of 3 or 4 is a client or subject relation, Rule 10.1.1), standard diplomatic interaction governance applies (Part 22 — Faction Law governs inter-civilizational faction dynamics at this scale).

> **Governance architecture note:** Part 22's inter-faction framework is the mechanics engine for civilization-to-civilization political relationships when the CCT gap is below 5. Part 23 (Affiliation Law) does NOT directly govern civilization-to-civilization relationships — it governs the faction-layer cascade effects when civilization-level political events force internal factions to make affiliation choices (Part 23 Area 20). The three-layer governance sequence is: (1) Part 21 Area 10 governs the civilization-level strategic dynamic; (2) Part 22 provides the interaction mechanics framework; (3) Part 23 Area 20 governs how internal factions must respond to those civilization-level events. Writers should not apply Part 23's AFF-1 through AFF-14 spectrum directly to civilization relationships — these codes apply at the faction layer. Civilization-level political postures are expressed using Part 21's CCT dynamics and Part 22's interaction framework.

**Rule 10.2.3 — Contact Above Five-Tier Gap:** When the CCT gap is 5 or more, the higher-tier civilization's interaction with the lower-tier civilization is governed as a civilization-environment event rather than a diplomatic event. The lower-tier civilization cannot meaningfully negotiate with the higher-tier entity as a peer.

### 10.3 — Territorial Contest Between Civilizations

**Rule 10.3.1 — Contest Legitimacy:** Territorial contest between civilizations at similar CCT levels (within 2 CCT levels) is legitimate civilizational conflict governed by Part 22 at the inter-civilization scale and Part 5 at the combat scale.

**Rule 10.3.2 — Imbalanced Contest:** A civilizational conflict between CCT levels separated by 3+ levels is not a "war" in any conventional sense. It is a subjugation event or extinction event.

**Rule 10.3.3 — CCT Effect of Territorial Loss:** LA reduction from territorial contest may trigger CCT recalculation (Rule 4.4.1 assessment trigger). If LA drops more than two levels below the minimum for the current CTD (Area 5), or the synthesis (Area 4.2) now gives a lower tier, CCT regression begins.

### 10.4 — The Absolute Authorization Protocol

**Rule 10.4.1 — When the Protocol Applies:** Deploying a weapon of Weapon Scale Class WS-VI (Planetary Class) or WS-VII (Existential Class) (Part 33 Area 1.2) needs the Absolute Authorization Protocol. A faction cannot run it: Part 33 Area 10.1 stops a faction at WS-V with civilian authority. The Protocol belongs to the civilization that fields the weapon.

**Rule 10.4.2 — The Council:** The Protocol is run by the civilization's Authorization Council. This is the body that holds the power over war and peace for the whole civilization under its own constitution (one of the governance archetypes of Part 22 Area 22.1: a single sovereign, a council oligarchy, a federated council, or the like). The Protocol creates no new body. Where the civilization governs on more than one track (Area 6), both tracks hold seats in it. The Council's in-world name, its seats and its place of meeting are OPEN-AREA for each civilization.

**Rule 10.4.3 — Quorum and Decision:** The Council is in quorum only when every seat is present in person, with no proxy, and it decides by the consent of every seat. One seat that withholds consent stops the deployment. A civilization whose constitution puts the power over war in one person has a Council of one; the Protocol then adds the declaration and the consequence below, not a second voice. The decision is declared aloud before the weapon is armed for release, in the F1 Ceremonial register (Part 29 Area 6.2), naming the weapon, the target and the seats that consented. A WS-VI release is legitimate only in an E6 or E7 conflict and a WS-VII release only in an E7 conflict (Part 33 Area 11.2; Part 5 Area 5).

**Rule 10.4.4 — The Consequence:** A deployment under the Protocol is graded under Part 27 Area 1.2 at CSS-8 or above for WS-VI and CSS-9 or above for WS-VII, in the Systemic domain with civilization-scale reach (Part 27 Area 5.2, IC-4 to IC-6). Authorization does not remove the consequence; it assigns it. The civilization carries it, and so does each seat that consented, by name (Part 27 Area 7). Where the target is inhabited, the Atrocity Consequence Cascade (Part 27 Area 14.3) applies in full.

## Area 11 — Civilization and Cultivation Interface

### 11.1 — Cultivation as Civilizational Infrastructure

Cultivation is not merely individual power in the Starward Saga — it is civilizational infrastructure. A civilization's CA value represents cultivators functioning as:

- **Administrative infrastructure:** Soul-sense-based communication, record-keeping, and surveillance exceeding early technology equivalents.
- **Economic infrastructure:** CPM production, refinement, and trade as economic substrate.
- **Military infrastructure:** Cultivators as force multipliers that scale non-linearly with technology military force.
- **Research infrastructure:** Soul-sense observation contributing to scientific prerequisites (Part 10 Area 7).
- **Spiritual infrastructure:** Cultivation as the cultural value system that motivates population behavior (Part 25 adjacent governance).

### 11.2 — The Sacred Ground Civilization Milestone

Sacred Ground formation (Part 13 Area 9) within a civilization's territory is one of the most significant CA and EA advancement events possible. It:

- Drives CA upward by providing ambient energy Class 7 cultivation environments that accelerate practitioner advancement.
- Drives EA upward by potentially providing Divine Liquid formation zones at S-Grade 5+.
- Drives SA upward by creating the ideal environment for technology-cultivation synergy experiments (Part 31 Area 10).
- Increases the civilization's status and attractiveness as a destination, driving population and practitioner immigration.

**Rule 11.2.1 — Sacred Ground Ownership:** The faction or civilization that administers a Sacred Ground gains the full CA, EA, and SA benefits. Contest over Sacred Ground administration is among the most common causes of inter-civilization conflict.

### 11.3 — Cultivation Realm Distribution and CA Assessment

CA is not assessed by the single peak cultivator — a civilization where one Divine Sea Realm cultivator exists among a billion non-cultivators is not CA-12. Distribution matters:

**Distribution Requirements for CA Assessment:**
- CA-8 and below: Peak realm is sufficient indicator if the civilization has functional institutional structures.
- CA-9 and above: Both peak realm AND institutional distribution (percentage of population cultivating, Area 2.1 table) must satisfy the criteria in Area 2.1.
- CA-13 and above: The civilization must have governance infrastructure specifically designed to identify, develop, and utilize high-realm cultivators at civilizational scale.

## Area 12 — Civilization and Technology Interface

### 12.1 — Technology as Civilizational Infrastructure

The TA value governs what Part 8 infrastructure is present. Part 21 does not re-govern technology capability — it receives TA from Part 8 and Part 9. What Part 21 adds:

**Rule 12.1.1 — Technology Tier Governance Deference:** All technology tier capability definitions, advancement prerequisites, and regression mechanics are governed by Part 8 and Part 9. Part 21 uses TA as an input.

**Rule 12.1.2 — Technology and Civilization Scale:** The civilization's TA gates what territory is practically administrable (contributing to the LA assessment). A civilization with TA-4 (digital global) cannot practically administer a stellar system (LA-6 territory) — administrative infrastructure at that scale requires TA-6 to administer without overreach; below that the excess is LS-Type overreach (Area 6.6) and counts toward LA only where it is defended.

**Rule 12.1.3 — Technology Ceiling Persistence:** Part 8's Technology Ceiling (P8-2) applies within Part 21's framework. No TA axis value above the civilization's sustainable ceiling is counted.

### 12.2 — The 7-of-14 Rule in Part 21

Part 21 adopts Part 8's 7-of-14 majority domain rule for TA assessment. A civilization's TA = Part 8 majority-domain technology tier. This is the standard unless Part 8 is revised.

## Area 13 — Civilization Regression and Recovery

### 13.1 — Regression Classification

Civilizations in regression are classified by their regression type:

| Regression Type | Definition | Narrative Character |
|---|---|---|
| Axis Regression | One axis declining while others remain stable | The civilization is losing capability in a specific domain; not yet CCT-declining |
| CCT Regression | The synthesis formula now produces a lower CCT | The civilization has crossed a tier threshold downward; visible structural change |
| Cascade Regression | Multiple axes declining simultaneously | Crisis state; CCT declining rapidly; civilization in existential risk |
| Terminal Regression | All axes declining with no recovery mechanisms | Civilization ending; may produce Remnant entities or complete dissolution |

### 13.2 — Recovery Trajectories

Recovery from regression follows Part 9 re-advancement mechanics for TA. CA recovery follows cultivation generational timelines:

- CA recovery from 1 level below: 2–4 generations of institutional investment.
- CA recovery from 2 levels below: 4–8 generations of institutional investment.
- CA recovery from 3+ levels below: Potentially irreversible without external cultivation knowledge transfer or Sacred Ground access.

LA recovery: Requires either political re-annexation (governance re-establishment) or military reconquest with viable administrative infrastructure.

EA recovery: Follows CPM resource rediscovery or new formation zone access. If energy infrastructure technology is intact, EA can recover faster than CA.

SA recovery: Requires rebuilding integration institutions after collapse; typically slower than TA or EA recovery due to the institutional depth required.

### 13.3 — Irreversible Regression Conditions

**Rule 13.3.1 — Cultivation Knowledge Loss:** If cultivation academies and technique libraries are destroyed and no practitioners above a certain realm threshold survive, CA may face irreversible regression below the level where re-establishment of institutional cultivation infrastructure is possible. This is one of the most catastrophic forms of civilizational regression.

**Rule 13.3.2 — Sacred Ground Destruction:** Part 13 governs Sacred Ground collapse; the effect on Part 21 is permanent EA and CA loss at the level of the destroyed Sacred Ground's contribution.

**Rule 13.3.3 — Technology Regression:** Per Part 9 Area 8, complete loss of institutional knowledge in 7+ technology domains drives TA below recovery threshold — "civilizational dark age" conditions.

## Area 14 — Civilization Narration Standards

### N21-1 — CTD Primacy in Prose

All references to a civilization's tier in narrative prose use Civilization Tier Designations, not axis values or interim notation:
- **Correct:** "The Stellar civilization of the Veil Cluster..."
- **Correct:** "An Interstellar-tier empire that had recently crossed the void..."
- **Correct:** "A Clusteral power — the greatest the northern quadrant had ever seen."
- **Incorrect:** "The CCT-7 civilization..."
- **Incorrect:** "A CA-8, TA-7 civilization..."

Axis values (CA, TA, EA, LA, SA) appear in world-building governance documents, academic references within the narrative, and canon reference materials — not in narrative prose.

### N21-2 — Type Designation in Prose

Civilization Types may appear in prose as background flavor:
- **Correct:** "A Cultivation-Sovereign Stellar civilization" / "a CS-Type Stellar entity"
- **Correct:** "a Balanced-Path civilization" / "they had pursued the Balanced-Path since the great unification"
- Types are not mandatory in narrative prose — they are available color, not required identification.

### N21-3 — Cross-Book Consistency

A civilization's CTD must be consistent across books unless a CCT advancement or regression event has been narratively depicted and authorized. Authors must flag any tier-level change to ensure canon consistency.

### N21-4 — Avoiding False Precision

Authors should not attempt to derive exact axis values from prose descriptions. CCT and Civilization Tier Designation are the canonical precision level for in-story civilizational assessment. Axis values are governance tools, not narrative tools.

### N21-5 — The Tier Gap and Narrative Realism

When depicting interaction between civilizations of significantly different CCT levels, the narrative must reflect the power asymmetry. A CCT-4 (Planetary) civilization interacting with a CCT-9 (Expansionary) civilization cannot be depicted as diplomatic equals. The tier gap creates a realistic power relationship that Part 28 is required to override.

## Area 18 — Part 21 and Location / Distance (Parts 13, 14 Interface)

### 18.1 — LA Axis and Parts 13, 14

The LA axis is calibrated against Part 13's cosmological hierarchy and Part 14's CMS distance scale:
- LA-5 (single World) = Part 13 World (W-class) territory; Gm to AU scale (Part 14)
- LA-7 (full Star System) = Part 13 Star System (SS-6 to SS-10); light-minute to light-hour scale (Part 14)
- LA-10 (Star Cluster) = Part 13 Star Cluster (SC-class); light-year to kly scale (Part 14)

- LA-16 (Galaxy Cluster) = Part 13 Galaxy Cluster (GC-class); 10–100 Mly scale (Part 14)
- LA-17 (Galaxy Supercluster) = Part 13 Galaxy Supercluster (GSC-class); 100 Mly to 1 Gly scale (Part 14)

**Rule 18.1.1 — LA Cannot Exceed Administrative Reality:** LA claims based on territory that is not structurally administered are invalid. A civilization that has sent probes to neighboring star systems but administers none of them has LA-5 (single World), not LA-8.

### 18.2 — Distance and CCT

Part 14's FTL distance governance (P14-FTL-1 through P14-FTL-4) constrains how quickly LA can be expanded. The early interstellar threshold at TA-8 corresponds to first interstellar reach (LA-8); before it is achieved, LA cannot advance beyond LA-7 (full Star System) regardless of other axis values.

**Rule 18.2.1 — LA-8 Requires Interstellar Reach:** LA-8 or above requires early interstellar capability (Part 8 Space Technology Level 8: near-lightspeed travel or a first FTL prototype; Part 10 FTL Physics Framework prerequisite satisfied). This creates a hard gate — TA minimum 8 is required for LA-8.

## Area 20 — Part 21 and Factions (Part 22 Interface)

### 20.1 — Civilization vs. Faction

Part 21 governs civilizations. Part 22 governs factions. The distinction:

**Civilization (Part 21):** An entity with unified governance structure, defined territory, and multi-generational institutional continuity. Assessed by CCT Designation. A civilization may also be a group of polities that share a calendar, treaties and cultural institutions while no one government rules them all; it is assessed on what the whole structurally administers and defends (P21-7), and each polity keeps its own kingdom-state ledger. *(Pointer: the calendar that the polities of the Nine Suns Continent share is the Nine Suns calendar, whose year, months, week and date line are owned by Part 41, Calendar Law, Area 1, Founding and scope, and Area 2, Epoch, year and months; the Calendar System field of the civilization ledger points there.)*

**Faction (Part 22):** An organized power group operating within or across civilizations. Factions may achieve CCT-equivalent power outputs but are structurally different — they lack the population-wide, territory-wide administrative reach that defines a civilization.

**Rule 20.1.1 — Faction CCT Proxy:** For narrative purposes, powerful factions within a civilization may be given a "Faction Power Tier" numbered 1 to 20 like the CCT but named by the scale in Part 22 Area 21.2 — this is a power proxy, not a formal CCT assessment. A faction is comparable in reach to the scale named in its FPT row (Part 22 Area 21.2): an FPT-6 faction within a Galactic civilization is Empire scale in reach; it is not a civilization.

### 20.2 — Factions Operating at Civilization Scale

When a faction controls territory, population, and institutions at the scale of a full civilization and operates independently of any larger civilization governance structure, Part 22 refers to Part 21 for its CCT classification. The boundary between "powerful faction" and "independent civilization" is governed by Part 22.

## Area 21 — Author Quick Reference

### 21.1 — Five Questions Before Writing a Civilization

**Question 1: What is this civilization's Designation?**
Use the CCT synthesis formula (Area 4) or assign directly from the tier profile descriptions (Area 8). Examples: Stellar, Clusteral, Galactic.

**Question 2: What is this civilization's Type?**
CS-Type, TS-Type, BP-Type, ES-Type, LS-Type, or CP-Type? The type shapes the civilization's character, strengths, and vulnerabilities.

**Question 3: Which axes are imbalanced?**
Identify any axis significantly above or below the CCT. That gap is the civilization's narrative identity — where its tension lives.

**Question 4: What CCT level is the civilization interacting with?**
The tier gap determines whether interaction is diplomatic, client-state, or existence-threatening.

**Question 5: Is this a Remnant, Nascent, Fractured, or Standard civilization?**
Special classifications (Area 7) add narrative texture and governance nuance.

### 21.2 — Common Author Errors

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Calling a civilization "Civ-7" or "Hold-7" | Use CTD: "Stellar" or "CCT-7" |
| Assigning CCT based on highest individual cultivator | CA is distributional; peak realm is one criterion among four |
| Treating LA-territorial claims as LA actual value | LA = administered territory, not claimed territory |
| Treating SA-0 as a civilization deficiency | SA-0 pure civilizations are structurally complete; they face an upper cap, not a penalty |
| Depicting CCT-4 and CCT-9 civilizations as diplomatic equals | The tier gap creates a power asymmetry; use Rule 10.1.1 |
| Advancing CCT after a single breakthrough event | CCT advancement requires 2-generation sustaining (Rule 4.4.2) |

### 21.3 — CTD to Narrative Scale

| Designation (CCT) | What the Narrative Feels Like |
|---|---|
| Subsistence to Industrial (CCT-1 to CCT-3) | Intimate; personal power matters enormously; institutions are human-scale |
| Planetary to Solarian (CCT-4 to CCT-6) | Planetary to stellar; institutions are vast; individual power is still pivotal but context is larger |
| Stellar to Clusteral (CCT-7 to CCT-10) | Stellar to cluster; civilizational architecture is the backdrop; extraordinary individuals shape civilizations |
| Galactic to Superclusteral (CCT-11 to CCT-14) | Galactic and beyond; individual scale is dwarfed; themes are civilizational and cosmological |
| Liminal-Universal and above (CCT-15+) | Part 28 territory; the physics of the universe are story elements |

### 21.4 — Worked Example: A Sword-Era Kingdom

**Story need:** a sword-era world is one kingdom of iron blades and walled towns, where a known few have reached Xiantian and most people do not cultivate. The kingdom is unnamed and is an illustration of the method, not a setting; the civilization ledger of the story's own world carries the real figures.

1. **Designation (Question 1).** The Area 8 profile for CCT-2 names early kingdoms, small sects and a rare, legendary Xiantian. Start from Formative and let the formula confirm it.
2. **Axes.** CA-4: Xiantian reached by a known few, regional sects (Area 2.1). TA-2: iron weapons, fortresses and writing (Part 8 Tier 2, Area 2.2). EA-2: trace CPM, individual use only (Area 2.3). LA-3: one kingdom (Area 2.4). SA-0: no institution joins cultivation and technology (Area 2.5).
3. **Formula (Area 4.2).** Base = (4 × 0.25) + (2 × 0.25) + (2 × 0.20) + (3 × 0.20) + (2 × 0 × 0.10) = 2.50. The range of CA, TA, EA and LA is 2, so there is no penalty. A half rounds down, so the tier is CCT-2. The caps agree: the lowest axis is 2 (Rule 4.1.1 allows up to CCT-4), TA and EA tie for lowest (Rule 4.1.2 allows up to CCT-3), and the SA-0 cap is the average of the four axes, 2.75, rounded down to 2 (Rule 4.1.3). Every Area 5 minimum for CCT-2 is met.
4. **Type (Question 2, Area 6.1).** In the order CP, ES, LS, CS, TS, BP: CP does not fit (SA doubled is 0, far from the other axes), ES does not fit (EA does not lead), LS does not fit (LA does not lead) and CS fits (CA leads TA by 2). The Type is CS.
5. **Phase (Area 3.3).** The final value 2.50 is N+0.50 for N = 2, inside the Apex band (N+0.17 to N+0.50). The kingdom is an Apex Formative civilization, which is the level the Area 8 profile describes.
6. **Imbalance and contact (Questions 3 and 4).** CA runs 2 levels above TA and EA: the kingdom's strength sits in a few cultivators, not in its tools or its treasury, and that gap is where its story tension lives. Neighbors at CCT-2 or CCT-3 meet it as peers; a visitor 3 or more tiers higher meets it under Rule 10.1.1.
7. **Special classification (Question 5).** Standard. A CCT-2 civilization with clear momentum may also be called a Nascent Civilization (Area 7.3).

**Ledger lines** (Civilization Ledger, Sections 1 and 2):

- Full Name: OPEN-AREA
- CCT Tier and Phase: CCT-2 Formative, Apex phase
- Civilization Type: CS (Cultivation-Sovereign)
- CA 4, TA 2, EA 2, LA 3, SA 0
- CS% and TS%: inside the Area 3.2.1 CCT-2 ranges (CS% 8%–22%, TS% 6%–20%), with TS% at the low end because the Type is cultivation-led

In prose the kingdom is "a Formative-tier kingdom" (N21-1); Type and phase words are optional color (N21-2, Area 3.3). A figure the story has not yet shown is entered as OPEN-AREA, and the first scene that shows it fixes it.

**Default for a kingdom-scale opening world:** when the story names only a kingdom and gives no other figures, start from the Area 8 profile that matches its technology (Part 8 Tier 2 gives Formative, Tier 3 gives Industrial), enter the axes as above, and leave unshown figures OPEN-AREA. The Chapter Spec and the civilization ledger prevail over this default.
