## Overview

Part 35 is the Starward Saga Canon's Content Density Law (CDL-2) — the governing domain for content-balance enforcement across all Starward Saga prose. It replaces the retired CDL-1 single combat percentage with a multi-lens scoring architecture: eleven independent content categories, each measured on its own 0–100% scale, with four Universal Anchor Floor categories (★ COMBAT, ★ CULTIVATION GROWTH, ★ DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION, ★ LORE & WORLD-BUILDING) that carry a flat 20% reference line at Section-close in every Section, reported as information only.

From these foundations, Part 35 builds outward across 7 Areas: the ten founding principles (CDL2-P1 through CDL2-P10) and the full definitions and scoring guidance of the eleven content categories (Area 1); the four-step Area scoring protocol, the word-count-weighted Section-aggregate averaging formula, the three-level enforcement pyramid, and the CDL-2 Section Override Protocol (Area 2); the canonical Phase 1 Section × Category Low–Anchor–High target table, the Universal Anchor Floor Verification Table, and the per-Section narrative derivation notes (Area 3); the exact Completeness Indicator block formats for the Area Score, Chapter-Level Aggregate, and Section-Close Audit, the order of the scorer's report, and the Section tracker schema (Area 4); the seven governing laws of the framework (CDL2-L1 through CDL2-L7) (Area 5); the relationship to the retired CDL-1 framework and the supersession of its universal combat floor (Area 6); and the master canon dependency and cross-reference map, including the Part 36 Sovereignty Delineation (Area 7).

The Governing Authority paragraph common to all 7 Areas is stated once in Appendix B. This file is the sole authority for Part 35.

## Area 1 — Founding Principles of the Content Density Law

### 1.1 — The Ten Governing Principles of CDL-2

**CDL2-P1 — The Starward Saga is a Cultivation-Technology-Fight Novel Series.**
The series' primary identity — as established in Part 30, Laws P30-3 and P30-14 — is cultivation progression, technology mastery, Named Move development, power-system mechanics, combat strategy, and dual-track synergy. Content Density Law CDL-2 does not dilute this identity. It governs it with precision: every category exists in service of the story's core axis, and the weight given to each category in each Section reflects what that Section's story requires to make the cultivation-technology-fight axis land with maximum force. Lore earns the stakes. Dialogue earns the bonds. Consequence earns the weight of the fight.

**CDL2-P2 — Eleven Categories, Not One.**
CDL-1's single combat percentage is replaced by eleven independent content categories, each measured on its own 0–100% scale. An Area that is 80% Combat AND 60% Lore is not contradicting itself — it is combat rich with world-context. The categories are lenses, not buckets. An Area is scored on all applicable categories simultaneously; totals per Area routinely exceed 100%. The eleven categories collectively map the full narrative DNA of every Area and every Section.

**CDL2-P3 — Individual Areas Are Free; Sections Are Governed.**
No individual Area is required to carry any specific content percentage in any category. An Area may be 100% combat with zero lore. An Area may be 100% dialogue with zero combat. This freedom enables authentic storytelling: the story's moments are what they need to be. The Section aggregate is the level at which the report is read. Over the span of a full Section, the weighted aggregate of all Areas in that Section is compared with the Low–High range of each category's Section target as information; the comparison governs nothing, and the confirmed spec chain and Part 30 decide what the Section holds.

**CDL2-P4 — Four Universal Anchor Floors.**
Four categories are designated as Universal Anchor Floors — each has a flat 20% reference line at Section-close in every Section, shown in the Section-close report as information. These four are: COMBAT, CULTIVATION GROWTH, DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION, and LORE & WORLD-BUILDING. A Section that falls below 20% Anchor on any of these four categories at Section-close is shown as below the reference line in the Section-close report. It is not a failure, it blocks nothing, and nothing is added to the prose to reach the line. These reference lines exist because these four categories are the structural pillars of the Starward Saga's identity — the report shows where a Section stands against them, and the confirmed spec chain and Part 30 decide what the Section holds.

> **Anchor Floor definition.** The flat **20%** line at Section-close is a reference line and informs only. The Section Anchor value in the Area 3 Target Table is a steering value, and the Low–High range is an information band for every category. No line in this Part fails a Section or an Area; the confirmed spec chain and Part 30 prevail. Cite by rule ID (CDL2-P4, CDL2-P7, Area 2.3 Level 4, Area 3.1 star legend).

**CDL2-P5 — Independent Lenses, Not Compositional Splits.**
Each category is scored independently on the full 0–100% scale. The categories do not divide an Area's word count into mutually exclusive slices. Instead, each category represents a **dimension of narrative content** that may be simultaneously present with any other. A scene containing combat, named-move deployment, cultivation pressure, and tactical dialogue is scored at meaningful percentages in all four simultaneously. This architecture prevents the false choice between "combat scene" and "dialogue scene" — the best scenes in the Starward Saga are both at once.

**CDL2-P6 — Scoring is AI Self-Assessment by Word-Count Proportion.**
At Gate 2, after an Area is written, the AI assesses each of the eleven categories by estimating the proportion of the Area's word count that is meaningfully engaged in that category's content type. A combat exchange that occupies 1,400 words of a 2,000-word Area is scored at approximately 70% for COMBAT. A lore-heavy dialogue that runs 1,200 words of a 2,000-word Area is scored at approximately 60% for both LORE & WORLD-BUILDING and DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION. Every category scored above 40% carries one quoted line from the Area as its evidence, shown in the Area block (Area 4.1a); a score above 40% with no quoted line is void. The AI reports its scores transparently in the Completeness Indicator. Scores are honest assessments, not targets to hit — the Section target is a reference for the aggregate and governs nothing, and the individual Area score is not a target either.

**CDL2-P7 — Multi-Level Tracking with Tiered Enforcement.**
CDL-2 tracks content distribution at three levels of the narrative hierarchy: Area level (record and display; the P9 floor and the L6/L7 runs are reported to the author as information only), Chapter level (an information note if the aggregate trends below an Anchor line at Section-close), Section level (an information line for any of the four Universal Anchor Floor categories below 20% at Section-close; an information note for any category outside its Low–High range). The pyramid gives the author and the writer visibility at every level; it enforces nothing and changes nothing the story contains.

**CDL2-P8 — Section Targets Are Locked Canon Used as Guide Posts.**
The Section × Category target table in Area 3 of this Part is **Locked Canon** (Part 28 Area 2.2), used as guide posts: the figures steer and none of them is a hard line. The targets are canonical defaults derived from Part 30's Section arc definitions and approved by the user. A Section may follow its own distribution when the story calls for it, and no approval is needed. A **CDL-2 Section Override** is an optional note, recorded in the Advisory notes table of the Section tracker, that records a deliberate difference from the table for the author's reference; it needs no justification.

**CDL2-P9 — No Dead Weight at Any Content Level.**
Part 30, Law P30-3 (no dead weight) and Law P30-14 (progression axis centrality) remain in force and operate jointly with CDL-2. An Area that scores 0% in all eleven CDL-2 categories has no content of any recognized narrative type and is by definition dead weight. CDL-2 does not create new dead-weight tolerance by spreading content across eleven categories — every Area is read for meaningful content in at least two categories, and for a primary category scored at 40% or above on the Area score, as information only. The floor is read once per Area, in the block of Area 4.1a. A figure below either line is shown in the Area block as information; it raises no question, blocks nothing, and is never fixed by adding or trimming prose. Canon sets no number for "meaningful content": the scorer judges it, and the block names the categories it counted as meaningful. An Area that carries nothing decisive is dead weight under Part 30 Law P30-3, regardless of CDL-2 category compliance.

**CDL2-P10 — The Prose Depth and Immersion Law Applies Uniformly.**
Part 28's Prose Depth and Immersion Law (Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10) and the Area size range of Part 2 Rule P2-Area-7 remain in force and are not modified by CDL-2. CDL-2 governs *what* is in each Area at the category level; Part 28 governs *how deeply* each element is rendered. These two frameworks are complementary and simultaneously in force. A fast-paced combat scene scored at 90% COMBAT is read against both the CDL-2 category report (information only) and the Part 28 depth standard, weighed on the scene, the Area window (Part 2 Rule P2-Area-1), and not paragraph by paragraph (Part 28 Area 4.2). A paragraph may be quiet as Part 28 Area 4.5 allows, because the Area window carries every Pillar; a Pillar that the Area window has not carried is reported and judged on the Area (Part 28 Area 4.2). **Pacing and CDL-2 interact as follows:** Fast-paced Areas naturally spike single categories (COMBAT, TRAVEL); medium-paced Areas distribute more evenly; slow-paced Areas spread depth across LORE, DIALOGUE, EMOTIONS, and CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT. The Section aggregate across all pacing-varied Areas achieves the balanced distribution the Section targets require.

### 1.2 — The Eleven Content Categories

Each category is defined below with its governing Part dependencies, its narrative scope, and its classification criteria. Categories are non-exclusive — any single Area may carry content across multiple categories simultaneously. EMOTIONS and CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT are named categories, so that the felt life of a scene and the change in its people are each scored as themselves. LORE & WORLD-BUILDING is one category: lore (the world's history, cosmology and secrets) and world-building (its places, peoples and daily texture) are scored together.

---

**Category 1 — COMBAT**
*Governing Parts: Part 5 (Combat Law), Part 6 (Cultivation Move Taxonomy Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the direct or indirect expression of cultivation combat, physical conflict, tactical warfare, or power-system confrontation. COMBAT content is scored when the prose is materially engaged with: active fight scenes between cultivators, armies, or factions; sparring and training with combat intent and cultivation-energy expression; tactical planning sessions directly serving combat preparation; post-combat recovery and wound assessment; combat-driven power-state analysis; pre-battle strategic decisions; threat intelligence gathering whose direct purpose is combat readiness; weapon and combat-gear acquisition in a combat-readiness context; enemy power-level assessment.

**Scoring Guidance:** A full combat exchange (Named Move deployment, energy collision, injury assessment) scores 80–100% COMBAT. A tactical council with maps and formation decisions scores 50–70% COMBAT. A post-battle wound treatment scene with tactical review scores 40–60% COMBAT. A training spar between two protagonists with technique correction scores 60–80% COMBAT. Pure travel between combat zones without combat content scores 0% COMBAT.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — COMBAT has a 20% reference line at Section-close in every Section; a Section below it is reported as information only.

---

**Category 2 — NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES**
*Governing Parts: Part 6 (Cultivation Move Taxonomy Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the introduction, development, deployment, refinement, or formal naming of cultivation techniques, Named Moves, technology weapon systems, formation arrays, or combat-applicable abilities governed by Part 6. This category is distinct from COMBAT: COMBAT measures the presence of conflict; NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES measures the presence of the named technique architecture that is the Starward Saga's most distinctive power-system feature. A combat scene can score high COMBAT and low NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES (if the combat uses unnamed force); a technique-creation scene can score high NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES and low COMBAT (if it is a pure creation/naming moment without active conflict).

**Scoring Guidance:** A scene in which a protagonist formally creates and names a new cultivation technique scores 70–100% NAMED MOVES. A combat scene in which three Named Moves are deployed and described in full Part 6 nomenclature scores 50–80% NAMED MOVES. A training scene focused on refining an existing Named Move's mechanics scores 40–70% NAMED MOVES. A combat scene using only raw cultivation force with no named technique reference scores 0–15% NAMED MOVES.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — this category has no universal floor. Some Sections will naturally have lower Named Move density than others.

**Category 3 — CULTIVATION GROWTH**
*Governing Parts: Part 3 (Cultivation, Power, and Lifespan Law), Part 4 (Affinity, Heart and Soul Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the advancement, measurement, or experiential rendering of cultivation realm progression, breakthrough events, affinity development, dantian growth, meridian network expansion, CPM absorption, soul-stage transitions, or any cultivation-system growth event governed by Parts 3 and 4. CULTIVATION GROWTH is distinct from COMBAT: training combat scores COMBAT; the breakthrough event that training accumulation produces scores CULTIVATION GROWTH. Both may score simultaneously in a scene that features a breakthrough during combat.

**Scoring Guidance:** A full cultivation breakthrough scene (energy surge, meridian expansion, realm transition) scores 80–100% CULTIVATION GROWTH. An extended training session building toward a future breakthrough scores 40–60% CULTIVATION GROWTH. A cultivation assessment conversation between protagonist and instructor scores 30–50% CULTIVATION GROWTH. A combat scene in which a breakthrough occurs mid-battle scores both high COMBAT and 50–70% CULTIVATION GROWTH simultaneously. A pure political scene with no cultivation content scores 0% CULTIVATION GROWTH.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — CULTIVATION GROWTH has a 20% reference line at Section-close in every Section; a Section below it is reported as information only.

**Category 4 — TECHNOLOGY**
*Governing Parts: Part 8 (Technology Scale Law), Part 31 (Cultivation-Technology Synergy Law), Part 32 (Vehicle and Transport Law), Part 33 (Technology Weapon Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the deployment, description, development, or integration of technology — Tier 1 through Tier 6 per Part 8 — in any narrative context. TECHNOLOGY content includes: combat-technology integration (Valia's dual-track capability); vehicle and transport technology (Part 32 airships, ground vehicles); communication technology in diplomatic or military context; medical technology in post-combat recovery; artifact detection and analysis equipment; construction and infrastructure technology; technology-cultivation fusion events (Part 31 dual-track synergy moments); technology weaponry and its tactical deployment.

**Scoring Guidance:** A scene in which Valia deploys technology-integrated cultivation techniques in combat scores 60–80% TECHNOLOGY and 70–90% COMBAT simultaneously. An airship travel scene whose prose focuses on the vessel's systems and capabilities scores 50–70% TECHNOLOGY and 70–90% TRAVEL & DISCOVERY simultaneously. A diplomatic communication via technology relay scores 30–50% TECHNOLOGY. A cultivation-only combat scene with no technology content scores 0–10% TECHNOLOGY.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Technology density appropriately varies by Section arc and available technology infrastructure.

**Category 5 — LORE & WORLD-BUILDING**
*Governing Parts: Part 13 (World Systems Location Law), Part 14 (World Systems Distance Law), Part 21 (People, Society, and Civilization Law), Part 25 (Culture and Social Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the introduction, expansion, or deepening of the Starward Saga's world — its geography, history, cultivation-system architecture, social structures, civilizations, ancient forces, cosmological context, faction histories, kingdom cultures, beast ecosystems, relic lore, and all other elements of the world's texture and depth. LORE & WORLD-BUILDING is scored whenever the prose materially advances the reader's understanding of the world the story inhabits — not through exposition dumps, but through rendered experience: a protagonist encountering the World Tree and sensing its ancient energy; a diplomatic summit at which the Nine Suns Continent's political ecology becomes visible; a border village scene that establishes the texture of daily life at cultivation-world margins.

**Scoring Guidance:** A World Tree discovery sequence (first approach, first sensing, first contact) scores 80–100% LORE. An ensemble scene at a diplomatic summit where the continent's political factions become visible scores 60–80% LORE. A cultivation breakthrough scene whose context includes detailed description of the Sacred Ground's energy type and historical function scores 40–60% LORE alongside high CULTIVATION GROWTH. A pure combat scene in a location with no environmental or historical rendering scores 0–15% LORE.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — LORE & WORLD-BUILDING has a 20% reference line at Section-close in every Section; a Section below it is reported as information only.

**Category 6 — DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION**
*Governing Parts: Part 29 (Dialogue and Social Interaction Law), Part 25 (Culture and Social Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the direct or rendered interaction between characters — through dialogue, social exchange, relationship development, ideological conversation, familial bonding, ensemble cohesion moments, cultural exchange between characters of different origins, and the social texture of the cultivation world's interpersonal dynamics. DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION is the category that carries the ensemble's emotional life: the friendship between Varya and Aegon, the alliance between Varys and Aeries, the deep bond between Valia and Aalia, the cultural navigation of Hercules and Bison — all rendered through actual interaction, not description of interaction.

**Scoring Guidance:** A full relationship scene between two protagonists (extended dialogue, emotional revelation, ideological exchange) scores 80–100% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL. A tournament banquet scene with political small-talk and relationship undercurrents scores 50–70% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL alongside moderate POLITICAL & FACTION. A combat scene in which characters call to each other, strategize in motion, and reveal relationship through action scores 25–45% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL alongside high COMBAT. A pure solo cultivation breakthrough scene with no social content scores 0–10% DIALOGUE & SOCIAL.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** YES — DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION has a 20% reference line at Section-close in every Section; a Section below it is reported as information only.

**Category 7 — INFORMATION & INTRIGUE**
*Governing Part: Part 24 (Information and Secrecy Law)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the gathering, withholding, misrepresentation, revelation, or strategic deployment of information — in any context. INFORMATION & INTRIGUE is the category of secrets, intelligence, espionage, dramatic irony, information asymmetry, faction intelligence operations, reconnaissance, the Third Faction's hidden movements, the World Tree's communicated knowledge, the Olympus suppression backstory's gradual revelation, and all other moments in which *what is known by whom* is the scene's primary axis. This category is distinct from POLITICAL & FACTION (which governs the political stakes of information) and from LORE (which governs world-knowledge delivery): INFORMATION & INTRIGUE governs the *strategic dynamics* of information in the narrative.

**Scoring Guidance:** A scene in which Valia perceives a hidden political signal that others miss scores 70–90% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE. A scouting mission reporting on an enemy faction's troop movements scores 60–80% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE alongside moderate COMBAT (if the scout encounters resistance). A diplomatic summit scene in which hidden agendas are present beneath formal courtesies scores 40–60% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE alongside high DIALOGUE & SOCIAL. A full combat scene between two explicitly identified combatants scores 0–15% INFORMATION & INTRIGUE (unless a hidden combatant or hidden capability is revealed).

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Information & Intrigue density appropriately varies by Section arc.

**Category 8 — EMOTIONS**
*Governing Parts: Part 26 (Psychological Law), Part 28 (Creative Flexibility Law), Part 36 (Narration Novel Thematic Law), Part 39 (Character Behavioral Synthesis)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the rendering of felt emotion — grief, joy, fear, pride, shame, determination, and the mixed and quiet states between them — as the point-of-view character lives it: what the feeling does to the body, the thought and the choice, at the specificity Part 36 Area 8 requires. EMOTIONS is scored when the prose stays inside a feeling long enough for the reader to feel it rather than be told of it. It anchors the cultivation and combat progression in genuine human (and Demigod) emotional reality. This category includes: post-battle emotional reckoning; breakthrough moments' inner experience beyond the physical; Hercules and Bison's displacement psychology; a relationship reckoning inside a scene; the quiet after a loss. It is distinct from DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION (feeling carried between people through speech and exchange) and from CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT (a change in who a person is): a character may feel deeply without changing, and may change without a scene of feeling.

**Scoring Guidance:** A post-battle scene in which a protagonist processes a loss and its meaning scores 80–100% EMOTIONS. The Olympus Suppression Seal's suppressed-power-glimpse moment (Hercules feeling Demigod power pressing against the cap) scores 70–90% EMOTIONS alongside moderate COMBAT. A First Awakening scene that renders Varya's inner experience of cultivation energy for the first time scores 60–80% EMOTIONS alongside high CULTIVATION GROWTH. A pure combat scene between unfamiliar combatants with no interiority rendered scores 0–15% EMOTIONS.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Emotions density appropriately peaks in certain Sections (S4, S7, S8) and is lower in others (S2's high-action tournament arc).

**Category 9 — CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT**
*Governing Parts: Part 16 (Character Classification), Part 17 (Hero Law), Part 18 (Villain Law), Part 19 (Side Character Law), Part 20 (Ideology Law), Part 26 (Psychological Law), Part 27 (Consequence Law), Part 39 (Character Behavioral Synthesis)*

**Definition:** Prose content whose primary narrative function is the change in who a character is: an arc phase reached or tested, a belief or ideology shifting, a habit or fear overcome or hardened, a relationship deepening or breaking, a role or duty taken up or refused, and the consequences of earlier events as they land on the person and show in the character's later choices (Part 27). CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT is scored when the prose shows change through choice and cost rather than by statement: a decision made against the character's own habit; a loss that alters what the character is willing to do; a lesson taken in or refused; a registered behavior that shifts under pressure (Part 39). It is the category that ensures the Starward Saga's events *cost* something and that its characters are altered by what happens to them. This category includes: Varya and Aegon's growth alongside cultivation growth; Hercules and Bison's movement from displacement toward engagement; the political and personal consequences of diplomatic failures as they land on the people who bear them; the war's toll on the ensemble. It is distinct from CULTIVATION GROWTH (the advance of power), from EMOTIONS (the felt moment) and from DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION (exchange between people): CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT scores the change itself.

**Scoring Guidance:** A scene in which a protagonist makes a costly choice against their own habit or belief, and the prose shows what the choice makes of them, scores 80–100% CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT. Hercules's movement from displacement toward engagement, shown across a scene of choice, scores 70–90% CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT. A scene in which a protagonist takes on a role or duty they had refused, with what it costs them, scores 60–80% CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT. A pure combat scene between unfamiliar combatants in which no choice, cost or change is shown scores 0–15% CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT.

**Universal Anchor Floor:** NO — Character Development density appropriately peaks in certain Sections (S4, S7, S8) and is lower in others (S2's high-action tournament arc).

## Area 2 — Scoring Methodology and Enforcement Architecture

### 2.1 — Area Scoring Protocol

At Gate 2, after each Area is written and before the Completeness Indicator is finalized, the AI performs a CDL-2 scoring pass on the Area:

**Step 1 — Identify Primary Content Blocks.** Mentally divide the Area into thematic content blocks by estimated word count. A 2,000-word Area might have: 700 words of combat exchange, 400 words of tactical dialogue, 350 words of cultivation description, 300 words of lore rendering, and 250 words of interior emotion and consequence. A 1,600-word combat-primary Area might have 1,100 words of combat, 250 words of tactical dialogue, and 250 words of consequence.

**Step 2 — Score Each Category.** For each of the eleven categories, estimate the percentage of the Area's total word count that is meaningfully engaged in that category. Using the 2,000-word example above: COMBAT ≈ 55% — the 700 words of direct combat exchange plus the 400 words of tactical dialogue, which is combat-engaged, gives 1,100/2,000 = 55%. Per CDL2-P2 the categories are lenses, not buckets: those same 400 words also score in full toward DIALOGUE & SOCIAL, and a word counted for COMBAT is never thereby withheld from another category. Scores are approximate and honest, not precise fractions.

**Step 3 — Record Scores in the Completeness Indicator.** Every CDL-2 category score is recorded in the Area's Completeness Indicator (format defined in Area 4) and as one row of the Section tracker (Area 4.5).

**Step 4 — Update Section Running Totals.** After each Area is scored, the AI updates the running Section aggregate for each category (weighted by Area word count). This running total is visible in the Section running aggregate lines of the Area block. If a written Area is revised, the revised scores replace that Area's earlier scores and the Section running aggregate is recomputed; an Area that a rewind or a redraft archives is removed from it. A scene that continues across consecutive Areas is scored in each of its Areas; no category is estimated a second time over the whole scene.

### 2.2 — Weighted Averaging for the Section Aggregate

Section-aggregate CDL-2 scores are computed as **word-count-weighted averages** across all Areas completed in the Section. This ensures that a long, combat-heavy Area contributes proportionally more to the COMBAT aggregate than a short non-combat interlude. The weighting formula is:

> **Section Aggregate (Category X) = Σ (Area_wordcount_i × Score_i) / Σ (Area_wordcount_i)**

The AI tracks this running weighted average and displays it in the Section running aggregate lines of the Area block at each Completeness Indicator. A Chapter aggregate uses the same formula over the Areas of that Chapter.

### 2.3 — Enforcement Pyramid

The pyramid has three levels, numbered 2 to 4: the Area, the Chapter and the Section. The Area is the written unit and carries the first reading.

**Level 2 — Area Level (Record and Display).**
(Amended 2026-10-07: for a Chapter built whole, the Chapter is scored once, one Chapter-level block, in the same way.) At each Area, the written unit of Part 2's five-tier hierarchy, the CDL-2 Area score is displayed in the block of Area 4.1a and recorded in the Section tracker. No prose is changed at the Area level. The content floor of CDL2-P9 and the Named-Move runs of CDL2-L6 and CDL2-L7 are read at this level and reported to the author as information only; a figure below the floor, or a run that reaches the line of CDL2-L6 (Section 8: passes the five that Law CDL2-L7 allows), is shown in the block as information; it raises no question and is never fixed by adding or trimming prose. The Area scores serve as an early-warning signal if the Section trajectory is drifting away from its targets and contribute to the Chapter and Section aggregates.

**Level 3 — Chapter Level (CDL-2 note).**
At the close of each Chapter, if the Chapter-level CDL-2 aggregate for any Universal Anchor Floor category (COMBAT, CULTIVATION GROWTH, DIALOGUE & SOCIAL, LORE & WORLD-BUILDING) is tracking below 20%, the AI shows a **CDL-2 note** in the Completeness Indicator. The note names the category and its current trajectory, as information. It asks for no adjustment in later prose, needs no acknowledgement, and blocks nothing; the confirmed spec chain and Part 30 decide what the next prose contains.

**Level 4 — Section Level (information line for Anchor Floors; Advisory WARN for all others).**
At Section-close, the AI performs a full CDL-2 Section Audit:
- **Anchor line:** If any of the four Universal Anchor Floor categories (★ COMBAT, ★ CULTIVATION GROWTH, ★ DIALOGUE & SOCIAL, ★ LORE & WORLD-BUILDING) falls below 20%, the Section-close report shows it as below the reference line. Nothing is added to reach it, no override is needed, and the Section is not failed.
- **Advisory WARN:** If any category falls below its Section Low or above its Section High without breaching the 20% floor, the AI issues an Advisory WARN. Advisory WARNs are recorded in the Advisory notes table of the Section tracker and do not block Phase progression, but they are visible for editorial review.

> **The pyramid's levels are guide posts.** The levels of §2.3 above — and Laws CDL2-L1, CDL2-L2, and CDL2-L6 in Area 5 — are written as information and agree with this note, which says what the AI does when a level fires. Every CDL-2 percentage is a **guide post, not a physical law**: it is computed and reported plainly, whether it holds or not, and a WARN or an Anchor-line result is **shown to the author as information** and is never silently remedied. No agent adds, expands, trims, re-scopes, or pads prose in order to move an aggregate back inside a range. The author may act on a figure at any time — accept it and proceed; direct a revision, with suggestions; revise the spec so the plan matches the story being told; or set the number aside for that unit — and none of these is asked for or required. Where satisfying a percentage would make the prose mechanical — a quota-filler beat inserted to lift a category, a living beat cut to clear a High — **that conflict is itself the finding** and is surfaced as such: Parts 28, 29, and 36 (Authority Tier 0) outrank numeric compliance. No pipeline agent asks the author about a figure on its own. An agent that finds a figure outside a line shows it in the report, and an agent never adds, trims or pads prose to move it. No margin table, no near-miss band, and no iteration counter exists. Governing text: `.claude/guardrails/quality/guardrail-threshold-consultation.md`.

### 2.4 — CDL-2 Section Override Protocol

When a Section's content distribution differs from the targets in the Area 3 table and the author wants the difference recorded, a **CDL-2 Section Override** note is written as follows (optional; the difference itself needs no approval):

1. The AI identifies the deviation and its narrative justification
2. The note names the specific category, the target vs. actual value, and the story reason
3. The author is told of the note (no approval is needed)
4. The override is documented in the Advisory notes table of the Section tracker with: date, Section identifier, category, original target, override value, an approval reference if the author gave one, and narrative justification
5. No formal Canon Amendment is required — the Advisory notes entry is sufficient

A note for a Section below a 20% Anchor line is written the same way; the story reason is recorded for the author's reference and no approval is needed.

### 3.5 — Section Derivation Notes

Each Section's CDL-2 targets are derived from Part 30's Section arc definitions. The narrative rationale for each Section's distinctive profile is recorded below.

**Section 1 — Azure Sky and Gale (1yr)**
Root-planting. The world must be *shown*, relationships *built*, and the ensemble *established as people* before combat can carry weight. Lore & World-Building (55% Anchor) and Dialogue & Social (58% Anchor) dominate — these are the categories that make the world worth protecting. Combat is present through cultivation practice scenes, adult protagonist level-establishment moments and, from the second half of the Section, low-scale raids and skirmishes with rival kingdoms (42% Anchor), though no formal set-piece battle is fought. Named Moves stay modest (18% Anchor): the first named techniques appear in the first half of the Section and become commonplace only in the second half, as the skirmishes begin. Political & Faction begins its cultivation (28% Anchor) through the diplomacy scenes, the alliance establishment and the first pressure from rival kingdoms.

**Section 2 — The First Trial of Blades (1.5yr)**
First testing. The tournament arc, training deepened, and First Awakening drive Combat (62% Anchor) and Named Moves (40% Anchor) up from Section 1's levels, as named moves become everyday and the raids that began in late Section 1 intensify. Cultivation Growth rises sharply (50% Anchor) — the First Awakening at the S1→S2 boundary is Phase 1's first major cultivation event. Lore drops (32% Anchor) — the world is established; now it is tested. Dialogue & Social holds strong (42% Anchor) through training relationships and tournament social dynamics.

The political Section. Dialogue & Social peaks to its second-highest Phase value (60% Anchor) — diplomatic exchange is by definition social interaction. Information & Intrigue surges (48% Anchor) — the Third Faction's hidden movements are the Section's dramatic engine. Combat holds (48% Anchor) through training, faction-friction events and the raids that strike around the summit.

**Section 4 — The Fallen Demigods (1yr)**
Action-reset and ensemble completion. The border crisis and ensemble convergence produce the second-highest Combat anchor in Phase 1 (72% Anchor). Emotions and Character Development peak, tied with S8, at their highest anchor outside S7 (48% Anchor each) — Hercules's arc of displacement-to-engagement and the suppressed-power-glimpse moment are Phase 1's most psychologically charged events. **Section 5 — Guilds, Quests, and the World's Edge (1.5yr)**
Exploration. Travel & Discovery reaches its second-highest anchor (58% Anchor) — the entire Section is a continental quest-exploration arc. Combat (68% Anchor) and Named Moves (48% Anchor) remain strong through quest combat. Political & Faction rises (45% Anchor) — the guild ecosystem is a continental political-economic structure even in wilderness contexts. Cultivation Growth (50% Anchor) builds toward the Section 6 Second Awakening.

**Section 6 — The Seven World Trees (1.5yr)**
Phase 1's most category-dense Section — three major threshold events run simultaneously. Lore & World-Building reaches its second-highest Anchor (65%) with the World Tree discovery. Cultivation Growth hits its Phase peak (62% Anchor) — the Second Awakening is the largest single cultivation event in Phase 1. ### 4.4 — Report Order and the Voice and Variety Block

The Area Scorer's report for one Area is emitted in this order: (1) the CDL-2 Area Block (4.1a); (2) the Voice and Variety block of the Area; (3) when the Chapter completion flow (`/chapter-spec complete`, reached through `/next-chapter`) asks for it, the Chapter-Level Aggregate Block (4.2); (4) when `/next-section` or `/next-phase` asks for it, the Section-Close Audit Block (4.3).

The Voice and Variety block is report-only. It is produced for each Area, and it is shown in chat with the rest of the report; it is not stored, and no file, the Section tracker included, records it. It has six rows, which the Area Scorer's charter names and lays out (lecturing; even polish; feelings named, not felt; one voice for everyone; openings and closers; recurring images and callbacks); Part 36 (dimensions 16-J and 16-K, Areas 18 to 21) defines what they observe. The block adds no category to this Part and carries no percentage in any CDL-2 category, no target, no status and no line, and it is never scored into a category. Any count it carries is a guide post: the counting method is stated (no tokenizer exists in this repository), and the figure is shown to the author as information and is never fixed by editing the prose. The Chapter-level and Section-close blocks do not include it.

### 4.5 — The Section Tracker

The Section tracker is the file `.claude/story-memory/cdl2-density-tracking/phase-N/section-M-aggregate.md` (N the Phase, M the Section). The Area Scorer is its only writer; it creates the file for a Section at that Section's first scored Area, from the headings below, and it is returned to its empty template by a reset (`/reset-chapter`, `/reset-phase`, `/reset-story`, `/hard-reset`) for the Areas the reset removes. The tracker is a tracking file, not novel output. Every number in it is a guide post, never a physical law (`.claude/guardrails/quality/guardrail-threshold-consultation.md`). Its sections, in order:

1. **Header.** The Section's Phase, number and name; the governing law and guardrails; the maintainer; the date established; and a Status line (OPEN while Areas are being scored).
2. **Counting method.** The stated method for every figure in the file: the word count is the whitespace-delimited token count of the narrative body of the Area, leaving out the Title Page, the Chapter Header Block, the Area Title Block, the date line, horizontal rules and HTML comments; the category score is the Area 2.1 procedure; the Section aggregate is the Area 2.2 formula.
3. **Per-Area scores.** One row per scored Area: the Area reference (Phase.Section.Part.Chapter.Area), the Chapter, the words, the eleven category scores, the CDL2-P9 floor result, the Named-Move run, and the date scored and whether the row was revised.
4. **Section running aggregate.** The Anchor Floor status table for the four starred categories (aggregate, the 20% reference line, margin, status at this grain) and the table of all eleven categories (aggregate, Low, Anchor, High, status ON TRACK or WARN), with the Section's targets from Area 3. Chapter aggregates are the Area 2.2 formula over the per-Area rows of one Chapter.
5. **Advisory notes.** One row for each Advisory WARN and each documented Section Override (Area 2.3 and Area 2.4): date, Section, category, target (Low–Anchor–High), actual or override value, approval reference, justification.
6. **Findings routed to the orchestrating command for author consultation.** Findings identified and not remedied; no pipeline agent asks the author.
7. **Forward projection.** A Section-close forecast is issued only from specced Areas, reported by both the strict (zero-default) and residual (non-zero default) readings for every category where they disagree.
8. **Aggregate history.** One row for each Area added: date, Area added, Areas scored, Section words, note.

## Area 5 — Governing Laws of the Content Density Framework

**Law CDL2-L1 — Section Aggregate Supremacy.**
The Section-aggregate CDL-2 score is the one figure the report is built on. No individual Area or Chapter aggregate passes or fails CDL-2, and the Section-close report shows the Section figures as information; nothing passes, fails or blocks on them.

**Law CDL2-L2 — The Four Anchor Lines Inform Only.**
A Section in which any of the four Universal Anchor Floor categories (★ COMBAT, ★ CULTIVATION GROWTH, ★ DIALOGUE & SOCIAL INTERACTION, ★ LORE & WORLD-BUILDING) falls below 20% at Section-close is shown in the Section-close report as below the reference line. It is not a failure, needs no override, and blocks no Phase progression; nothing is added to the prose to reach the line.

**Law CDL2-L3 — Independent Lenses Cannot Be Traded Against Each Other.**
Exceeding one category's target does not compensate for falling below another. A Section with 95% COMBAT aggregate does not receive credit against LORE & WORLD-BUILDING falling below its Anchor floor. Each category is an independent measure. The readings are category-by-category, not holistic.

**Law CDL2-L4 — CDL-2 Does Not Supersede Part 28 Depth or Part 30 Story Law.**
CDL-2 governs content distribution at the category level. It does not override Part 28's Prose Depth and Immersion Law, Part 2's pacing vocabulary (Part 2 Area 1 §1.6.2.1) or Area size range (Rule P2-Area-7), or any Part 30 arc mandate. CDL-2 is information only: it is neither necessary nor sufficient for Section-level canon compliance, and Part 30 and the confirmed spec chain prevail over every figure.

**Law CDL2-L5 — CDL-2 Governs Phase 1; Future Phases Require Parallel Tables.**
The Phase 1 Section × Category targets in Area 3 are specific to Phase 1's story arc as defined in Part 30. When Part 30 is expanded for Phase 2, Part 35 must be expanded with a corresponding Phase 2 Section × Category table derived from Phase 2's Section arc definitions. The framework architecture, eleven categories, Anchor floors, and enforcement pyramid apply universally across all Phases; only the per-Section target values change.

**Law CDL2-L6 — A Run of Areas Without Named Moves Is Shown as Information.**
Even outside of Named Move-heavy Sections, the Starward Saga's move architecture usually keeps named cultivation techniques present in the narrative fabric. If three consecutive Areas within a Section score 0% NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES, the Area block of the third Area shows the run as a CDL-2 note, for information. It raises no question and asks for no Named Move; no Area is forced to carry one, and the confirmed spec chain and Part 30 decide whether Named Moves appear. The run is counted over Areas, the written unit (Part 2 Area 1 §1.5.1). The note lets the author see, without being asked, when the move taxonomy has been absent from a long run of Areas.

**Law CDL2-L7 — Section 8 Consolidation Exemption for Named Moves.**
Section 8's NAMED MOVES & TECHNIQUES target of 8–15–25% (the Phase minimum) reflects the canonical consolidation arc of Phase 1's close. Section 8 is the sole Section in which CDL2-L6's consecutive-zero rule is modified: the CDL2-L6 note shows, as information, when the run passes five consecutive Areas (the sixth), and the 8–25% range is an advisory band only. This exemption exists because Section 8's relational and discovery focus is canonical by Part 30 law and does not represent a narrative failure.

## Area 6 — Relationship to Retired CDL-1 Framework

### 6.1 — CDL-1 Retirement Statement

The Combat Density Law (CDL-1), which governed all Sections across all Phases with a single universal combat floor, is **retired**. CDL-1 is superseded in its entirety by Part 35 CDL-2. All future authorship of Starward Saga prose is governed by CDL-2 exclusively for content density enforcement.

**What CDL-1 protected, CDL-2 preserves and extends:**
CDL-1's fundamental insight — that the Starward Saga is, at its core, a cultivation-technology-fight novel series and that combat must be quantifiably central — is preserved and extended in CDL-2. The Universal Anchor Floor for COMBAT (a 20% reference line at every Section-close) shows where combat stands in every Section; the confirmed spec chain and Part 30 decide how much combat a Section holds. The COMBAT target Anchors across Phase 1 range from 30% (Section 8 consolidation) to 85% (Section 7 war climax) — demonstrating that combat is the single most tracked category in the framework. CDL-2 does not reduce combat. It gives combat the company it needs — lore, dialogue, consequence, cultivation growth — to land with full pacing-appropriate immersive force.

**What CDL-1 overconstrained, CDL-2 corrects:**
CDL-1's single universal floor was blind to story context. A diplomatic Section, a relational coda, or a world-discovery Section cannot and should not be held to a combat floor without degrading the story's foundations. CDL-2 provides Section-specific targets: Section 8 runs at 30% COMBAT Anchor because Part 30 mandates it as an airship-and-discovery coda; Section 7 runs at 85% COMBAT Anchor because Part 30 mandates it as the war climax. The story's identity is preserved; its architecture is honored.

### 6.2 — Cross-References to CDL-1 in Other Parts

All references to CDL-1 and its universal combat floor in other Canon Parts and ledger files are treated as superseded by Part 35. When encountered in prose authorship context, any CDL-1 citation should be internally redirected to the CDL-2 framework. Part 30 holds no CDL-1 text; this Area is the retirement notice.
