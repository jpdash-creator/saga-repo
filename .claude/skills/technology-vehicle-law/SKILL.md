## Overview

Part 32 is the Starward Saga Canon's Vehicle Law — the governing domain for vehicle identity, classification, and governance at every scale of the setting, from the hand-pulled cart of a Tier 1 village to the universe-crossing Celestial Ark of a Tier 15–16 civilization. Part 15 (Travel Law) defines how travel works — speeds, transit times, FTL drive physics, gate mechanics, infrastructure, and travel cost; Part 32 defines what a vehicle *is*: its paradigm band, tier, scale class, configuration, propulsion system, construction prerequisites, operational law, combat classification, economic position, and cultural presence in prose. The two Parts are joint authorities over every technology travel scene.

Area 1 establishes the classification architecture the rest of the Part depends on: the five Transport Paradigm Bands (ST/OS/IS/GT/CT) corresponding to Part 15's five technology travel paradigms, the seven-level Scale Class hierarchy (SC-I Personal Scale through SC-VII Civilization Scale), the twelve Configuration Types (MIL, MIL-CAP, PAT, FRT, PAS, EXP, COL, MED, DIP, DUL, DUL-H, SYN) with their combat-law status under Part 5, and the canonical [Band]-[Tier]/[Scale Class]/[Config Code] notation. Areas 2–6 populate the five bands with named vehicle classes: Surface Transport (Area 2, Tiers 1–5), Orbital and Inner-System Transport (Area 3, Tiers 5–7), Interstellar Transport (Area 4, Tiers 8–10), Galactic Transport (Area 5, Tiers 11–14), and Cosmological Transport (Area 6, Tiers 15–20 — partially deferred scope requiring Part 28 authorization for narrative use). Area 7 defines the five Cultivation-Hybrid Vehicle classes (CHV-A through CHV-E); Area 8 the six-category Propulsion Systems Taxonomy (P-I through P-VI); Area 9 the multi-domain construction prerequisites; Area 10 the operational law of crews, fuel, and maintenance; Area 11 vehicle combat classification within Part 5's escalation framework; Area 12 vehicle economic law; Area 13 vehicle-infrastructure integration; Area 14 vehicle narration standards; Area 15 the cross-Part dependency master table; Area 16 the Part 15 joint-governance boundary; and Area 17 the domain-ownership disambiguation against Parts 15, 8, 12, and 5.

## Area 1 — Vehicle Classification System

### 1.1 — The Five Transport Paradigm Bands

All vehicles in the Starward Saga are classified within one of five transport paradigm bands that correspond to the five technology travel paradigms defined in Part 15, Area 8.1. The paradigm band is the primary organizational category — it establishes the scale, environment, and physics context within which the vehicle operates.

| Paradigm Band | Designation | Technology Tier Range | Operational Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface Transport | ST-Band | Tiers 1–5 | Planetary surface, atmosphere, ocean; no sustained orbital operation. The three Tier 5 transition craft (EVC-2, ASC-3, PDC-2) are listed in Area 2 by tier and operate under OS-Band rules |
| Orbital and Inner-System Transport | OS-Band | Tiers 5–7 | Orbit to stellar inner system; no FTL; subluminal inter-planetary |
| Interstellar Transport | IS-Band | Tiers 8–10 | Between star systems; FTL or near-c drive required; includes gate-capable vessels |
| Galactic Transport | GT-Band | Tiers 11–14 | Galaxy-scale and intergalactic; high-FTL lanes and gate network integration |
| Cosmological Transport | CT-Band | Tiers 15–20 | Universe-scale and beyond; partially deferred scope |

### 1.2 — Vehicle Scale Class

Within each paradigm band, vehicles are further classified by their **Scale Class** — an independent dimension from tier that defines the physical size, crew complement, and throughput capacity of the vehicle. Scale Class uses a universal designation system of seven levels (SC-I through SC-VII) applicable across all bands.

| Scale Class | Designation | Typical Size | Crew Range | Payload/Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC-I | Personal Scale | 1–3 m length | 1 operator | Single-person transit, solo operations |
| SC-II | Light Scale | 3–20 m length | 1–6 | Small crew; courier, scout, personal transport |
| SC-III | Medium Scale | 20–100 m length | 6–50 | Commerce, patrol, short-range operations |
| SC-IV | Heavy Scale | 100–500 m length | 50–500 | Military patrol, medium freight, extended operations |
| SC-V | Capital Scale | 500 m–5 km length | 500–10,000 | Major warships, large freighters, colony vessels |
| SC-VI | Fleet Scale | 5–50 km length | 10,000–100,000 | Fleet carriers, megafreighters, station-ships |
| SC-VII | Civilization Scale | 50 km+ length | 100,000+ | Mobile civilizations, generation arks, orbital fortresses |

### 1.3 — Vehicle Configuration Type

Every vehicle also carries a configuration type that describes its primary operational purpose. Configuration type governs legal and narrative treatment under Part 5's combat law.

| Configuration Code | Full Name | Combat Status | Primary Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIL | Military Configuration | Combat vessel (designated combat vessel — DC) | Offensive/defensive operations; Part 5 combat rules apply |
| MIL-CAP | Capital Military | DC — apex combat vessel | Fleet command, system denial, civilization-scale combat |
| PAT | Patrol Configuration | DC — limited engagement authority | Law enforcement, customs, system patrol |
| FRT | Freight Configuration | Non-Combat Civilian (NC) | Cargo transport; protected by combat law as civilian target |
| PAS | Passenger Configuration | NC | Civilian passenger transit; highest protection classification |
| EXP | Exploration Configuration | NC (science/survey) | Survey, first contact, scientific investigation |
| COL | Colony Configuration | NC (humanitarian scale) | Population transit, colonial establishment |
| MED | Medical Configuration | NC (protected) | Emergency response, medical evacuation; protected as medical asset |
| DIP | Diplomatic Configuration | NC (protected) | Diplomatic transit; immune from interdiction under standard law |
| DUL | Dual-Use Light | DU — civilian-primary | Commercial vessel with civilian credentials but limited defensive capability |
| DUL-H | Dual-Use Heavy | DU — armable | Major commercial or government vessel configurable for active combat if armed |
| SYN | Synergy Configuration | Special — see Part 31 | Cultivation-Technology hybrid; governed by Part 31 for synergy outputs |

### 1.4 — Canonical Vehicle Notation

The canonical notation for any Starward Saga vehicle combines its paradigm band, tier, scale class, and configuration:

**[Band]-[Tier]/[Scale Class]/[Config Code]**

Examples:
- `ST-3/SC-IV/FRT` — Surface Transport, Tier 3 construction, Heavy Scale, Freight Configuration (a steamship freighter)
- `IS-9/SC-V/MIL` — Interstellar Transport, Tier 9 construction, Capital Scale, Military Configuration (a Tier 9 warship)
- `GT-11/SC-VI/FRT` — Galactic Transport, Tier 11, Fleet Scale, Freight Configuration (a galaxy-spanning megafreighter)

This notation is the canonical reference format for all vehicle specifications within the Starward Saga Canon. When authors reference specific vehicles in narrative notes or plot documents, this notation provides unambiguous identification.

## Area 2 — Surface Transport Vehicles (ST-Band, Tiers 1–5)

### 2.1 — Vehicle Type Philosophy: ST-Band

Surface Transport vehicles exist within the gravitational well and atmosphere of a single planetary body. They are bound by atmospheric physics, terrain constraints, and the energy limits of pre-orbital technology. The ST-Band spans from the first wheeled cart to the hypersonic orbital shuttle — from Tier 1 primitive transport through Tier 5, where the boundary between atmosphere and orbit begins to dissolve.

ST-Band vehicles are organized into five operational categories: **Ground Vehicles**, **Watercraft**, **Atmospheric Craft**, **Hybrid Ground-Atmospheric**, and **Sub-Orbital Transitionals**. Each category has defined naming conventions unique to the Starward Saga universe.

---

### 2.2 — Tier 1 Ground Vehicles (ST-1/SC-I to SC-III)

**Category: Earthbound Conveyances (EBC)**

*EBC-1 Trailwalker:* Single-person wheeled or skid-based cart, hand-pulled or animal-pushed. No mechanical propulsion. SC-I. Used for personal goods transport in pre-road civilizations. Speed: 3–6 km/h. Range: terrain-constrained.

*EBC-2 Beastdray:* Animal-drawn wheeled platform of variable size. The standard heavy transport of Tier 1 civilizations. SC-I to SC-II. Pulled by domesticated draft animals (oxen, horses, water buffalo equivalents, or alien-equivalent beasts). Speed: 5–8 km/h sustained. Load capacity: 0.5–2 tons. The backbone of agricultural and trading economies.

*EBC-3 Ridebeast:* Animal-mounted transport — the cultivator's horse equivalent. SC-I (rider only). Speed: 10–60 km/h depending on beast training and cultivation enhancement. An EBC-3 ridden by a powerful cultivator has speeds modified by the rider's cultivation ability.

*EBC-4 Riverskiff:* Primitive watercraft for river and coastal transit. Dugout, reed-hull, or plank-bound construction. SC-I to SC-II. Propelled by oar, pole, or early sail. Speed: 3–12 km/h.

*EBC-5 Coastal Runner:* Early sailing watercraft, ocean-capable but without deep-water navigation. SC-II to SC-III. Lateen or square sail configurations. Speed: 5–15 km/h wind-dependent. Range: coastal routes, 200–360 km per day in favorable conditions.

---

### 2.3 — Tier 2 Ground and Water Vehicles (ST-2/SC-I to SC-IV)

**Category: Road-Bound Vessels (RBV)**

Tier 2 introduces systematic road infrastructure, bridge networks, and the social institution of organized trade routes. Vehicles become standardized in a way that Tier 1's artisanal constructions are not.

*RBV-1 Express Carriage:* Four-wheeled enclosed passenger carriage, horse-drawn on organized road networks. SC-II. The personal transit vehicle of the Tier 2 noble class and wealthy merchant. Speed: 15–25 km/h on maintained roads. Comfort features: suspension systems, enclosed passenger cabin, weather protection. In-world cultural significance: express carriage ownership signals social status in CPT-1 through CPT-4 civilizations.

*RBV-2 Merchant Hauler:* Large horse-drawn or oxen-drawn goods wagon with reinforced frame and weatherproofing. SC-III. The workhorse of Tier 2 commerce. Cargo capacity: 2–5 tons. Speed: 10–18 km/h. Typically travels in caravans for security, making the *Merchant Hauler convoy* a standard feature of Tier 2 trade culture.

*RBV-3 Relay Post Rider:* Single-rider courier system utilizing organized relay horse stations at 50–80 km intervals. SC-I. Courier-speed transit across road networks. Not a vehicle class as such — it is a *system* of EBC-3 Ridebeasts and road infrastructure. Speed: 300–500 km/day via relay chains. This is the fastest Tier 2 information and small-package transit system.

*RBV-4 River Barge:* Large flat-bottomed river watercraft for bulk cargo transit, horse-pulled or sail-assisted upstream, current-assisted downstream. SC-III to SC-IV. The most cost-efficient Tier 2 freight system for river-connected regions. Cargo capacity: 20–100 tons. Speed: 8–20 km/h downstream; 4–10 km/h upstream (towed or sailed).

**Category: Blue-Water Vessels (BWV)**

*BWV-1 Deep-Sea Merchantman:* Ocean-going sailing ship with full deep-water navigation capability, multi-mast configuration. SC-III to SC-IV. The flagship vessel of Tier 2 maritime trade. Cargo capacity: 100–800 tons. Speed: 10–20 km/h in favorable conditions. Duration at sea: weeks without resupply. Range: global ocean transit via prevailing wind routes.

*BWV-2 War Galleon:* Military configuration deep-sea vessel, armed with long-range projectile weapons (ballistae, early cannon equivalents), reinforced hull, elevated firing platforms. SC-IV. The Tier 2 apex naval combat vessel. Combat range: 200–500 m. Crew: 100–400. The BWV-2 War Galleon is the primary platform for naval E3–E4 escalation events in Tier 2 civilizations.

*BWV-3 Fast Courier Sloop:* Light, swift sailing vessel optimized for speed over cargo. SC-II to SC-III. The diplomatic, courier, and scout vessel of Tier 2 naval operations. Speed: 5–15 km/h in ideal conditions. Small crew (10–30), minimal cargo, maximum sail-to-hull ratio.

---

### 2.4 — Tier 3 Industrial Vehicles (ST-3/SC-I to SC-V)

**Category: Ironway Runners (IWR) — Rail Vehicles**

The introduction of steam power at Tier 3 creates the first technology-driven (non-biological-propulsion) vehicle class, and with it, the first standardized mass-transit network.

*IWR-1 Ironhull Freight Train:* Steam or early combustion engine locomotive pulling a chain of enclosed cargo wagons on fixed rail track. SC-IV as a complete train unit. Cargo capacity per train: 500–2,000 tons. Speed: 50–100 km/h. The defining infrastructure of Tier 3 continental economic integration. An *Ironhull* train requires the rail network to exist — without the network, the vehicle has no operational territory.

*IWR-2 Passenger Express:* Steam/combustion locomotive configured for passenger transit with enclosed heated/cooled cabins, defined schedules, multi-class boarding. SC-IV. Speed: 80–150 km/h on premium rail. Capacity: 200–1,000 passengers per train. The *Passenger Express* is the first truly mass public transit system — and in many Tier 3 civilizations, it becomes the backbone of the national identity, connecting previously isolated regional cultures.

*IWR-3 Military Transport:* Rail vehicle configured for rapid troop and equipment deployment. SC-IV to SC-V. Reinforced chassis, wide-gauge military rail. Can deploy a full military division (10,000+) across continental distances in 24–48 hours via coordinated rail network use.

**Category: Road-Bound Engine Vehicles (REV)**

*REV-1 Personal Groundrunner:* Early internal combustion engine personal vehicle (equivalent to early automobile). SC-I. Speed: 40–80 km/h (Tier 3 early) to 120–200 km/h (Tier 3 advanced). Range: 300–800 km per fuel fill. The first personal mechanized transport — a status object in Tier 3 societies before becoming a commodity.

*REV-2 Cargo Hauler Truck:* Heavy combustion/diesel engine freight vehicle for road-based cargo transit. SC-II to SC-III. Cargo capacity: 5–50 tons. Speed: 60–120 km/h. Replaces the Merchant Hauler as the primary overland freight vehicle where rail is absent.

*REV-3 Armored Warcrawler:* Early military armored vehicle — tracked or wheeled, with direct-fire weapon systems and armor plating. SC-II to SC-III. The Tier 3 ground combat vehicle. Speed: 30–80 km/h. Part 5 combat: the Warcrawler functions as a heavy combat asset at E3–E4 escalation range; its technology Force Category maps to a Mortal Tier 2 Senior–Warrior equivalent in destructive output. The Warcrawler is categorized MIL/PAT.

**Category: Sky-Bound Craft (SBC) — Early Atmosphere**

*SBC-1 Light Airskimmer:* Early propeller or early jet aircraft, single to twin engine, limited range. SC-I to SC-II. Speed: 150–500 km/h. Range: 1,000–5,000 km. The first viable atmospheric transit beyond the ground. Used initially for reconnaissance and courier roles before civilian adoption.

*SBC-2 Transport Skycarrier:* Multi-engine atmospheric aircraft designed for passenger or cargo transit. SC-III. Speed: 400–900 km/h. Capacity: 20–200 passengers or 5–30 tons cargo. The first instrument of practical intercontinental transit without maritime infrastructure.

*SBC-3 War-Wing:* Military-configured fixed-wing aircraft with weapons systems, from early fighters to early bombers. SC-I to SC-III depending on configuration. Part 5 combat: air warfare enters the E3–E4 range at Tier 3; formation-level War-Wing operations enter E5 at Tier 3 apex. Classified MIL.

**Category: Deep-Water Vessels (DWV)**

*DWV-1 Steam Ironhull:* Steam-powered iron or steel-hulled oceangoing vessel replacing sail. SC-IV. Speed: 25–45 km/h. Cargo capacity: 2,000–15,000 tons. The industrial-era freighter that makes truly global trade economically viable.

*DWV-2 Ironhull Warship:* Steel-hulled, steam-powered military warship with long-range projectile and torpedo weapon systems. SC-IV to SC-V. The apex Tier 3 naval combat vessel. Direct successor of the BWV-2 War Galleon but orders of magnitude more destructive. Part 5 combat: fleet-scale naval combat at Tier 3 enters E5.

*DWV-3 Deeprunner Submarine:* Submarine vessel capable of extended submerged operations. SC-III to SC-IV. Speed: 30–60 km/h submerged. Depth: up to 500 m. The first stealth military naval platform — undetectable to surface vessels without acoustic detection infrastructure.

### 2.5 — Tier 4 Advanced Atmospheric Vehicles (ST-4/SC-I to SC-V)

**Category: Highwind Craft (HWC) — Advanced Atmosphere**

*HWC-1 Stratosleeker:* Advanced high-speed jet aircraft for personal, courier, or luxury transit. SC-I to SC-II. Speed: 800–1,500 km/h. Range: 5,000–15,000 km. The global-reach personal transit vehicle of Tier 4 — used by high-status individuals, government officials, and wealthy private parties.

*HWC-2 Mass Skyvessel:* Large jet aircraft for mass passenger transit. SC-III to SC-IV. Speed: 850–1,200 km/h. Capacity: 150–500 passengers. The backbone of Tier 4 global passenger transit. The HWC-2 is the vehicle that makes global transit accessible to non-elite populations for the first time.

*HWC-3 Cargo Skyvessel:* Jet-powered heavy cargo aircraft. SC-III to SC-IV. Cargo capacity: 50–300 tons. Speed: 700–1,000 km/h. Range: 8,000–20,000 km per flight.

*HWC-4 Precision Strike Warwing:* Advanced military jet aircraft with precision-guided weapon systems, electronic warfare capabilities, and stealth coating equivalents. SC-I to SC-II. Speed: Mach 2–4. The apex Tier 4 atmospheric combat vehicle. Part 5 combat: a single HWC-4 provides E3–E4 combat output; coordinated formations reach E5. Classified MIL.

**Category: Subterranean Rail (STR)**

*STR-1 Undercity Express:* Underground electromagnetic rail transit system for urban and metropolitan mass transit. SC-III to SC-IV (as a system). Speed: 80–200 km/h. Capacity per train: 200–2,000 passengers. The STR-1 is the backbone of Tier 4 urban mobility — its existence or absence defines urban livability at this tier.

*STR-2 Continental Velocity Rail:* High-speed magnetic levitation rail (maglev) for intercontinental passenger transit. SC-IV. Speed: 350–800 km/h. The fastest ground transit of Tier 4, competing with HWC-2 Skyvessel routes economically. Range: continental with transfer hubs.

**Category: Early Void Craft (EVC)**

*EVC-1 Orbital Climber:* Early rocket or electromagnetic launch vehicle capable of reaching low orbit. SC-II to SC-III. Not a true spacecraft — cannot maneuver in orbit; launch only. Speed at apogee: 7.9 km/s (orbital velocity). The first vehicle to leave the planetary atmosphere intentionally. Cultural significance: enormous — the EVC-1 Orbital Climber is typically the most-celebrated technological milestone of Tier 4, equivalent to the first ocean-crossing vessel for Tier 2 civilizations.

*EVC-2 Orbital Station:* Not a transit vehicle but an orbital infrastructure platform — the fixed base that early space-capable vehicles dock at. SC-IV to SC-V in scale. Governs the first permanent human presence off the surface.

**Category: Deepwater Apex Vessels (DAV)**

*DAV-1 Nuclear Ironhull:* Nuclear-powered naval warship of enormous scale and range. SC-V. Speed: 60–100 km/h. Operational duration: months without refueling. The apex Tier 4 naval combat platform, carrying aircraft, missile batteries, and electronic warfare suites. Part 5 combat: a single DAV-1 fleet operates at E5–E6.

*DAV-2 Deep Pressure Sub:* Nuclear or advanced-propulsion submarine capable of operating at extreme depths (>5,000 m). SC-III to SC-IV. The apex undersea stealth combat vehicle at Tier 4. Armed with long-range strike weapons, capable of operating undetected for months.

### 2.6 — Tier 5 High Atmosphere and Early Space Vehicles (ST-5/SC-II to SC-VI)

**Category: Ascendancy Craft (ASC) — Orbital Transition**

*ASC-1 Orbital Shuttle:* Reusable surface-to-orbit vehicle capable of routine transition between planetary surface and low orbital space. SC-III. Speed: 7.9–11 km/s during ascent. Transit time surface to orbit: 8–30 minutes. The first *routine* space access vehicle — not a research mission, but a transit commuter to orbital infrastructure.

*ASC-2 Skyhopper:* Hypersonic atmospheric transit vehicle capable of surface-to-surface planetary transit via ballistic arc or suborbital skip trajectory. SC-II to SC-III. Speed: Mach 10–25 (12,000–30,000 km/h). Any point on a planet: 30 minutes to 2 hours. The ASC-2 makes the planet feel small — a Tier 5 civilization with broad ASC-2 deployment has effectively connected all its surface populations into a single transit system.

*ASC-3 Interplanetary Scout:* Small, fast spacecraft capable of chemical or early ion drive transit between inner system planetary bodies. SC-II. Speed: 20–100 km/s. Range: inner stellar system. The first dedicated spacefaring vehicle with destination-specific mission profiles.

**Category: Planetary Defender Craft (PDC)**

*PDC-1 Atmospheric Defense Wing:* Advanced atmospheric military aircraft capable of operating at the edge of atmosphere and contributing to orbital defense. SC-II to SC-III. The Tier 5 military vehicle that bridges ground air defense and orbital defense. Part 5: E4–E5.

*PDC-2 Early Orbital Defense Platform:* Fixed or semi-mobile orbital weapons platform. SC-IV to SC-V. The first dedicated military infrastructure in space. Not a transit vehicle — a weapon system in orbit. Part 5 combat: orbital defense platforms enter E5–E6 range.

**Category: Surface Mastery Vehicles (SMV)**

*SMV-1 Groundmaster Maglev:* Magnetic levitation surface transport with intercontinental routing and speeds exceeding conventional aircraft for surface-bound routes. SC-IV. Speed: 1,000–5,000 km/h on dedicated tubes. Any surface point on the planet: hours. The SMV-1 makes surface transit competitive with atmospheric routes by eliminating all terrain constraints via tube/tunnel networks.

*SMV-2 Deep Arcology Vessel:* Mobile surface habitat capable of sustained cross-terrain travel — a city that moves. SC-V to SC-VI. Used in hostile surface environments, military deployment zones, or frontier planetary surfaces where fixed settlement is premature. Speed: 5–50 km/h depending on terrain system.

## Area 3 — Orbital and Inner-System Transport Vehicles (OS-Band, Tiers 5–7)

### 3.1 — Vehicle Type Philosophy: OS-Band

OS-Band vehicles operate between planetary bodies within a single star system — from orbital altitude above a planet through the full reach of the outer system. They are subluminal vessels operating in the vacuum of space, powered by drives ranging from chemical rockets through fusion and antimatter systems. The OS-Band is where vehicles first become truly three-dimensional, where the concept of "a ship" transitions from a surface metaphor to a genuine three-dimensional space vessel, and where military vessel classes first develop the doctrine of space combat.

OS-Band vehicles are organized into four primary operational categories: **System Runners** (civilian transit), **System Warcraft** (military), **System Workers** (infrastructure and industrial), and **Deep Station Platforms** (fixed or near-fixed infrastructure).

---

### 3.2 — Tier 5–6 Inner System Vessels

**Category: System Runner Vessels (SRV)**

*SRV-1 Orbital Commuter:* Small personnel transit vehicle for low-orbit to station and station-to-station transit. SC-II. Speed: 7.9–50 km/s orbital range. Capacity: 4–20 passengers. The space-equivalent of a city bus — mundane but essential for orbital infrastructure workers and commuters. Crew: 1–2.

*SRV-2 Inner-System Clipper:* Passenger transit vessel for inner planetary body transit (planet to moon, inner to middle planets). SC-III to SC-IV. Speed: 100–500 km/s (fusion drive). Transit time: 3.5–17 days per AU. Capacity: 20–200 passengers. The standard short-to-medium range civilian people-mover.

*SRV-3 System Merchant:* Civilian cargo transport for bulk goods between inner planetary bodies. SC-IV to SC-V. Speed: 100–300 km/s. Cargo capacity: 5,000–50,000 tons. Transit time: 6–17 days per AU. The backbone of inner-system commerce.

*SRV-4 Luxury System Liner:* High-comfort passenger transit vessel with extended life support, entertainment and hospitality infrastructure, scenic routing capability. SC-IV. Capacity: 100–500 passengers. The transit vessel for wealthy individuals and diplomatic parties in inner-system circuits.

**Category: System Warcraft (SWC)**

*SWC-1 System Patrol Craft:* Small, fast military vessel for customs, law enforcement, and system patrol operations. SC-II to SC-III. Speed: 500–2,000 km/s (performance drive). Weapons: light direct-fire and guided munitions. Crew: 5–30. Configuration: PAT. The everyday law enforcement vehicle of a Tier 5–6 civilization's system authority.

*SWC-2 System Strike Fighter:* SC-I to SC-II single or two-person military attack craft optimized for speed, maneuverability, and weapons capacity in inner-system engagement ranges. Speed: 1,000–5,000 km/s burst. Combat doctrine: dogfighting, escort, strike. Part 5 combat: SWC-2 fighters operate in E3–E4 as individual units; coordinated squadrons reach E5.

*SWC-3 Corvette-Class Warship:* SC-III medium warship with balanced speed, firepower, and crew. The standard small military vessel of a Tier 5–6 star system defense force. Crew: 30–150. Armament: medium-range weapons array. Part 5 combat: E4–E5 individually.

*SWC-4 Destroyer-Class Warship:* SC-IV military warship with heavy weapons load and anti-capital-ship capability. Crew: 150–600. Speed: lower than a corvette but significantly higher firepower. The backbone combat vessel of most Tier 5–6 system defense fleets.

*SWC-5 Battleship-Class Warship:* SC-V capital warship — the apex Tier 5–6 military vessel. Crew: 600–5,000. Immense weapons capacity, heavy armor equivalent, flagship-capable command infrastructure. Part 5 combat: a single Battleship-class operates at E5–E6. Classified MIL-CAP.

**Category: System Workers (SWK)**

*SWK-1 Orbital Constructor:* Specialized vessel with extended manipulator arms, micro-thrust precision control, and construction material bays for assembly of orbital infrastructure. SC-III to SC-IV. Not a transit vehicle — an industrial vehicle that operates at the intersection of vehicles and infrastructure.

*SWK-2 System Tanker:* Bulk fuel and resource carrier, typically slower than passenger vessels but with enormous cargo capacity. SC-IV to SC-V. The vehicle that makes refueling stations viable.

*SWK-3 Salvage and Recovery Vessel:* Multi-role utility craft for space debris clearance, ship recovery, and emergency response. SC-III. The space-equivalent of a tow truck and ambulance combined.

*SWK-4 Deep Miner:* Mining vessel for asteroid belt and outer-planet resource extraction. SC-III to SC-IV. Heavy drill and processing equipment; extremely slow transit (mining-optimized, not speed-optimized). The primary resource extraction vehicle for civilizations whose planetary surface resources are exhausted.

### 3.3 — Tier 7 Full System Vessels

**Category: System Range Runners (SRR)**

*SRR-1 Deep System Explorer:* Survey vessel optimized for outer system transit — heliopause-capable, extended mission endurance, science instrumentation and probe launch capability. SC-III to SC-IV. Speed: 2,000–10,000 km/s. Mission duration: 1–5 years. Crew: 20–100. The vehicle of first exploration of a star system's full extent.

*SRR-2 Outer-System Freighter:* Heavy cargo vessel for outer-system resource depots and stations. SC-IV to SC-V. Speed: 500–2,000 km/s. Cargo capacity: 50,000–500,000 tons. Transit time for 50 AU route: 1.4–5.7 months. The economic lifeline for outer system industrial operations.

*SRR-3 Frontier Colony Ship:* SC-V to SC-VI vessel designed to transport a population colony of 5,000–100,000 to a destination within the same stellar system. Not yet capable of interstellar transit, but represents the maximum civilian transit unit of Tier 5–7 civilizations for within-system colonization.

**Category: Full System Warcraft (FSW)**

*FSW-1 Fleet Carrier:* SC-VI capital vessel carrying 100–1,000 SWC-2 Strike Fighters and supporting combat vessels. The full-system military apex platform of Tier 7 civilizations. Crew: 10,000–80,000. Part 5 combat: a Fleet Carrier with full wing represents E6–E7 level destructive output. Classified MIL-CAP.

*FSW-2 System Fortress:* Not a transit vessel — a heavily armed, lightly mobile orbital defense installation. SC-VI to SC-VII. Designed for permanent system denial rather than fleet maneuver. Armed with planet-facing and deep-space-facing weapons capable of engaging entire enemy fleets without moving significantly. Part 5 combat: E7 installation-scale.

## Area 4 — Interstellar Transport Vehicles (IS-Band, Tiers 8–10)

### 4.1 — Vehicle Type Philosophy: IS-Band

IS-Band vehicles operate between star systems. They require propulsion systems that either approach or exceed the speed of light — near-c drives, FTL drive systems, or gate-transit capability. The IS-Band is the civilization-defining tier of vehicle technology: a civilization that cannot field IS-Band vehicles is star-bound; one that can is multi-stellar.

IS-Band vehicles are organized into: **Void Runners** (civilian transit), **Void Warcraft** (military), **Void Explorers** (science/survey), **Void Carriers** (colonization), and **Gate-Capable Vessels** (the specialized class that can transit star gates).

---

### 4.2 — Tier 8 Interstellar Vessels — The Threshold Class

**Category: Threshold Vessels (TV)**

Tier 8 vehicles are the first to cross the interstellar threshold. They are massive, expensive, and slow by later standards — but they are the beginning of multi-stellar civilization.

*TV-1 Generation Ark:* SC-V to SC-VII near-lightspeed vessel designed for multi-generational voyages to neighboring star systems. Speed: 0.05–0.3c. Transit time to nearest stars: 15–80 years subjective. Crew/population: 5,000–500,000. A Generation Ark is not merely a vehicle — it is a society in transit. It contains its own ecosystems, governance structures, educational systems, and cultural institutions. The vehicle must sustain all aspects of civilized life for decades.

*TV-2 Rapid Projection Vessel:* Near-c high-speed military or exploration craft at 0.5–0.9c. SC-III to SC-V. Transit time to nearest stars: 4–8 years objective. Small crew (20–500). Used for first-contact missions, military projection of presence, and scientific advance missions ahead of colony fleets. Relativistic time dilation becomes significant: crew who completes a 0.9c round-trip mission ages less than a decade while 20+ years pass at home.

*TV-3 FTL Research Vessel (late Tier 8):* Early FTL prototype — limited range (≤5 light-years reliable), imprecise navigation (±1–5 AU arrival error), not suitable for routine operations. SC-II to SC-IV. Crew: 5–100. The first FTL-capable craft are not production vehicles — each one is essentially a research platform. Governed by Part 9, Section 4.1.1 (Prototype-State Technology Governance).

**Category: Pre-FTL Military Projection (PMP)**

*PMP-1 Battle Ark:* Military-configured Generation Ark — a combination colony ship and warship capable of bringing a full military force to another star system over decades. SC-V to SC-VII. The "slow knife" of Tier 8 military doctrine: not fast, but unstoppable once launched. Arriving at a target system with a full army is decisive even if it took 40 years.

---

### 4.3 — Tier 9 FTL Vessels — The Network Class

**Category: Lightleaper Vessels (LLV) — The Standard Tier 9 FTL Ship**

Tier 9 FTL becomes routine. Ships are designed from the outset for FTL operation, not modified from subluminal frames. The naming convention *Lightleaper* applies to the full range of Tier 9 standard FTL craft.

*LLV-1 Lightleaper Scout:* SC-II fast FTL vessel for survey, courier, and advance reconnaissance. FTL speed: 50–200× light. Transit time to nearest star: 8–24 days at baseline FTL. Crew: 5–30. The fastest vessel in a Tier 9 fleet and the first to arrive anywhere.

*LLV-2 Lightleaper Clipper:* SC-III FTL passenger and light cargo vessel. The courier and premium transit vehicle of Tier 9 civilization. Capacity: 20–150 passengers or 500–3,000 tons light cargo. FTL speed: 100–500× light. The Tier 9 equivalent of an express airline route.

*LLV-3 Lightleaper Merchant:* SC-IV to SC-V FTL cargo freighter. Cargo capacity: 10,000–200,000 tons. FTL speed: 50–200× light (cargo-optimized, slower than scout). Transit time between star systems: 1–8 weeks. The economic spine of Tier 9 civilization — without the LLV-3, there is no multi-system trade economy.

*LLV-4 Lightleaper Colony Ship:* SC-V to SC-VI FTL colony transport carrying populations of 5,000–100,000. FTL capable; transit time between systems: weeks rather than generations. This ends the Generation Ark era — colonization becomes viable on decadal rather than generational timescales.

*LLV-5 Lightleaper Hospital Ship:* SC-IV FTL medical vessel, equipped with the most advanced medical facilities of Tier 9 civilization. Classified MED. Transit time to any system in the network: weeks. The first time emergency medical response becomes possible across star systems.

*LLV-6 Lightleaper Diplomatic Vessel:* SC-III to SC-IV, configured DIP. Designed for inter-system diplomatic missions with secure communication arrays, formal reception spaces, and enhanced defensive systems. Classified DIP.

**Category: Void Warcraft Tier 9 (VWT)**

*VWT-1 FTL Interceptor:* SC-II high-speed military FTL vessel for rapid response, border patrol, and pursuit operations. FTL speed: 200–1,000× light (military premium drives). Crew: 10–60. Weapons: precision strike, interdiction systems. Part 5 combat: E3–E4 individually; used in squadrons at E5.

*VWT-2 FTL Frigate:* SC-III military FTL vessel — the workhorse patrol and enforcement ship of Tier 9 multi-star military doctrine. Crew: 60–300. Balanced weapons, FTL speed, and endurance. Part 5 combat: E4–E5.

*VWT-3 FTL Cruiser:* SC-IV heavy military FTL vessel with dedicated weapons arrays, extended mission capability, and command facilities. Crew: 300–2,000. Part 5 combat: E5–E6.

*VWT-4 FTL Dreadnought:* SC-V apex military FTL vessel — the highest combat output single vessel of Tier 9 doctrine. Crew: 2,000–15,000. Heavy weapons systems, layered defense, fleet command infrastructure. Part 5 combat: E6 as a single vessel; E7 in formation with supporting fleet.

*VWT-5 FTL Carrier Group:* SC-VI fleet vehicle carrying hundreds of VWT-1 Interceptors and VWT-2 Frigates. The apex Tier 9 military deployment platform. Crew: 15,000–100,000. A Carrier Group is a mobile star-system-threatening installation. Classified MIL-CAP.

**Category: Jump-Drive Specialized Vessels (JDV)**

A sub-class of Tier 9 vehicles using Jump Drive architecture (point-to-point instantaneous jumps to mapped gravity anchors) rather than continuous FTL propulsion. See Part 15, Area 14.1 for Jump Drive mechanics.

*JDV-1 Jump Runner:* SC-II to SC-III rapid point-to-point courier using Jump Drive for instantaneous system-to-system hops. Range per jump: ~20 ly to charted gravity anchor. Transit time between mapped systems: minutes. Limitation: jump destinations must be pre-mapped; unmapped territory requires approach via conventional FTL first.

*JDV-2 Jump Freighter:* SC-IV cargo vessel using Jump Drive for near-instantaneous freight delivery between established jump-point routes. The fastest cargo delivery in Tier 9 network commerce, but infrastructure-dependent.

### 4.4 — Tier 10 Stellar Cluster Vessels — The Gate Class

**Category: Cluster-Range Vessels (CRV)**

Tier 10 FTL becomes colony-scale infrastructure. The defining feature of Tier 10 vehicles is gate-transit capability — the ability to pass through Tier 10 star gate structures.

*CRV-1 Gate Runner:* SC-II to SC-III vehicle specifically designed for high-frequency gate transit. Optimized hull profile for gate aperture clearance (max 1–10 km diameter). Speed outside gate network: 1,000–5,000× light. Speed via gate: near-instantaneous. Crew: 10–100. The workhorse of gate-network commerce and transit.

*CRV-2 Cluster Merchant:* SC-IV to SC-V cargo vessel gate-optimized with modular cargo systems for rapid loading/unloading at gate hub stations. Cargo capacity: 100,000–1,000,000 tons. The container ship of Tier 10 civilization.

*CRV-3 Cluster Liner:* SC-IV to SC-V luxury passenger vessel designed for both gate-transit and long-haul FTL routes within a star cluster. Capacity: 500–5,000 passengers. The premiere transit vehicle for high-status civilian travel in Tier 10 civilizations.

*CRV-4 Deep Cluster Explorer:* SC-III to SC-IV survey vessel for mapping unmapped regions of a star cluster beyond the gate network frontier. Combines FTL with comprehensive sensor arrays for cartographic operations.

**Category: Gate Warcraft (GWC)**

*GWC-1 Gate Guard:* SC-III to SC-IV military vessel permanently stationed at a star gate to control transit access. Part 5 combat: E4–E5 baseline; an E7 engagement is achievable if the gate itself is weaponized. The most strategically important military vessel in Tier 10 doctrine — controlling gates IS controlling civilization.

*GWC-2 Gate Breaker:* SC-V specialized military vessel carrying weapons systems specifically designed to destroy enemy gate structures. Part 5 combat: E6–E7. Gate destruction is classified under Part 5's Annihilation Conflict tier regardless of original engagement escalation. Classified MIL-CAP.

*GWC-3 Cluster Battlefleet Carrier:* SC-VI apex Tier 10 military platform — a fleet carrier that is itself gate-capable, carrying 500–2,000 strike craft plus capital warships. The full Cluster Battlefleet Carrier represents civilization-scale destructive capability. Part 5 combat: E7. Classified MIL-CAP.

## Area 5 — Galactic Transport Vehicles (GT-Band, Tiers 11–14)

### 5.1 — Vehicle Type Philosophy: GT-Band

GT-Band vehicles operate at galactic scales — across thousands to millions of light-years. They require drives that make even Tier 10 FTL look slow, and gate networks that span a full galaxy. At this tier, vehicles are not merely machines — they are extensions of civilizational will, each one representing centuries of industrial and scientific investment.

---

### 5.2 — Tier 11 Galactic Vessels

**Category: Galactic Traverse Vessels (GTV)**

*GTV-1 Galactic Scout:* SC-III rapid FTL survey vessel for galactic mapping and first-contact operations. FTL speed: 50,000–200,000× light. Transit time to galactic core (50,000 ly): 3–10 months. Crew: 20–200. The tip of the spear of Tier 11 civilizational expansion.

*GTV-2 Galactic Merchant:* SC-V to SC-VI cargo vessel for inter-civilizational trade across galactic distances. Cargo capacity: 1,000,000–10,000,000 tons. FTL speed (cargo-optimized): 10,000–50,000× light. The vehicle of galaxy-spanning economic systems.

*GTV-3 Galactic Ark:* SC-VI to SC-VII population carrier for civilizational-scale migration across galactic distances. Population capacity: 100,000–10,000,000. Transit time (50,000 ly): weeks via gate network, months via raw FTL. The vehicle of galactic civilizational diaspora.

*GTV-4 Galactic Gate Tender:* SC-V specialized construction and maintenance vessel for Tier 11 gate network infrastructure. Carries gate construction components, maintenance crews, and emergency repair capability for the most strategically critical infrastructure of galactic civilization.

**Category: Galactic Warcraft (GWF)**

*GWF-1 Galactic Warship:* SC-V to SC-VI capital warship for galactic-scale military operations. Part 5 combat: a single GWF-1 typically operates at E7 — its weapons systems have civilization-scale destructive potential. Classified MIL-CAP. A GWF-1 requires Part 28 authorization before deployment as a narrative element in active storylines.

*GWF-2 Galactic Fortress Ship:* SC-VII mobile fortress — a vessel that is simultaneously a warship, a military headquarters, and a self-sustaining civilization node. Crew: 100,000–1,000,000. Classified MIL-CAP. Requires Part 28 authorization.

### 5.3 — Tiers 12–14 Intergalactic Vessels

**Category: Expanse Travelers (ET)**

*ET-1 Intergalactic Runner:* SC-IV rapid FTL vessel for transit between galaxies within a galaxy cluster. FTL speed sufficient to cross 1,000,000 ly in weeks via established intergalactic lanes.

*ET-2 Supercluster Transit Ship:* SC-V to SC-VI long-haul vessel for transit across galaxy superclusters. Wormhole-network-dependent for practical transit at Tier 13.

**Category: Wormhole-Transit Vessels (WTV)**

At Tier 13, wormhole technology (permanently open spatial tunnels, unlike gates which require both ends active) enables a new vehicle category: vessels optimized specifically for wormhole transit.

*WTV-1 Wormhole Clipper:* SC-III to SC-IV high-speed vessel sized for wormhole aperture transit. Optimized for rapid back-and-forth transit through established wormhole networks.

*WTV-2 Wormhole Freighter:* SC-V cargo vessel for bulk inter-galactic-cluster freight via wormhole network. The equivalent of the CRV-2 Cluster Merchant at wormhole scale.

## Area 7 — Cultivation-Hybrid Vehicle Classes

### 7.1 — Overview of Cultivation-Hybrid Vehicles

A Cultivation-Hybrid Vehicle (CHV) is any vehicle that integrates cultivation-derived technologies — formation arrays, CPM power systems, soul-interface controls, or relic components — into its core operational architecture. CHVs are joint domain objects governed simultaneously by this Part (vehicle identity and classification), Part 12 (formation arrays and relic objects), Part 11 (CPM fuel), and Part 31 (synergy outputs).

CHVs exist at all technology tiers where cultivation and technology civilizations intersect. Their defining characteristic is that they cannot be fully understood or operated without knowledge of both cultivation and technology governance.

### 7.2 — CHV Classification Tiers

| CHV Class | Name | Integration Depth | Technology Tier Range | Cultivation Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CHV-A | Formation-Assisted Vehicle | Light integration: formation arrays supplement technology propulsion | Tiers 3–7 | Xiantian minimum for formation maintenance |
| CHV-B | Formation-Primary Vehicle | Formation arrays are primary propulsion; technology provides structural and life support | Tiers 5–9 | Core Realm (subluminal); Divine Transformation (fold drive) minimum for primary formation propulsion |
| CHV-C | Soul-Interface Vehicle | Vehicle control directly interfaces with pilot's soul cultivation for navigation and weapons | Tiers 8–12 | Core Realm soul rung minimum (Part 4, Area 2) |
| CHV-D | Synergy-Class Vehicle | Full cultivation-technology integration: Part 31 Model C or D synergy; vehicle is a combined cultivation-technology existence | Tiers 9–15 | Heaven Treading minimum for full synergy activation |
| CHV-E | Primordial-Class Vehicle | Vehicle construction uses Tier 4 divine relics and Primordial-scale cultivation formations | Tiers 13–20 | Divine Sea Realm minimum |

### 7.3 — Formation-Assisted Vehicle (CHV-A) Details

CHV-A vehicles supplement conventional technology propulsion with formation array systems. Common applications:
- **Speed Enhancement Formations:** Formation arrays that reduce drag, enhance thrust, or provide temporary velocity boosting. Effective in atmospheric transit and inner-system travel.
- **Shielding Formations:** Defensive formation arrays replacing or supplementing conventional armoring.
- **Energy Efficiency Formations:** Formation arrays that reduce conventional fuel consumption by supplementing with CPM energy feed.

Rule 7.3.1 — CHV-A formations are governed by Part 12 as objects and Part 11 for their CPM fuel requirements. The vehicle tier remains determined by the technology construction tier, not the formation tier.

Rule 7.3.2 — A CHV-A vehicle cannot exceed its Part 12 formation array's output ceiling. The formation is the limit, not an infinite enhancement.

### 7.4 — Formation-Primary Vehicle (CHV-B) Details

CHV-B vehicles use formation arrays as primary propulsion, with conventional technology providing structural integrity, life support, and non-propulsion systems. The signature vehicle type of CHV-B is the **Formation Drive Vessel**:

*Formation Drive Vessel (FDV):* A spacecraft that uses a Tier 2–3 (Part 12) formation array as its subluminal drive (Part 15 Qi-Thrust Array), or a Tier 4 array inscribed by a Divine Transformation master as its fold drive (Part 15 Spatial-Fold Array Drive). Because formation arrays can produce spatial manipulation outputs (dimensional stepping, spatial compression), an FDV with a fold drive can achieve effective FTL-equivalent speeds through spatial manipulation rather than physical propulsion. The FDV is the dominant vessel type in cultivation-heavy civilizations of Tier 6–9, where formation arts may outpace raw technology in specific domains.

Rule 7.4.1 — FDV spatial transit is governed by Part 12, Area 7.2 for the array itself, and by Part 15, Area 8 for the travel mechanics. The FDV does not bypass FTL physics — it achieves FTL-equivalent results through spatial manipulation, a distinct mechanism.

Rule 7.4.2 — FDV capability scales with inscriptor realm. The range, speed, and safety of formation-drive transit are functions of the soul cultivation rung and formation mastery of the inscribing practitioner. An FDV inscribed by a Divine Realm formation master has fundamentally different capabilities from one inscribed by a Core Realm practitioner, even if both are installed in identical physical hulls.

### 7.5 — Soul-Interface Vehicle (CHV-C) Details

CHV-C vehicles achieve direct neural-cultivation integration between pilot and vessel — not a control interface but a genuine extension of the pilot's cultivation sense and intent through the vehicle's systems. Key properties:

- **Sense Extension:** A CHV-C pilot with adequate soul cultivation can extend their soul sense throughout the entire vessel — perceiving through sensor arrays, detecting threats via soul sense amplified by the vehicle's formation infrastructure.
- **Intent Navigation:** The vehicle responds to cultivated intent rather than mechanical input. Navigation and weapons fire are controlled as naturally as the pilot controls their own body movements.
- **Realm Scaling:** A higher-realm pilot extracts more capability from the same CHV-C hull. A Core Formation pilot controlling a CHV-C achieves approximately 60% of the vessel's maximum capability; a Heaven Treading pilot controlling the same vessel achieves its full designed ceiling.

Rule 7.5.1 — CHV-C soul interface damage (damage to the interface formation while the pilot is connected) causes soul feedback injury. The soul cultivation damage is governed by Part 4, Area 2 (soul damage taxonomy). This is a narration-critical rule: destroying a CHV-C vehicle while its pilot is soul-interfaced causes automatic soul damage.

### 7.6 — Synergy-Class Vehicle (CHV-D): The Apex Hybrid

CHV-D Synergy-Class vehicles represent the apex of cultivation-technology vehicle integration. They are jointly governed by Part 31 (Synergy Law) for their synergy outputs, Part 12 for their formation array objects, and Part 32 for their vehicle identity.

Defining characteristics:
- **Dual-Track Propulsion:** Both conventional FTL technology and formation-drive spatial manipulation operate simultaneously, providing redundancy and allowing the pilot to choose optimal propulsion for each context.
- **Sacred Ground Amplification:** Operating a CHV-D within a Sacred Ground zone provides the Sacred Ground Synergy Step-Up (Part 31, Area 10) — an effective CI Technology level one higher for threshold purposes (Part 31, Area 2.3).
- **Integrated Combat:** CHV-D weapons systems integrate cultivation technique outputs directly — a Heaven Treading pilot can execute Named Move-equivalent attacks through the vehicle's weapons arrays. Part 6 governs the technique classification; Part 5 governs the combat escalation; Part 32 governs the vehicle platform.

*Synergy Dawncraft:* The canonical Tier 9–11 CHV-D class vehicle. SC-III to SC-IV hull. Combines FTL propulsion with Tier 3 formation-primary drive systems and CHV-C soul interface. The vehicle most associated with integrated practitioners (Part 31, Area 12) in the Starward Saga narrative vocabulary.

## Area 8 — Propulsion Systems Taxonomy

### 8.1 — Propulsion Classification Framework

Every vehicle has a primary propulsion system that determines its operational physics, fuel requirements, and capability ceiling. Propulsion systems are classified into six categories, each with named subtypes unique to the Starward Saga universe.

### 8.2 — Category P-I: Chemical and Thermal Drives (Tiers 1–5)

*P-I-1 Biomechanical Drive:* Animal muscle power, human muscle power. ST-Band only. No fuel cost independent of biology.

*P-I-2 Aerodynamic Drive:* Wind and current exploitation via sail, rudder, and hull design. ST-Band, water and air environments.

*P-I-3 Combustion Drive:* Internal combustion, steam engine, diesel engine. Chemical fuel (refined hydrocarbons). Tiers 3–5. The first mechanical drive not dependent on weather.

*P-I-4 Rocket Drive:* Chemical rocket propellant generating thrust through directed exhaust. Tiers 4–6. The bridge between atmospheric and orbital transport.

### 8.3 — Category P-II: Fusion and Antimatter Drives (Tiers 5–8)

*P-II-1 Helion Fusion Drive:* Fusion reactor generating continuous thrust through controlled helium-isotope fusion. High thrust efficiency. Tiers 5–7. The standard inner-system propulsion of OS-Band civilizations. Named for the iconic helion isotope reaction cycle.

*P-II-2 Torchfire Drive:* Continuous-thrust fusion rocket with very high exhaust velocity. Tiers 6–8. Provides near-constant acceleration rather than impulse thrust — the "always-on" drive that makes outer-system transit practical.

*P-II-3 Annihilator Drive:* Matter-antimatter annihilation drive. Tier 7–8. Maximum specific impulse achievable with matter-based propulsion. Expensive to fuel (antimatter production costs); extraordinary performance. Near-c transit is achievable with Annihilator drives.

*P-II-4 Coherence Drive:* Advanced energy-to-thrust system using highly coherent electromagnetic energy as propulsion. Tiers 7–9. Fuel-efficient; high precision; often used in conjunction with FTL jump systems.

### 8.4 — Category P-III: FTL Drives (Tiers 8–12)

*P-III-1 Warpstream Drive:* Contracts space ahead of the vessel, expands space behind — the vessel "surfs" a spatial wave at apparent FTL speeds without locally exceeding light speed. High energy cost; continuous drive; good at long-haul sustained transit. The dominant FTL drive of Tier 9 commercial vessels.

*P-III-2 Riftjump Drive:* Creates a brief dimensional transition into an adjacent spatial layer with compressed distances, then returns to normal space at a displaced location. The "Hyperspace" equivalent. Instantaneous transit per jump but requires jump point identification; range limited by transition energy. Common in military doctrine for its unpredictability.

*P-III-3 Vaultfold Drive:* The Fold Drive equivalent — compresses space around the vessel through brute-force spatial manipulation, achieving effective FTL. Very energy intensive; range limited; high precision in arrival. Preferred for precision operations.

*P-III-4 Gravitation Jump Drive:* Exploits naturally occurring gravitational anomalies (jump points) for instantaneous point-to-point transit. Infrastructure-dependent (requires mapped jump points). Maximum range per jump ~20 ly (Tier 9 baseline). The Jump Drive vehicle class. Instantaneous transit but destination-constrained.

### 8.5 — Category P-IV: Formation Drive Systems (CHV Propulsion) (Tiers 5–20)

*P-IV-1 Spatial Step Array:* Formation array that generates localized spatial folding — the formation-drive equivalent of a Fold Drive. Drive range determined by inscriptor realm. The canonical propulsion of CHV-B and CHV-D vessels.

*P-IV-2 Void Compression Array:* Formation array generating continuous spatial compression in the vessel's direction of travel — formation equivalent of the Warpstream Drive but governed by formation laws rather than technology physics.

*P-IV-3 Dimensional Anchor Engine:* Formation array creating a gravitational anchor in the vehicle's destination space and pulling the vehicle toward it — range-limited by formation tier but with no fuel cost beyond CPM.

### 8.6 — Category P-V: Advanced and Post-FTL Drives (Tiers 11–20)

*P-V-1 Galactic Voidstream Drive:* High-efficiency FTL drive operating at 50,000–2,000,000× light. Tiers 11–14. Required for practical galactic-scale transit without gate reliance. High power consumption; requires Tier 11+ energy technology for continuous operation.

*P-V-2 Wormhole Transit System:* Not a drive — the vehicle modification that enables transit through constructed wormhole infrastructure. Tiers 13+. Requires compatible entry/exit alignment systems.

*P-V-3 Existence Transit Engine:* Tier 17–20 propulsion concept — the vehicle interacts with existence itself as a medium, rather than space as a substrate. Joint governance with Part 10 Science Law.

### 8.7 — Category P-VI: Gate Transit Systems

Gate transit is not a vehicle drive — it is an infrastructure access system. Vehicles transit through gates as passive objects; the gate generates the traversal. However, vehicles require a Gate Transit Interface (GTI) to safely pass through gate apertures:

*GTI-1 Standard Gate Interface:* Standard gate-compatible hull shielding and transition buffer systems for Tier 10 technology gates. Required for any vessel transiting a technology star gate.

*GTI-2 Formation Teleportation Interface:* Compatible hull modifications for CHV vehicles transiting Formation Teleportation Arrays (Part 12, Part 15). Governed by Part 12 (the FTA) and Part 32 (the vehicle's interface systems).

## Area 10 — Vehicle Operational Law

### 10.1 — Crew Governance Principles

**Rule 10.1.1 — Minimum Crew Certification:** Operating any vehicle above SC-II at IS-Band or above requires certified crew trained specifically for that vehicle class's drive systems, navigation architecture, and emergency protocols. Part 9's institutional prerequisite model (Area 3.2) governs crew training infrastructure.

**Rule 10.1.2 — Cultivator Crew and Vessel Tier:** A cultivator's realm does not substitute for technical training in operating IS-Band and above vehicles. A Divine Realm cultivator without navigation training cannot operate an LLV-3 Lightleaper Merchant's FTL drive safely. Cultivation enhances what trained crews can do; it does not replace the training. Exception: CHV-C and CHV-D vehicles where soul-interface directly substitutes for some conventional training requirements.

**Rule 10.1.3 — Realm Augmentation of Vehicle Performance:** Cultivators operating vehicles within their realm compatibility range can push vehicle performance beyond design specifications through direct qi/energy injection. However:
- Performance boost ceiling: +30% above design maximum for Xiantian; +60% for Core Realm; +150% for Divine Realm
- Each instance of performance boost causes structural stress (tracked narratively)
- Accumulated structural stress requires repair before the next boost; ignoring accumulated stress produces failure events
- Part 3's energy expenditure rules apply: qi-boosting a vehicle is an expenditure event

### 10.2 — Vehicle Resource and Fuel Governance

**Rule 10.2.1 — Fuel Is Not Optional:** Every vehicle has defined fuel requirements. Narrative that removes or contradicts a vehicle's fuel need (a drive with no fuel, unlimited range, a refuel that changes nothing) is a breach of canon that only a [CANON AMENDMENT] (guardrails/commands/guardrail-command-law.md) can permit. The fuel type and consumption rate for each vehicle class are defined by its propulsion category (Area 8).

| Propulsion Category | Fuel Type | Consumption Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| P-I (Chemical/Thermal) | Biological or refined chemical | Continuous consumption during operation |
| P-II (Fusion/Antimatter) | Fuel isotopes or antimatter | High burn during acceleration; minimal during coast |
| P-III (FTL Drives) | Exotic energy (Type 7 Level 6+) | Large consumption at FTL initiation; sustained during transit |
| P-IV (Formation Drive) | CPM (grade determined by array tier) | Continuous CPM consumption during formation drive operation |
| P-V/Gate Transit | Major energy infrastructure | Gate activation cost is enormous; individual vessel contributes zero — the gate is powered by civilization-scale infrastructure |

**Rule 10.2.2 — CPM Fuel Grade Compatibility (CHV Vehicles):** Formation Drive vehicles require CPM grade matched to their array tier per Part 11 and Part 12. Mismatched CPM grade causes absorption conflict equivalent (Part 11, Area 7.6) in the vehicle's energy systems — not in biological meridians, but in the formation energy pathways. This causes degraded performance and eventual formation damage.

### 10.3 — Vehicle Maintenance and Degradation

**Rule 10.3.1 — Technology Ceiling and Vehicle Degradation:** Per Part 8, P8-2, technology above the civilization's Technology Ceiling degrades. A civilization that loses one tier of technology advancement cannot maintain its highest-tier vehicles — they begin to degrade within years and become non-functional within decades without the industrial infrastructure to service them (Part 9, Area 8.2).

**Rule 10.3.2 — Formation Component Degradation:** Formation array components in CHV vehicles degrade differently from technology components. Formation arrays that are not re-inscribed by a qualified practitioner at their maintenance interval lose effectiveness at 5–10% per year until they fall below functional threshold. This is the cultivation-technology hybrid maintenance burden: you need both a qualified engineer AND a qualified formation master for sustained CHV operation.

## Area 11 — Vehicle Combat Classification

### 11.1 — Vehicle Combat in the Part 5 Framework

All vehicle combat occurs within Part 5's engagement and escalation framework. Part 32 governs the vehicle capabilities; Part 5 governs the legitimacy, escalation, and outcome structure of all combat involving vehicles. The following rules govern the integration:

**Rule 11.1.1 — Vehicle Tier Maps to Force Category:** Part 8, Area 8.2's Technology Force Category table applies to all vehicles. The table determines what cultivation-equivalent combat output a vehicle of a given tier produces. A vehicle does not have a different Force Category from its tier — the tier IS the Force Category.

**Rule 11.1.2 — Configuration Code Governs Engagement Legitimacy:** Under Part 5, Force Legitimacy Law, a vehicle's Configuration Code determines its status:
- MIL and MIL-CAP vessels are legitimate combat targets in any active E3+ engagement.
- NC-configured vessels (FRT, PAS, EXP, COL, MED, DIP) are protected civilian targets. Engaging them outside defined combat contexts is an unlawful use of force under Part 5.
- DU-configured vessels are MIL-equivalent once they activate weapons systems; NC-equivalent when weapons are decommissioned.

**Rule 11.1.3 — Scale Class Suggests a Typical Escalation Tier:** Vehicles above SC-IV typically engage at E4 or above when deployed as combat assets, and SC-VI and SC-VII vehicles at E6 or above. These are typical values, not binding minimums: Part 5 sets the tier from participant count, scope, duration, faction commitment and stated objectives (Part 5 Area 5.1). The reference steers narrative use of massive military vessels away from "small skirmish" framing unless the story gives a reason.

## Area 14 — Vehicle Narration Standards

### 14.1 — Vehicle Introduction Standards

**N32-1 — First Appearance Consistency:** When a vehicle type appears for the first time in a narrative, it is usually presented with sensory detail proportional to its significance: a Tier 2 carriage may be a sentence of visual description; a Tier 9 FTL warship may be a paragraph of spatial presence — its scale, the sound of its drives, the light it reflects or emits, the environmental effect of its arrival or departure (guidance; the Chapter Spec sets pacing and size). Vehicle introduction follows Part 2's deep-POV immersion requirements.

**Worked example (Tier 2 carriage, two sentences, model only; the wording is not to be reused):**

> The carriage stood in the inn yard with the mud of a long road on its wheels, its black lacquer gone dull as rain cloud and a gilt crest on the door that the stable boys took care not to look at. When the driver let down the step, the springs gave a long soft sigh, and she understood from that sound alone what kind of family kept it.

**N32-2 — Vehicle Scale Consistency:** Once a vehicle's scale class is established, all subsequent narrative references must maintain consistency with that scale. An SC-V vessel cannot be "a small ship" in one scene and then "the enormous flagship" in another without in-universe explanation (such as observational perspective shift).

**N32-3 — Technology Tier Specificity:** Authors do not narrate vehicles with technology anachronisms. A Tier 3 civilization's vehicles reflect Tier 3 capability — not Tier 5 or Tier 7. If a character encounters a vehicle inconsistent with the local civilization's tier, that inconsistency is a narrative signal (Remnant technology, contraband, alien origin) that must be acknowledged in the character's perception.

### 14.2 — Vehicle Transit Narration

**N32-4 — Transit Experience Has Phenomenology:** Vehicle travel is not invisible. A Warpstream FTL transit creates the visual phenomenon of starfield compression; a Riftjump creates the subjective experience of a brief dimensional transition (described per the Part 15 phenomenology standards for that drive type). Surface vehicle transit has weather, terrain sound, the sensation of motion. Vehicle travel is always sensory.

**Worked example (Tier 5 shuttle launch, two sentences, model only; the wording is not to be reused):**

> The engines struck up beneath her like a dropped anvil, and the weight on her chest climbed until the straps were the only thing holding her ribs apart from the seat. Then the roar fell away all at once, the window went from blue to black, and her pencil lifted off her knee and turned slowly in the air.

**N32-5 — FTL Transit Renders Through Characters:** FTL transit in a vehicle is experienced by the characters aboard, not narrated from outside. A passenger aboard an LLV-2 Lightleaper Clipper entering warp experiences the transition — the hum of the Warpstream Drive, the slight visual compression as the drive activates, the transition to the characteristic starfield distortion of warp transit. Authors use Part 15's FTL phenomenology guidance (Area 19) and Part 32's propulsion taxonomy together.

**Worked example (Warpstream entry, two sentences, model only; the wording is not to be reused):**

> A hum came up through the deck plates and settled in her back teeth, and the stars in the forward port drew in toward one another as if a hand were closing. When the hum steadied they had stretched into long pale threads that slid past the glass, and the man across the aisle let out a breath he had been holding since the count began.

**N32-6 — Vehicle Damage Renders Truthfully:** Vehicle damage in combat must reflect the actual scale and capability of the vehicle. An SC-V Dreadnought does not "shudder" and carry on after taking a weapons hit that would be classified as an E5-scale event. Damage is proportional; structural failure is catastrophic at scale.

### 14.3 — Cultural Vehicle Narration

**N32-7 — Vehicle as Cultural Artifact:** Part 25's culture governance applies to vehicles. A Tier 2 noble's express carriage carries social status (CPT-1/CPT-4 prestige object). A Tier 9 diplomatic vessel embodies the civilization's design aesthetic, material culture, and technological self-image. Vehicles are not neutral objects — they carry cultural meaning that authors render through the POV character's perception.

**N32-8 — Vehicle Name Conventions by Culture:** Cultivation civilizations name their vehicles differently from purely technological civilizations. CS-Type civilizations (Cultivation-Sovereign) name vessels with cultivation-resonant terminology; TS-Type (Technology-Sovereign) use technical/engineering nomenclature; BP-Type (Balanced-Path) blend both. Authors follow the cultural conventions of the civilization building the vehicle when naming specific vessels in narrative.

## Area 16 — Vehicle Law and Part 15 Joint Governance Clarification

### 16.1 — Governance Boundary Statement

The governance boundary between Part 15 (Travel Law) and Part 32 (Vehicle Law) is defined as follows:

**Part 15 Governs:**
- What travel speeds and transit times are achievable at each technology tier
- What FTL drive types exist and how they physically operate (Area 14)
- What gate network mechanics govern gate transit (Area 13)
- What infrastructure exists and its travel effect (Area 16)
- Travel cost and resource consumption during transit (Area 17)
- Narration standards for depicting travel (Area 19)

**Part 32 Governs:**
- What specific named vehicle types exist at each tier
- How vehicles are classified, designated, and notated
- What scale classes vehicles belong to
- What construction prerequisites are required
- What propulsion systems power vehicles and how those systems are named
- What cultivation-hybrid configurations exist
- What the vehicle's internal operational and crew governance is
- What the vehicle's typical combat escalation tier is (Area 11)
- What the vehicle's economic and market position is (Area 12)

**Joint Governance:**
- All narrative scenes involving a technology vehicle in transit require both Parts simultaneously
- All CHV vehicle scenes require Part 12 (formation array objects) and Part 32 (vehicle) and Part 31 (synergy output) simultaneously
- Vehicle combat requires Part 5 (combat framework) and Part 32 (vehicle capabilities) simultaneously

### 16.2 — Conflict Resolution

If any specification in Part 32 conflicts with Part 15 regarding travel speeds or mechanics, **Part 15 is authoritative** — it is the domain authority for travel mechanics. Part 32 vehicle specifications may not exceed Part 15's capability tables for their corresponding tier.

If any conflict exists between Part 32's vehicle combat classification and Part 5's escalation framework, **Part 5 is authoritative** for the combat outcome. Part 32's typical escalation tier references (Area 11) are derived from Part 5, not independent of it.

If any conflict exists between Part 32's CHV classification and Part 12's formation array tier, **Part 12 is authoritative** for the formation array object. Part 32 governs the vehicle; Part 12 governs the formation.

## Area 17 — Vehicle Law Domain Ownership and Disambiguation

### 17.1 — This Part vs. Part 15 (Travel Law)

Part 15 owns travel mechanics; Part 32 owns vehicle classification. An author asking "How fast can a Tier 9 ship travel?" consults Part 15. An author asking "What type of ship is this, what does it look like, what is its scale class, and what is it named?" consults Part 32. An author asking "What happens when this ship enters warp?" consults both Parts simultaneously.

Part 8 owns technology tier classification at the civilization level; Part 32 owns vehicle classification at the vehicle level. Part 8 Type 9 establishes the 20-level framework that Part 32 expands. Part 8's Technology Force Category table is authoritative for combat output; Part 32's Area 11 typical escalation tier references derive from that table and cannot override it.

### 17.3 — This Part vs. Part 12 (Energy Relics and Artifacts Law)

Formation arrays aboard CHV vehicles are Part 12 objects. Their tier, output ceiling, and collapse behavior are all governed by Part 12. Part 32 governs the vehicle that houses them and the interaction between the formation and the vehicle's overall operational envelope.

### 17.4 — This Part vs. Part 5 (Combat and Fight Law)

All vehicle combat is governed by Part 5's engagement framework. Part 32 provides the force output and combat capability data that Part 5's framework operates on. Part 32 cannot authorize combat escalation or determine combat legitimacy — only Part 5 can.
