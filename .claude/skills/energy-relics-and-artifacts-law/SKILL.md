## Overview

Part 12 is the Starward Saga Canon's Energy, Relics, and Artifacts Law — the governing domain for every powered non-substance object in the world: relics, artifacts, formation arrays, and enchanted objects. Part 11 governs the raw CPM substances — what they are, how they form, their states and grades. Part 12 governs the constructed objects that use CPM as raw material: what those objects are, how they are made, what power they can express, who can safely activate them, how they are owned and bonded, and how they must appear on the page.

## Area 1 — Object Classification System

### 1.1 — The Four Object Classes

All objects governed by this Part fall into one of four classes. Classification is not about power level — it is about the nature of how the object stores and expresses energy.

---

**Class I — Relics**

A Relic is an object whose power was imprinted through extended immersion in extremely high-density energy fields rather than through deliberate crafting inscription. Relics form naturally (Naturally Formed Relics) or acquire power through proximity to a specific event or being over long periods (Historically Imprinted Relics). The key characteristic of a Relic is that its power architecture is holistic and emergent — it was not designed by a crafter but shaped by the energy conditions of its formation.

*Naturally Formed Relics:* Physical matter — stone, metal, crystal, bone, wood — that has spent centuries in a high-class Formation Zone (Part 11, Area 3; Part 13) and gradually absorbed CPM energy until it achieved a structural crystallization. These are not CPM substances — they are objects that have absorbed so much CPM energy over time that their physical structure has been permanently altered. The result is an object that continues to emit, focus, or channel energy without requiring an external power source. Natural Relics are rare, unpredictable in function, and cannot be fully specified in advance.

*Historically Imprinted Relics:* Objects that have acquired power through prolonged contact with an extremely powerful cultivator, presence during cosmological events (major battles, Divine Sea Realm manifestations, mass death events), or extended residence in Sacred Grounds. A sword that a Divine Realm cultivator used daily for ten thousand years may become Historically Imprinted — it carries the cultivator's technique patterns, their qi signature, their intent. When a cultivator of compatible realm and affinity holds it, the sword resonates and produces output that the current holder's cultivation alone could not generate.

---

**Class II — Artifacts**

An Artifact is a deliberately crafted object whose power is the result of intentional construction — inscription of energy patterns, embedding of CPM materials, binding of formation arrays, or infusion of cultivator energy during manufacture. Artifacts are designed with specific functions and have predictable performance within their design parameters.

All four construction methods detailed in Area 3 (Inscription, Tempering, Formation Binding, and Soul Architecture) produce Artifacts. The crafter's cultivation level determines the maximum tier achievable.

---

**Class III — Formation Arrays**

A Formation Array is a constructed spatial architecture — a network of energy-conducting elements (formation stones, CPM nodes, inscription lines, spatial anchors) that defines a zone and produces an ongoing energy effect within it. Formation Arrays are not single objects — they are systems. A cultivation chamber's five-wall inscription matrix is a Formation Array. A sect's defensive perimeter is a Formation Array. A Grand Array that covers a continent is a Formation Array.

Formation Arrays are classified separately from Artifacts because their governance involves spatial law (Part 13), faction territorial law (Part 22), and combat law (Part 5) in ways that single-object relics do not. Their cultivation environment effects are governed by Part 3, Area 14.6. Their destruction in combat is governed by Part 5.

---

**Class IV — Enchanted Objects**

An Enchanted Object is an ordinary-material object that has been given a single, simple energy imprint — a minor permanent effect at low energy cost. A lantern that burns with qi-fire indefinitely without fuel. A garment that repels rain. A lock that opens only for a specific qi signature. Enchanted Objects are the widest category and the lowest tier. They are not cultivation tools in a serious sense — they are the background material culture of a cultivation civilization.

---

### 1.2 — Terminology Standards

| Term | Formal Meaning | Common Usage | Note |
|------|---------------|--------------|------|
| Relic | Naturally formed or historically imprinted power object | Any high-tier cultivation object | The common "relic" usage (for any powerful cultivation object) is colloquially acceptable in prose |
| Artifact | Deliberately crafted cultivation object | Spirit Tool, Spirit Weapon, Magic Weapon | In-world names vary by faction; all map to Artifact class |
| Formation Array | Spatial energy architecture | Array, Formation, Matrix | "Formation" alone often refers to a Formation Array in-world usage |
| Enchanted Object | Low-tier single-imprint ordinary object | Enchanted gear, magic item | Lowest class; used as background culture detail |
| Treasure | Colloquial catch-all for any high-value cultivation object | Used loosely | Not a formal class; maps to Relic or Artifact by formal classification |

## Area 2 — The Five Classification Tiers

### 2.1 — The Tier System

All objects governed by this Part are classified into one of five Tiers based on their effective power level, construction material requirement, and the cultivation realm required for safe activation. The Tier system maps directly to the Major Realm structure of Part 3, Area 1.

---

**Tier 1 — Mortal Grade**

*Power range:* Equivalent to Mortal Tier 2 cultivator output (Heavenly Scale Grades 1–5)
*Construction material:* Power Crystal infusion (Grades 5 to 6) as minimum; peak Tier 1 artifacts use a Gem of Grades 1 to 5
*Activation requirement:* Safe activation by Mortal Tier 2 Senior and above; forced activation possible (with injury risk) by exceptional Mortal Tier 1 Soul-stage cultivators.
*Lifespan:* Centuries without significant energy decay if properly stored
*Rarity:* Common; actively manufactured by cultivation societies

*What Tier 1 objects can do:* Enhance physical attributes within Mortal Tier 2 parameters, produce technique-equivalent energy effects at Mortal Tier 2 output, provide basic protective energy fields, store small volumes of CPM, generate cultivation environments at Class 2–3 ambient energy density, and perform all standard quality-of-life functions (qi-fire, preservation, identification, minor sensing).

*Examples:* A steel sword with qi-conducting inscriptions that increases the wielder's technique output by 15%. A storage ring that holds physical objects in a small spatial pocket. A cultivation chamber formation array that produces Class 3 ambient energy density. A diagnostic formation that identifies a person's Meridian Root class.

---

**Tier 2 — Xiantian Grade**

*Power range:* Equivalent to Xiantian cultivator output (Heavenly Scale Grades 6–9)
*Construction material:* Gem infusion (Grades 6 to 7) minimum; high-Xiantian artifacts require a Shard of Grades 1 to 5
*Activation requirement:* Safe activation by Xiantian Demigod and above; forced activation by a cultivator below that realm follows the Activation Safety table in Area 4.2.
*Lifespan:* Millennia without significant decay
*Rarity:* Uncommon to rare; major sect heirlooms; battlefield-grade weapons

*What Tier 2 objects can do:* Produce energy effects at Xiantian output levels, create spatial pockets large enough to inhabit, generate Class 4–5 cultivation environments, provide passive defense against Mortal Tier 2 attacks without cultivator activation, enable sensing at extended range, and begin to exhibit spatial properties (dimensional storage, minor spatial distortion).

*Examples:* A shield that generates a Demigod-class defensive field when activated. A spatial ring large enough to contain a small building. A sect formation array that provides Class 4–5 cultivation environment across the entire sect compound. An assessment stone that reads affinity and soul sensitivity.

---

**Tier 3 — Core Grade**

*Power range:* Equivalent to Core Realm cultivator output (Heavenly Scale Grades 10–12)
*Construction material:* Shard infusion (Grades 6 to 7) minimum; peak Core artifacts require a Core infusion
*Activation requirement:* Safe activation by Core Formation and above; forced activation by a cultivator below that realm follows the Activation Safety table in Area 4.2.
*Lifespan:* Tens of millennia; many approach permanence
*Rarity:* Rare; national strategic resources; legacy weapons of the most powerful sects

*What Tier 3 objects can do:* Produce output at geological scale, create large inhabited spatial dimensions (rooms, estates, mountain ranges within a spatial artifact), sustain Class 6 cultivation environments indefinitely, perform surgical healing at the level of dantian damage repair, suppress lower-tier formation arrays, and begin to exhibit reality-adjacent properties (time dilation within small spatial constructs, enhanced causal sensing).

*Examples:* A spear whose strike produces a Heavenly Scale Grade 11 Core Roar shockwave when a Core Realm cultivator activates it (a weaker wielder gets the lower ceiling of Area 4.1). A spatial ring containing a full mountain valley. A healing formation capable of stabilizing dantian structural damage. A grand formation array that creates a Class 6 Vortex environment across a territory.

---

**Tier 4 — Divine Grade**

*Power range:* Equivalent to Divine Realm cultivator output (Heavenly Scale Grades 13–17)
*Construction material:* Core infusion (Grades 4 to 6) or Divine Liquid; construction requires a Divine Realm crafter
*Activation requirement:* Safe activation by Divine Transformation and above; forced activation by a cultivator below that realm follows the Activation Safety table in Area 4.2.
*Lifespan:* Effectively permanent under normal conditions; requires active destruction to break
*Rarity:* Extremely rare; most are historically imprinted rather than crafted; each known Divine-grade artifact is individually documented in cultivation civilization's historical records

*What Tier 4 objects can do:* Produce reality-altering effects, manipulate space-time at local scale, contain and express Divine-tier force, cross the Major Realm barrier in offensive applications (the cross-Major-Realm exception referenced in Part 3, Rule 17.3.3(b)), perform miracles of healing (including partial lifespan restoration and soul shattering recovery), house Divine Liquid safely, and house entire dimensional constructs large enough to contain civilizations.

*Examples:* The cultivation world's legendary weapons — swords capable of cutting through divine sea extensions, shields that have absorbed divine energy strikes, spatial artifacts containing entire hidden realms inhabited by thousands of cultivators, healing artifacts that can restore decades of lifespan lost to Internal Reversal.

---

**Tier 5 — Primordial Grade**

*Power range:* Beyond classification; the Heavenly Scale Grade system becomes inadequate as a descriptor
*Construction material:* Materials whose origin predates the current world's energy system, or Divine Liquid as the minimum detectable component
*Activation requirement:* Unclear; behavior varies by object; some activate on proximity, some require specific lineage or affinity, some respond to soul cultivation rung
*Lifespan:* Permanent by definition; Primordial Relics are older than cultivation civilization
*Rarity:* Near-mythic; most are known only through historical record; many have not been confirmed as existing in the current age

*What Tier 5 objects can do:* Primordial Grade objects operate outside the normal power scaling of the cultivation world. Their effects may include: permanent alteration of a location's physical laws (hastening or deepening a Sacred Ground that Divine Sea Realm presence has begun), suppression of Divine Sea Realm cultivators, time manipulation at more than local scale, fundamental affinity shifts in living beings, awakening of Transcendent Spectrum talent in those who lack it, and interaction with the universe's fundamental energy in ways that cannot be governed by any Part of the SSC Canon; any such effect needs the author's authorization under Part 28 (Creative Flexibility Law).

*The Narrative Rule for Tier 5:* Primordial Grade objects must never appear as tools. They are catalysts, inheritance challenges, and narrative anchors — objects that change the story's trajectory rather than solve combat problems. Their appearance must be authorized under Part 28 (Creative Flexibility Law).

### 2.2 — Tier Identification in Prose

**Rule 2.2.1 — Introduce Tier Before Function.**
In narrative, a relic's Tier (or the sensory cues that indicate Tier) must be established before its function is deployed. The reader must understand the object's power class before they witness its effect. A character cannot produce a devastating Core-grade artifact attack without the prose having established that the object in question is Core-grade — through sensory description, another character's assessment, or environmental response to its presence.

**Rule 2.2.2 — In-World Assessment Language.**
Characters in the Starward Saga use in-world language to describe relic tiers: "Mortal-grade" and "Xiantian-grade" spirit tools are common parlance. Higher tiers are typically referred to by origin or reputation rather than tier label: "a relic from the Core Realm ancestor's treasury," "the Divine Weapon that ended the War of Broken Heaven," etc. Tier labels are assessment tools for the author; in-world language uses reputation, origin, and sensory description.

**Rule 2.2.3 — Named Relics and Named Artifacts.**
High-tier relics and artifacts of narrative significance are Named. Named Relics, Named Artifacts, named Formation Arrays and named Energy Resource Caches are set in italic (plain italic, never bold and never bold-italic; Part 28 Rule 20.8.2), and each is registered in the named-item register `ledger-named-items` (Type relic or artifact, formation array, or energy cache) with a pointer to its descriptive entry in `ledger-energy-relics-artifacts`, which stays the home of every descriptive fact (Part 37 Area 3.8). Named Moves stay bold-italic under Part 2 Rule 3.2.1. "She raised the *Heaven-Shattering Edge*" — not "she raised her sword." Named status indicates narrative significance, not merely Tier. The registered name never includes the leading article: the article stays in plain type before the italic name (`the *Heaven-Shattering Edge*`, and `The *Heaven-Shattering Edge*` at the start of a sentence; Part 28 Rule 20.8.2(b)). A manual is a named item and not a Named Relic or Artifact, unless this Part registers that one manual as a relic (Part 42 Rule 42.1.1.3).

## Area 3 — Creation Methods and Crafting Law

### 3.1 — The Four Crafting Methods

All deliberately created Artifacts are produced through one of four primary crafting methods, or a combination of them. Each method has different material requirements, skill requirements, and output properties.

---

**Method 1 — Inscription (Script-Based Crafting)**

The crafter inscribes energy-channeling patterns directly into a physical substrate — metal, stone, bone, wood, or CPM itself. Inscription defines how energy flows through the object: the pathways it takes, the nodes where it concentrates, the output points where it expresses. Inscription is the most precise crafting method and produces the most predictable Artifact behavior, but it is also the most technically demanding: inscription errors cannot be corrected once committed — a flawed inscription must be removed and rebegun, which damages many materials.

*Crafter requirement:* The inscriber must have cultivation equal to or higher than the intended Artifact's Tier (Tier 1 requires at minimum a Mortal Tier 2 Champion cultivator; Tier 3 requires Core Realm; Tier 4 requires Divine Realm). An under-realm crafter can attempt inscription at the limit of their reach — the success rate for one-Tier-beyond inscription is the rate in the crafting-risk table below, and approaches zero for craftsmen attempting two Tiers beyond their realm.

*Output characteristics:* Inscribed Artifacts are efficient, predictable, and long-lasting. The inscription channels energy along defined pathways, minimizing waste. They are the dominant method for Formation Arrays (where precision of spatial definition is paramount) and assessment tools (where reliability is non-negotiable).

---

**Method 2 — Tempering (Force-Integration Crafting)**

The crafter repeatedly exposes the Artifact material to high-intensity cultivation energy — typically through combat use, deliberate qi infusion at extreme intensity, or immersion in high-grade CPM baths — until the material absorbs and crystallizes that energy into its structure permanently. Tempering is a slower method than inscription but produces Artifacts with more organic, adaptable energy architecture. A tempered weapon develops characteristics that reflect the cultivation style of its crafter.

*Crafter requirement:* Tempering requires the crafter to personally invest cultivation energy — sometimes for years or decades. The crafter's realm ceiling determines the maximum Tier achievable: a Core Formation cultivator can temper a material to Core-grade over sufficient time; an Xiantian cultivator cannot achieve Core-grade tempering regardless of time invested because they cannot generate Core-tier energy.

*Output characteristics:* Tempered Artifacts resonate more naturally with cultivators whose qi signature resembles their creator's. They are less precise in function than inscribed Artifacts but develop emergent properties over time — a tempered weapon used consistently by a single cultivator gradually adapts to that cultivator's technique patterns. Tempered Artifacts are the preferred method for personal weapons and cultivation catalysts.

---

**Method 3 — Formation Binding (System-Architecture Crafting)**

The crafter constructs a network of energy-conducting elements — formation stones, CPM nodes, spatial anchors — and establishes the governing logic of how those elements interact. Formation Binding produces Formation Arrays (Class III objects) rather than single-object Artifacts. The crafter is not inscribing or tempering a single object; they are designing a system architecture.

*Crafter requirement:* Formation specialists — a specific sub-discipline of cultivation that prioritizes system-design knowledge over personal combat power. A Formation Master at Mortal Tier 2 Ruler level with exceptional Formation knowledge can produce Tier 2 Formation Arrays that exceed what an Xiantian combat cultivator with no Formation knowledge could produce. Formation Binding is the area where technical expertise is most detached from personal cultivation power.

*Output characteristics:* Formation Arrays produce ongoing spatial energy effects. Their performance is determined by the quality of their design, the grade of their CPM fuel source, and the maintenance of their physical components. A poorly designed Formation Array can collapse under load, producing a catastrophic energy release. A masterfully designed one can sustain indefinitely given sufficient fuel.

---

**Method 4 — Soul Architecture (Soul-Crafting)**

The crafter embeds not just energy but soul-cultivated intent, will, and perception into the Artifact — producing objects that can perceive, respond to stimuli, and adapt their function. Soul Architecture creates Artifacts that are not merely passive tools but semi-active systems with primitive awareness. The highest expressions of Soul Architecture produce Artifacts with something approaching genuine consciousness — capable of independent judgment within their designed function domain.

*Crafter requirement:* Soul Architecture requires the crafter to have active soul cultivation (Part 4, Area 2) at a rung commensurate with the intended awareness depth. A cultivator at Rung 3 (Soul Form) can embed basic sensory awareness. A cultivator at Rung 7 (Soul Fortress) can produce an Artifact with genuine decision-making capability within its domain. The physical realm ceiling still applies: a Core Formation cultivator with a Soul Fortress soul rung can produce a soul-aware Core-grade Artifact.

*Output characteristics:* Soul Architecture Artifacts are responsive, adaptive, and capable of acting without the user's conscious direction. A soul-aware defensive artifact can anticipate attacks based on sensory patterns. A soul-aware weapon can redirect its strike trajectory mid-flight to track a moving target. The governing trade-off: soul-aware Artifacts are significantly more expensive to produce and their soul-awareness is permanently fixed at the soul-rung of their creator — they cannot grow beyond their designed awareness ceiling.

---

### 3.2 — Hybrid Methods

Most high-tier Artifacts combine two or more methods:

- **Inscription + Tempering:** The most common combination for combat weapons. Inscribed patterns define the energy channels; tempering integrates the crafter's personal cultivation style into the object's character. The result has both precision and organic adaptability.
- **Formation Binding + Inscription:** The standard method for permanent Formation Arrays. Formation Binding establishes the system architecture; inscription defines the precise energy flow within each component.
- **Soul Architecture + Any Method:** When soul awareness is desired on top of a base construction, the Soul Architecture layer is added as the final stage. The physical construction must be complete before soul-intent embedding can begin.
- **Tempering + Soul Architecture:** The combination used for "living weapons" — artifacts that develop over time through both physical tempering (continued use) and soul co-development. These are among the most powerful but rarest of crafted objects.

---

### 3.3 — Material Requirements

The base material (the physical substrate) and the CPM infusion together determine the Artifact's potential:

| Artifact Tier | Minimum Base Material | Minimum CPM Infusion | Typical CPM Infusion |
|--------------|----------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Tier 1 | High-density alloys, high-grade natural materials, properly prepared bone | Power Crystal, Grades 5 to 6 | Power Crystal, Grades 7 to 9 |
| Tier 2 | Metal alloys or natural materials capable of holding Gem-density energy; specific rare materials (star-iron, deep-earth ore) | Gem, Grades 6 to 7 | Gem, Grades 8 to 9, or a Rare Gem |
| Tier 3 | Core-bearing materials — ores formed in high-energy Formation Zones; beast-bone of Core Realm beasts; materials aged in Vortex-class environments | Shard, Grades 6 to 7 | Shard, Grades 8 to 9, or a Core of Grade 1 to 2 |
| Tier 4 | Divine-touched materials — metal alloys exposed to Divine Realm energy for centuries; bone or organ material of Divine Realm beasts; naturally Sacred Ground-formed crystal | Core, Grades 4 to 6 | Core, Grades 7 to 9, or Divine Liquid-infused |
| Tier 5 | Pre-world materials; origin unknown or cosmological in nature | Divine Liquid, any grade, as the minimum detectable component | Unknown |

**Rule 3.3.1 — Material Ceiling Is Absolute.**
An Artifact's power ceiling is set by the lower of: (a) the CPM infusion grade ceiling and (b) the physical base material's energy absorption capacity. A flawless inscription with a Grade 9 Shard infusion, on a base material that can only sustain Grade 6 of the infusion's own state, has an effective ceiling of Grade 6 of that state.

**Rule 3.3.2 — Material Degradation.**
Artifacts lose power over time as their CPM infusion depletes (unless recharged) or as their base material degrades under energy stress. A well-maintained Tier 2 Artifact might function for a thousand years without significant degradation. A Tier 1 Artifact used daily at maximum output might degrade within decades.

**Rule 3.3.3 — Recharging.**
CPM infusion in an Artifact can be replenished — additional CPM of the appropriate grade can be introduced through the same infusion pathways created during original construction. Recharging extends the Artifact's functional lifespan but does not increase its power ceiling beyond original construction parameters.

### 3.4 — Crafting Failure and Risk

Crafting failures are not rare events. The success rates below represent skilled practitioners working within their tier competence:

| Tier Being Crafted | Crafter's Realm | Base Success Rate | Failure Mode |
|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Tier 1 | Same tier (Mortal Tier 2) | 70–85% | Minor: material waste; the inscription fails to hold; Artifact has reduced output |
| Tier 2 | Same tier (Xiantian) | 55–70% | Moderate: material damaged; energy feedback causes mild cultivation injury to crafter |
| Tier 3 | Same tier (Core Realm) | 35–55% | Serious: CPM explosive release on failure; crafter requires recovery; material destroyed |
| Tier 4 | Same tier (Divine Realm) | 20–40% | Severe: Divine energy backlash; material catastrophically released; crafter injured |
| One Tier Above | Any | 5–15% (15–20% for an expert inscriber) | All failure modes above, elevated; one-step Deviant Breakthrough risk for crafter |

**Rule 3.4.1 — Standard Failure vs. Catastrophic Failure Distinction:**
Each tier has two failure sub-types based on how far the crafting process progressed before failure:

- **Standard Failure:** The crafting process fails during the inscription or energy induction phase before significant CPM has been committed. The failure is a "clean collapse" — the inscription simply does not hold, or the energy pathways refuse to form. Results: material partially salvageable (30–60% depending on tier), CPM partially recoverable, crafter sustains minor cultivation injury at most. This is the most common failure type for skilled crafters.

- **Catastrophic Failure:** The crafting process fails during or after the primary CPM infusion phase — after significant energy has been committed and partially organized. The partially organized energy becomes unstable and releases in an uncontrolled discharge. Results: material destroyed (unrecoverable), CPM fully lost, crafter sustains serious cultivation injury (cultivation deviation risk increases with tier), and the surrounding environment may be affected by the energy release (harm radius from the crafting site: about 10 m at Tier 1, 100 m at Tier 2, 1 km at Tier 3 and 10 km at Tier 4; the harm is greatest at the center and fades toward the edge, and a Standard Failure has no radius beyond the crafting circle). Catastrophic failures at Tier 3 and above qualify as environmental hazard events under Part 11 Area 9.4.

**Probability Split:** Standard failure is the more likely failure type at Tier 1–2 (70% of failures are standard, 30% catastrophic). At Tier 3–4, this reverses — 40% of failures are standard, 60% are catastrophic — because by the point of failure, significant CPM has typically already been integrated. At Tier 5 (Primordial), all failures are catastrophic by definition — the energy involved is cosmological in scale.

**Narrative Distinction:** A standard failure is a setback — costly but survivable and recoverable. A catastrophic failure is a crisis — it ends the crafting session, may injure the crafter seriously, and may destroy the workshop or surrounding area. They require different narrative weight and different recovery arcs for the crafter character.

## Area 4 — Power Ceilings and Realm Interaction

### 4.1 — The Output Ceiling Principle

A relic or artifact's maximum output is bounded by three independent ceilings, the lowest of which governs:

1. **Material Ceiling:** Determined by the CPM grade embedded in construction (Area 3.3)
2. **Activation Ceiling:** Determined by the activating cultivator's qi output — they can only channel as much energy through the relic as their own output capacity allows (Part 3, Area 16.5.3)
3. **Realm Ceiling:** Determined by the activating cultivator's current realm (Part 3, Area 16.5.3) — a cultivator can only channel energy through a relic at the level their cultivation supports

**Output Ceiling Rule:** The relic expresses power at the lowest of these three ceilings. A Tier 4 relic activated by a Core Formation cultivator does not produce Tier 4 output — it produces output at the Core Formation cultivator's activation ceiling. The Tier 4 relic's full potential becomes accessible only when a Divine Realm cultivator activates it.

---

### 4.2 — Safe Activation Thresholds

Safe activation means the cultivator can activate the relic without cultivation injury. The energy feedback from a relic whose internal energy tier significantly exceeds the cultivator's realm creates the same injury profile as CPM grade-mismatch absorption (Part 3, Area 18.3):

| Relic Tier vs. Activator Realm | Activation Safety | Feedback Profile |
|-------------------------------|------------------|------------------|
| Same Tier | Safe | No feedback; full Activation Ceiling applies |
| One Tier Above | Risky | Grade 1–2 Qi Deviation risk; partial activation possible |
| Two Tiers Above | Dangerous | Grade 3–4 Qi Deviation; activation possible but injurious; only Emergency Protocols allow |
| Three or More Tiers Above | Catastrophic | Potential fatal Qi Deviation; meridian destruction; attempted activation by most is suicide |

**Emergency Activation:**
In extremis, a cultivator may attempt to activate a relic above their safe threshold. The partial power produced (typically 10–30% of the relic's full potential) may still exceed anything the cultivator could produce from their own cultivation. But the cultivation injury cost is immediate and severe. Emergency activation is a narrative moment of desperation — it must be treated as such in prose.

---

### 4.3 — Cross-Major-Realm Barrier Relics

Part 3, Rule 17.3.3(b) identifies Tier 3–4 Artifacts specifically engineered to cross Major Realm barriers as one of the exceptional mechanisms by which a lower-Major-Realm cultivator can produce meaningful harm against a higher-Major-Realm target. This Area governs those objects in full.

**Definition:** A Cross-Realm Barrier Relic (CRBR) is an Artifact specifically constructed to penetrate the passive energy field that makes lower-realm attacks meaningless against higher-realm cultivators. The key mechanism is **energy concentration and penetration architecture** — the relic concentrates energy through a narrowly defined point or channel, producing a penetrating force density that the target's passive energy field cannot diffuse before structural impact.

**Governing Rules:**

**Rule 4.3.1 — CRBR Is Always Tier 3 Minimum.**
Crossing a single Major Realm barrier (e.g., allowing a Xiantian output to harm a Core Realm cultivator) requires minimum Tier 3 construction. The energy concentration architecture required to produce this penetrating effect demands Core-grade materials and crafting.

**Rule 4.3.2 — CRBR Does Not Bridge Two or More Realm Gaps.**
A Tier 3 CRBR can allow a sufficiently skilled Xiantian cultivator to produce meaningful (not fatal — meaningful) harm against a Core Formation cultivator. It cannot allow a Mortal Tier 2 cultivator to harm a Core Realm cultivator, and it cannot let a Mortal Tier 2 cultivator harm a Xiantian cultivator, because no Mortal Tier 2 cultivator can safely activate a Tier 3 CRBR. Each Major Realm barrier requires its own tier of crossing capability.

**Rule 4.3.3 — CRBR Does Not Equalize Power.**
Even with a Tier 3 CRBR, a Xiantian cultivator attacking a Core Formation cultivator produces harm at a fraction of what a Core Formation cultivator produces against another Core Formation cultivator. The CRBR makes harm possible; it does not make it equivalent. Combat outcome probability between a Tier-3 CRBR-armed Absolute God cultivator and a Core Formation cultivator might be 85–15 in favor of the Core Formation cultivator. The CRBR eliminates the categorical impossibility; it does not invert the 3-Level Rule.

**Rule 4.3.4 — CRBR Are Extraordinarily Rare.**
These objects are among the most prized artifacts in any cultivation civilization precisely because they neutralize the standard hierarchical guarantee of safety. Core Realm cultivators go to significant lengths to ensure CRBR are not widely available. Factions that possess working CRBRs treat them as strategic state secrets.

### 4.4 — Relic Power in Narration

**Rule 4.4.1 — Never State Numbers.**
Like cultivation power (Part 3, Area 3.4.1), relic power in prose is expressed through sensory, environmental, and comparative description — not numerical output figures. "The sword cut through the defense formation in one stroke, and the air behind it kept ringing" is the appropriate form. "The sword outputs 50,000 units of energy" is not.

**Rule 4.4.2 — The Artifact Must Do What Its Tier Allows.**
Prose depictions of artifact power must be consistent with the Tier system. A Tier 1 artifact cannot crack mountains. A Tier 3 artifact cannot be casually blocked by a Mortal Tier 2 cultivator. Internal consistency with the Tier → Heavenly Scale Grade mapping (Area 2.1) is mandatory.

**Rule 4.4.3 — Relic Power Is Expressed Through Effect.**
The most powerful way to show a relic's tier in prose is through the environment's response to it — the same method as Heavenly Scale (Part 3, Area 3.3). A Tier 4 relic whose power is drawn out fills the surrounding air with the same reality-distortion signatures as a Grade 13+ cultivator. The world responds. The prose captures that response.

## Area 5 — Relic Types by Primary Function

### 5.1 — The Eight Functional Categories

Every governed object in this Part has a primary function that determines how it interacts with the other Parts of the Canon. The eight functional categories are not mutually exclusive — many Artifacts serve multiple functions — but every Artifact has a primary function that governs its primary cross-Part dependency.

---

**Category A — Offensive Relics (Weapons)**

Objects designed to produce destructive force — swords, spears, bows, staffs, hammers. The defining characteristic is that their primary function is to deliver harm.

*Cross-Part interactions:* Offensive relics function as Named Move force amplifiers — they channel the cultivator's Named Move activation through a higher-tier medium, producing output at the relic's effective output ceiling rather than purely the cultivator's output. An Absolute God cultivator using a Tier 3 sword channels their Named Move at an output ceiling that rises toward the sword's material ceiling but stays at the Absolute God's activation ceiling, with the Risky feedback of Area 4.2. Part 6 governs the Named Move classification; this Part governs the relic's contribution to output ceiling. The resulting combined output is assessed under Part 3's comparison rules.

*Narrative rule:* The Named Move and the weapon are both present in combat prose. "He executed ***Heaven Piercing Thrust*** with the *Edge of the Broken Heaven*" — the Named Move is the technique architecture; the Named Relic is the medium of its expression.

---

**Category B — Defensive Relics (Armor, Shields, Wards)**

Objects designed to absorb, deflect, or negate incoming force. Includes worn armor, held shields, and passive ward-type objects that project defensive fields.

*Cross-Part interactions:* Defensive relics add to the cultivator's effective durability by layering an additional energy field on top of the cultivator's passive qi-field (Part 3, Area 17.2). A Tier 2 defensive artifact worn by a Xiantian cultivator does not merely add a Tier 2 field on top of their native Xiantian defense — the two fields interact, and the combined defense is assessed at the effective Heavenly Scale Grade of the higher tier (with the lower tier providing additional depth rather than multiplying the higher tier). This means defensive relics are most valuable to cultivators whose native defense tier is significantly below the relic's tier.

*Special class — Passive Ward:* A ward is a defensive relic that activates automatically when a triggering threshold of incoming force is detected, without requiring the cultivator's conscious activation. Wards are the most valuable defensive relics for surprise encounters. Their automatic activation draws from stored CPM fuel rather than the cultivator's reserve (Part 3, Area 12).

---

**Category C — Utility Relics (Tools, Instruments)**

Objects designed for tasks other than combat — storage, sensing, communication, transportation, assessment, containment. The most diverse functional category.

*Primary sub-types:*

**Storage Relics (Spatial Objects):**
Objects that contain extradimensional space — pockets, rings, bags. The most commonly encountered and universally desired utility relic type. Every significant cultivator above Mortal Tier 2 Warrior level aspires to possess a storage relic. Spatial capacity scales with Tier:
- Tier 1: Small spatial pocket (cubic meter range); objects placed inside are kept but not preserved (time runs as outside)
- Tier 2: Significant spatial dimension (building to estate scale); time suspended (Area 9.2)
- Tier 3: Large inhabited dimensions (mountain valley to small city scale); time runs at a different rate inside
- Tier 4: Continental-scale dimensions; may contain independent ecosystems and self-sustaining energy environments

**Sensing/Assessment Relics:**
Objects designed to perceive and measure — cultivation realm detection, affinity assessment, soul sensitivity measurement, CPM grade assessment, spatial anomaly detection. These require Soul Architecture (Method 4) components for active sensing above Tier 1.

**Communication Relics:**
Objects that transmit information across distance — jade slips that record and play back energy impressions, formation-linked crystals that allow real-time information exchange between paired objects, long-distance sound transmission spheres. Communication relic range scales with Tier and available CPM fuel.

**Transportation Relics:**
Objects that assist or enable movement — speed-enhancement boots, flight-enabling weapons (aerial swords), spatial folding devices. A Transportation Relic adds no speed or range of its own: its travel is read from Part 15's own rows by Tier, and the three-ceiling rule of Area 4.1 decides how much of that row a given wielder reaches.

| Relic Tier | Travel the relic gives | Part 15 row it is read from |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 (Mortal Grade) | A ground speed bonus | Mortal Tier 1 and Mortal Tier 2 ground speeds (Part 15, Areas 3.1 and 3.2) |
| Tier 2 (Xiantian Grade) | Flight | The Demigod row (Part 15, Area 4.2) |
| Tier 3 (Core Grade) | Core Realm travel | The Core Realm rows (Part 15, Area 5.2) |
| Tier 4 (Divine Grade) | Spatial fold, as far as the wielder's own realm allows | The Divine Realm rows (Part 15, Areas 6.2 and 6.3) |

**Category E — Healing Relics**

Objects designed to repair cultivation-related injuries — dantian damage, Core damage, meridian damage, soul damage. Healing relics are among the most valuable in any cultivation civilization because the consequences they address (permanent talent reduction, cultivation arrest, soul shattering) are otherwise largely irreversible.

**Category F — Containment Relics**

Objects specifically designed to safely store CPM substances, particularly high-grade substances whose energy density would otherwise damage ordinary containers. Divine Liquid cannot be held in any ordinary vessel (Part 11, Area 2.5) — containment relics at Tier 3–4 minimum are required.

Objects designed to identify, measure, and verify other objects and cultivators — relic tier assessment, CPM grade verification, talent root reading, soul rung assessment. Critical infrastructure for the cultivation economy (Part 11's economic law; Part 22's faction structure). This includes fusion stabilization artifacts (structural templates that hold partially fused cultivation architecture in a stable state during fusion), dual-path catalysts (energy objects pre-harmonized across two affinities to reduce conflict risk during absorption), and fusion expression relics (objects designed to receive and channel fused-path energy output without internal interference). Fusion-support relics must meet minimum Tier requirements per Rule 13.3.1. **Rule 6.1.2 — Relic Power Does Not Override Escalation Tier Intensity Ceilings.**

**Rule 6.1.3 — Named Relic + Named Move = Combined Output.**
When a cultivator activates a Named Move while using a Named Relic, both are present in the combat prose and the output is assessed at the effective ceiling of the combination. The Named Move's success rate (Part 6) is not modified by the relic, but the output ceiling of a successful execution reflects the relic's tier contribution.

For powerful relics, carrying them is itself a combat declaration — their presence is detectable at range.

## Area 7 — Formation Arrays

### 7.1 — What Formation Arrays Are

A Formation Array is a constructed spatial architecture — a network of energy-conducting elements that defines a bounded zone and produces an ongoing energy effect within it. Unlike single-object Artifacts, Formation Arrays are systems: multiple physical components (formation stones, inscribed walls, CPM node clusters, spatial anchors) operating according to the governing logic established by their design.

**The Governing Architecture:**
Every Formation Array has three architectural layers:
1. **The Input Layer:** The energy source — CPM node clusters, ambient energy absorption mechanisms, or direct qi input from a cultivator-controller
2. **The Distribution Layer:** The network of energy-conducting elements that route input energy through the formation according to the design logic
3. **The Output Layer:** The effect zone where the formation's designed purpose is expressed

A formation that lacks any one of these layers is incomplete and non-functional.

---

### 7.2 — Formation Array Types

**7.2.1 — Cultivation Environments:**
Formation arrays that artificially elevate the ambient energy density within a bounded space. The most common type across cultivation civilization. Governed in Part 3, Area 14.6 for their effect on cultivation; governed here for their construction and maintenance.

The **canonical metric distances** governing formation array effect radii are established in **Part 14 (Distance Law), Area 6.1** and are authoritative for all distance-specific governance:
> - Tier 1 (Mortal Grade) arrays: standard effect radius 1–50 m; maximum ~200 m (room to compound scale)
> - Tier 2 (Xiantian Grade) arrays: standard radius 1–5 km; maximum ~10 km (sect ground to town-scale)
> - Tier 3 (Core Grade) arrays: standard radius 100–300 km; maximum ~1,000 km (regional to city/capital protection scale)
> - Tier 4 (Divine Grade) arrays: standard radius 10,000–30,000 km; maximum full planetary scale
> - Tier 5 (Primordial) arrays: star system scale and above — governed by Part 28

The qualitative scale labels in this Part (room, sect, city, territory) are narrative proxies for Tier 1 through Tier 4 respectively. Any narration requiring specific metric values must use Part 14 Area 6.1 directly.
>

**7.2.2 — Defensive Arrays:**
Formation arrays that produce protective fields — energy barriers, spatial distortion fields, suppression zones that impair intruders' cultivation output, or detection networks that alert controllers to intrusion. The primary defensive infrastructure of major sects and cultivation cities.

**7.2.3 — Offensive Arrays:**
Formation arrays designed to produce destructive force within or from their zone. Used in siege warfare and territory defense. The most powerful offensive arrays rival individual Divine Realm cultivators in output — but they are fixed installations that cannot pursue targets.

**7.2.4 — Suppression Arrays:**
Formation arrays designed to inhibit cultivation within their zone — blocking qi circulation, preventing breakthrough, suppressing technique use, or binding specific cultivators. Used as prisons, arenas, and examination environments. Some suppression arrays target specific cultivation tiers (suppressing Core Realm output to Xiantian levels within the zone).

**7.2.5 — Healing and Recovery Arrays:**
Formation arrays that produce healing-oriented ambient conditions — accelerating recovery from Qi Deviation, providing medicated energy for dantian damage recovery, sustaining soul stability during soul damage healing. These are hospital-grade infrastructure in healing sects and major medical factions.

**7.2.6 — Sensing Arrays:**
Formation arrays that extend detection capability across a zone — identifying cultivation levels, detecting qi circulation, registering spatial anomalies, monitoring CPM substance movement. The intelligence infrastructure of major factions.

**7.2.7 — Absorption Arrays:**
Formation arrays that stand between a cultivator and an energy substance and lower the substance's effective grade before it enters the body, so that a substance of a higher grade is safe for a lower realm to absorb. Part 11 Area 7.4 governs what is absorbed; this Area governs the array. An absorption array is Tier 2 (Xiantian Grade) at the lowest, and no Tier 1 array can mediate. A Tier 2 array lowers the effective grade by 1 and a Tier 3 array by 2, and no array lowers it by more than 2 (Part 11 Area 7.4). Absorption arrays are the equipment of sect training halls and the training grounds of major factions: a Tier 2 array is uncommon and a Tier 3 array is rare (Area 2.1). It is built by the methods of Area 3.

**Fuel.** An absorption array runs on the energy sources of Area 7.3. Its running fuel is CPM placed in its input nodes, at the level its scale and Tier need (the table in 7.2.1), with ambient harvesting for the baseline where the ambient energy class of the place allows it (Area 7.4). The substance being absorbed passes through the distribution layer; it is not the array's fuel, and the array does not consume it.

**Collapse.** An absorption array that fails while a substance is in it releases the burst of the Area 7.5 row for its Tier, and the stored energy of the substance goes with it. A cultivator who is absorbing at that moment takes the substance at its full unmediated grade (Part 11 Area 7.4). This is why destroying or starving a training hall's array during a session is a real tactic.

### 7.3 — Formation Energy Sources

**CPM-Powered Arrays:**
The CPM fuel placed in the array's input nodes is consumed over time to sustain the output effect. The array's power output is proportional to the grade of CPM supplied. When the CPM supply runs out, the array collapses. Collapse is not always gradual — many arrays fail catastrophically when fuel drops below a minimum threshold, releasing stored energy in a burst. This collapse mode is what makes destroying formation fuel stores a viable military tactic.

**Ambient-Harvesting Arrays:**
Some formations are designed to harvest ambient energy from the environment rather than consuming stored CPM. These arrays cannot exceed the ambient energy class of their location — an ambient-harvesting array in a Class 2 environment can only produce Class 2 effects. However, ambient-harvesting arrays can run indefinitely given sufficient environmental energy. Over long operation in high-class environments, they may slightly deplete the local ambient energy (Part 3, Area 14.6 caveat).

**Cultivator-Input Arrays:**
Arrays powered by direct qi input from one or more cultivators — channeling their personal reserve into the array's input layer. This method produces the highest output ceiling (limited only by the cultivators' own realm) but cannot be sustained indefinitely. Cultivator-input arrays are typically reserved for peak-combat activation or emergency defensive use.

**Hybrid Arrays:**
Most major sect arrays combine ambient harvesting for baseline operation with CPM input nodes for burst performance and cultivator-input mechanisms for peak emergency output. The most sophisticated formation array designs allow seamless switching between input sources based on operational demand.

### 7.4 — Formation Interaction with Part 3's Ambient Energy Classification

Formation arrays that produce cultivation environment effects interact with Part 3, Area 14 (Cultivation Environment and Ambient Energy) as follows:

**The Ceiling Rule:**
A formation array cannot elevate ambient energy beyond one class higher than its Tier supports. A Tier 1 formation array can achieve Class 2–3; Tier 2 arrays reach Class 4–5; Tier 3 arrays reach Class 6; Tier 4+ arrays approach Class 7 (Sacred Ground equivalent).

**Foundation Quality Benefit:**
Cultivators who do the majority of their formative cultivation within a well-maintained formation array develop the Foundation Quality benefits described in Part 3, Area 14.3. This is the concrete mechanical reason that sect resources matter — not just faster cultivation speed, but structurally better outcomes at breakthrough points.

**Environmental Depletion Risk:**
Ambient-harvesting arrays operating in average environments (Class 2) for centuries can reduce local ambient class to Class 1. Over-extraction by major sect formations is a recognized environmental cultivation degradation risk — and a source of territorial conflict when a faction's formation infrastructure drains the surrounding region.

## Area 8 — Healing Relics and Restoration

### 8.1 — Governing Principle

Healing relics address the most feared non-lethal cultivation injuries: dantian damage (Part 3, Area 8.3), Core damage (Part 3, Area 8.3), meridian destruction and scarring (Part 3, Area 13.4), soul damage including Shattering (Part 4, Area 2.7), and lifespan loss from Internal Reversal (Part 11, Area 8.5). Without healing relics, most of these injuries are permanent or fatal. This makes healing relics among the most strategically valuable objects in cultivation civilization — not weapons, but what makes weapons survivable.

---

### 8.2 — Dantian and Core Healing Relics

**Dantian Stabilization Artifacts:**
Used at Mortal Tier 2 and Xiantian realms for dantian damage. A dantian stabilization artifact is placed in contact with the body at the dantian point and applies a precisely graded energy field that reinforces the dantian's structural integrity, prevents further degradation, and slowly restores damaged energy channels within the dantian structure.

*Tier requirement:* Minimum Tier 2 for meaningful dantian stabilization; Tier 3 for complete repair of severe dantian damage.
*Treatment duration:* Weeks to months even with Tier 3 artifacts for severe damage.
*Limitation:* Dantian artifacts treat the structure — they cannot restore talent root quality that has been degraded through Deviant Breakthrough scarring. Foundation quality loss is not reversible by any known artifact except Primordial Grade objects.

**Core Stabilization and Repair Artifacts:**
Core damage is rarer but more dangerous. A Core healing artifact must be Core-grade (Tier 3) at minimum because the energy density required to interact beneficially with a Core — rather than being overwhelmed by it — is itself Core-tier. A Tier 2 healing artifact would be absorbed by an active Core rather than healing it.

*Treatment approach:* Core healing artifacts produce a stabilizing resonance field that the Core can synchronize with, reducing the Core's internal chaos and providing a scaffold for structural self-repair. The Core's own regenerative capacity (Part 3, Area 12.4) is the primary healing mechanism; the artifact amplifies and guides it.
*Tier requirement:* Tier 3 minimum.
*Limitation:* Catastrophic Core damage (Core shattering below Divine Realm — Part 3, Area 8.2) that results in Core Solidification-class catastrophic failure may be beyond Tier 3 artifact repair without Divine Liquid infusion.

---

### 8.3 — Meridian Healing Relics

**Meridian Restoration Artifacts:**
Target meridian damage types (Part 3, Area 13.4) ranging from micro-tear acceleration to channel destruction repair.

| Meridian Damage Type | Applicable Artifact Tier | Treatment Duration | Recovery Outcome |
|---------------------|--------------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Micro-tear | Tier 1 (acceleration only) | Hours to days | Full recovery; accelerates natural healing |
| Channel collapse | Tier 1–2 | Days to weeks | Full recovery with proper artifact support |
| Scarring (partial) | Tier 2–3 | Months | Significant reduction; rarely full reversal |
| Scarring (permanent, severe) | Tier 3 with Divine Liquid infusion, or Tier 4 | Months to years | Up to 70–90% reversal for severe cases; Tier 4 approaches full reversal |
| Meridian destruction (single channel) | Tier 3 | Months to years | Partial restoration (50–75%) in most cases |
| Meridian destruction (multiple channels) | Tier 4 | Years | Possible partial restoration only; some destruction is permanent below Tier 5 |

**Rule 8.3.1 — Scarring Reversal Requires Part 11 Divine Liquid Component.**
Part 3, Area 13.4 states that permanent meridian scarring requires "Divine Liquid or specialized relics (Part 12)" for reversal. The mechanism is that full scarring reversal requires the construction of new meridian tissue within existing channels — a process that demands Divine Liquid as both the energy substrate and the bio-regenerative catalyst. A Tier 3 healing relic using Divine Liquid as its CPM power source can achieve up to 70–90% reversal for severe scarring. Without Divine Liquid, Tier 3 artifacts achieve at most 30–40% improvement in scarred channels.

---

### 8.4 — Soul Healing Relics

Soul damage healing is among the rarest capabilities in cultivation civilization because it requires both Soul Architecture (Method 4) in construction and high-tier CPM material. A healing relic that can address soul damage is simultaneously a soul-perceiving instrument (to assess the damage) and a soul-interacting tool (to guide repair). Shattering and fragmentation repair need Tier 3 minimum; erosion repair and suppression relief can be done at Tier 2.

**Soul Erosion and Fragmentation Healing:**
Addressed by Tier 2–3 soul healing artifacts that inject stabilizing soul-force into the damaged structure. The artifact's soul-perception capability must be sufficient to navigate the specific damage pattern — soul damage is highly individual in its configuration.

**Soul Suppression Relief:**
Suppression effects from soul attacks or hostile soul suppression formations can be lifted by appropriate Tier 2 artifacts. This is among the most accessible soul artifact applications.

**Soul Shattering Recovery:**
Part 4, Area 2.7 identifies soul shattering as potentially permanent without rare resources (Part 12). The full governing rule: soul shattering recovery requires a soul healing artifact specifically designed for structural reconstruction — rebuilding the soul's coherent architecture from surviving fragments. The artifact must be Tier 4 and must include Soul Architecture depth equivalent to Rung 9+ (Soul Sea) in its construction — providing a scaffold pattern against which the shattered soul can reorganize.

The process is:
1. Immediate stabilization using Tier 3 soul healing artifact to prevent further fragmentation and keep the cultivator alive
2. Structural reconstruction using Tier 4 soul healing artifact over weeks to months
3. Post-reconstruction integration cultivation — the cultivator must actively participate in re-establishing their soul's coherent identity through cultivation

Recovery completeness varies: mild shattering with rapid Tier 4 intervention recovers 90–100% function. Severe shattering with delayed treatment may recover 50–70% function. Some soul shattering is permanent regardless of resources — the governing variable is the proportion of the soul's coherent structure that survived intact.

---

### 8.5 — Lifespan Restoration Relics

Internal Reversal from Core substance absorption (Part 11, Area 8.5) can cause catastrophic lifespan loss — decades or more within minutes. Part 11 references "Divine Liquid or equivalent resources (Part 12 healing relics of the highest grade)" as potentially capable of intervention.

**The Governing Rule:**
Lifespan loss from Internal Reversal is not a cultivation injury in the standard sense — it is the accelerated consumption of the cultivator's fundamental entropy resistance (Part 3, Area 2.1). Reversing it requires restoring that entropy resistance, which means restoring the cultivation structure responsible for generating it.

**What is possible:**
- **Immediate stabilization (Tier 3–4 healing relic + Divine Liquid):** Halts the ongoing Internal Reversal process. If caught within the first minutes, most of the lifespan loss can be prevented.
- **Partial recovery (Tier 4 healing relic + Divine Liquid):** If significant lifespan was lost before stabilization, partial recovery (30–60% of lost years) is possible with prolonged treatment.
- **Complete recovery:** No known Artifact below Primordial Grade (Tier 5) can completely restore significant lifespan loss. The governing physical constraint is that the fundamental cultivation structure responsible for entropy resistance cannot be fully re-created by external means — it must be cultivated.

## Area 9 — Containment and Storage Relics

### 9.1 — CPM Containment Requirements

High-grade CPM substances impose specific containment requirements because their energy density exceeds what ordinary materials can sustain without beginning to absorb and degrade the substance (or be degraded themselves).

**Rule 9.1.1 — Divine Liquid Containment Vessels Are Tier-Classified Artifacts.**
Divine Liquid containment vessels are among the most precisely crafted of all utility relics. The vessel must be constructed from materials that do not absorb Divine Liquid energy — which eliminates most naturally occurring metals and stones. The confirmed material classes are:
- Core substance-grade base materials (Tier 3 minimum)
- Naturally Sacred-Ground-formed crystal (historically imprinted Class I Relic repurposed as vessel)
- Specific rare materials whose origin is Divine Realm beast organ or bone

The vessel must also be sealed with inscription that prevents leakage (Divine Liquid will escape through any insufficiently sealed boundary over time) and that prevents the vessel from becoming destabilized by the enclosed energy.

### 9.2 — Spatial Storage Artifacts (Deep Rules)

Spatial storage artifacts (storage rings, spatial bags, dimensional pouches) are governed by the following additional rules beyond the Tier classification in Area 5.1:

**Time in Storage:**
Objects stored in Tier 1 spatial artifacts experience real time. A living being placed in a Tier 1 storage artifact will suffocate; food will spoil; CPM will gradually interact with the stored environment.

Tier 2 spatial artifacts can maintain stable, suspended conditions — time effectively stops within the dimensional pocket in terms of biological processes. Living beings cannot survive long storage in most Tier 2 artifacts (the environment requires active energy management for life-support), but CPM, objects, and preserved specimens can be stored indefinitely.

Tier 3 and above spatial artifacts contain genuine extradimensional space that can support independent life. The interior may have its own ambient energy class, its own atmospheric conditions, and — in the most sophisticated constructs — its own formation arrays.

**Rule 9.2.1 — Spatial Artifacts Cannot Store Other Spatial Artifacts of Equal or Higher Tier.**
Attempting to place a Tier 2 spatial artifact inside another Tier 2 spatial artifact creates a dimensional resonance conflict. The inner artifact destabilizes, potentially releasing its contents into uncontrolled dimensional space — a potentially lethal event for any stored living beings. Spatial artifacts may contain lower-tier spatial artifacts without conflict.

**Rule 9.2.2 — Spatial Artifacts and Living Beings.**
Storing a living being in a spatial artifact without their awareness and consent is one of the gravest violations in cultivation social law (Part 25) — equivalent to imprisonment. The physical mechanics do not prevent it; the legal and social consequences are severe (Part 27).

### 9.3 — Narration of Storage and Retrieval

Objects retrieved from spatial artifacts appear in prose with the same narrative weight as any other element — they are not narrated as "appearing from nowhere." The cultivator reaches into the artifact (touches the ring, opens the pouch, makes a gesture indicating access), and the object emerges. Cultivation readers understand the spatial storage mechanism; what matters is that the action is shown, not glossed over.

## Area 10 — Assessment and Authentication

### 10.1 — Assessment Methods

The ability to accurately assess a relic or artifact's tier, function, and condition is a critical service in cultivation civilization — fraudulent relics are a constant economic hazard (analogous to CPM counterfeiting in Part 11, Area 12.4).

**Method 1 — Cultivator Sensory Assessment:**
A cultivator of sufficient realm can assess a relic's energy tier by direct qi-sense — feeling the energy field the relic emits, noting its density and character. This method works reliably up to the assessor's own Tier and approximately, with up to one Tier of error, one Tier above. A Core Formation cultivator can assess Tier 1–3 objects with confidence. Assessment of an object more than one Tier above the assessor's realm is unreliable — the assessor's qi sense is overwhelmed by the object's energy field.

*Accuracy:* Within one grade for same-tier objects; one Tier margin for objects one Tier above; unreliable for objects more than one Tier above.

**Method 2 — Formation-Based Assessment:**
Assessment formations (soul-aware artifacts with sensing functions — Tier 1 for basic, Tier 2–3 for precise) channel their own sensory qi through the assessed object and analyze the energy pattern. These formations are more precise than cultivator sensory assessment and are the standard for commercial transactions above Tier 1 value.

*Accuracy:* Within one grade standard; below one grade for Tier 3 assessment formations.

**Method 3 — Soul Sense Assessment:**
A cultivator with active soul cultivation (Part 4, Area 2) can perceive the relic's energy architecture directly through soul sense — seeing not just the energy tier but the construction method, any inscription patterns, Soul Architecture components, and historical energy imprints. Soul sense assessment is the most informative method but requires advanced soul cultivation and cannot be employed by the majority of cultivators.

*Accuracy:* Near-perfect for same-tier and one-tier-below objects; reliable one-grade precision for all tiers within soul-sense range.

---

### 10.2 — Authentication of Named Relics and Historical Artifacts

High-value historical artifacts and Named Relics face a specific authentication challenge: confirming that the object is what it is claimed to be. Counterfeiting a Named Relic (producing a Tier 2 artifact that resembles a famous Tier 4 relic) is an enormous fraud that occurs within cultivation civilization's history.

**Authentication Elements:**
1. **Energy Signature Match:** Every powerful relic or artifact has a distinctive energy signature shaped by its origin, its CPM components, its construction method, and — for historically imprinted relics — its history of use. This signature is like a fingerprint.
2. **Inscription Analysis:** Crafted Artifacts carry inscription patterns whose style is characteristic of their era, school, and crafter. Master-level inscription analysis can identify forgeries by pattern anachronisms (a modern inscription style on an "ancient" artifact).
3. **Historical Imprint Reading:** Through soul sense (Part 4, Area 2) or specialized historical impression artifacts, an authentic historically imprinted relic contains energy impressions of its past — technique patterns from its wielders, environmental imprints from historical events it witnessed.
4. **CPM Material Analysis:** The specific CPM grade and type embedded in an artifact is difficult to fake because CPM grade-matching is detectable at formation-assessment precision levels.

**Rule 10.2.1 — Named Relic Authentication Is a Formal Process.**
In high-value contexts (legal disputes, inheritance claims, faction negotiations), Named Relic authentication employs all four elements simultaneously and requires assessors at or above the object's Tier in cultivation. Casual authentication is one element; formal authentication is all four.

## Area 11 — Bonding and Ownership

### 11.1 — The Four Bonding Types

**No Bond (Unbound):**
An unbound relic or artifact can be activated by any cultivator of sufficient realm. It has no preference, no resonance advantage, and no loyalty. Most commercially produced Tier 1 artifacts are unbound. They are tools, not companions.

**Qi Marking (Soft Bond):**
A cultivator imprints their personal qi signature on an object, establishing a recognition relationship. The object responds more efficiently to the marked cultivator — activation costs are 5–15% lower, and the object self-returns to the marked cultivator's qi-space if separated by less than its recall range (Rule 11.1.1). Qi marking does not prevent others from activating the object; it merely provides the marking cultivator with an advantage. Qi marks can be overwritten — a stronger cultivator can replace an existing mark.

**Soul Bond (Hard Bond):**
A cultivator who has active soul cultivation (Part 4, Area 2) at sufficient rung (Rung 4 — Soul Echo minimum) can form a genuine soul bond with an artifact. The bond integrates the artifact into the cultivator's extended soul architecture. The bonded relic:
- Responds to the cultivator's intent without conscious activation gesture
- Cannot be activated by any other cultivator without first breaking the soul bond (extremely difficult — equivalent to damaging the cultivator's soul)
- Develops a resonance history with the cultivator over time — a tempered relic under soul bond begins to learn and anticipate its bonded owner's technique patterns
- Cannot be permanently separated from the bonded cultivator without soul injury to both — the bond has a bond range (Rule 11.1.1) beyond which the cultivator begins to feel a persistent pulling discomfort, escalating to pain and then soul structural stress at extreme separation

*Breaking a soul bond:* Soul bonds end naturally if the cultivator dies. During life, a soul bond can be dissolved voluntarily (a deliberate releasing process that takes hours and causes mild soul fatigue) or violently (destroying the artifact while the bond is active, producing soul injury — severity proportional to bond depth).

**Legacy Bond (Inheritance Bond):**
A unique bond type available only at Tier 4 and above, where the relic's soul-aware architecture (constructed via Method 4) forms a relationship not with a specific individual but with a lineage, affinity type, or cultivation path. Legacy bonds are found in ancient relics and family heirlooms. The relic recognizes those within its bonded lineage or path and provides resonance advantages to them. It rejects those outside the bond's scope — not preventing access, but making the activation friction high enough that the unrelated cultivator must overcome significant resistance.

*Narrative function:* Legacy bonds create the classic "chosen weapon" narrative — the relic that has waited for the right person for generations, that rejects unworthy wielders, that empowers its true heir.

**Rule 11.1.1 — Ranges and Rung Levels.**
Two distances and one map settle the thresholds above.

| Tier of the object | Recall range (Qi Marking) and bond range (Soul Bond) |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 (Mortal Grade) | 100 m |
| Tier 2 (Xiantian Grade) | 500 m |
| Tier 3 (Core Grade) | 2 km |
| Tier 4 (Divine Grade) | 10 km |
| Tier 5 (Primordial Grade) | Set by the object (Area 2.1); no figure is given (OPEN-AUTHOR) |

- *Recall range.* A Qi-marked object returns by itself to the marked cultivator's qi-space when it is separated from them by less than the recall range of its Tier.
- *Bond range.* A soul-bonded cultivator begins to feel the persistent pulling discomfort when the bonded object is farther away than the bond range, which is the same distance as the recall range of the object's Tier. How the discomfort rises through pain to soul structural stress is left to the scene (OPEN-AREA).
- *Rung to Tier map.* For Rule 11.2.4, and for any rule that compares a soul rung with a Tier, Rungs 1 to 4 count as Tier 2, Rungs 5 to 7 as Tier 3, and Rungs 8 and above as Tier 4 (Part 4, Area 2.2). A cultivator with no soul cultivation counts as Tier 1.

---

### 11.2 — Ownership and Transfer Law

**Rule 11.2.1 — Soul-Bonded Objects Cannot Be Freely Transferred.**
A soul-bonded relic's practical ownership transfers only when the soul bond is voluntarily dissolved by the bonded party, when the bonded party dies, or when the relic is violently destroyed (which also destroys much of its value). Seizing a soul-bonded relic from a living cultivator through force does not make it functional for the seizer — it merely inflicts soul injury on the original owner and produces an artifact that resists all activation attempts until the original bond dissipates through death or dissolution.

**Rule 11.2.2 — Faction Inheritance Rules for Relics.**
When a cultivator dies within a faction structure, their unbound artifacts typically revert to faction inventory per Part 22's governance. Their soul-bonded artifacts become inert (bond dissolves with death) and may or may not then be claimed by the faction depending on faction law. Part 22 governs these disputes.

**Rule 11.2.3 — Contested Relic Rights.**
Historical artifacts and Named Relics are among the most contested objects in cultivation civilization. Multiple factions may claim legitimate ownership through different lines of inheritance, historical possession, or discovery rights. These disputes are governed by Part 22's faction political law and Part 25's cultural law regarding relic inheritance traditions.

**Rule 11.2.4 — Soul Bond Voluntary Transfer Protocol.**
A soul-bonded relic can be transferred to a new owner through a three-phase voluntary process:

1. **Bond Dissolution Phase:** The bonded owner consciously releases their soul bond through sustained meditation contact with the artifact (hours to days depending on bond depth and tier of the artifact — Tier 1–2: hours; Tier 3: 1–3 days; Tier 4: up to one week). The owner experiences progressive soul fatigue proportional to bond depth. A deeply tempered bond (years of resonance history) causes more fatigue to dissolve than a recently formed bond.

2. **Rest Phase:** After dissolution, the artifact enters an unbound state and cannot form a new bond for a recovery period: Tier 1: 24 hours; Tier 2: 3 days; Tier 3: 1–2 weeks; Tier 4: 1–3 months. Attempting to force a new bond during the rest phase creates an unstable partial bond that will fracture within weeks and injure both parties.

3. **New Bond Formation Phase:** The new owner forms a soul bond using the standard soul bond process (soul cultivation at Rung 4+ minimum). If the new owner's soul rung is significantly lower than the artifact's tier (more than 2 Tiers below the artifact's Tier, counting the owner's soul rung by the map of Rule 11.1.1), the new bond forms slowly and incompletely — providing basic soul bond properties but not the full resonance response of a well-matched bond.

**Legacy Bond Transfer:** Legacy bonds do not transfer through this protocol — they are lineage or path-bound by construction. A Legacy bond transfers to new generations automatically when an eligible heir or path-compatible cultivator holds the object and is recognized by its soul-aware architecture. No meditation protocol is involved; the relic itself determines eligibility.

**Narrative Note:** Voluntary bond transfer is one of the most significant acts a cultivator can perform with a bonded relic — it is typically accompanied by deep personal meaning. The dissolution phase, where the cultivator surrenders years of resonance history with an object that has learned them, is an emotionally significant scene that the prose must render with appropriate weight.

## Area 12 — Origin Classification

### 12.1 — Why Origin Matters

The origin of a relic or artifact determines its nature, its energy character, its potential for bonding, and its narrative role. Two Tier 3 artifacts of identical functional output may carry entirely different narrative weight based on their origin: one crafted last century by a living Core Realm inscriber and one inherited through fifteen generations of a noble cultivation house.

---

**Origin Class 1 — Contemporary Crafted:**
Artifacts created within the current era by identified craftsmen using known methods. The most common class. Predictable, assessable, commercially available for tiers 1–2; uncommon but obtainable for Tier 3; extraordinarily rare and commissioned for specific patrons for Tier 4.

**Origin Class 2 — Historically Imprinted (Modern):**
Objects that have acquired energy imprinting through extended association with specific cultivators or events in the current era (within the last few thousand years). These are the swords of legendary cultivators, stones from major battlefields, objects present at world-changing events. Their power is documented and their history is verifiable.

**Origin Class 3 — Ancient Inheritance:**
Objects whose construction predates current civilization — created in historical eras whose techniques are partially or fully lost. Ancient Inheritance objects sometimes exhibit properties that contemporary inscribers cannot replicate, using material combinations or inscription patterns whose governing principles are no longer understood. Assessment of Ancient Inheritance objects is more difficult because contemporary assessment methods were calibrated on contemporary construction styles.

**Origin Class 4 — Unknown Origin:**
Objects that cannot be traced to any known crafter, event, or material. Unknown Origin objects may be Ancient Inheritance objects whose history has been lost, or they may be something else — objects formed by processes outside known formation and crafting theory. These objects carry the most narrative mystery and the highest assessment uncertainty. The probability that an Unknown Origin object is what it appears to be must be considered alongside the probability that it is something fundamentally unexpected.

**Origin Class 5 — Primordial:**
Objects whose origin predates the current world's energy system, or whose formation occurred through cosmological processes rather than cultivation-civilization processes. Primordial objects do not conform to the Tier classification system because the Tier system was calibrated on CPM substances and cultivation energy — and Primordial objects may not use either. Their assessment requires Primordial-equivalent soul sense, which no currently known living cultivator possesses.

### 12.2 — Indicative Price Bands

Prices apply to Origin Class 1, Contemporary Crafted, the class that is sold. Classes 2 to 5 are priced by history, owner and need, and may cost many times the band. A Named Relic is not priced by band. Figures are in the tier's own substance, at the rates of Part 11 Area 12.2.

| Tier | Name | Price band | Who sells |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Mortal Grade | about 100 to 10,000 Crystals (1 to 100 Gems) | Guild and sect shops, openly. |
| Tier 2 | Xiantian Grade | about 50 to 5,000 Gems | Commissioned from Xiantian crafters. Sect heirlooms are not sold. |
| Tier 3 | Core Grade | about 5 to 500 Shards | Rare; sold by private treaty between sects and kingdoms. |
| Tier 4 | Divine Grade | not priced | No market. Each piece is individually documented and changes hands by gift, treaty or war. |
| Tier 5 | Primordial Grade | not priced | Not an object of trade. |

## Area 13 — Fusion Interface (Part 7 Joint Governance Framework)

### 13.1 — Governing Principle

Part 7 (Fusion Law) governs the fusion of cultivation paths, cultivation and technology, and multiple cultivation dimensions. Relics and artifacts play a specific role in fusion cultivation: they can serve as external anchors, stabilizers, catalysts, and containers for fusion processes that would be unstable without physical support infrastructure. This Area operates as a joint governance framework between Part 12 (object-layer rules) and Part 7 (process-layer rules); Part 7, Area 9 (Fusion Resources and Support Infrastructure) is the governing authority for the fusion processes these relics support.

---

### 13.2 — Fusion-Support Relic Types

**Fusion Stabilization Artifacts:**
Objects that hold a cultivator's partially fused cultivation architecture in a stable state during the fusion process. The physical analogue is a mold — the artifact provides a structural template against which the fusion can organize. Without stabilization artifacts, many fusion processes produce the catastrophic Fusion Collapse outcomes described in Part 7, Areas 8.3 and 8.4. Fusion stabilization artifacts are among the most specialized and rarest utility relics.

*Governing principle:* A fusion stabilization artifact must be constructed to match the specific cultivation path combination being fused. A general-purpose fusion stabilizer is largely ineffective — the stabilization architecture must reflect the specific energy signature interaction being managed.

**Dual-Path Catalysts:**
Objects infused with energy that carries characteristics of two distinct affinities or cultivation paths simultaneously (rather than a single affinity type like standard CPM substances — Part 11, Area 5). These catalysts provide both paths' energy inputs in a pre-harmonized form, reducing the risk of the two energies conflicting during absorption. Producing a dual-path catalyst requires a crafter with both affinities at high level — an extremely rare prerequisite.

**Fusion Expression Relics:**
Objects designed to receive and express the output of a fused cultivation path — weapons or formation arrays built to channel and amplify energy that combines multiple cultivation dimensions. Standard Artifacts are designed for a single energy type; a fusion expression relic can accommodate multiple types without the internal interference that standard Artifacts would experience.

---

### 13.3 — Rules Governing Fusion Relics

**Rule 13.3.1 — Fusion Relic Tier Requirements.**
Fusion processes generate energy interactions that are more complex and more volatile than single-path cultivation. Fusion-support relics must be at least one Tier higher than the highest-Tier element of the fusion being supported. A fusion combining Mortal Tier 2 and Xiantian cultivation paths requires a minimum Tier 3 fusion stabilization artifact.

**Rule 13.3.2 — Part 7 Is the Governing Authority.**
This Area establishes the framework and the object-class definitions. All specific fusion relic rules — what combinations require what support, what the failure modes are, what the outcome uplift is — are governed by Part 7, Area 9 (Fusion Resources and Support Infrastructure). Part 12 governs the relic as an object; Part 7 governs its function within the fusion process. In any conflict between this Area and Part 7, Area 9 is the controlling authority on process-side questions.

## Area 14 — Narration Standards

### 14.1 — The Narrative Introduction Sequence

High-tier relics must be introduced narratively before they are deployed functionally — the same principle as CPM substances (Part 11, Area 14) and the Heavenly Scale descriptor-first standard (Part 3, Area 3.4). The sequence:

1. **Sensory establishment:** The relic's presence is registered through sensory description before it acts. A Tier 3 sword is felt in the surrounding environment — the Core Pulse it produces, the way lesser cultivators instinctively lean away from its bearer. The relic's power announces itself before the bearer acts.

2. **Character or narrator acknowledgment:** Another character assesses or reacts to the relic, or the POV character recognizes what they are dealing with. This anchors the reader's understanding of its tier.

3. **Named introduction:** When a Named Relic first appears on the page, its name appears in context — spoken by a character, inscribed on the object, or introduced by the narrator with the same care as the introduction of a Named Move.

4. **Functional deployment:** Only after steps 1–3 does the relic act in the scene.

*For Named Relics already established in a reader's awareness:* Steps 1–3 compress into brief sensory re-establishment rather than full introduction. A Named Relic that appeared three chapters ago needs only a brief re-presence description before deployment.

Steps may be merged or reordered; a surprise may land effect-first from a victim's viewpoint if the tier is shown within the same beat. The test is that the reader knows the tier before the next relic action, not that all four steps appear.

---

### 14.2 — Relic Presence in Scenes

**Tier 1–2 Relics:**
Treated as sophisticated equipment — worth noting when first introduced, unremarkable when regularly used. A character who has used their storage ring for fifty chapters does not re-introduce it every time they reach into it.

**Tier 3 Relics:**
Their presence is always felt. A Core-grade sword has a passive environmental effect (Area 6.3) that persists as long as it is carried unsealed, sheathed or not. Scenes involving Tier 3 relics should consistently note the energy presence they impose on their surroundings. Other characters react — experienced cultivators feel the weight of the object; less experienced ones feel vague unease they cannot explain.

**Tier 4 Relics:**
Their presence in a scene is a narrative event in itself. A Tier 4 relic appearing on the page should feel like the moment before something irrevocable. The prose slows, the environment responds, and every cultivator present registers the change. This is not optional — the environmental response rules (Area 6.3 + Part 3, Area 20) are non-negotiable for Tier 4 presence.

**Tier 5 Relics:**
Appear only as described in Area 2.1 — as catalysts, not tools. Their prose treatment is mythopoetic rather than mechanical. They are not described in terms of what they can do; they are described in terms of what it feels like for the world to be near them.

---

### 14.3 — Relic Damage and Destruction

Relic damage is not silent. When a Tier 2+ relic is damaged in combat — cracked, partially destroyed, destabilized — the energy release and the cultivator's response must be rendered with the same care as significant physical injury. A bonded relic's damage is also a soul-structural event (Area 11.1). These are not just object-loss moments — they are character moments, because the relic carried history, effort, and often a piece of the cultivator's own cultivation development.

**Rule 14.3.1 — Relic Destruction Releases Energy.**
Destroying a relic does not make its stored energy disappear. It releases it — the scale of release proportional to the relic's Tier and remaining CPM charge. A destroyed Tier 2 relic in a confined space is a hazard. A destroyed Tier 3 relic mid-combat is an explosion event at geological scale. This is not just flavor — it is a combat mechanic and a Part 5 battlefield event.

## Area 16 — Domain Ownership and Disambiguation

### 16.1 — This Part vs. Part 11

**Part 11 governs:** The raw CPM substance — what it is, how it forms, its states and grades, its usage rules, its hazard profiles, its currency function.

**Part 12 governs:** The constructed objects that use CPM as raw material — relics, artifacts, formation arrays. When the CPM is embedded in a relic, it ceases to be governed by Part 11 as an independent substance and becomes governed by Part 12 as a component of the constructed object. The constructed object's power ceiling, usage safety, assessment, and narration are Part 12's domain.

**Joint governance area:** The moment of CPM integration into a relic during construction — which grade of CPM is appropriate for what tier, and what happens when too-high-grade CPM is forced into an insufficiently prepared base material during construction. This is jointly governed: Part 11 defines the substance tier; Part 12 defines the construction material ceiling.

---

### 16.2 — This Part vs. Part 6

**Part 6 governs:** Named Moves that produce formation-like effects (Formation-function moves, Area 3.6.2). The key distinction: a Part 6 Formation-function move is a cultivator-executed technique that creates a temporary zone effect requiring the cultivator's ongoing energy. When the cultivator stops channeling or is disrupted, the technique ends.

**Part 12 governs:** Permanent or semi-permanent formation arrays installed as physical infrastructure. These continue operating without the cultivator's ongoing technique execution.

**The interaction:** Formation-function Named Moves can be used to activate, amplify, or disrupt formation arrays (Part 12 objects). A **Formation Activation Technique** is a Named Move (Part 6) that triggers a Part 12 formation array's full output. A **Formation Disruption Strike** (Part 6, Control/Disrupt function) may specifically target a formation array's energy flow. The Named Move governs the technique; the formation array governs the object.

---

### 16.3 — This Part vs. Part 13

**Part 13 governs:** The spatial properties of Formation Zones (Part 13 Area 10 — Passive, Active, Vortex and Celestial Formation Zones) — where they are geographically, what their spatial characteristics are, what their boundary enforcement rules are, how overlap and zone priority work, and what collapse conditions terminate them.

**Part 12 governs:** What objects — artifacts and formation arrays — are constructed in Formation Zones, using the high-energy environment for their production.

**The interaction:** Part 13 defines the location; Part 12 defines what can be made there and the advantage the location provides to construction.

---

### 16.4 — This Part vs. Part 8

**Part 8 governs:** The technology civilization tier prerequisites required to construct Technology-Relic Hybrids — specifically the minimum levels in the Materials Science, Energy Technology, and Cultivation Integration Technology domains (Part 8, Area 6.3). Part 8 also governs the technology-side interface architecture: how CPM energy from relics is routed, channeled, and converted within technology systems (Part 31, Area 7.3 and Part 8, Area 6.4).

**Part 12 governs:** The Technology-Relic Hybrid object itself as a constructed physicalized energy structure — its Tier classification, material ceiling (Area 4.1: relic component grade sets the ceiling), construction mechanics (Area 3), Hybrid Class (Class 1, 2 or 3 — Part 7, Area 5.2), soul bonding (Area 11), and assessment/authentication (Area 10).

**The key rule:** Part 8 sets the door (whether the technology civilization can attempt the construction). Part 12 governs what is built once the door is open. A civilization that meets Part 8's tier thresholds but constructs a Hybrid that violates Part 12's Tier, ceiling or construction rules (Areas 2 to 4) has satisfied Part 8 and violated Part 12 simultaneously — both are independent satisfaction requirements.

**Joint governance area:** The moment of construction itself — determining whether the civilization's current technology tier can produce the specific Hybrid being attempted, while simultaneously satisfying Part 12's construction material ceiling, formation rules, and Tier classification. Neither Part alone governs the complete construction event.
