# Monster Law

**Authority Tier:** 1 (Domain Authority — Character; see `.claude/guardrails/authority/guardrail-authority-tier-system.md`)

## Overview

Part 40 is the Starward Saga Canon's Monster Law: the one home for the beasts of the world that are dangerous in their own right, and for the talking beasts that the story will meet in later phases. Before this Part, beasts appeared only as scattered lore: Beast Cores and Beast Attraction in Part 11, the Beast World in Part 13, the Monster Hunting Corps in Part 22, the Beast Carrier in Part 24, and the Cultivated Beast (Awakened) in Part 16 Area 12.2. Part 16 Area 1.1 lists non-sentient animals as environmental elements, and Part 30 plans monsters for Phase 1 (out of the forest beside the border village, and on the airship voyage) with no Part that governed them. Part 40 gives them one home, so that a monster is recorded once, behaves as itself, and is ruled by the same ladder and the same laws as every other being.

**Scope.** This Part governs the monsters of the Starward Saga: monster types of many kinds; sentient monsters who talk like humans, whose power is Void God and above, and who do not appear in Phase 1; a ledger that tracks all monsters (`ledger-character-monster`); tracked behaviour traits; most monsters sit at Tier C and D; and monsters follow the same cultivation hierarchy as all characters, with no separate ladder.

**What Part 40 owns and what it points at.** It owns: what a monster is and is not; the Kind and Individual entries; the monster classes; the stage field of a monster; the twelve behaviour traits; the four monster depth tiers and the rule for what comes to the author; the monster ledger's content; the Harvest line; the sentient reserve; the line between monster, villain and side villain; and monster naming. It does not take over Part 3 (the ladder, breakthrough, comparison), Part 5 (combat), Part 6 (the Named Move taxonomy and register), Part 11 (Beast Cores, Beast Attraction, the organic sources), Part 12 (relic materials), Part 16 (what a character is, species), Part 18 (villains), Part 19 (the Depth Spectrum it reuses), Part 37 (the ledger schema), Part 39 (behaviour codes) or Part 28 (the Naming Law). It points at them. None of those Parts may absorb monsters quietly; this Part is the owner.

**How the Areas run.** Area 1 defines the monster and its boundary. Area 2 sets the two entry kinds and the first class list. Area 3 puts monsters on the one ladder. Area 4 governs their moves. Area 5 sets the twelve traits. Area 6 sets the depth tiers and the rule for what reaches the author. Area 7 describes the ledger. Area 8 covers habitat, range and the Harvest line. Area 9 covers monsters among people. Area 10 covers combat. Area 11 reserves the sentient monsters and fences Phase 1 against them. Area 12 draws the line between monster, villain and side villain. Area 13 governs names. Area 14 holds the narration standards. Area 15 is the dependency table.

**One home for each rule.** Where another Part must carry a pointer to a rule of this Part, it carries a short pointer and this Part stands on any difference. Two rules are stated a second time because the place that applies them is elsewhere: the Phase 1 gate is also a Law of Part 30 (Law P30-L9), because Gate 1 Check #11 reads Part 30; and the villain-classification clause is also stated at Part 37 Threshold C-2, because that threshold is where the question fires.

This file is the single authority for the whole of Part 40; there is no layer beneath it.

## Area 1 — Foundational Principles of the Monster

### 1.1 — What a Monster Is

A **monster** is a beast that the story tracks because it is dangerous in its own right. It is one of two kinds:

- **(a) A beast that holds its own qi**, from Senior Level 1 Low upward. Mortal Tier 1 has no qi of its own (Part 3 Area 1), so Senior Level 1 Low is the lowest stage at which a beast holds qi.
- **(b) A body-refined beast below Senior** that is dangerous from strength alone: a huge bear at the Muscle or Bone stage, with no qi. It holds a Mortal Tier 1 stage name of Part 3.

**Rule 40.1.1.1 — The Two Kinds Are One Class.** A beast of kind (a) and a beast of kind (b) are both monsters. They are recorded in the same ledger and follow the same ladder. Whether a beast is dangerous enough to be a monster is for the story to say. No count, no power figure and no size decides it.

**Rule 40.1.1.2 — Ordinary Animals Are Not Monsters.** Oxen, horses, Beastdray draft animals and Ridebeasts (Part 32 EBC-2 and EBC-3), game, vermin, and every other animal that is not dangerous in its own right are not monsters and are not tracked in the monster ledger. They remain the environmental elements of Part 16 Area 1.1 and appear in a location's Flora and Fauna line (Part 37 Area 2.4). A domesticated animal stays an animal however long it is kept. A wild monster that is tamed stays a monster (Rule 40.9.3.1).

**Rule 40.1.1.3 — Sentience Still Decides Character Rules.** A non-sentient monster is not a Part 16 character, because Part 16 Area 1.1 counts only sentient individuals as characters, and that sentience criterion is unchanged. A sentient monster is a Part 16 character and is governed by Area 11.

### 1.2 — What a Monster Is Not

| Not a monster | Where it is governed |
|---|---|
| A person of any species. A Minotaur is a bipedal sentient species and Bison is a hero; the species entry is in the civilization ledger. | Part 16 Area 18; `ledger-people-society-civilization`; Part 17 |
| A hybrid sentient (the dragon-human hybrids) | Part 16 Area 17 |
| Another sentient being (non-bipedal, incorporeal, hive, planar, elemental) | Part 16 Area 19 |
| A World Tree. Part 30 says it is not a monster. | Part 30 (Phase 1 World Tree passages) |
| A Formation Spirit or a Relic Intelligence | Part 12; Part 16 Area 12.2 |
| An AI, a construct or an automated system | Parts 10 and 12 |
| An environmental spirit without independent will, or a natural phenomenon | Part 16 Area 1.1 (the spirit or anomalous class of Rule 40.2.2.1 is reserved and defines nothing) |
| A beast people: a culture with language and rites, such as the beast civilizations of a Beast World | Part 16 Areas 18 and 19; Part 21; Part 13 |

**Rule 40.1.2.1 — A Being Is Filed in One Place.** A being is a monster or one of the things in the table, never both. A Kind that proves to be a people moves out (Rule 40.2.1.5).

### 1.3 — The Principles

**Rule 40.1.3.1 — No Survival Protection.** A monster can be wounded, driven off, killed, tamed or sealed, and nothing shields it. Its death is a story event with consequences (Part 27), not the defeat of a villain (Part 18).

**Rule 40.1.3.2 — The Same World, the Same Laws.** A monster cultivates by Part 3, uses moves by Part 6, fights by Part 5, yields its body's materials by Parts 11 and 12, and causes and suffers consequences by Part 27. There is no monster physics.

**Rule 40.1.3.3 — Wild Pressure Is Not Opposition.** A non-sentient monster is a force of the world. Its hostility is hunger, territory, fear or instinct, and it is not an ideology. It is wild pressure on the story, not an opposing force, and it is never a villain (Area 12).

**Rule 40.1.3.4 — Designed Standalone, Compared With No One.** Each Kind and each Individual is designed on its own. Overlaps with another monster's traits are permitted. No entry derives from, or records, a comparison with another monster or with any person. A mirror or foil relationship is recorded only on the author's own ruling, after each is designed standalone (Part 19 Function IV).

**Rule 40.1.3.5 — Story First, No Counts.** No figure in this Part is a count, a gate or a threshold. The tiers, the traits and the stage fields are records, not quotas, and no counter of monsters, attacks, appearances or kills exists. The confirmed spec chain (Part 38) and Part 30 prevail over every figure in the Canon (the precedence note of Part 30).

## Area 2 — Monster Classification

### 2.1 — Kind and Individual

**Rule 40.2.1.1 — Two Entry Kinds in One Folder.** The monster ledger holds two kinds of entry. A **Kind** is the record of a kind of creature: what it is, where it lives, how it behaves, what it can do, what its body yields. An **Individual** is a named monster of a Kind. Both live in `ledger-character-monster/` (Area 7).

**Rule 40.2.1.2 — A Kind Holds the Baseline.** The Kind entry holds the Baseline (B) of the creature: its body, its class, its stage band, its cultivation varieties (Rule 40.3.4.2: affinity, soul and the rest), its habitat, its twelve traits (Area 5), its distinct abilities (Area 4), its Harvest line (Rule 40.8.4.1) and its depth tier (Rule 40.6.1.4). It is the species-level record.

**Rule 40.2.1.3 — An Individual Points at Its Kind and Records Only What Is Its Own.** The Individual entry names its Kind and records its name or epithet, its exact stage, its own history, the points where it differs from the Kind (B), and its current state, place, wounds, handler and grudges (L). A fact the Kind holds is never copied into the Individual: the Kind owns it, and on a difference the Kind stands (Part 37 Area 5, one owner for a fact held in two places).

**Rule 40.2.1.4 — Packs and Unnamed Members.** A pack, herd, swarm or brood that an Area treats as one body is carried by its Kind and, where the pack acts with story consequence, by one Individual entry that names the pack. An unnamed member of a registered Kind is background texture (Part 28 Rule 20.2), covered by the Kind. Anything that passes the Narrative-Significance Test of Part 28 Area 20 is named (Area 13) and recorded.

**Rule 40.2.1.5 — A Kind Is Not a Species or a Civilization.** A Kind is not a Part 16 species and not a Part 21 civilization (Part 37 Threshold V-1). If a Kind proves to have a culture, a language and rites, it is a people: it moves to Part 16 Area 18 or 19 and to the civilization ledger, and its Kind row is marked migrated. The Minotaur is such a species already and stays in the civilization ledger.

### 2.2 — The First List of Monster Classes

**Rule 40.2.2.1 — The List.** The author adopted this list as the first shelf. A class is a shelf for Kinds and nothing more: it carries no stage, no tier and no power. A Kind has one primary class. A class with weak or no lore support is marked Reserved or OPEN-AUTHOR and holds no Kind until the author opens it. The author may add classes at any time.

| # | Class | What it covers | Lore support | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Forest and woodland ground beasts | Beasts of forest, wood and scrub that live and hunt on the ground | Strong that such monsters exist: Part 30 plans monsters out of the forest beside the border village at Senior or Champion level. The first Kind itself is not stated. | Open. The first Kind is defined and named at its Chapter Spec (Rule 40.7.5.1) |
| 2 | Sky and wing hunters | Winged or gliding hunters of the air | Partial: Part 30 has Ruler-level monsters attacking the airship of the Phase 1 voyage; that they fly is inferred from the target | Open |
| 3 | Affinity-typed beasts | Beasts whose body and qi carry one affinity (fire, lightning, water and the rest of Part 4) | Strong for Core Realm beasts (Part 11 Area 11.1, Affinity Typing: every Core Realm beast has an inherent affinity); inferred below Core Realm | Open |
| 4 | Swarm and minor pack beasts | Small beasts that come in numbers or packs and are drawn by Power Crystals | Strong that minor beasts exist and are drawn by Crystals (Part 11 Area 8.3); swarms are not stated | Open |
| 5 | Territorial apex beasts | Core Realm and above, with Beast Cores; "a territorial power or a legend" (Part 11 Area 11.1) | Strong (Part 11 Area 11.1) | Reserved: not in Phase 1 |
| 6 | Exceptional-lineage and ancient beasts | Rare to Mythic lineages (Part 11 Area 11.1) and the beasts whose bone and organ are relic materials (Part 12) | Strong that they exist | Reserved: not in Phase 1 |
| 7 | Mountain, water and cold-land beasts | Beasts of ranges, rivers, seas and the frozen lands | Partial: the places exist in the location ledgers, and no beast is recorded in any of them | OPEN-AUTHOR for each Kind |

**Rule 40.2.2.2 — Not on the List, on Purpose.** There is no dragon-kind class. A dragon-kind monster needs an explicit author ruling and a check against Law P30-L8 before it is proposed anywhere. Corrupted or qi-deviated beasts, and plant, fungal, insect, undead, construct and demon types, are not listed: none has lore support in the Canon, and each is added only on the author's ruling.

### 2.3 — Origin and Affinity

**Rule 40.2.3.1 — Origin.** A Kind records its origin as `wild-born`, `lineage` (a heritable line of exceptional-lineage beasts, Part 11) or `OPEN-AUTHOR`.

**Rule 40.2.3.2 — Affinity.** Every Core Realm beast has an inherent affinity, and its Beast Core carries it (Part 11 Area 11.1, Affinity Typing). Below Core Realm a Kind records an affinity when the story shows one, and reads `OPEN-AREA` otherwise. Part 4 owns the affinity types and levels.

## Area 3 — Monster Cultivation and Power

### 3.1 — The One Ladder

**Rule 40.3.1.1 — No Ladder of Its Own.** Monsters follow the same cultivation hierarchy as all characters, with no separate ladder. This Part adds no level name, rank, grade or scale for monsters. Every stage of a monster is a Part 3 name with its Part 3 Level and sub-level: a Mortal Tier 1 stage name, or Senior, Warrior, Champion, Ruler, Monarch, a Xiantian stage, a Core Realm stage or a Divine Realm stage. A reader reads a wolf at Champion Level 3 Mid the way they read a man at Champion Level 3 Mid. Warrior is a Part 3 realm and not a class of monster.

**Rule 40.3.1.2 — The Stage Field: a Band for a Kind, an Exact Stage for an Individual.** An Individual holds an exact stage: realm, Level and sub-level. A Kind holds a band, written as two Part 3 stages, for example Warrior Level 2 to Level 6. An Individual's exact stage lies inside its Kind's band unless the Individual's own entry records, and the story shows, why it does not.

**Rule 40.3.1.3 — A Personal Variation Is Allowed.** As for a cultivator, an Individual may carry the optional Relative Power line of Part 3 Area 1.6: the table value for its stage with the actual figure and its cause, when the author has set one that differs from the table. With no such line the table value applies (Part 3 Area 1.2.2).

**Rule 40.3.1.4 — Comparison Is Part 3's.** A monster is compared by the laws that compare people. The Major Realm barrier is a law of kind, the 3-Level Rule counts minor steps (Part 3 Area 7), and the Categorical Impossibility Law holds (Part 3 Area 17.3). No monster-only comparison table exists, and no entry compares one monster with another (Rule 40.1.3.4).

### 3.2 — Phase Ceilings

**Rule 40.3.2.1 — The Phase 1 Ceiling Binds Monsters.** The Phase 1 narrative ceiling of Part 3 Rule 1.7.3 binds a monster exactly as it binds a character: in Phase 1 prose a monster is never named above Ruler Level 9 Low, and the forbidden vocabulary of that rule applies (Part 3 Rule 1.7.3). Part 30's own plan for Phase 1 sets lower bands for the monsters it plans (Rule 40.7.5.1), and a Kind written for Phase 1 stays inside them.

**Rule 40.3.2.2 — The Sentient Floor.** A sentient monster is Void God Level 1 Low or above and never below (Rule 40.11.2.1).

### 3.3 — Growth

**Rule 40.3.3.1 — The Same Breakthrough Rules.** A monster grows by the same rules as a person (Part 3 Area 4). A stage is crossed by an event that is prepared, shown and paid for on the page, and never by a line of ledger. There is no separate evolution event and no second ladder.

**Rule 40.3.3.2 — The Beast Core.** A beast reaches a Beast Core at Core Realm (Part 11 Rule 11.1.1). Below Core Realm a beast has none.

**Rule 40.3.3.3 — Recording a Rise.** An Individual's breakthrough is a Canon event: its exact stage (L) changes, a Section 10 row is written, and a Section 12 entry is written where a trait moves with it. A Kind's band (B) changes only by Canon Amendment at the author's word.

**Rule 40.3.3.4 — No Growth Pace.** The story decides when a monster rises and how fast. No timetable, quota or counter of growth exists for monsters.

### 3.4 — Soul, Heart and the Rest of Part 4

**Rule 40.3.4.1 — Recorded Only When the Story Shows It.** The soul ladder begins at Demigod (Part 4 Area 2), so a monster's Soul Rung is recorded only at Xiantian and above. Any Part 4 value of a monster that the story has not shown reads `OPEN-AREA`, and nothing is invented to fill it.

**Rule 40.3.4.2 — A Kind Carries Every Cultivation Variety.** A Kind carries affinity, soul and every other cultivation variety that Parts 3 and 4 name, and so does an Individual. The Kind entry has a field for each, and the vocabulary and the values are those of the owning Part, used by reference and never copied or restated here: Affinity Type(s) and Affinity Level (Part 4 Area 1); Soul Rung, Soul Type and Soul Talent (Part 4 Area 2); Heart Level (Part 4 Area 3); Meridian Root (Part 3 Area 5); and any other cultivation variety that Part 3 or Part 4 defines. A Kind records each as a band or a typical value for the Kind, an Individual records its own, and a field the story has not shown reads `OPEN-AREA`. The Soul fields are recorded only at Xiantian and above (Rule 40.3.4.1).

## Area 4 — Monster Moves and Innate Abilities

**Rule 40.4.1.1 — Distinct Abilities and Regular Attacks.** What a monster can do is one of three things:
- **A distinct body-based ability** that is executed without channelling qi (a crushing coil that does something no coil does, a roar that does something no roar does): species-innate, substrate S-3 (Part 6 Area 12.3).
- **A distinct qi-based ability**: cultivation-energy, substrate S-1 (Part 6 Area 12.2.1).
- **A regular attack, and the body's plain capability**: ordinary punches, kicks, bites, claws, goring, charges and tail-sweeps, and a bear's strength, a hawk's eye, a boar's tusk used as tusks are used. This is plain description. It is raw biological capability and is governed as environmental or biological description, with no Part 6 registration (the closing paragraph of Part 6 Rule 12.3.1).

**Rule 40.4.1.2 — Every Distinct Ability Is Named and Entered in the One Register.** Every distinct ability of a Kind or of an Individual, as Rule 40.4.1.3 tests it, is named and entered in the single Named-Move register `ledger-named-moves/_index.md` (Part 6 Rule 6.5; Part 37 Threshold O-5), with the Kind or the Individual as the user. A regular attack is never named or entered. The Kind's or Individual's own entry (its Section 3, Signature Named Moves or Innate Moves) holds the defining block and every value of the move, the register row and the block each point at the other, and the entry owns every value (Part 6 Rule 6.5.6). The Kind entry is the "species entry" of Part 6 Rule 6.5.2 for a move used by the whole Kind. The kind in the register's Classification cell is `Innate Move` for an S-3 move and `Signature Named Move` for an S-1 move.

**Rule 40.4.1.3 — How "Distinct" Is Tested.** An ability is distinct when it is memorable: a reader would remember it afterwards and could call it by a name, because it does something that a regular punch, kick, bite, claw, charge or other ordinary attack does not do, or does an ordinary thing in a way particular to this creature (its own shape, its own effect, its own cost). A regular attack that is only bigger, faster or harder, or made by a stronger body, is not distinct. The test is the story's judgment. It has no count, no power figure, no number of uses and no minimum: a Kind may have many distinct abilities, one, or none. The drafting agent makes the call, and the author's veto is `[REVISE AREA]`; where it is genuinely unclear, the agent asks the author through the orchestrating command and does not guess. A distinct ability that the whole Kind shows is entered with the Kind as its user, and one that only an Individual shows is entered with that Individual as its user, S-3 if it is body-based and S-1 if it is qi-based. For Part 6 Rule 12.3.1(d), a distinct body-based ability of a monster meets the codified-pattern condition by being recorded in its own Kind or Individual entry as its own, which is the monster reading of Part 6 Rule 12.3.7.

**Rule 40.4.1.4 — Access and Scaling.** S-1 abilities are gated by the realm Access Table (Part 6 Area 4). S-3 abilities are not gated by realm and scale with the monster's anatomical development (age, size, conditioning, injury state; Part 6 Rule 12.3.3), recorded in the Kind entry as description. The Species Anatomical Development Scale of Part 16 Area 18.5 belongs to bipedal species and is not applied to monsters. S-3 abilities are immune to the Firmament Lock and to cultivation-energy suppression, and not to anatomical damage (Part 6 Rule 12.3.4).

**Rule 40.4.1.5 — Form.** A Named Move keeps its bold-italic form (Part 6 Rule 6.5.4), whoever uses it. The monster's own name is set in roman (Rule 40.13.1.1). A move is a Named Move and is never a named item (Part 28 Rule 20.8.1(b)).

**Rule 40.4.1.6 — Registration at Gate 2, or at the Author's Interview.** A distinct monster ability is entered in the register by the Post-Write Ledger Update of the Area that first writes its name, on the `NAMED MOVES (new in this Area)` hand-off, with the Kind or Individual as the user; an ability the author sets in a `/new-character` or `/update-ledger` pass is entered in that same pass, its First Area Appearance reading `Not yet established`. An ability named only in a spec enters nothing (Part 38 Rule 1.3.2).

## Area 5 — Monster Behaviour Traits

Monsters also have behaviour traits, and those traits are tracked.

### 5.1 — The Twelve Traits

**Rule 40.5.1.1 — Twelve Tracked Traits.** Every Kind records the twelve traits below as its Baseline (B). Every Individual records the points where it differs (B) and its current state (L).

**Rule 40.5.1.2 — Each Trait Reuses a Part 39 Dimension Where One Exists.** A trait line is written in plain words and names, beside its value, the Part 39 dimension it reuses (the Layer and dimension code, for example `L27-FEAR`, with an option number where one fits). The reuse is a pointer, so that the existing behaviour check can read the line in light form (Rule 40.5.4.1). A trait line is not a Layer register, carries no `ASKED`, `DERIVED` or `N-A` provenance tag, and registers no Signature. A trait with no Part 39 dimension is plain description.

| # | Trait | What it records | Part 39 dimensions reused |

### 5.2 — Baseline and Living

**Rule 40.5.2.1 — Baseline on the Kind, Living on the Individual.** The Kind's trait lines are Baseline (B) and change only by Canon Amendment. An Individual's trait entries are Baseline where they record a difference from its Kind that its history fixed, and Living (L) where they record its current state: hungry, rutting, wounded, enraged, brooding; where it is now; the grudges it holds; its handler; its encounters.

**Rule 40.5.2.2 — Drift Is Logged.** A genuine change of an Individual's habit (a stage advance, a changed temper after a wound, a taming) is a Section 12 entry and never a silent edit. A genuinely new habit that an Area shows without an entry is a finding of the light reading (Rule 40.5.4.1).

### 5.3 — Standing Rules

**Rule 40.5.3.1 — Difference Is Structural, Not Decorative.** Part 39 Rule 39.36.1.1 carries over: a beast whose only difference from an ordinary animal is a mannerism or a name has not been written as a monster. The difference shows across several traits and in the body.

**Rule 40.5.3.2 — Demonstrated, Not Named.** A trait is shown by what the monster does. A sentence that states a trait as a property the narrator asserts ("the boar was territorial") is read as Part 39 Check #20 element 20-9 reads it.

**Rule 40.5.3.3 — Standalone.** The traits of a Kind are not derived from, and are not compared with, those of another Kind or of a person (Rule 40.1.3.4).

### 5.4 — How the Traits Are Audited

**Rule 40.5.4.1 — Light Reading by Check #20.** Gate 1 Check #20 reads a monster's recorded traits in light form: element 20-2 asks that no rendered behaviour contradicts a recorded Baseline trait of the Kind or a Living trait of the Individual. A finding is cleared by revising the Area or by the author's word, never by a drift entry. Element 20-1 does not apply to a non-sentient monster, which is not a character and holds no registration; a sentient monster is registered in full (Rule 40.11.6.1). No new check is added, and the Gate 1 count stays 22.

## Area 6 — Monster Depth Tiers

### 6.1 — The Four Tiers

**Rule 40.6.1.1 — Tier A to D Are Depth of Record.** The tiers mean what they mean for side characters (Part 19 Area 1.2): how much the ledger keeps of the monster. A Kind and an Individual each carry a tier (Rule 40.6.1.4). The tiers do not mean power, danger, realm or rank. The same four tiers apply to side villains in `ledger-character-villain` (Part 18 Area 6.3).

| Tier | The monster (Kind or Individual) is | The record is |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier A** | A monster or Kind central to the story, or recurring across it | A full file (Part 37 Area 3.10), built by hand with the author |
| **Tier B** | A monster or Kind with a defined part in one stretch of the story | A full file, populated as far as the part needs, built by hand with the author |
| **Tier C** | A named incidental monster, or a Kind met incidentally | A short entry in `_index.md`: for an Individual its Kind, its stage, a behaviour line of the six traits temperament, drives, fears and aversions, attack pattern, social structure and response to people, its one distinct ability or habit if it has one, and its Current Condition; for a Kind its Kind Registry row with the same six-trait behaviour line in its Behaviour Note cell |
| **Tier D** | A named monster whose whole use is a few lines of the story, or a Kind met once | One row in `_index.md`: for an Individual its Kind, a one-line descriptor, a one-line Behaviour Note (no code) drawn from the Kind's Baseline, and its Current Condition; for a Kind its Kind Registry row with a one-line Behaviour Note (no code) |

**Rule 40.6.1.2 — No Link to Power.** A Tier D boar and a Tier A boar can stand at the same stage. Most monsters will be Tier C and D, because most monsters are met once and left.

**Rule 40.6.1.3 — Who Assigns the Tier.** The tier is the one the story gives the monster. An agent assigns Tier C and Tier D autonomously, to a Kind and to an Individual alike. Tier A and Tier B, and every sentient monster, are built by hand with the author (Rule 40.6.3.1).

**Rule 40.6.1.4 — A Kind Carries a Tier, and Has a File at Three Points Whatever Its Tier.** A Kind carries a depth tier A to D like an Individual, in the Classification cell of its Kind Registry row. Separately, and whatever its tier, a Kind is promoted to its own full file at its first Named Move (Rule 40.4.1.2), when it recurs at Section scale, or when it is sentient (Part 37 Threshold M-1). The file is a change in the form of the record, not a promotion of the tier: it needs no ruling, the Kind keeps its tier, and the Post-Write Ledger Update makes it. A Kind's tier changes only by the author's ruling (Rule 40.6.2.3).

### 6.2 — What Comes to the Author

**Rule 40.6.2.1 — A Tier C or D Monster Acts Freely.** A Tier C or Tier D monster attacks, injures, flees and is killed freely. None of that is a ruling for the author, and none of it promotes the monster.

**Rule 40.6.2.2 — Four Things Bring It to the Author.** A Tier C or D monster comes to the author when it:
1. kills or maims a named person;
2. destroys a named place or a named thing;
3. becomes a recurring individual that the story must remember; or
4. is tied to a Named Move.

The author rules each time: promote it to the next tier, or keep it where it is and let the moment stand.

**Rule 40.6.2.3 — Nothing Promotes on Its Own.** Recurrence alone, the number of its attacks, the number of its wounds or kills, and being killed never promote a monster. An agent that finds one of the four triggers stops, and the orchestrating command asks the author with `AskUserQuestion`. The agent never decides, by promoting, by declining to promote, or by reading the act down so that the question does not arise (Part 19 Rule 19.1.2.4 pattern; `guardrails/quality/guardrail-threshold-consultation.md`, Routing). A Kind's tier is likewise never changed by an agent: only the author's ruling changes it (Rule 40.6.1.4).

**Rule 40.6.2.4 — Timing.** The question is raised at the moment a trigger is identified: at spec time if the spec plans it, at draft time if the draft reaches it, at Gate 1 if an audit finds it. Drafting of that act waits on the answer.

### 6.3 — The Two Routes

**Rule 40.6.3.1 — Route 1 and Route 2.** A Tier A or Tier B Individual or Kind, and every sentient monster, is built by hand with the author before it appears (`/new-character <name> monster-kind` for a Kind, `/new-character <name> monster` for an Individual; Route 1), as a hero is. A Tier C or D monster that a spec names is registered before drafting, at the tier the spec gives. A Tier C or D monster the drafting agent improvises in an Area is registered at Gate 2 from what that Area established, every field the Area did not settle marked `OPEN-AREA` (Route 2).

## Area 7 — The Monster Ledger

### 7.1 — Folder and Entries

**Rule 40.7.1.1 — One Folder.** `ledger-character-monster/` is the twentieth ledger folder (Part 37 Area 4). Its schema is Part 37 Area 3.10, and its creation thresholds are M-1, M-2 and M-3 (Part 37 Area 1.2). This Part states what the ledger holds; Part 37 states how it is laid out.

**Rule 40.7.1.2 — What the Folder Holds.** `_index.md`, which carries the Kind Registry, the Individual tables by tier, the Reserved table of sentient monsters, the pointer rows of monsters migrated to the villain ledger, the pending author confirmations and the lore compliance block; a file for each Tier A and Tier B Individual and each Tier A and Tier B Kind; and a Kind file at the three points of Rule 40.6.1.4 whatever the Kind's tier. A Tier C or Tier D Kind with none of those points is a row of the Kind Registry, and Tier C and Tier D Individuals are entries and rows of `_index.md`.

### 7.2 — What Each Entry Carries

- **A Kind carries:** its name and aliases; its depth tier (Rule 40.6.1.4); its class (Rule 40.2.2.1); its body and size; its origin; its stage band; its cultivation varieties (Rule 40.3.4.2: affinity type and level, soul rung, type and talent, Heart level, Meridian Root and the rest, each by reference to the owning Part); its habitat and range, with pointers to `ledger-worlds-location`; its twelve Baseline traits; its distinct abilities with their register rows; its Harvest line; how people treat it; and, if it is sentient, its way of speaking and its language (Area 11).
- **An Individual carries:** its Kind (a pointer); its name and epithets; its exact stage and any variation line; its own cultivation varieties (Rule 40.3.4.2); its own history; the points where it differs from its Kind; its current state, place, wounds, handler and grudges; its Threat Role and Survival Status; and, if tamed, its handler.

### 7.3 — Threat Role and Survival Status

**Rule 40.7.3.1 — Threat Role.** A Threat Role is the part a monster plays in the story. It is not a villain status. A Kind records its usual role, and an Individual its own; a Tier C or Tier D Individual, whose entry carries no Threat Role column, takes the usual role of its Kind unless its entry says otherwise. The values are:
- **Hazard**: a danger of a place, avoided or passed rather than fought.
- **Territorial Guardian**: defends a range, a lair or a place of its own.
- **Pursuer**: tracks and hunts a party or a prey.
- **Raider or Swarm**: strikes settlements, caravans or stores, often in numbers.
- **Quarry**: hunted by people for its Harvest.
- **Tamed or Companion**: kept by a handler (Rule 40.9.3.1).
- **Nemesis**: a recurring non-sentient foe of a named party (Rule 40.12.1.2).

**Rule 40.7.3.2 — Survival Status.** `Active`; `Tamed`; `Driven off`; `Killed`; `Sealed`; `Migrated` (the sentient monster's record moved to the villain ledger, Rule 40.12.3.1). A killed monster's entry is preserved as permanent record and marked Inactive.

### 7.4 — Before and After an Area

**Rule 40.7.4.1 — Pre-Write and Post-Write.** The Pre-Write Ledger Check reads the Kind and Individual entries of every monster the Area will use, and refuses a first appearance of a Tier A or Tier B Individual, or of any sentient monster, that has no entry, pointing the author to `/new-character <name> monster-kind` for a Kind and `/new-character <name> monster` for an Individual. The Post-Write Ledger Update writes the Kind row, the Tier C and D entries and rows, the Section 10 and Section 13 changes, and the Named Move rows (Rule 40.4.1.6) of the Area (`guardrails/ledger/guardrail-ledger-update-law.md`).

### 7.5 — The Beasts of Phase 1, and the Frozen Files

**Rule 40.7.5.1 — "Define During Chapter-Spec".** Part 30 plans non-sentient monsters for Phase 1 and fixes their bands: monsters out of the forest beside the border village at Senior or Champion level, and Ruler-level monsters attacking the airship on the Section 8 voyage. This Part creates no Kind for them. The Kind of those beasts is defined and named at the Chapter Spec that plans them, as a proposal in the spec (Part 38 Rule 1.3.2), and its ledger Kind row follows by Threshold M-1, at the Gate 2 of the Area that first describes it, or in the same pass when the author sets the Kind in an interview, or together with a Tier C or Tier D Individual that the Chapter Spec names (Threshold M-2). Until then the Kind's name is `OPEN-AUTHOR`. The beasts are wild pressure, not an opposing force (Rule 40.10.2.1).

**Rule 40.7.5.2 — Existing Mentions Are Read, Not Edited.** The Minotaur species entry in `ledger-people-society-civilization`, the hero ledgers, and Part 30's own Phase 1 monster lines are not edited by this Part. They are pointed at: a Minotaur is a species and Bison a hero (Area 1.2), and Part 30's lines are the source of the first Kinds.

**Rule 40.7.5.3 — The Folder Starts Empty.** The folder is created with `_index.md` alone and its tables empty. No Kind, no Individual and no sentient monster is entered by this Part.

## Area 8 — Monster Ecology and Habitat

### 8.1 — Habitat, Range and Rhythm

**Rule 40.8.1.1 — Habitat Is the Kind's.** A Kind records the places it lives as pointers to `ledger-worlds-location`, and the Ambient Energy Class of its habitat (Part 3 Area 14.2: in Class 3 the wildlife is "denser, more intelligent", and in Class 5 there is "intense animal qi sensitivity"). Habitat is the hook from place to power: a Kind's stage band is read beside the Ambient Energy Class where it lives.

**Rule 40.8.1.2 — The Kind Owns, the Location Mirrors.** The Kind entry owns the habitat. Each location's `Monster Kinds Present` line (Part 37 Area 2.4) mirrors it by pointer, and on a difference the Kind stands (Part 37 Area 5).

**Rule 40.8.1.3 — Range and Rhythm.** A Kind's territory is sized by the footprint of its stage (Part 3 Area 20) and recorded in the territory and range trait. Season, migration and sleep are the rhythm trait. Neither is a count.

### 8.2 — Beast Attraction and the Hunting Economy

**Rule 40.8.2.1 — Read, Not Restated.** Power Crystals, Gems and Shards draw beasts of rising stage (Part 11 Area 8.3), and the hunting of beasts for their Cores is a parallel supply economy (Part 11 Area 11.3). A Kind's drives and triggers (Area 5) record what draws it, by pointer to those rules.

### 8.3 — The Beast World

**Rule 40.8.3.1 — A World of Beasts.** The Beast World of Part 13 is dominated by powerful beast populations. A beast people with a culture is a people (Rule 40.2.1.5); the monster Kinds of such a world are recorded here as any other.

### 8.4 — The Harvest Line

**Rule 40.8.4.1 — Every Kind Carries a Harvest Line.** A Kind's Harvest line states what its body yields, limited by what Parts 11 and 12 allow at its stage:
- a Beast Core only from Core Realm upward (Part 11 Rule 11.1.1);
- Essence Blood and Spirit Marrow from high-tier beasts (Part 11 Area 11.4);
- the beast-bone of Core Realm beasts, and the bone and organ of Divine Realm beasts, as relic materials (Part 12);
- nothing under Part 11 or Part 12 below those levels, and so nothing from a Phase 1 monster.

The line points at those rules and restates none. Ordinary butchery (meat, hide) is plain description and is not a Harvest.

## Area 9 — Monsters and People

### 9.1 — Fear and the Hunting of Monsters

**Rule 40.9.1.1 — People Answer Monsters.** The Monster Hunting Corps (Part 22: dangerous beast and anomalous entity elimination, licensed, for public safety) and ordinary villagers answer monsters by the means their world has. How a place treats a Kind is recorded in the Kind entry.

### 9.2 — Tamers and Beast Carriers

**Rule 40.9.2.1 — Handlers.** A beast kept by a handler is a tamed monster. The Beast Carrier of Part 24 (a trained spirit beast of Xiantian-equivalent stage or above) is one. A Ridebeast or a Beastdray animal is not (Rule 40.1.1.2).

### 9.3 — The Tamed Monster

**Rule 40.9.3.1 — A Tamed Monster Stays a Monster.** A tamed monster keeps its Kind and its entry, and carries Survival Status `Tamed` and a pointer to its handler. If the story makes it a person-like companion, the author rules on whether its record moves to the side-character ledger. The agent never moves it.

### 9.4 — Culture, Consequence and Weight

**Rule 40.9.4.1 — The People's Side.** How the local people name, fear, honour or avoid a Kind is Part 25 culture, recorded as description in the Kind entry. A monster's killing of a person and a person's killing of a monster are consequence events (Part 27) and carry their weight for the people involved (Part 26). A cultivator who absorbs many high Beast Cores may take on traits of the beast type (Part 11 Rule 11.1.2); that is a cultivator's drift, recorded in the cultivator's ledger, and is not a monster's.

## Area 10 — Monsters in Combat

**Rule 40.10.1.1 — Part 5 Governs.** Combat with a monster is governed by Part 5. A monster is a non-person adversary: FC-1 applies to the person, CR-2 and the protected-status rules do not apply to the adversary, and the fight is classified at E1 unless the counts in Part 5 Area 5 say otherwise.

**Rule 40.10.1.2 — A Monster Fights as Itself.** It fights from its attack-pattern trait (Area 5), uses its abilities (Area 4), and is bound by the 3-Level Rule and the Major Realm barrier (Rule 40.3.1.4).

**Rule 40.10.2.1 — Wild Pressure Versus Opposing Force.** A non-sentient monster is wild pressure on the story, never an opposing force. A spec that plans "no opposing force on the page" is consistent with monsters on the page.

**Rule 40.10.3.1 — How a Monster Ends.** A monster is killed, driven off, tamed or sealed, and its Survival Status says which. The death of a non-sentient monster is not a Part 18 defeat type.

**Rule 40.10.4.1 — Wounds Last.** A monster's wounds persist until they heal. They are recorded in Section 13 Current Condition for a Tier A or Tier B Individual, and in the two lines of the Current Condition column for a Tier C or D Individual: the first line the body, each wound with how long it lasts, the second the monster's temper and state. A non-sentient monster has no soul line to fill.

## Area 11 — Sentient Monsters (Reserved)

### 11.1 — What a Sentient Monster Is

**Rule 40.11.1.1 — The Awakened Cultivated Beast.** A sentient monster is the Cultivated Beast (Awakened) of Part 16 Area 12.2: a beast that has developed human-equivalent cognition and will. It is a character under full Part 16 governance, with an ideology (Part 20), a psychology (Part 26) and a consequence arc (Part 27). It is not a creature of the environment, and it is never rendered as one (Standard N40-7). Sentient monsters exist who can talk like humans; they do not appear in Phase 1, and their power is Void God and above.

### 11.2 — The Floor

**Rule 40.11.2.1 — Void God Level 1 Low and Everything Above, Inclusive; None Below, Ever.** The floor for a sentient, talking monster is Void God Level 1 Low and every stage above it, that stage included. Void God is the second Xiantian stage (Part 3), so the class begins there and runs up through the rest of Xiantian, the Core Realm and the Divine Realm. No beast below that floor speaks as a person, reasons in words, bargains or shows an ideology, whatever its age, size or lineage. This puts a floor under the Awakened Cultivated Beasts of Part 16 Area 12.2 that they did not have.

### 11.3 — The Phase 1 Gate

**Rule 40.11.3.1 — None in Phase 1.** Phase 1 is kept free of sentient monsters by four things: Law P30-L9 in Part 30, beside Law P30-L8, which Gate 1 Check #11 element 11-3 reads; a clause in Part 3 Rule 1.7.3 that the Phase 1 ceiling binds monsters; a standing exclusion in `/section-spec`, `/part-spec`, `/chapter-spec` and the Part 38 template; and this rule. No Phase 1 prose, and no Phase 1 spec item, may name, mention, depict, refer to or allude to a sentient monster, by epithet, rumour, legend, idiom or proverb included. The Phase 1 beasts that Part 30 plans are non-sentient and speak no word. The ledger side is lawful: the Reserved table of the monster index, empty, and this Part, are ledger-reference facts and not prose permission.

### 11.4 — Where They May Live

**Rule 40.11.4.1 — Not Decided; OPEN-AUTHOR.** Where sentient monsters may live is not decided. It will come in future phases. No sentient monster is placed in the Sol Star System, or anywhere else, until the author opens the class, and no entry may record a home for one. The narrative ceiling of Rule 40.3.2.1 is settled and binds every monster in Phase 1 prose. Only whether the Layer 1 physical ceilings of Part 3 Rule 1.7.3, which that rule words for "any cultivator born or residing" in the Sol Star System, bind a sentient monster that lives there is `OPEN-AUTHOR` together with this question and is not ruled here.

### 11.5 — How They Speak

**Rule 40.11.5.1 — Two Ways, Set per Kind.** A sentient monster speaks in one or both of these ways, and the Kind entry records which:
- **(a) Human speech in the common tongue**, set as speech aloud (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1).
- **(b) Mind-voice through the soul-sense channel**, set in single angle quotation marks with a sentence that names the channel and whose voice it is (Part 29 Rule 29.14.3.3, form (1)).

**Rule 40.11.5.2 — An Ancient Language.** Some Kinds carry an ancient language, introduced with that Kind. The language's name and words read `OPEN-AUTHOR` until the author gives them. An own-language term is set in italics without a gloss in the Area (Part 16 N16-14), and a body-based ability named in it takes the Species-Cultural Proper Name form with its ledger gloss (Part 6 Rule 12.3.2).

**Rule 40.11.5.3 — A Registered Voice.** A sentient monster that recurs has its Part 29 voice registered as any recurring named speaker does (Part 29 Area 4; Rule 29.4.4.1).

### 11.6 — Registration

**Rule 40.11.6.1 — Built by Hand, Registered in Full.** A sentient monster, and a sentient Kind, is never Tier D (Part 16 Rule 16.1.1.1; Part 39 Rule 39.3.5.6 item 2). It is Tier A or Tier B by default, and Tier C only on the author's ruling. It is built by hand with the author before it appears. It takes the full Part 39 registration (every Layer, with `L32-TYPE-06`, Other sentient being), the Part 20 Ideology Profile in Section 4, and the Part 26 psychology in Section 5. If it takes a bipedal form it stays under the Cultivated-Beast framework and not under Part 16 Area 18 (Part 16 Area 18.1).

**Rule 40.11.6.2 — The Reserved Table.** Sentient monsters are listed in the Reserved table of the monster index until a ruling migrates one (Rule 40.12.3.1). The table is empty, with no names, no numbers and no entries, and it stays so until the author opens the class.

## Area 12 — Monsters, Villains and Side Villains

### 12.1 — The Line

**Rule 40.12.1.1 — A Monster Without a Mind Is Never a Villain.** A villain needs a worldview: evil without ideological coherence is not canonical (Part 18 Rule 18.1.1.2). A non-sentient monster has none. Only a sentient monster can move to the villain ledger.

**Rule 40.12.1.2 — Nemesis.** A non-sentient monster that keeps coming back stays in the monster ledger with the Threat Role Nemesis. A man-eating tiger is a menace and not a villain.

### 12.2 — The Villain-Classification Trap

**Rule 40.12.2.1 — A Non-Sentient Monster's Hostility Is Not Qualifying Behaviour.** Part 37 Threshold C-2 fires the villain-classification question when a newly introduced named character's first appearance shows violence against, or in opposition to, a hero or their allies, among other behaviours. Read literally, every monster attack would fire it. It does not. The attack, pursuit, hostility or killing of a non-sentient monster is not qualifying behaviour for Threshold C-2, and the question is never put for a non-sentient monster. The same holds for the Pre-Write refusal text of `guardrails/ledger/guardrail-ledger-update-law.md`.

**Rule 40.12.2.2 — Sentient Monsters, Once.** A sentient monster whose first appearance shows ideology-driven opposition to a hero or their allies raises the same question as Threshold C-2 (Threshold M-3), once, at that first appearance: Villain, or stays a monster.

### 12.3 — Migration

**Rule 40.12.3.1 — One Canonical File.** When a sentient monster is classified Villain, its record moves to `ledger-character-villain` as one canonical file (Part 37 Area 5, one owner). The monster index keeps a pointer row marked `migrated`, and the Kind stays in the monster ledger. The villain index lists the move in its pointer table. The villain's tier is named only when the author maps one (Rule 40.12.4.2); until then it reads unmapped.

### 12.4 — Side Villains and Villain Tiers

**Rule 40.12.4.1 — Side Villains.** A side villain is a minor antagonist (Part 18 Rule 18.1.1.3) or a Sub-Villain (Part 18 Rule 18.8.1.2): a named antagonist who serves a larger villain structure or appears in a smaller part of the story. The rule lives in Part 18 Area 6.3.

**Rule 40.12.4.2 — Tiers on the Villain Side.** `ledger-character-villain` is arranged by the same four tiers of depth of record: Tier A and Tier B are full Villain Ledger files, reserved and empty until the macro story maps them; Tier C and Tier D are the side villains, as short entries or rows (Part 18 Area 6.3). A villain's tier is named only when the author maps one: the Phase 1 Section 7 villain of Part 30 stays unmapped until the author maps it, and an unmapped villain is filed and registered as before, in full (Part 18 Rule 18.6.3.3).

**Rule 40.12.4.3 — A Beast Serving a Villain Is Still a Beast.** A monster that a villain commands, or fights beside, stays a monster in the monster ledger and is not a villain (Part 19 Rule 19.7.2.1 pattern). The villain is the person.

## Area 13 — Monster Naming

### 13.1 — Form

**Rule 40.13.1.1 — Roman Type.** A monster is a living being and sits with persons and species, not with made things. Part 28 Rule 20.8.1 limits named items to made or used things and excludes persons and species. Monster names, Kind names and Individual names are therefore set in roman. They are not italic, bold or bold-italic. A move a monster uses keeps the bold-italic Named Move form (Rule 40.4.1.5). A sentient monster's own-language term follows Part 16 N16-14. A Kind name is a common name of a category and is written in lower case in running prose.

### 13.2 — How a Name Is Made

**Rule 40.13.2.1 — The Part 28 Sequence.** A Kind name and an Individual name are made by the Rule 20.3 sequence of Part 28: the ledger scan first, which now includes `ledger-character-monster/_index.md`, then the register of the naming culture, the convention, the story resonance, the distinctiveness check, and the originality check. Immutability, the collision scan and the Gate 1 Check #17 sub-checks (a) to (e) apply unchanged, and the ledger row written at Gate 2 satisfies Check #17(c).

**Rule 40.13.2.2 — Who Names a Beast.** The local people name a wild beast, in the Part 25 register of the region, because the monster has no culture of its own. A sentient monster's own language names it, or a human-register name it has adopted as a character's choice (Part 16 Rule 19.19.3.2).

**Rule 40.13.2.3 — Both Names Are Given.** The Kind carries its common name. An Individual that passes the significance test gets a personal name or epithet. Naming is the recommended default and is never counted as a cost (Part 28 Rule 20.9).

### 13.3 — MAJOR and MINOR

**Rule 40.13.3.1 — Who Chooses.** These monster names are MAJOR, and the author chooses from two or three candidates with SKIP (Part 28 Rule 20.4): every sentient monster, every Tier A and Tier B Individual, every Tier A and Tier B Kind, and any Kind that recurs at Section scale. Every other monster name, a Tier C or Tier D Kind or Individual included, is MINOR and is made by the assistant, with `[REVISE AREA]` as the author's veto.

## Area 14 — Narration Standards for Monsters

**N40-1 — Not a Human With Fur.** A monster is written as a beast. Its difference from a person shows in its body and across several of its traits, never as one decorative mannerism (Rule 40.5.3.1).

**N40-2 — Not a Stat Sheet.** Danger is shown by what the monster does and what it costs. A realm name is never a substitute for a scene, and the level designations that do appear keep the form of Part 3 Rule 1.7.6.

**N40-3 — Behaviour Is Shown, Not Named.** The twelve traits appear in conduct. A line that states a trait as a narrator's property fails Check #20 element 20-9 (Rule 40.5.3.2).

**N40-4 — Wonder and Fear Are Allowed.** A monster may be awesome, terrible or beautiful. It is not evil: its hostility is hunger, territory, fear or instinct (Rule 40.1.3.3).

**N40-5 — Consistency With the Kind.** An Individual behaves as its Kind unless its own recorded differences or its current state say otherwise. A departure has a cause on the page.

**N40-6 — Cost Is Visible.** A monster's attack costs the people it strikes, and a fight costs the monster. Wounds persist (Rule 40.10.4.1), and neither side is free (Part 27).

**N40-7 — The Beast Does Not Speak, and the Speaker Is Not a Beast.** A non-sentient monster never speaks, bargains, plans in words or shows an ideology. A sentient monster is never rendered by default as a creature, a spectacle or a force (Part 16 N16-10 and N16-17).

**N40-8 — The Forms.** Monster names in roman; Named Moves in bold-italic; named items in italic; mind-voice in single angle quotation marks naming its channel (Rules 40.13.1.1, 40.4.1.5 and 40.11.5.1).
