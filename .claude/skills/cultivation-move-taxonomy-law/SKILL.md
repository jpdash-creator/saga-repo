## Overview

Part 6 is the Cultivation Move Taxonomy Law — the canon Part that governs every Named Move in the Starward Saga: what a Named Move is, how it is classified, how it is created and named, how it costs and depletes energy, how it is learned and transmitted, how it collides with other moves in combat, how Signature moves bind to a cultivator's identity, how Fusion moves merge distinct power streams, how every move must be rendered in prose, and, in Area 12, what substrate a move's operative principle is actually made of (cultivation energy, bloodline resonance, species-innate anatomy, relic channel, weapon bond, technology aid, formation mediation, or hybrid composite).

Part 6 does not govern cultivation itself (Part 3), affinity or soul cultivation (Part 4), or the combat engagement framework a move is deployed within (Part 5) — those are the domains of their respective Parts. Part 6 governs the technique layer that sits on top of them: the Named Move as a discrete, classifiable, transmissible, narratable canon object. Every Named Move has a Type (Area 2, complexity and success-rate tier), a Combat Function (Area 3, what it does in a fight), a realm-access ceiling (Area 4), an energy cost profile (Area 5), a naming and creation lineage (Area 6), a learning and transmission history (Area 7), collision behavior against other moves (Area 8), and — for the rarest techniques — Signature (Area 9) or Fusion (Area 10) status. Every Named Move must be rendered in prose according to the DP-9 three-layer narration standard (Area 11). And every Named Move rests on exactly one primary substrate from the eight-category taxonomy that governs its gating, suppression behavior, and collision interactions (Area 12).

This skill file holds the 12 Areas of Part 6 in one reference, under their own `## Area N` sections, in Part 6's Area order (Areas 1–12). The cross-reference table listing the `cultivation-combat-and-fight-law` HARD link and the one-paragraph "Governing Authority" statement that apply to every Area stand once, at the end of this document, in "Cross-References and Governing Authority".

This file is the single authority for the whole of Part 6, and there is no layer beneath it.

## Area 1 — Moves and Cultivation

### 1.1 — Cultivation Realm and Moves

A cultivator's realm position on the ladder (Part 3, Area 1) is the primary structural constraint on what moves are possible. This operates on three levels: access (which move types are available), output (how powerful a move's effect is), and expression (how the move physically manifests).

**Mortal Tier 1 — Physical Architecture Moves:**
Mortal Tier 1 cultivators have no qi circulation and thus no energy-based moves. All Named Moves at this tier are physical-architecture techniques — precise applications of refined body mechanics. These moves are not weak. A Soul-stage Mortal Tier 1 cultivator's **Iron Bone Shattering Stamp** is stronger than an Unawakened human's equivalent action (the Soul stage stands at 1.7 times Unawakened on the Part 3 Area 1.6 Relative Power Table). What limits these moves is their ceiling — without energy, they cannot produce environmental effects, ranged attacks, or persistent phenomena. Move types accessible at Mortal Tier 1 (Part 3 Area 16.2): Basic; Intermediate only for near-Soul-stage physical specialists (Rule 4.2.2). Advanced, Elite, Fusion, Catastrophic, and Mythic are categorically inaccessible — they require energy substrates that do not exist at this tier.

*The move expression of a Mortal Tier 1 Named Move is always physical: strikes, grips, sweeps, throws, kicks, footwork patterns, weapon handling techniques, and refined body-movement sequences. Every Named Move at this tier is ultimately a pattern of biomechanical excellence.*

**Mortal Tier 2 — Qi-Physical Hybrid Moves:**
Mortal Tier 2 introduces qi circulation. Moves at this tier combine physical structure with energy expression, producing techniques that can project force beyond physical contact, create brief environmental disturbances, and begin interacting with the world beyond the cultivator's body. This is the tier where affinity first becomes relevant (typically from Senior onwards, though genuine affinity expression is most pronounced at Warrior and above).

Move type ceiling at Mortal Tier 2 rises with the sub-realm (Part 3 Area 16.2): Senior through Warrior reach Basic and Intermediate; Champion through Ruler reach Advanced; Monarch reaches Elite. Signature achievable for exceptional cultivators; Fusion absent from Senior through Ruler (no awakened soul exists before Monarch Level 9 Peak, Part 4 Area 2.5) and rare and unstable at Monarch Level 9 Peak, where an early-awakened soul allows a qi-plus-soul Fusion (Rule 4.2.3); Catastrophic and Mythic categorically inaccessible.

*Named Moves at Mortal Tier 2 begin to have environmental signatures — light effects, pressure waves, qi tremors, brief elemental discharges. These environmental signatures become more pronounced as the cultivator advances through the tier.*

**Xiantian Realm — Energy Expression Moves:**
The Xiantian Realm (Demigod through Absolute God) represents the first tier where pure energy expression makes moves capable of affecting the environment at meaningful range without physical contact. Named Moves at this tier can reshape terrain, interact with atmospheric forces, and produce effects measurable at distances the cultivator does not physically occupy. Soul moves become fully accessible at this tier (requiring soul cultivation — Part 4, Area 2).

Move type ceiling at Xiantian (Part 3 Area 16.2): Demigod through Void God reach Basic through Elite, with Catastrophic first possible; True God and Absolute God reach Basic through Catastrophic as standard, and Mythic is understood but not deployable from True God; a first attempt is possible only at the extreme peak of Absolute God, and it is the rarest achievement at this tier.

*Named Moves in the Xiantian Realm have strong environmental expression that aligns with the Heavenly Scale Grade signatures (Part 3, Area 3.3). A Void God's Attack moves generate the "wall of pressure" effect described at Grade 7. A True God's moves interact with atmospheric forces consistent with Grade 8.*

**Core Realm — Core-Powered Moves:**
The Core Realm (Core Formation through Core Solidification) fundamentally transforms technique execution. Because the Core functions as an internal reactor rather than a finite tank of qi, Core Realm moves can be sustained, repeated, and executed at output levels impossible for cultivators still drawing on circulating qi. The Core's rhythmic output becomes audible and physical to those in range — a Core Realm cultivator's techniques carry the distinctive pulse described in Part 3, Grades 10–12.

Move type ceiling at Core (Part 3 Area 16.2): Core Formation through Core Assimilation reach Basic through Catastrophic, and Mythic is first practically accessible; Core Solidification has full access to all eight types. Catastrophic moves become routine combat tools (not just emergency deployments) by Core Solidification. Mythic moves remain genuinely difficult even there.

*Named Moves in the Core Realm carry the Core's distinctive resonance — the deep internal pulse that witnesses feel in their bones. High-tier Core Realm techniques produce geological-scale effects at their peak.*

**Divine Realm — Reality-Interaction Moves:**
Divine Realm moves do not merely apply force — they interact with the fundamental laws of reality. Named Moves at the Divine Realm level can manipulate space, time, causality, and the fabric of existence. At Divine Transformation, techniques begin bending space-time locally. At Sea Formation, moves interact with dimensional layers. At Heaven Treading, spatial techniques become second nature. At Divine Ascension and Divine Sea Realm, moves operate at scales that register as weather events, geological shifts, and cosmological disturbances.

All eight move types are accessible and fully deployable at the Divine Realm. Mythic moves — reality-bending by definition — are most naturally produced here, though even Divine Realm cultivators face the 10% base success rate challenge for Mythic-type executions.

*Named Moves in the Divine Realm have consequences that persist beyond the immediate combat. The world is changed by their use. A Part 27 consequence event is automatically triggered by any Divine Realm Named Move at Advanced or above that operates in a populated or historically significant area.*

**Move Output Scaling Rule:**
A Named Move's output scales directly with the cultivator's Heavenly Scale Grade (Part 3, Area 3.3). The same named technique — say, **Heaven Piercing Thrust** — executed by a Grade 3 cultivator versus a Grade 10 cultivator produces categorically different results. The move's TYPE remains constant (it is the same technique). Its FUNCTION remains constant (it is still a Penetrating Attack). Its OUTPUT is entirely different. This is a critical canon principle: technique inheritance does not confer the power of the original creator.

### 1.2 — Cultivation Affinity and Moves

Affinity (governed in full by Part 4, Area 1) is the resonance relationship between a cultivator's qi and a specific force of nature or metaphysical principle. It shapes moves in two distinct ways: it enables affinity-typed moves, and it amplifies the execution of any technique aligned with the cultivator's affinity.

**Affinity-Typed Moves:**
A cultivator with Fire Affinity can develop Named Moves that embody fire's principles — combustion, expansion, consuming light, transformative heat. These are not simply "fire-effect" moves; they are moves that draw on the cultivator's genuine resonance with fire as a universal force. An affinity-typed move at high affinity level does things that energy without affinity cannot replicate — a Fire Affinity move at Harmony level (Level 6+) does not merely produce fire; it produces fire whose behavior is governed by the cultivator's will at a level of precision that non-affinity fire techniques cannot match.

**Affinity Level and Move Quality:**
The nine affinity levels (Null through Origin Convergence — Part 4, Area 1.2) determine how powerfully affinity expression shapes a move:
- **Null / Whisper / Echo (Levels 1–3):** Affinity affects efficiency only (Part 4 Area 1.2), not the move's behaviour. The cultivator can develop affinity-typed moves but they show only surface-level alignment — primarily visual and atmospheric.
- **Resonance / Attunement (Levels 4–5):** Affinity begins to alter the move's mechanical behavior. A Water Affinity move flows around obstacles differently from a standard move. Techniques gain a secondary resonance effect that interacts with affinity-compatible environments.
- **Harmony (Level 6):** The Affinity Advantage modifier (Part 3, Area 7.2; Part 4, Area 1.5) activates. Affinity-aligned technique output is amplified by at most one minor step for technique execution in aligned contexts. Affinity-typed moves at this level are distinctly superior to non-affinity equivalents in the same domain.
- **Communion / Sovereignty (Levels 7–8):** Affinity-typed moves begin to interact with the affinity's environmental expression — a Fire Affinity at Communion level turns environmental fire into an extension of the technique rather than background noise. Sovereignty-level moves in the affinity domain are effectively incomparable to standard techniques; they operate by the rules of the affinity type, not merely by cultivation qi.
- **Origin Convergence (Level 9):** Techniques at this level transcend the affinity as a category and embody its principle directly. A Space Affinity Origin Convergence cultivator does not use space techniques — they enact spatial law. Named Moves at this level are unique in the universe; no one else's technique can replicate them.

**Multi-Affinity Moves:**
A cultivator with multiple affinities (Affinity Depth of 2 or more — Part 4, Area 1.4) can develop moves that draw on more than one affinity simultaneously. Multi-affinity moves are categorically classified as Fusion-type moves in the Move Type system, because merging two affinity streams in a single technique is an act of fusion. All Fusion move rules (Area 10) apply. Multi-affinity moves are among the most complex and unpredictable techniques in existence.

**Non-Primary Affinity Moves:**
A cultivator can use techniques that belong to an affinity they do not possess or possess weakly. However, such techniques operate without the amplification, resonance precision, and environmental interaction that affinity provides. A cultivator without Water Affinity using a water-based technique is producing water-effect qi expression, not genuine affinity interaction. The move is valid but significantly weaker in the affinity domain than the equivalent technique used by a genuine Water Affinity cultivator.

### 1.3 — Cultivation Power Density and Moves

Power Density (Part 3, Area 3) is the concentration of cultivated force per unit of the cultivator's will deployment. It affects moves in ways distinct from realm position: two cultivators at the same realm can have different power densities and thus produce meaningfully different outputs from the same Named Move.

**Density and Move Output:**
Within the same realm, each minor step (Low, Mid, High, Peak, then the next Level's Low) is one row of the Relative Power Table (Part 3, Area 1.6), and the table gives each position its own value. A cultivator at Level 9 Peak stands one minor step above one at Level 9 High, and the table values for the two positions differ accordingly. This means the same Named Move executed by these two cultivators produces visibly different outputs. **Thunderstep Advance** by a Level 9 Peak Void God hits harder, reaches farther, and creates a more pronounced environmental response than the same technique executed by a Level 5 Mid Void God.

**Density and Move Success Rate:**
Power density directly modifies the success rate of move types above Basic:
- A cultivator with **high power density relative to the move's requirements** (strong energy foundation) gains a success rate bonus of up to +5% on Intermediate through Catastrophic moves
- A cultivator with **low power density relative to the move's requirements** (stretching beyond natural energy capacity) suffers a success rate penalty of up to −10% on the same types
- Mythic moves are not modified by power density in the same way — their uncertainty is metaphysical, not energetic

**Density and Move Sustainability:**
A cultivator with exceptionally high power density for their realm can execute techniques that lower-density cultivators cannot sustain. Channeled techniques (Area 5) that require sustained energy output are directly limited by the cultivator's energy reserve, which scales with density. A Core Assimilation cultivator at Level 9 Peak can sustain channeled techniques far longer than a Core Assimilation cultivator at Level 1 Low — the same move, but incomparably different utility in extended combat.

**Density-Based Move Variants:**
Some Named Moves have density-dependent variations — the same technique that produces a Minor variant at low density produces a Major or Grand variant at high density. These are not separate moves — they are the same move, recognized by the same name, but their scale of expression varies with the cultivator's density. A move's **maximum variant tier** is established when the move is created or registered.

**A limit-broken technique (pointer, 2026-10-17).** A technique used in a limit break (Part 3 Area 7.5) is still an ordinary Named Move of its own type and grade, named under Area 6, and a limit break gives no move a new type, grade or name. The burst lifts the user's effective level for the moment and nothing else about the move.

### 1.4 — Cultivation Root and Moves

The Meridian Root (Part 3, Area 5) governs the efficiency of energy absorption, circulation, and storage. While it does not define what moves a cultivator can learn, it profoundly affects how well they execute them and how many they can deploy in sustained combat.

**Root and Execution Efficiency:**
A higher-quality Meridian Root translates to less energy wasted per technique execution. A Golden Root cultivator executing **Crimson Spiral Burst** wastes perhaps 15% of the technique's energy to inefficiency. An Iron Root cultivator executing the same technique wastes 40–60%. The technique looks the same in its core expression but the Iron Root cultivator depletes their reserves far faster in sustained combat.

**Root and Move Learning Speed:**
Higher-quality Roots absorb technique patterns faster. A Heavenly Root cultivator observing a technique once and practicing it briefly may achieve Practiced mastery in days. A Raw Root cultivator studying the same technique diligently for years may only achieve Novice mastery within a practical timeframe. The Roots do not change what moves are possible — they determine how quickly and thoroughly those moves can be learned.

**Root and Move Innovation:**
Refined and Transcendent Spectrum Roots (Part 3, Area 5.2) have unique properties that create corresponding unique move capabilities:
- **Void Root:** Can absorb hostile techniques during combat and incorporate their principles into original moves. A Void Root cultivator who experiences an opponent's **Sky Piercing Lance** may, over time, develop a variant that incorporates its penetrating principle. This absorption is not instantaneous — it is a learning process that occurs between fights.
- **Eternal Root:** The perpetual cultivation means technique fundamentals deepen continuously even without deliberate practice. An Eternal Root cultivator's basic techniques become increasingly precise over time, even if the cultivator never deliberately drills them.
- **Celestial Root:** Techniques aligned with cosmic timing (celestial events, tidal forces, planetary positions) express amplified output during cosmically significant moments. A Celestial Root cultivator's moves are not constant — they are at their weakest in ordinary moments and incomprehensibly powerful at cosmically significant junctures.
- **Infinite Root:** No hard ceiling on technique depth. Every move an Infinite Root cultivator masters continues to deepen and evolve indefinitely — the technique ceiling that others hit does not apply.

**Root and Move Mastery Caps:**
Raw through Bronze Root cultivators face mastery caps that are slightly below the theoretical maximum for Fusion, Catastrophic, and Mythic moves. This reflects the reality that the energy architecture required for these move types is difficult to sustain through lower-quality meridian channels. Specifically: Raw Root maximum mastery for Fusion is 30% (vs. 50% for others); Iron Root is 35%; Bronze Root is 40%. Silver Root and above achieve the standard maximum mastery rates defined in Area 2.

**Eternal Root and Soul Move Ceiling (Rule 1.4.5):**
The Eternal Root's perpetual cultivation property interacts uniquely with soul moves. Because the Eternal Root continuously deepens technique fundamentals without deliberate drilling, soul moves developed by an Eternal Root cultivator benefit from continuous passive deepening — even during physical cultivation sessions. The Eternal Root soul move ceiling is the same as any other root (constrained by soul rung per Area 1.6), but the **time cost to advance mastery grades is significantly reduced**: an Eternal Root cultivator advances soul move mastery at the same continuous-deepening rate as physical moves. Additionally, the Eternal Root's perpetual cultivation does not create soul force on its own — soul cultivation must still be awakened deliberately (Part 4, Area 2.5). The Eternal Root accelerates mastery speed for soul moves already being cultivated; it does not auto-generate soul techniques or soul rung advancement.

### 1.5 — Cultivation Heart and Moves

The Cultivation Heart (Part 4, Area 3) governs how much of a cultivator's theoretical maximum capability is accessible when pushed to ultimate limits. It interacts with moves not through power modification but through **performance consistency under pressure** and **crisis access**.

**Heart and High-Pressure Move Execution:**
When a cultivator is under extreme combat pressure — physiologically damaged, emotionally stressed, facing death — their effective cultivation access drops. A cultivator without an awakened Heart operates below their true capacity under these conditions. Their Named Moves execute with correspondingly reduced output and elevated failure rates. The Heart prevents this drop from occurring (Part 4 Area 3.1 gives the idea; the rule is the list below).

The Heart level sets move success rates under ultimate pressure:
- **No Heart / Ember Heart:** Under maximum pressure, success rates for Advanced and above moves drop by 10–20% from base. The cultivator's execution is compromised by their internal state.
- **Iron Heart:** Under maximum pressure, Advanced move success rates hold; Elite and Signature see 5% reduction. Basic and Intermediate maintain full rates.
- **Blazing Heart:** Under maximum pressure, all move types up to Elite maintain base success rates. Signature sees 5% reduction; Fusion/Catastrophic see 10% reduction.
- **Unbreakable Heart:** All move types up to Signature maintain base success rates under maximum pressure. Fusion and Catastrophic see only 5% reduction. Additionally, aura suppression from opponents reduces effectiveness by 1 Grade less than it otherwise would (Part 4, Area 3.3) — meaning technique output is less degraded by overwhelming opponent presence.
- **Sovereign Heart:** All move types maintain their mastery-level success rates even under maximum pressure. The cultivator accesses their true ceiling regardless of circumstances. Aura suppression reduces effectiveness by 2 Grades less. A Sovereign Heart cultivator executing a Mythic move when facing death performs exactly as well as they would in optimal conditions.

**Heart and Signature Moves:**
The Cultivation Heart has the most profound specific interaction with Signature moves. Signature moves are identity-bound techniques that draw on the cultivator's deepest personal truth. A cultivator without any Heart awakening may technically have a Signature move, but its expression is shallow — it relies on cultivated proficiency rather than genuine personal resonance. A cultivator with an awakened Heart, particularly at Blazing level or above, finds their Signature moves expressing something beyond technique — a lived truth that manifests as power. This is not a mechanical modifier; it is a narrative quality that the prose must render at the appropriate depth.

**Heart and Move Evolution:**
The Heart state directly influences the likelihood of a technique evolving to the next type. A move evolution event (Area 9.3) is significantly more likely during states of genuine Heart engagement — moments of profound crisis, genuine emotional reckoning, or the exact kind of lived experience that causes Heart advancement. This creates a narrative structure where a character's inner journey and technique development are intertwined, not parallel.

**Heart State × Mastery Grade Interaction (Rule 1.5.6):**
Heart-based success rate reductions under pressure apply on top of mastery grade bonuses — they are not cancelled by mastery. A cultivator with Expert mastery (+20% success rate bonus) still loses that bonus to Heart-based pressure penalties. The net result:
- **No Heart / Ember Heart** under maximum pressure: Advanced moves drop 10–20% from base *after* mastery bonus is added. Example: Expert mastery Advanced move = Base 60% + 20% bonus = 80%. Under Ember Heart maximum pressure, 80% − 15% = 65% effective success rate.
- **Iron Heart** under maximum pressure: Advanced moves hold; Elite/Signature see 5% reduction applied after mastery bonus. Mastery bonuses still meaningfully matter.
- **Blazing Heart and above**: Mastery bonuses apply cleanly in all conditions up to the Heart's pressure ceiling — Heart state removes the need to "fight through" the pressure drop, allowing mastery bonuses to realize their full value.
- **Sovereign Heart**: Mastery bonuses apply at their full value in every condition. A Sovereign Heart cultivator's Expert mastery Advanced move always executes at 80% or above regardless of combat conditions — this is the complete "what you train is what you get" standard that Sovereign Heart achieves.

**Practical implication:** Mastery improvement is most valuable to cultivators with higher Heart stages, because a lower Heart stage sees a portion of that mastery investment negated under real combat pressure. A cultivator with No Heart / Ember Heart benefits from mastery training but will consistently underperform their theoretical ceiling when it matters most. A Sovereign Heart cultivator realizes the full return on every hour of practice.

### 1.6 — Cultivation Soul and Moves

Soul cultivation (Part 4, Area 2) creates an entirely distinct layer of Named Moves that operate on the metaphysical dimension rather than (or in addition to) the physical dimension. Soul moves do not replace physical moves — they add a second combat axis that interacts with the physical in complex ways.

**The Nature of Soul Moves:**
Soul moves are Named Moves that deploy soul force — the metaphysical energy developed through soul cultivation — rather than or alongside qi. Their targets may be physical (the soul force damages the physical body through the metaphysical layer), metaphysical (the soul force interacts with the target's soul cultivation directly), or environmental (the soul force affects the landscape of the metaphysical layer that all cultivators inhabit to varying degrees).

Soul moves follow all the type classification rules (Area 2) and combat function rules (Area 3) of this Part. A soul move can be Basic or Mythic in type. It can be an Attack or Control in function. The distinction is the energy substrate (soul force vs qi) and the targeting layer (metaphysical vs physical).

**Soul Move Access Requirements:**
Soul moves require active soul cultivation. A cultivator who has not yet awakened their soul cultivation (Part 4, Area 2.5) has no soul moves regardless of their physical cultivation level. Early-awakening cultivators (possible from Mortal Tier 2 Monarch peak) may develop Basic and Intermediate soul moves before reaching the Xiantian Realm. Late-awakening cultivators develop their soul move repertoire more quickly after awakening because they are compensating for lost time.

**Soul Move Types in Relation to the Eleven Soul Technique Types:**
The eleven soul technique types defined in Part 4, Area 2.3 (Weapon, Defense, Trap, Convert, Phantom, Devour, Healing, Bind, Resonate, Nullify, Sense) map to the combat function classification of Area 3 as follows:
- **Weapon Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Attack sub-function (Soul Strike)
- **Defense Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Defense sub-function (Soul Shield)
- **Trap Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Trap sub-function (Soul Trap; Trap)
- **Convert Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Defense sub-function (Soul Convert; Absorb) — the most dangerous soul move category
- **Phantom Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Control sub-function (Soul Illusion; Confuse)
- **Devour Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Attack sub-function (Soul Devour) — damages by absorbing
- **Healing Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Support sub-function (Soul Restoration)
- **Bind Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Control sub-function (Soul Bind)
- **Resonate Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Combination sub-function (Soul Resonance)
- **Nullify Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Defense sub-function (Soul Nullification)
- **Sense Soul techniques** = Soul-Specific Support sub-function (Soul Perception)

**Soul Moves and Physical Defense:**
The defining property of soul moves is that they bypass the cultivator's physical defensive layer. A Core Solidification cultivator's body may be effectively indestructible by physical attacks — but a sufficiently powerful soul attack can still damage their soul structure, which then produces real consequences for physical cultivation. Conversely, soul defenses do not protect against physical attacks. Soul and physical combat operate simultaneously but on different targeting layers.

**Simultaneous Physical and Soul Moves:**
A cultivator with both physical cultivation and soul cultivation can execute a Named Move on both layers simultaneously — one physical technique and one soul technique in the same combat action. This is the most advanced form of cultivation combat and is almost universally described as terrifying to face. The naming convention for simultaneous dual-layer techniques typically reflects both dimensions: **Crimson Flame Strike — Soul Invasion** indicates a physical fire attack accompanied by a soul penetration attempt.

**Soul Rung and Move Ceiling:**
The cultivator's soul rung (Part 4, Area 2.2 — Soul Wisp through Eternal Soul) constrains the maximum complexity of soul moves accessible, in the same way cultivation realm constrains physical move access. The soul rung ceiling by move type:
- **Soul Wisp / Soul Shade (Rungs 1–2):** Basic and Intermediate soul moves only
- **Soul Form / Soul Echo (Rungs 3–4):** Up to Advanced; Elite possible
- **Soul Anchor / Soul Domain (Rungs 5–6):** Up to Elite; Signature soul moves achievable
- **Soul Fortress / Soul Ignition (Rungs 7–8):** All types through Catastrophic
- **Soul Sea / Heaven Soul (Rungs 9–10):** All types; Mythic soul moves accessible
- **Ascending Soul / Eternal Soul (Rungs 11–12):** Full access; Mythic soul moves achievable with high mastery

**Soul Move Success Rate Modifiers:**
Soul moves use the same base success rates and mastery grades as physical moves (Area 2.1). However, soul moves have additional success rate modifiers specific to the soul layer that apply on top of the standard modifiers (Area 2.1 combat condition modifiers):

| Soul Move Modifier | Effect on Success Rate |
|---|---|
| Target has no active soul cultivation | +15% — undefended souls are easier to affect |
| Target's soul rung ≥ attacker's soul rung | −15% to −30% depending on differential — soul defense naturally resists comparable or superior attacks |
| Target has active Defense Soul or Nullify Soul technique | −10% to −25% — active soul defense significantly reduces soul move effectiveness |
| Attacker's soul is damaged (fragmentation, exhaustion) | −10% to −20% — soul damage degrades move execution precision |
| Attacker is in a high soul energy environment (Sacred Ground S-Grade 3+) | +5% to +10% — soul energy density improves soul move execution |
| Target is distracted or emotionally destabilized | +5% to +15% — emotional turbulence weakens soul defense coherence |
| Attacker attempts soul move while physically attacking simultaneously | −5% per additional simultaneous physical technique — split attention reduces soul move precision |

These modifiers stack with the standard combat condition modifiers from Area 2.1 (injury, energy depletion, opponent disruption). Soul moves with a combined modifier below −30% from all stacked sources are at high failure risk even at Expert or Master mastery.

## Area 2 — Move Type Classification

### 2.1 — Overview

Move Types classify the **complexity, rarity, and execution difficulty** of Named Moves. They are the vertical axis of the taxonomy — from the simplest, most reliable techniques to the rarest, most uncertain reality-bending executions. Every Named Move belongs to exactly one of the eight Move Types. Type does not change after a move's creation unless the move undergoes a formal Type Upgrade (Area 9.3).

**The Mastery System:**
Practice and repeated use improve success rates. Each move type has a **base success rate** (achieved by a practitioner with minimal mastery) and a **maximum mastery rate** (achieved after sustained dedicated practice). Between these are five mastery grades:

| Mastery Grade | Definition | Success Rate Modifier |
|--------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Novice | First-learned; mechanically understood but inconsistent | Base rate |
| Practiced | Regular use; execution reliable in optimal conditions | Base + 5% |
| Adept | Internalized; reliable under moderate combat stress | Base + 10% |
| Proficient | Deep familiarity; reliable even under significant pressure | Base + 15% |
| Expert | Near-complete mastery; fails only under extreme conditions | Base + 20% |
| Master | Complete mastery; at maximum mastery rate | Maximum rate |

No grade exceeds the type's Maximum Mastery Rate; Master always equals it. At Catastrophic, Expert and Master coincide; at Mythic, the grades from Adept up coincide; mastery then shows in speed and cost (Rule 5.4.3).

**Success Rate and Combat Conditions:**
Success rates are stated for standard conditions (no injury, no disruption, a healthy energy reserve). The following modifiers apply:
- **Severe injury:** −5% to −15% depending on injury severity
- **Energy depletion:** the Depletion States of Part 3 Area 12.4 (see also Part 3 Area 16.4) set the penalty; this Part holds no separate figure
- **Opponent active disruption:** −5% to −20% depending on disruption technique
- **Optimal conditions (full energy, no disruption, preferred terrain):** +5% on all types except Basic
- **Heart State modifiers:** See Area 1.5 for Heart-based success rate adjustments under pressure

### 2.2 — The Eight Move Types

---

#### Type 1 — Basic

**Definition:** The first spark of intentional power expression. Basic moves are the foundational building blocks of all cultivation combat — simple techniques that establish control over energy flow, physical execution, and the fundamental principles of combat engagement. They are not beginner moves in the sense of being limited; they are essential moves in the sense of being the most reliable expressions of a cultivator's power.

**Base Success Rate:** 100% — A cultivator who has learned a Basic technique executes it reliably every time under non-disrupted conditions. This is the only Type where Novice mastery already achieves 100%.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 100% (already achieved at Novice; mastery deepens output refinement and speed, not reliability)

**Nature of Practice:** "Dependent on practice and usage" means the following for Basic moves: practice does not improve the success rate (already 100%) but significantly improves **execution speed**, **energy efficiency**, **output precision**, and **integration with other techniques**. A Master-level Basic move executes in a fraction of the time of a Novice-level Basic move and depletes far less energy for the same output. A cultivator who has used their Basic moves for decades makes them look effortless — because they are.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Accessible from the first moment of cultivation. A Mortal Tier 1 cultivator can have Basic Named Moves (physical techniques). Basic moves remain valid and useful at every cultivation level — a Divine Sea Realm cultivator's Basic techniques are among the most destructive actions possible in the world, simply because they have Divine-Sea-Realm power behind them.

**Move Output:** Scales entirely with the cultivator's current power density and Heavenly Scale Grade. The technique is basic in complexity; the output is proportional to the cultivator's tier.

**Energy Cost:** Minimal to Low — Basic moves are optimized for efficiency. Even Mortal Tier 2 practitioners can deploy Basic moves repeatedly without meaningful energy drain.

**Narrative Role:** Basic moves are the combat vocabulary. They appear most in sustained exchanges, rapid sequences, and situations where reliability matters more than complexity. A master making a Basic move look devastating is a classic cultivation narrative moment — the gap between the cultivator's power and the technique's simplicity reveals just how advanced they are.

**Examples of Basic Move Names:** **Iron Body Strike**, **Swift Stepping Wind**, **Qi Surge Guard**, **Earth Pressure Stance**, **Single Point Release**

---

#### Type 2 — Intermediate

**Definition:** Refined execution adding efficiency and minor tactical depth to the cultivator's combat expression. Intermediate moves require genuine understanding of energy circulation patterns beyond the basic, allowing the cultivator to introduce secondary effects, improved targeting, or efficiency gains that Basic moves cannot achieve.

**Base Success Rate:** 80% — Under standard combat conditions, an Intermediate technique executes successfully in 4 out of 5 attempts. The 20% failure rate at Novice mastery reflects the additional complexity of managing secondary effects and refined energy flows.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 100% — Full mastery eliminates the failure rate. An Intermediate move at Master level is executed with complete reliability, making it functionally equivalent to a Basic move in terms of success guarantee, while retaining its additional tactical depth.

**Nature of Practice:** Intermediate moves reward consistent practice with incremental reliability gains. A cultivator who regularly deploys their Intermediate techniques in combat and training will advance from Novice to Master over months to years (depending on talent and conditions). "Dependent on practice and usage" means that an Intermediate technique which is never used under pressure remains at Novice or Practiced mastery — theoretical knowledge is not the same as combat-conditioned execution.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Accessible from Mortal Tier 2 Senior and above. Near-Soul-stage Mortal Tier 1 physical specialists operating near their physical peak may develop physical Intermediate techniques (Rule 4.2.2), but this is uncommon.

**Secondary Effects:** Intermediate moves typically introduce one secondary effect beyond the primary action — a binding element in a strike, an efficiency multiplier in a defensive technique, a minor terrain interaction in a mobility move, etc.

**Energy Cost:** Low to Moderate.

**Narrative Role:** Intermediate moves are the workhorses of cultivator combat at the lower and middle tiers. They represent the gap between raw strength and genuine combat intelligence. A cultivator who has mastered Intermediate-level techniques before advancing in realm is a significantly more dangerous fighter than one who has not.

**Examples:** **Double Spiral Release**, **Flowing River Guard**, **Qi-Threaded Footstep**, **Twin-Point Pressure Strike**, **Wind Peel Dodge**

---

#### Type 3 — Advanced

**Definition:** Complex techniques with layered effects that begin shaping the battlefield. Advanced moves operate on multiple levels simultaneously — primary effect, secondary effect, and a tertiary influence on the combat environment or on the opponent's cultivation function. They require deep internalization of qi behavior, affinity expression (if applicable), or soul force management.

**Base Success Rate:** 60% — Advanced moves fail in 40% of attempts at Novice mastery. The complexity of managing layered effects introduces genuine execution risk that cannot be eliminated by power alone.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 90% — Even fully mastered Advanced moves retain a 10% failure possibility under standard conditions. This residual uncertainty reflects the inherent complexity of multi-layered technique management. Under pressure (injury, disruption), Master-level Advanced moves may still fail, making them unsuitable as the sole strategy in crisis situations.

**Nature of Practice:** Advanced moves show the most dramatic improvement from practice — the jump from 60% (Novice) to 90% (Master) is the largest success rate gain in the system. A cultivator who commits to mastering their Advanced techniques transforms their combat profile more noticeably than any other investment. This rewards specialization: a cultivator who focuses on one or two Advanced techniques will dramatically outperform one who collects many without mastering any.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Advanced techniques become accessible at Mortal Tier 2 Champion (Part 3 Area 16.2) and reliably accessible at the Xiantian Realm. Below Champion they are categorically inaccessible. Exceptional Mortal Tier 2 Champion-to-Monarch cultivators with exceptional talent can develop Advanced physical-qi hybrid techniques, but these are uncommon and require extraordinary conditions.

**Battlefield Shaping:** Advanced moves begin to shape the immediate battlefield through their tertiary effects — terrain modification, energy field distortion, cultivation disruption in the immediate area, or persistent environmental interactions that outlast the technique's initial execution.

**Energy Cost:** Moderate to High.

**Narrative Role:** Advanced moves mark the transition from reactive combat to tactical combat. A cultivator who deploys Advanced techniques successfully is thinking several moves ahead. Their failure rate creates genuine narrative tension — will it land?

**Examples:** **Cascading Stone Burial**, **Crimson Tide Dispersal**, **Three-Breath Wind Lock**, **Soul Veil Pressure**, **Fractured Ground Ascent**

---

#### Type 4 — Elite

**Definition:** Highly optimized moves that combine precision, power, and strategic dominance. Elite techniques represent the upper tier of what cultivators in the intermediate cultivation ranges can aspire to — they are the signature capabilities of powerful cultivators, the techniques that define combat reputations and terrify opponents.

**Base Success Rate:** 50% — An Elite technique at Novice mastery succeeds half the time. This is the critical threshold — below 50% and a technique becomes too risky to deploy as a primary strategy. At 50%, it remains viable as a high-risk, high-reward option.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 80% — A Master-level Elite technique succeeds in 4 of 5 attempts under standard conditions. Under pressure, with Heart State modifiers, a Blazing Heart master keeps the full 80% Elite reliability — representing the genuine upper echelon of non-Signature, non-Fusion offensive capability.

**Nature of Practice:** Elite moves require the most deliberate practice methodology of the standard types. Simply deploying them in combat is insufficient — understanding their underlying principle deeply enough to correct execution errors is essential. Many cultivators reach Proficient (65%) and stagnate there, unable to achieve the final insight that pushes to Expert and Master levels. The breakthrough typically requires a conceptual understanding of why the technique works, not just how.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Elite techniques are the natural ceiling of accomplished Xiantian cultivators. Core Realm cultivators develop Elite moves readily. Below Mortal Tier 2 Monarch they are categorically inaccessible (Part 3 Area 16.2); Monarch practitioners very rarely achieve genuine Elite-level techniques — when they do, it becomes a defining narrative fact about that character.

**Precision Over Power:** Elite moves are not necessarily the most destructive moves a cultivator uses. What defines them is precision and optimization — the exact right technique at the exact right moment, wasting nothing and achieving the maximum effect with the minimum input. An Elite-level deflection technique might be more decisive than a Catastrophic-level attack in the right combat moment.

**Energy Cost:** High — Elite techniques are among the most energy-intensive standard-type moves. Their efficiency advantage comes from precision, not energy minimization.

**Narrative Role:** Elite moves are the moves that define combat ability. When a cultivator pulls off an Elite move successfully, it is a statement. When they attempt one and fail (50% base), it is a turning point.

**Examples:** **Heaven's Edge Penetration**, **Mirror Void Reversal**, **Ten-Direction Seal**, **Core Pulse Scatter**, **Sovereign Pressure Descent**

---

#### Type 5 — Signature

**Definition:** Unique, identity-bound techniques that define a cultivator's personal legend. A Signature move is not merely a powerful technique — it is an expression of who the cultivator fundamentally is. It embodies their cultivation philosophy, their personal history, their most profound understanding of their own power. No two cultivators can have the same Signature move, because no two cultivators have the same identity.

**Base Success Rate:** 40% — Signature moves succeed less than half the time at Novice mastery level. This is not because they are inherently unstable (like Fusion) but because they draw on something personal and deep that cannot be accessed on command. A cultivator who has not yet fully inhabited their Signature move's principle will fail to connect with it under combat pressure.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 70% — Even a fully mastered Signature move succeeds only 70% of the time under standard conditions. The residual 30% represents the inherent unpredictability of identity-expression — the Signature move depends not just on technique but on the cultivator's present state, their emotional truth in the moment, their contact with their own deepest self. In moments where that contact is compromised, the Signature move will not fully manifest.

**Identity Binding:** A personal Signature move belongs to one cultivator (a Signature-type move in an institutional manual is a School Move, Rule 9.1.1). It cannot be learned by another through observation alone or by reading a transmission manual — because the technique's core is not the mechanics but the identity that animates them. Another cultivator might learn the physical form of the technique, but without the originator's specific cultivation history and inner truth, the technique becomes an Advanced-type imitation that lacks the Signature's depth and power. See Area 9 for full Signature Move Identity Law.

**Nature of Practice:** Signature moves develop differently from all other move types. They are not practiced to mechanical perfection — they are deepened through lived experience. Every major combat, every crisis of character, every significant moment in the cultivator's personal journey has the potential to deepen the Signature move's resonance. A cultivator in narrative stagnation will find their Signature move stagnating. A cultivator going through genuine growth will find their Signature move growing with them.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Signature moves can theoretically exist at any cultivation level, but they practically emerge most commonly at Xiantian and above, when the cultivator has enough accumulated cultivation experience to have developed a genuine identity. Mortal Tier 2 cultivators with extraordinary depth of personal experience can develop Signature moves, and when they do, this is a significant character-defining event.

**Energy Cost:** Variable — the Signature move's energy cost is determined by its combat function and type expression, not by a fixed scale. Some Signature moves are surprisingly efficient because the cultivator's deep understanding minimizes waste. Others are enormously costly because they express the full weight of who the cultivator is.

**Narrative Role:** The deployment of a Signature move is always a narrative event. It reveals character, expresses identity, and carries emotional weight beyond its combat function. A Signature move that succeeds is a moment of authentic expression. A Signature move that fails is a crisis of self.

**Examples:** Signature moves are named with deep personal significance. No two are alike. A cultivator who fights with a relentless will might name their Signature move **Unyielding Mountain's Roar**. A cultivator who has survived impossible odds might develop **Still Water After the Storm**. The name encodes the cultivator's story.

---

#### Type 6 — Fusion

**Definition:** Hybrid abilities formed by merging different power streams, affinities, cultivation dimensions, or technique principles into a single move that draws on unpredictable synergy. Fusion moves are not simply "two techniques used at once" — they are the genuine integration of two distinct power streams into a new expression that neither stream could produce alone.

**Base Success Rate:** See Part 7 Rule 11.2.1 (`cultivation-fusion-law`). Fusion moves are among the least reliable at Novice mastery. The low success rate reflects the inherent instability of merging distinct power streams, which tend to conflict, cancel, or produce unintended interactions when not perfectly integrated.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** Fusion moves have a hard ceiling well below every other type. This ceiling exists because the elements being fused are genuinely different from each other — no amount of practice eliminates the fundamental tension between distinct power streams; exceptional affinity for both elements being fused can raise it slightly, but the instability never disappears. The Fusion Move success rates (base, maximum, the exceptional-affinity case and the architectural-fusion Stage rates) are stated only in Part 7 Rule 11.2.1 (cultivation-fusion-law).

**Failure Modes for Fusion:** When a Fusion move fails, the failure is not simply "the technique doesn't work." Fusion failure produces one of four outcomes (determined by the specific elements being fused and the circumstances):
1. **Fizzle:** The integration attempt fails cleanly; the energy disperses harmlessly. Best-case failure.
2. **Partial Expression:** One of the two streams dominates; the technique produces a weaker Advanced-type effect from only one stream.
3. **Distortion:** The streams conflict before integration; the move produces an unintended effect that may be useful, harmful to the user, or neutral.
4. **Backlash:** The streams collide destructively within the cultivator's meridians; energy damage to the cultivator proportional to the move's intended power level. Most dangerous failure mode.

**What Can Be Fused:**
- Two distinct affinity streams (the most common form — Fire + Earth, Space + Time, Life + Death, etc.)
- Physical qi and soul force (creating a simultaneous physical-metaphysical attack)
- Cultivation technique and technology-derived energy (Part 7 governs this at the cultivation-path level; a specific Fusion move is the technique expression of it)
- Two entirely different technique principles (one cultivator's two Elite-level techniques merged into a single deployment)
- Alliance Fusion: two cultivators simultaneously deploying coordinated techniques whose interaction creates a shared Fusion effect

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Fusion techniques require sufficient cultivation development to simultaneously manage two distinct power streams. In practice, this begins at the Xiantian Realm for affinity fusions. Physical + soul fusions require active soul cultivation (Part 4). Technology fusions require access to the technology systems governed by Parts 7–10.

**Energy Cost:** Very High — Fusion moves are among the most energy-intensive techniques in existence. Managing two streams simultaneously costs significantly more than the sum of either stream alone.

**Narrative Role:** A successful Fusion move is a breakthrough moment — the cultivator achieving something genuinely new and unexpected. A failed Fusion move, particularly a Backlash failure, creates the kind of mid-combat crisis that defines combat narratives.

**Examples:** **Thunder-Earth Fracture Pulse** (Thunder + Earth affinity fusion), **Soul-Flame Execution** (physical fire technique + soul strike simultaneous), **Void-Time Obliteration Step** (Space + Time affinity fusion at Harmony level)

---

#### Type 7 — Catastrophic

**Definition:** Devastating techniques capable of overwhelming entire battlefields. Catastrophic moves are defined by their destructive scale — not by their complexity (which may be moderate) but by the sheer volume and intensity of energy deployment that makes them possible. A Catastrophic move is not a refined technique; it is a controlled release of energy at a scale that alters terrain, overwhelms multiple opponents simultaneously, and leaves lasting marks on the environment.

**Base Success Rate:** 20% — Catastrophic moves are as unreliable at Novice mastery as Fusion moves (whose rates are in Part 7 Rule 11.2.1), but for different reasons. Fusion fails due to instability between power streams. Catastrophic moves fail because the energy volume required for their execution often exceeds reliable control capacity. A cultivator attempting a Catastrophic technique is pushing their energy deployment to or beyond their comfortable limit. The failure mode is different from Fusion:
- **Underpowered Expression:** The technique deploys but at reduced scale — Advanced-type output instead of Catastrophic
- **Premature Release:** The energy releases before the technique is fully formed — creates an explosive burst centered on the cultivator rather than at the intended point
- **Collapse:** The technique simply fails to form; massive energy expenditure with no output (devastating resource cost for no effect)

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 40% — Master-level Catastrophic technique execution achieves 40% success. The ceiling sits below the Fusion Move ceiling (Part 7 Rule 11.2.1) because Catastrophic techniques depend on raw energy volume that genuinely approaches the cultivator's sustainable limits. A cultivator who has mastered a Catastrophic technique still faces the physics of deploying more energy than they can fully control with certainty.

**Scale Requirement:** A Catastrophic move must qualify at the battlefield scale appropriate to its user's cultivation level. At Xiantian, it might level a district. At Core, it might devastate a city-scale area. At Divine, it could affect continental geography. The designation "Catastrophic" is relative to the tier — it means this move is at the destructive ceiling of its tier.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Catastrophic techniques are first possible at Xiantian Demigod through Void God and standard from True God (Part 3 Area 16.2). Mortal Tier 2 cultivators cannot execute Catastrophic moves (Part 3 Rule 16.2.1); a move that is severe for its tier (building-scale, hundred-person scale) is classified at the type its realm allows. Core Realm is the natural tier for Catastrophic move development. At Divine, Catastrophic becomes the standard upper-tier combat tool.

**Energy Cost:** Extreme — Catastrophic techniques consume a significant portion of the cultivator's energy reserves in a single deployment. Multiple Catastrophic deployments in a single combat are uncommon at lower realms. Core Realm and above can sustain more, but even Core Solidification cultivators typically deploy Catastrophic techniques as finishing moves rather than opening gambits.

**Narrative Role:** Catastrophic moves signal that a fight has reached its extreme. Their deployment is a tactical decision — the risk of failure is real, the energy cost is high, but the potential to decisively end a combat justifies the risk. A successful Catastrophic technique usually ends the immediate combat situation.

**Examples:** **Heaven-Sealing Collapse**, **Thousand Sun Detonation**, **Earth-Splitting Divine Fist**, **All-Consuming Void Expansion**, **Core Obliteration Pulse**

---

#### Type 8 — Mythic

**Definition:** Reality-bending moves that transcend logic and echo across legends. A Mythic technique is not merely powerful — it is an intervention in the structure of reality itself. It operates by principles that lie outside standard cultivation mechanics, drawing on forces or interactions that should not be achievable at any cultivation level. Mythic moves are unique events: they create effects that persist beyond the combat, alter the fabric of the location where they are used, and leave traces in cultivation history.

**Base Success Rate:** 10% — A Mythic technique succeeds 1 in 10 attempts at Novice mastery. This is not a limitation of power — even the most powerful cultivators in the world attempt Mythic techniques at 10% base. The uncertainty is metaphysical: Mythic moves attempt something the universe has a natural resistance to. Reality does not easily permit the kinds of interactions these techniques demand.

**Maximum Mastery Rate:** 20% — Even the greatest masters of a Mythic technique succeed only 20% of the time. There is no version of mastery that makes Mythic techniques reliable. They remain inherently uncertain at 1 in 5 attempts even when perfectly understood. This is by design — Mythic moves are meant to be events, not tactics. A Mythic technique that could be deployed reliably would break the narrative structure of what these techniques represent.

**Failure Modes for Mythic:** Mythic failure is never trivial:
1. **Reality Rejection:** The universe simply refuses the interaction. The energy dissolves, the technique does not form, and the cultivator is left depleted.
2. **Partial Manifestation:** The technique partially realizes — creating a profound but incomplete effect that may still alter the battlefield significantly.
3. **Uncontrolled Manifestation:** The technique activates but outside the cultivator's control, producing effects the cultivator did not intend and cannot direct.
4. **Backlash of Reality:** The universe's resistance to the technique's demands rebounds into the cultivator, producing damage to their cultivation structure, essence, or lifespan (the cultivation attempt drew on essence rather than cultivated energy).

**What Makes a Move Mythic:**
A technique qualifies as Mythic when it does one or more of the following: reverses a fundamental physical law within its scope, directly interacts with space-time at a level that creates lasting alterations, achieves an effect that cannot be explained by standard cultivation energy mechanics, creates changes to the targeted location that persist permanently and become narrative facts of the world, or operates by drawing on the cultivator's essence or lifespan rather than cultivated energy alone.

**Cultivation Level Requirement:** Mythic techniques become conceptually accessible at Xiantian True God (a first attempt is possible only at the extreme peak of Absolute God, Access Table in Area 4.1), first practically accessible at Core Formation through Core Assimilation, and fully accessible at Core Solidification (Part 3 Area 16.2). Below True God they are categorically inaccessible, though theoretical comprehension may exist — a cultivator might understand the principle of a Mythic technique without being able to deploy it. At Divine, Mythic is the only classification that applies meaningfully. Even there, only cultivators who have achieved deep enlightenment of the technique's principle approach the 20% maximum mastery rate.

**Mythic Move and the World:**
A successful Mythic technique execution is a world event. It is registered in cultivation records. The location where it occurred is altered. Other cultivators can sense the residue of its reality-interaction for years or millennia afterward. A Part 27 consequence event is mandatory for any successful Mythic technique deployment.

**Energy Cost:** Reality-bending cost — Mythic techniques may deplete not just cultivated energy but draw on the cultivator's fundamental life force, cultivation root, or lifespan. This is why they cannot be casually repeated even by cultivators with enormous energy reserves — the cost is not just energetic but existential.

**Narrative Role:** A Mythic technique is a series-level event. Its deployment should feel like the story has crossed a threshold. Success or failure, the world is not the same after a Mythic technique is attempted.

**Examples:** **World-Shattering Truth**, **Causality's End**, **The Last Heaven**, **Eternal Still Point**, **Unmaking of the Forgotten**

## Area 3 — Move Combat Function Classification

### 3.1 — Overview

Move Type (Area 2) classifies how complex and difficult a technique is. Move Combat Function classifies what the technique does in a fight. Every Named Move has both a Type and a Function. The Function is the operational description of the move's combat role; the Type is the complexity grade of its execution. A simple punch can be a Basic-type Strike-function move. An elaborate technique that also happens to be a punch can be an Elite-type Strike-function move. Both are Strike-function; their Type differs.

Combat Functions are organized into eight primary categories and multiple sub-functions within each. A move may have a primary function and secondary functions — a strike that also creates a bind effect has a primary Attack function (Strike sub-function) and a secondary Control function (Bind sub-function).

---

### 3.2 — Attack Functions

Attack-function moves are designed to deliver force, damage, or harmful energy to a target. They are the offensive backbone of cultivation combat.

**3.2.1 — Strike (Physical Contact Attack):**
Direct delivery of force through physical contact or near-contact range. The simplest and most common attack function. **Iron Bone Shattering Stamp**, **Thunderclap Palm**, **Dragon Neck Grip Crush** are Strike-function moves. Strikes must close the distance to be effective; their advantage is reliability and force concentration.

**3.2.2 — Projectile (Ranged Energy Attack):**
Force delivered at range through emitted energy, condensed qi, or affinity expression that travels as a discrete projectile. **Crimson Fire Spear**, **Ten-Thousand Needle Rain**, **Heaven-Piercing Lightning Bolt** are Projectile-function moves. Projectiles can be dodged; their advantage is range and the ability to engage without physical proximity.

**3.2.3 — Area Attack (Zone Force):**
Force delivered simultaneously to multiple targets or across a defined zone. **Thousand Storm Scatter**, **Earth Upheaval**, **Heaven-Wide Thunder Sea** are Area-function moves. Area attacks are difficult to evade and can overwhelm multiple opponents; their disadvantage is energy cost and reduced penetrating force per target.

**3.2.4 — Penetrating Attack (Defense-Bypassing Force):**
Force specifically designed to bypass defensive barriers, shields, or qi reinforcement. Penetrating attacks interact with the target's defensive layer differently from standard strikes — they are designed to find gaps, exceed shield thresholds, or exploit physical-metaphysical boundary transitions. **Heaven's Edge Penetration**, **Soul-Piercing Lance**, **Void-Threaded Arrow** are Penetrating Attack-function moves.

**3.2.5 — True Damage Attack (Layer-Bypassing Force):**
The most dangerous attack sub-function. True Damage attacks bypass the physical layer entirely and target internal structures — the cultivation root, the dantian, the Core, the soul, or the lifespan itself. A True Damage attack that succeeds may deal no visible physical damage while producing catastrophic internal consequences. These attacks are typically soul-based (from Part 4's soul attack types) or specific Divine-level techniques with the ability to reach through physical armor. Governed jointly by Part 4 (soul damage rules) and this Part (classification and naming).

**3.2.6 — Suppression Attack (Continuous Force Application):**
A sustained attack that does not rely on a single decisive blow but on sustained pressure that wears down, distorts, and eventually overwhelms the target's defenses. **Relentless Tide Pressure**, **Eternal Weight Descent** are Suppression Attack-function moves. Suppression attacks are typically channeled techniques with ongoing energy cost.

---

### 3.3 — Defense Functions

Defense-function moves protect the cultivator from incoming force, damage, or harmful effects.

**3.3.1 — Shield (Interposition Barrier):**
Creates a physical, energetic, or metaphysical barrier between the cultivator and incoming force. The Shield function is the most straightforward defense — it interposes something between the attack and its target. **Iron Mountain Guard**, **Heaven's Dome Ward**, **Soul Crystal Barrier** are Shield-function moves. Shields can be overwhelmed by sufficient force; their strength is their reliability and coverage.

**3.3.2 — Deflect (Redirection Defense):**
Redirects incoming force along a new trajectory rather than blocking it. Deflect techniques require less energy than equivalent Shield techniques because they redirect rather than absorb. However, they require precise timing and directional accuracy. **River Pebble Deflect**, **Wind-Following Palm Redirect**, **Space Bend Parry** are Deflect-function moves.

**3.3.3 — Absorb (Force Conversion Defense):**
Takes incoming force and converts it — into cultivation energy, into the cultivator's own power reserves, or simply into neutralized dispersal. Absorb techniques are among the most advanced defensive functions because they require the cultivator to interact constructively with hostile energy. **Void Root Consumption** and similar techniques are Absorb-function moves. Specific to Void Root cultivators at its most extreme; other cultivators can develop limited Absorb techniques.

**3.3.4 — Reflect (Return Defense):**
Returns incoming force to its source. Reflect techniques are among the most difficult defensive functions — they require the cultivator to capture and reverse the direction of hostile energy, which demands perfect timing, precise energy management, and the right technique architecture. **Heaven's Mirror Return**, **Anti-Current Reversal** are Reflect-function moves. A successful Reflect against a Catastrophic attack is one of the most dramatic single-move outcomes in cultivation combat.

**3.3.5 — Nullify (Cancellation Defense):**
Specifically targets and cancels out the incoming technique at an energetic or structural level — not by blocking its force but by disrupting the energy pattern that makes the technique function. Nullify techniques are particularly effective against complex technique types (Advanced and above) because complex techniques have more patterns to disrupt. They are less effective against raw force applications. Soul Nullify techniques (Part 4) specifically disrupt soul technique patterns.

---

### 3.4 — Mobility Functions

Mobility-function moves govern movement in combat — not merely moving quickly, but the strategic dimension of occupying, leaving, or traversing specific spatial positions.

**3.4.1 — Step (Enhanced Movement):**
Named cultivation-enhanced movement techniques. A Step move is a structured movement pattern that maximizes speed, efficiency, or misdirection using cultivation energy. **Cloud-Parting Eight Steps**, **Iron Bone Charge Rush**, **Void-Following Sprint** are Step-function moves. Step techniques are among the most important combat tools because controlling distance controls combat.

**3.4.2 — Flash (Instantaneous Relocation):**
Near-instantaneous or fully instantaneous relocation within a defined range. Flash techniques require Space affinity or specific dimensional techniques at the highest levels; at lower levels, they are speed-enhanced movements so fast they appear instantaneous. **Shadow Blink**, **Instant Storm Cross**, **Heaven Treading Step** (for the appropriate realm) are Flash-function moves. Flash techniques at Xiantian and above begin involving genuine micro-spatial distortion.

**3.4.3 — Phase (Evasion through Layer Transition):**
Temporarily removes the cultivator from standard physical-layer targeting — through dimensional micro-transit, soul layer translucency, or spatial folding. Phase techniques do not move the cultivator to a new location in the physical world; they remove them from that layer briefly. Only one attack can reach them during a Phase: a True Damage attack targeting the layer they have transitioned to. Phase techniques are among the rarest mobility moves, accessible from late Xiantian onwards.

---

### 3.5 — Control Functions

Control-function moves restrict, impair, or dominate the opponent's ability to act, move, or use their cultivation.

**3.5.1 — Bind (Physical/Energetic Restraint):**
Restricts the target's movement through physical force, qi projection, affinity expression, or soul-based restriction. **Ice Coffin Seal**, **Ten-Direction Energy Lock**, **Stone Embrace Formation** are Bind-function moves. Binds can be broken by sufficient force but require the opponent to spend energy and attention doing so.

**3.5.2 — Seal (Cultivation Suppression):**
Prevents or impairs the target's use of their cultivation — qi circulation is disrupted, technique channels are blocked, or the dantian/Core function is temporarily impaired. Seals are extremely powerful if effective because they remove the opponent's cultivation advantage. **Meridian Suppression Strike**, **Core-Lock Formation**, **Divine Seal Brand** are Seal-function moves. Seals are difficult to maintain against cultivators significantly stronger than the Seal user.

**3.5.3 — Suppress (Power Reduction):**
Reduces the target's effective power output without eliminating it. Suppression techniques create an ongoing effect that lowers the opponent's Heavenly Scale effective grade for as long as the suppression is maintained. **Pressure Domain**, **Heaven Weight Descent**, **Soul Suppression Field** are Suppress-function moves. Unlike Seals, Suppressions do not block cultivation — they reduce its potency.

**3.5.4 — Disrupt (Technique Interruption):**
Breaks or degrades an opponent's currently executing technique, interfering with their mid-execution energy management. Disrupt techniques are specifically designed to interrupt channeled techniques, break formation maintenance, or destabilize a Fusion attempt mid-execution. **Ripple Shatter**, **Qi-Scatter Burst**, **Pattern Break Strike** are Disrupt-function moves. A Disrupt technique landing during a Fusion attempt forces the Fusion failure table and can trigger Backlash.

**3.5.5 — Confuse (Perception Distortion):**
Disrupts the opponent's perception, spatial awareness, technique targeting, or assessment accuracy. Confuse-function moves create false sensory data, obscure the cultivator's position, or distort the battle environment in ways that degrade the opponent's ability to effectively deploy their own techniques. **Mirror Scatter Field**, **Soul Mist Veil**, **Phantom Step Array**, **Illusion Formation** are Confuse-function moves. Soul Phantom techniques (Part 4) are among the most powerful Confuse-function moves.

---

### 3.6 — Trap and Environmental Functions

Trap and Environmental-function moves create persistent effects in the combat space that activate on conditions or reshape the battlefield.

**3.6.1 — Trap (Conditional Activation Technique):**
A Named Move that is placed in the environment and activates when specific conditions are met — proximity, specific energy signature, time, or movement pattern. **Heaven Breath Mine**, **Void Snare**, **Soul Pressure Tripwire** are Trap-function moves. Traps require the opponent to interact with the environment naively; they are most effective against opponents unaware of their presence.

**3.6.2 — Formation (Persistent Zone Effect):**
Creates an ongoing environmental effect across a defined zone. Formations require upfront energy investment and sustained maintenance but provide persistent battlefield advantages — energy zones, suppression fields, amplification areas, or spatial distortions. **Iron Mountain Sealing Formation**, **Heaven's Gate Convergence Array**, **Soul Domain Extension** are Formation-function moves. Formations interact with Part 5's Battlefield Spatial Constraints — large-scale Formations effectively alter the spatial parameters of the combat.

**3.6.3 — Contaminate (Hostile Energy Introduction):**
Introduces hostile, corrupting, or foreign energy into the target's meridians, Core, or cultivation structure. Contaminate-function moves create ongoing internal consequences rather than immediate external damage. **Qi Poison Needle**, **Core Infection Seal**, **Cultivation Corruption Brand** are Contaminate-function moves. Governed jointly by Part 3 (the Cultivation Poison damage type, Area 8.3) and this Part (classification and naming).

---

### 3.7 — Support Functions

Support-function moves enhance allies, restore damage, or expand perception rather than directly affecting opponents.

**3.7.1 — Heal (Restoration):**
Restores vitality, repairs damage, accelerates recovery, or stabilizes cultivation-system function. Healing moves are governed primarily by Healing Affinity cultivators (Part 4, Area 1.3 — Healing domain) but other affinity types can have healing sub-functions. **Life-Returning Palm**, **Soul Restoration Touch**, **Essence Mending Technique** are Heal-function moves.

**3.7.2 — Empower (Enhancement):**
Amplifies an ally's power output, speed, technique efficiency, or defensive capacity. Empower-function moves are most commonly found in Formation techniques (which can include empowerment zones) and in specific support cultivation paths. **Heaven's Grace Infusion**, **Twin Core Resonance Link**, **War God Blessing** are Empower-function moves.

**3.7.3 — Sense (Expanded Perception):**
Expands the cultivator's awareness beyond normal cultivation range — detecting hidden opponents, identifying concealed techniques, mapping spatial formations, or perceiving soul force activity. Sense-function moves interact directly with Seal and Confuse techniques; Sense moves help counter them. **Heaven's Eye Technique**, **Soul Scan**, **Void Space Reading** are Sense-function moves.

---

### 3.8 — Soul-Specific Functions

Soul-Specific function moves deploy soul force as defined in Part 4, Area 2. Every soul technique type from Part 4 maps to one of the combat function categories above, but Soul-Specific function moves have an additional designation because they operate on the metaphysical layer. When rendering soul-function moves in prose, both the physical-layer action (if any) and the metaphysical-layer action are rendered separately, per Part 2's combat prose requirements and Part 4's narration rules (Area 2.8).

Soul-Specific function moves inherit all the attack, defense, control, and support sub-functions described above, with the "Soul-" prefix designating metaphysical layer deployment. A **Soul Strike** is a Penetrating Attack-function Soul-Specific move. A **Soul Domain** is a Formation-function Soul-Specific move.

---

### 3.9 — Combination Functions

Combination-function moves deploy two distinct technique elements in a structured sequence or simultaneous convergence.

**3.9.1 — Sequence (Predetermined Chain):**
Two or more techniques performed in predetermined order as a single composite named action. **Heaven Dragon Three Steps** might involve a Flash (Step 1), a Penetrating Attack (Step 2), and a Suppress (Step 3) as a single Sequence-function Named Move. Sequence moves are classified by their highest-complexity component for success rate purposes.

**3.9.2 — Convergence (Simultaneous Dual-Layer):**
A single named action that simultaneously deploys on two layers — physical and soul, or two different affinity streams. Convergence moves are a subset of Fusion moves (they meet the Fusion Type criteria because they merge two streams). The distinction from standard Fusion is that Convergence moves have a deliberate dual-layer architecture rather than the unpredictable synergy of elemental fusion.

### 3.10 — Area-of-Effect Radius

An area-of-effect move is one whose force, barrier, zone or trap covers a space around a point and not one target: Area Attack (3.2.3), zone Control (3.5), Trap and Environmental functions (3.6), and area Defense (3.3). This Area sets the largest radius such a move may have.

**Rule 3.10.1 — Part 14 Owns the Ceiling:** The largest area-effect radius of a Named Move is the Maximum Area-Effect Radius that Part 14 Area 4.3 gives for the user's realm. Part 14 Area 4.3 is the largest reach of one deliberate technique; the footprints of Part 5 Area 9.2 are the usual spread of a whole fight. A Named Move does not go past its row without Part 28 authorization, and its radius does not exceed the soul sense active range of the same stage (Part 14 Area 4.1); where the two Part 14 figures differ, the lower upper bound applies. The figures are stated once, in Part 14 Area 4.3 (Mortal Tier 1 has no area-effect radius), and are not repeated here.

**Rule 3.10.2 — What the Move Type Decides:** The ceiling is a limit and not a default. How much of it a move uses follows its Move Type (Area 2.2), its Deployment Form (Area 5.2) and, for a Signature move, the depth level it has reached (Area 9.2):

| Move Type | Radius a move of this type uses |
|---|---|
| Basic, Intermediate | a small zone around the user or the target, far inside the ceiling |
| Advanced, Elite | a large share of the ceiling |
| Signature | set by its registered depth level; no more than its Move Type allows |
| Fusion | up to the ceiling of the user's realm |
| Catastrophic | the ceiling, usually by Cast deployment (Area 5.2) |
| Mythic | the ceiling; past it only with Part 28 authorization |

### 4.2 — Access Principle Rules

**Rule 4.2.1 — Realm Minimum, Not Realm Guarantee:**
Reaching a cultivation realm does not automatically grant access to that realm's move type ceiling. A Xiantian Demigod who just entered the realm has Standard access to Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced — but Elite, Signature, and above are still individual achievement targets that require talent, practice, and cultivation experience beyond realm position.

**Rule 4.2.2 — Mortal Tier 1 Physical Exception:**
Mortal Tier 1 cultivators near the Soul stage who are physical martial arts specialists may develop Intermediate physical-architecture techniques. This is the only Type 2 access at Mortal Tier 1, and it requires that the cultivator has achieved near-peak physical refinement. Part 3 Area 16.2 grants the same exception.

**Rule 4.2.3 — Fusion Physical Prerequisite:**
Fusion moves require two distinct controllable power streams. An awakened soul exists at Mortal Tier 2 only through early awakening at Monarch Level 9 Peak (Part 4, Area 2.5); before that point no Mortal Tier 2 cultivator has two controllable streams. A Monarch Level 9 Peak cultivator with both qi circulation AND an early-awakened soul may develop physical-soul Fusion techniques. This is extremely rare and narratively significant.

**Rule 4.2.4 — Mythic Reality Threshold:**
The Mythic type's "reality-bending" nature requires a cultivation level where the cultivator's power genuinely interacts with the fabric of reality. Part 3 Area 16.2 makes Mythic conceptually accessible from Xiantian True God (a first attempt is possible only at the extreme peak of Absolute God, Access Table above), first practically accessible at Core Formation and fully accessible at Core Solidification; the barrier is thinnest at the transition from Core to Divine. A Core Solidification peak cultivator approaching the Divine Transformation threshold may develop and attempt Mythic techniques, but even at their most favorable, the metaphysical barriers remain formidable.

**Rule 4.2.5 — Type Downward Compatibility:**
A Divine Realm cultivator can and should still use Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced moves. Move types are not replaced by higher types as cultivation advances — they are retained and remain valuable as reliable, efficient tools. A Divine cultivator who relies solely on Mythic and Catastrophic moves is tactically limited. The depth of a cultivator's move repertoire across all types is a measure of their combat sophistication.

### 4.3 — Forbidden and Taboo Technique Types

A forbidden or taboo technique is a Named Move that a jurisdiction or a culture treats as prohibited. Forbidden is a standing a move holds in a place. It is not a Move Type: the move keeps its Move Type, Combat Function and mastery grade (Areas 2, 3 and 9) and the access it needs in Area 4.1.

**Rule 4.3.1 — Three Kinds of Standing:**
- **Universal taboo.** A technique that canon already treats as transgressive across the saga. Two are named: Devour Soul used on a person (Part 4 Area 2.3: "among the most transgressive acts a cultivator can commit"), and the unwilling deep reading or taking of a mind, at Deep or Total depth or by memory-theft (Part 24 Areas 8.3 and 7.2, Rule P24-TT-1; Part 24 Area 8.1: a violation of personal autonomy recognized across all moral spectrum positions).
- **Jurisdictional prohibition.** A technique that a faction's or civilization's own law forbids (Part 22), such as a Lineage Technique used by someone outside the lineage (Area 7.2), or a technique the jurisdiction bans for its effect.
- **Cultural taboo.** A technique that a culture's taboo architecture forbids without a written law (Part 25), such as one whose effect desecrates Sacred Ground.

**Rule 4.3.2 — Capability Is Unchanged:** Forbidden standing never changes what a cultivator can do. A forbidden move needs the same realm, the same Move Type access (Area 4.1) and the same learning path (Area 7) as any other. The taboo concerns permission, not power.

**Rule 4.3.3 — What Use Does:** Using a forbidden technique is a Part 27 event (Part 27 Area 8.2, Forbidden Technique Use). It classifies the user in factional and political terms, it leaves a permanent record where it was witnessed, and it can bring enforcement. The CSS grade follows the effect and the target (Part 27 Area 1.2); the forbidden standing adds the Area 8.2 consequences on top.

**Rule 4.3.4 — Villains and Heroes Alike:** A villain's use of a forbidden technique carries its full Part 27 weight whatever the villain's narrative role (Part 18 Rule 18.1.4.1), and a hero's use does the same (Part 27 Area 9.1). The move is recorded with the character's other Named Moves, with the jurisdictions that forbid it noted beside it, and the choice to use it is explained by the character's ideology (Part 20) like any other act.

**Rule 4.3.5 — Which Techniques a Place Forbids:** Beyond the two universal taboos in Rule 4.3.1, the techniques a place forbids are set by that place's own ledger or by the first Area that shows it. No other technique is forbidden everywhere.

## Area 5 — Move Energy Cost and Sustainability Law

### 5.1 — The Energy Cost Principle

Every Named Move that involves cultivation energy depletes the cultivator's energy reserves. The cost is governed by: the move's Type (complexity), the move's Function (what it does), the cultivation realm of the user (which determines the total reserve and the cost-to-power ratio), and whether the technique is instant, channeled, or cast.

**Mortal Tier 1 Exception:** Physical techniques at Mortal Tier 1 have no energy cost — they are metabolic actions. However, they have a **stamina cost** governed by physical exertion. Sustained physical technique deployment depletes stamina rather than qi.

### 5.2 — Deployment Forms

Every Named Move is deployed through one of three forms:

**Instant Deployment:** The technique is formed and released in a single action. No sustained energy management is required after activation. The cost is a one-time depletion at the moment of use. Most attack moves are instant. Most Basic and Intermediate moves are instant.

**Channeled Deployment:** The technique requires ongoing energy investment to maintain its effect. The cultivator must continue directing energy into the move for as long as it is active — Formations, sustained Seals, ongoing Suppressions, and extended Healing are typically channeled. The cost-per-second of a channeled technique is lower than the equivalent instant technique's total cost, but the cumulative cost over time can be enormous. A cultivator who is channeling a technique is also managing their reserves in real time.

**Cast Deployment:** The technique requires a preparation phase during which energy is accumulated and structured, followed by a release. The preparation phase is vulnerable — a Disrupt move landing during casting forces the failure table for that technique's type. After release, the technique operates independently of the cultivator (like a Projectile) or creates an instant effect. Most Catastrophic and Mythic techniques have a Cast deployment form due to the enormous energy structuring required.

### 5.3 — Energy Cost by Move Type

| Move Type | Instant Cost | Channeled Cost/Cycle | Cast Accumulation | Reserve Impact |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|------------------|----------------|
| Basic | Minimal | Very Low | — | Negligible |
| Intermediate | Low | Low | — | Minor |
| Advanced | Moderate | Moderate | Low | Noticeable |
| Elite | High | High | Moderate | Significant |
| Signature | Variable | Variable | Variable | Significant to Extreme |
| Fusion | Very High | Very High | High | Major |
| Catastrophic | Extreme | — | Very High | Depleting |
| Mythic | Reality-level | — | Extreme | Potentially Lifespan |

**"Reserve Impact"** describes the percentage of a cultivator's total reserves a single deployment consumes:
- Negligible: less than 1%
- Minor: 1–5%
- Noticeable: 5–15%
- Significant: 15–30%
- Major: 30–50%
- Depleting: 50–80%
- Potentially Lifespan: may exceed energy reserves and draw on essence/lifespan

### 5.4 — Sustainability Rules

**Rule 5.4.1 — Core Realm Advantage:**
Core Realm cultivators (Part 3, Area 1.4) have an internal reactor rather than a finite qi reservoir. This means their energy cost-to-reserve ratio is fundamentally better than Xiantian cultivators. A Core Formation cultivator can sustain Elite moves at a pace that would exhaust an Absolute God cultivator's reserves.

**Rule 5.4.2 — Fatigue Accumulation:**
Even Core Realm cultivators face fatigue limits. Extended combat at maximum output depletes the Core's output capacity even though the Core is continuously generating energy. Part 3, Area 8.4.3 governs Core fatigue. For Named Move purposes the success-rate penalty at a low reserve is the Depletion States table of Part 3 Area 12.4 (see also Part 3 Area 16.4); this Part holds no separate figure.

**Rule 5.4.3 — Technique Efficiency and Mastery:**
Higher mastery levels reduce energy cost as well as improving success rates. A Master-level Basic technique uses perhaps half the energy of a Novice-level execution of the same technique. Mastery does not reduce Catastrophic or Mythic costs significantly — the energy requirement for those types is fundamental to their nature.

**Rule 5.4.4 — External Resource Enhancement:**
Energy resources (Part 11 — Energy Substance Law) can temporarily expand effective reserve capacity, accelerate recovery between techniques, or directly power specific techniques. A cultivator using a high-grade spirit stone to fuel a Catastrophic technique may achieve the same output at reduced personal reserve cost. Technique-specific resource usage and its governance are defined in Part 11.

**Rule 5.4.5 — Core Capacity Rule:**
The qi invested in one execution is bounded by the user's Realm output ceiling (Part 3, Area 12.2), not by the full reserve. A full reserve does not let a cultivator pour everything into one technique: the output ceiling sets what a single execution can carry, and the reserve sets how many executions can follow. Part 3 Principle P3-16.5.3 (The Instantaneous Output Ceiling) states the same limit from the Part 3 side.

## Area 6 — Move Creation, Naming, and Registration Protocol

### 6.1 — Why Naming Is Mandatory

In the Starward Saga's world, the naming of a cultivation technique is not merely cultural convention — it is the act that transforms raw power application into transmissible knowledge. An unnamed technique is applied force: it works, it may be powerful, but it is singular. It cannot be taught without showing it in person. It cannot be referenced in records. It cannot be developed systematically because it has no anchor for analysis. It is born when the cultivator uses it and dies when the cultivator dies.

A Named Move is codified principle: it encodes the underlying energy-circulation pattern, force-application structure, and philosophical principle of the technique into a transmissible form. The name is the identifier that allows the move to exist as a canonical object in the cultivation world — to be recorded in sect manuals, referenced by those who have seen it, studied by those who have experienced it, and improved upon across generations.

This is why Part 2, Area 3 Rule 3.2.1 mandates ***bold-italic*** formatting: the bold-italic signals to the reader that what is happening in the narrative is not casual force application but the invocation of a specific, canon-registered technique with history, properties, and classification. The bold-italic is visually distinct from both plain narration and italic dialogue — it marks the exact moment a canon-registered Named Move enters the scene.

**Principle of Naming:** A technique is named by its creator (in original creation), by its lineage (in inherited techniques), or by the community of cultivators who have observed and recorded it (in the rare case of observed-but-unclaimed techniques). A cultivator does not need to formally declare a name in a ceremony — the naming is complete when the cultivator has consistently referred to the technique by a specific name in their own cultivation practice and the technique has stabilized into a repeatable form.

### 6.2 — Move Naming Conventions

Named Moves in cultivation society follow several traditional naming patterns. None of these are mandatory — any name that the cultivator consistently uses for a codified technique is valid. However, the following conventions govern most named techniques:

**Pattern 1 — Principle + Action:**
Describes the underlying principle followed by the action it produces. Names the "why" and the "what." Examples: **Iron Bone Shattering Stamp** (Iron Bone principle + Stamp action), **Heaven Piercing Thrust** (Heaven-Piercing principle + Thrust action), **Still Water Mirror Slash** (Still Water principle + Slash action).

**Pattern 2 — Natural Force + Technique Form:**
Invokes a natural force or phenomenon as the primary descriptor, followed by the technique form. Common for affinity-aligned moves. Examples: **Crimson Tide Dispersal** (fire/red force + dispersal form), **Thunder Step Advance** (thunder force + advance form), **Void Space Collapse** (void principle + collapse form).

**Pattern 3 — Creator Reference:**
Names the technique after its creator (personal name, title, or legacy identifier) plus a descriptor. Most common for Signature moves and lineage-defining Elite techniques. Examples: **Elder Fen's Mountain Root Stance**, **Lin Shen's Unbreakable Fist**, **Seven Ancestors' Heaven Sealing**.

**Pattern 4 — Scale or Number + Effect:**
Invokes a scale reference or numerical principle. Common for Area-function moves and Catastrophic types. Examples: **Thousand Sun Detonation**, **Ten-Direction Seal**, **Ten-Thousand Needle Rain**, **Single Point Release**.

**Pattern 5 — State or Condition + Consequence:**
Names the cultivator's state or condition and the consequence it produces. Common for Signature moves with deep personal resonance. Examples: **Unyielding Mountain's Roar**, **Still Water After the Storm**, **Grief That Burns No Flame**.

**Invalid Names:** A move name is invalid if it is: identical to an already-registered move within the same sect/faction canon record without differentiation, non-specific to the point of being meaningless (a name like "Big Strike" has no principle-encoding function and is not treated as a formally named technique), or deliberately deceptive in a way that hides the technique's true nature (false naming to deceive opponents about technique function is a cultural violation per Part 25, though the technique remains valid — the naming violation is social, not mechanical).

**Length and situation (Part 42 Rule 42.12.2.3; Canon Amendment 2026-10-08):** Patterns 1 to 5 and their examples (which run to three, four and five words) are illustrations of how a name can be built. They are not minimum lengths and not models for a new name. A move name is one or two words (three only as Rule 42.12.2.3 allows), is tied to the weapon type and to the level and grade of the move, and never names a place, a setting, a tactic or an enemy. The example names throughout this Part (Areas 2, 3 and 11 and the worked passages) are illustrations in the same way, and a Signature move's "deep personal significance" (Area 2.2, Type 5) is carried in one or two words.

**Checks for a New Move Name:** Every new move name passes steps 1, 2 and 5 of Part 28 Area 20.3 before it is used in prose or registered: the ledger scan (the name does not collide with or closely echo a move name already in the ledgers) (the ledgers include the Manual Ledger), the producing culture's register (the linguistic and aesthetic register of that culture's Part 25 CPT), and distinctiveness (the name is phonetically and visually distinct from the other move names in the same scene and Chapter). It also passes the move-naming law of Part 42 Rule 42.12.2.3 (length, weapon and level, and nothing situational) (Canon Amendment 2026-10-08).

### 6.3 — The Move Creation Process

**Stage 1 — Principle Identification:**
The cultivator identifies the underlying principle they are trying to express — the specific energy pattern, force-application structure, or affinity expression that defines the technique. This is the conceptual foundation. Without a clear principle, the technique cannot be stabilized into a Named Move.

**Stage 2 — Form Development:**
The cultivator physically develops the technique — experimenting with energy circulation paths, body mechanics, affinity expression, and output geometry until the technique can be executed consistently. This stage is the practice phase. The technique exists but is not yet named (and thus not yet classified as a Named Move).

**Stage 3 — Stabilization:**
The technique has been executed enough times with sufficient consistency that its form is stable — it produces the same type of effect each time, even if success rate varies. A technique that produces wildly different effects each time is not stabilized and cannot be named.

**Stage 4 — Naming and Type/Function Assignment:**
The cultivator names the technique. At this moment, the technique becomes a Named Move and is subject to this Part's classification system. The cultivator assigns (consciously or intuitively through cultural convention) the technique's Type and Function. These classifications should reflect what the technique actually is, not what the cultivator wishes it to be.

**Stage 5 — Registration (Optional but Recommended):**
In most cultivation societies, Named Moves of intermediate-level and above are registered with the cultivator's sect, clan, or cultivation institution. Registration creates a canonical record: the move's name, creator, classification, energy substrate, and primary/secondary functions. Registration allows the technique to be transmitted formally, claimed as intellectual property by the sect, and included in lineage records. Unregistered Named Moves are still Valid Named Moves — registration affects their cultural and legal status (Part 23 — Affiliation Law), not their canonical classification under this Part.

### 6.4 — Move Name Stability

Once a technique is named, the name is stable. A cultivator who changes their technique's name loses the accumulated mastery record associated with the old name in cultivation records (though their personal mastery is retained — it is the social/historical record that is disrupted). Renaming a technique is treated as creating a new Named Move from the existing technique's form; the technique's mastery history in institutional records begins again from the new name's registration date.

If a technique evolves in Type (Area 9.3 — Type Upgrade), the evolved technique typically receives a new name or a named addition to its existing name (prefix or suffix indicating elevation). The original technique's record is preserved as the ancestor of the evolved form.

### 6.5 — The Ledger Register of Named Moves

**Rule 6.5.1 — One generic register.**
Every Named Move, Named Technique and Named Cultivation Practice (Part 2 Rule 3.2.1) is recorded in ONE generic register, `ledger-named-moves/_index.md` (Part 37 Area 3.9, Threshold O-5), whoever uses it and even if no one does yet. This ledger register is a different thing from the in-world registration of Stage 5 (Rule 6.3), which a sect, clan or institution makes and which affects a move's cultural and legal standing only. Stage 5 is unchanged.

**Rule 6.5.1a — The Manual Ledger is the register of manual-borne moves (Part 42).**
A Named Move that a manual of the Manual Ledger (`.claude/story-memory/ledger-manuals/`) carries is registered in the Manual Ledger by its ID, in the CSV files of that ledger, and `ledger-named-moves/_index.md` carries a pointer block for it and not a row for each move. A manual-borne move becomes a full row of the generic register when its name is first written in prose (Rule 6.5.5), and its First Area Appearance is then also entered in its CSV row. The Pre-Write Ledger Check and the Rule 6.2 collision scan read the Manual Ledger by ID and by name. The generic register still holds every Named Move that prose has written.

**Rule 6.5.2 — Registered twice, each pointing at the other.**
A move that a person uses is recorded in two places. (a) The generic register holds a row for it. (b) The user's own character-ledger entry holds the move in its existing Named Moves registry: Section 3 of the hero, villain and side-character ledgers (the `Signature Named Moves` subsection, or the `Innate Moves` subsection for an S-3 move). The row names the user in its Users cell and points at the user's entry; the move block in the user's entry ends with a `Register row` line that points at the row. A move used by a group and not by one person (a species-innate move, a lineage or school technique) is pointed at from the species, faction or school entry that holds it. *(A monster Kind or Individual in `ledger-character-monster/` is a user entry, and the Kind entry is the species entry for a move used by the whole Kind; Rule 12.3.7, Part 40 Area 4.)* The user's entry owns every value of the move for that user (Type, Function, mastery grade, substrate and the rest of the Area 6.3 and 12.6 fields); the register holds none of them (Rule 6.5.6). For a manual-borne move the user's entry holds a Manual Kit pointer (the manual IDs, Part 42 Area 11) in place of a move block. The Manual Ledger row owns every value of the move (Rule 6.5.6). The register row's Users cell points at the user's Manual Kit line, or, for a move no single person uses, at the institution's entry.

**Rule 6.5.3 — Kinds.**
The register's kind (its Classification cell) is one of: `Signature Named Move` (a move recorded under a character's Signature Named Moves); `Innate Move` (an S-3 species-innate move, Area 12); `Lineage or school technique` (Area 7.2, held by a lineage, school or sect); `Practice movement (unawakened)`; or `Other`. A manual-borne move is a `Lineage or school technique` (Part 42 Area 12). A personal Signature stays a `Signature Named Move`. A practice movement is a body or weapon movement given a proper name for practice at a school (a stance, a drill, a form, a weapon pattern), taught to people who are unawakened and not yet tied to a stage or a realm. Once named it is a Named Move (Stage 4 of Rule 6.3) like any other. Its Move Type, Combat Function and substrate read `OPEN-AREA` in its home entry until an Area ties them; Area 4.1 makes Basic the standard type at Mortal Tier 1, and this rule gives a practice movement no power and no access it does not already have.

**Rule 6.5.4 — Form: bold-italic is unchanged, and how it sits beside the item rule.**
A Named Move keeps its bold-italic form (Part 2 Rule 3.2.1, Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1d, Area 11.1), inside dialogue and outside it, with no dialogue exception. A practice movement is a Named Move once named and registered and takes the same form. The italic rule for named items (Part 28 Rule 20.8) does not touch Named Moves: a Named Move is not a named item (Rule 20.8.1(b)), so a move is bold-italic and a named item is plain italic (a Named Relic or Artifact is a named item and is plain italic too). The roman break that Rule 20.8.2(c) allows inside an italic span is for named items only and never applies to a move. Where a move's name contains the name of an item, the move's form governs and nothing nests (Rule 20.8.2(e)). The Entity Name cell of the register is written in the form `***Name***`.

**Rule 6.5.5 — Creation, at Gate 2.**
A Named Move is entered in the register by the same Post-Write Ledger Update, at the Gate 2 of the Area that first writes its name, and in the same pass in the user's entry with the pointers in both directions. There is no significance test: a move named once counts. A move named only in a spec enters nothing (Part 38 Rule 1.3.2). A move the author sets directly in a ledger (a `/new-character` interview, a `/update-ledger` pass) is entered in the register in that same pass, its First Area Appearance reading `Not yet established`. A new move name passes the checks of Rule 6.2 (the ledger scan now includes the register), and the register's Naming Checks cell records them. For a manual-borne move the same pass also sets the move's `first_area` in its CSV row (Part 42 Rule 42.12.5.1).

**Rule 6.5.6 — One owner.**
The user's entry (or the species, faction or school entry) owns every value of the move; the register mirrors only the name, the kind, the users (as pointers), the first Area and the status. On a difference the entry stands and the difference is a finding at the next ALCA pass (Part 37 Area 5). A move is never renamed in the register alone: a change of name is a new Named Move under Rule 6.4.

## Area 7 — Move Learning, Inheritance, and Transmission Law

### 7.1 — The Five Learning Methods

**Method L-1 — Direct Transmission:**
A technique is taught explicitly by a person who possesses it — through verbal instruction, physical demonstration, manual transmission, or qi-imprint teaching. Direct Transmission is the most reliable learning method and produces the fastest mastery development. The teacher's understanding of the technique's principle is transmitted alongside its form. A student who learns through Direct Transmission starts at Novice mastery with access to the teacher's conceptual framing, which accelerates advancement to Practiced and beyond.

**Method L-2 — Manual Study:**
Learning from a written transmission manual, recorded formation, jade slip, or equivalent encoded record. Manual Study is slower than Direct Transmission because the student must interpret the principle from textual or energetic encoding without the teacher's living guidance. Errors in interpretation are common. However, Manual Study allows techniques to be transmitted across generations and distances. Students learning through manuals typically begin at Novice but advance more slowly due to interpretive uncertainty.

> The manuals of the Manual Ledger (Part 42) are read by this method. One manual teaches one weapon type; the student starts at Novice mastery whatever the manual's grade (Part 42 Area 8); a character may hold a manual whose moves the character's realm does not yet allow, and each move is then Usable, Conditional or Latent for that character (Part 42 Area 3, Known but Latent). Area 4.1 is not changed by that.

**Method L-3 — Combat Observation:**
Observing a technique in use during combat and reverse-engineering its principle through analysis. This is the most common way cultivators develop partial understanding of opponent techniques. Combat Observation typically produces an approximate version of the original — functionally similar but missing the principle depth that Direct Transmission provides. A technique learned through Combat Observation is classified as a new Named Move (the observer's interpretation) rather than the original, unless it is developed to the point where the original's principle is correctly replicated.

**Method L-4 — Experiential Discovery:**
The cultivator independently develops a technique through intuition, experimental practice, and breakthrough insight. Experiential Discovery is the source of all original Named Moves. It is the slowest and most difficult method but produces the deepest personal understanding — a technique discovered rather than learned is often more profoundly mastered. Signature moves are almost exclusively produced through Experiential Discovery.

**Method L-5 — Absorption (Void Root Specific):**
The Void Root's ability to absorb and convert hostile qi (Part 3, Area 5.2) allows a Void Root cultivator to, over time, internalize the principles of techniques they have experienced. This is not instantaneous technique theft — it is an accelerated form of Combat Observation learning that draws directly on energy-level interaction with the original technique. The resulting Named Move is the cultivator's own interpretation, not a copy of the original. The Void Root is described in Part 3 Area 5.2; the learning rule is this Area.

**Open to anyone (anyone can learn, 2026-10-07):** Methods L-1, L-2 and L-3 are open to anyone, and no institution is required. An academy, hall or sect gives structured instruction, but others can train and be trained as well, and a person with no institution learns by any of these routes.

### 7.2 — Lineage Techniques

**What Is a Lineage Technique:**
A Lineage Technique is a Named Move that is formally registered as the intellectual and spiritual property of a sect, clan, family, or cultivation lineage (Part 22 — Faction Law). It can only be officially transmitted to members of that lineage by recognized authorities within it. Teaching a Lineage Technique to non-members without authorization is a violation of Part 23 (Affiliation Law) and carries significant cultural and political consequences.

**Bloodline Resonance:**
Some Named Moves are specifically developed using bloodline energy — cultivation qi derived from or amplified by a specific genetic heritage. These techniques may have reduced energy cost or amplified output when used by blood descendants of the creator. They cannot achieve the same depth in non-blood practitioners even if the mechanics are identical — the bloodline resonance is part of the technique's principle. Bloodline techniques are the most tightly held lineage secrets in cultivation society.

**Sect Exclusive Techniques:**
Techniques developed by a sect for its members are Sect Exclusive Techniques. Unlike bloodline techniques, they can be taught to any member (non-blood) and may achieve full depth in any practitioner — but they remain sect property. A cultivator who leaves a sect retains personal mastery of its techniques but loses the right to transmit them.

### 7.3 — Technique Conflict and Move Ownership

A Named Move has one creator of record. If two cultivators develop techniques that are mechanically similar but independently named, both are valid Named Moves — the similar mechanics are governed by separate names and separate lineages. If one was clearly derived from the other (determined by creation date and cultivation history), Part 23 governs the affiliation and ownership implications.

### 7.4 — Illegal Acquisition (Technique Theft)

The five learning methods of Area 7.1 are the lawful ways to learn a technique. Theft is not a sixth method: it is a way of getting the knowledge, and what it gets is then learned by one of the five.

**Rule 7.4.1 — What Counts as Illegal Acquisition:** A technique is acquired illegally when its knowledge is taken without the authority of its holder or of the lineage that owns it (Area 7.2). That covers a stolen manual, record or vault archive (Part 24 storage substrates); knowledge pulled from a person by soul interrogation or memory-theft (Part 24 Area 15 AM-1; Rule P24-TT-1); knowledge taken by physical infiltration (AM-4); and a member's transmission to an outsider without authority (Area 7.2). Watching a technique used in the open is Method L-3 and is not theft: a technique used publicly is no longer Tier I information (Part 24 Area 7.1).

**Rule 7.4.2 — What the Thief Holds:** Stolen knowledge enters Part 6 through the ordinary methods. A stolen manual or record is learned by Method L-2. Knowledge taken from a mind is theory without practice, because muscle memory and qi instinct cannot be extracted (Part 24 Area 8.5); it is learned like Method L-3, and the result is the thief's own interpretation, a new Named Move, until the original's principle is correctly replicated. In every case the thief starts at Novice mastery: the theft gives knowledge and no head start in skill, and the technique becomes dependable only after independent training. A personal Signature move cannot be taken as such, since it is bound to its creator's identity (Area 9.1, Rule 9.1.1); a copy is Advanced at best.

**Rule 7.4.3 — Who Governs What:** The theft itself, from finding the record to getting away with it, is a Part 24 intelligence operation (Part 24 Area 7.2, Rules P24-TT-1 and P24-TT-2; Area 15). If it turns into a fight, Part 5 governs the fight. Part 6 governs only what the thief now knows and can do.

**Rule 7.4.4 — Use After Theft:** A stolen lineage technique used where its lineage can recognize it exposes the thief. In a culture that holds technique theft a grave shame, such as CPT-1 Hierarchical-Sacral (Part 25), public use by a thief is a cultural violation and can raise a conflict to E2 or E3 whatever the original grievance was (Part 5 Area 5). The wider consequences are Part 27's, and ownership of the technique is Part 23's (Area 7.3).

## Area 8 — Move Collision and Interaction Law

### 8.1 — When Moves Meet

In cultivation combat, Named Moves frequently come into direct energetic or physical contact with opposing Named Moves, environment, and each other. The outcome of move collisions is governed by this Area in classification terms — the power outcome of the collision is governed by Part 3's power comparison rules. Part 6 governs what type of interaction occurs; Part 3 governs who wins at a given power level.

### 8.2 — Collision Outcome Classification

When two Named Moves meet directly, the outcome falls into one of five categories:

**CO-1 — Overwhelm:**
One move's force significantly exceeds the other's capability to resist or counter. The stronger move continues through the weaker one, possibly in a diminished form, and reaches its target. Governed by the 3-Level Rule (Part 3, Area 7.2) and power density comparison.

**CO-2 — Neutralization:**
Two moves of equivalent or near-equivalent force cancel each other out — neither reaches its intended target; both dissipate. Common when same-function moves of equivalent power meet directly. Environmental shockwave from the collision is proportional to the power level of both moves.

**CO-3 — Deflection:**
One move redirects the other's trajectory without fully nullifying it. The redirected move continues in a new direction at partial power. Deflect-function moves intentionally seek this outcome. Physics determines the deflection angle based on relative force and function type.

**CO-4 — Resonance:**
Two moves of compatible affinity or energy type interact constructively rather than destructively — their collision amplifies both, producing a combined effect greater than either alone. Resonance collisions are rare, typically unintended, and can be dangerous to both combatants. When two Fire-affinity moves at Harmony level or above collide, a Resonance collision may occur.

**CO-5 — Disruption:**
One move specifically disrupts the energy pattern of the other, preventing it from reaching its full expression. Disrupt-function moves intentionally seek this outcome against channeled or cast techniques. A successful Disruption may not neutralize the disrupted move's force but prevents its intended organized effect.

### 8.3 — Function-Specific Interaction Rules

**Attack vs. Shield:** The Shield's protection capacity is defined by its power level relative to the Attack. A same-level Shield against a same-level Strike produces CO-2 (neutralization) — the Shield holds, the Strike is absorbed. A Strike one Major Realm above the Shield overwhelms it (CO-1).

**Penetrating Attack vs. Shield:** A Penetrating Attack specifically bypasses Shields at power parity — it is designed to find the gap between power density and defensive architecture. A Penetrating Attack from a same-level cultivator against a same-level Shield produces CO-1 (penetration) rather than CO-2, unless the Shield is specifically designed as a Penetrating-type counter.

**Projectile vs. Deflect:** A Deflect function specifically targets Projectiles — it redirects them rather than absorbing. Against a same-level Projectile, a Deflect technique produces CO-3 (deflection) with high probability. Against a Projectile from a significantly stronger cultivator, deflection is partial (CO-1) and the Projectile continues at reduced power.

**Attack vs. Absorb:** An Absorb function converts incoming Attack force into energy. At power parity, Absorb produces CO-2 (absorption without damage). Against significantly more powerful Attacks, the Absorb is overwhelmed (CO-1). Against weaker Attacks, the Absorb converts them fully and the cultivator gains energy — a net positive outcome.

**Attack vs. Reflect:** A Reflect function returns the incoming force to its source. Success requires precise timing (a Reflect executed too early or late fails). A successful Reflect against a same-level Attack returns the full force to the attacker — the attacker must now defend against their own technique.

### 8.4 — Move Type and Collision Outcomes

Move Type interacts with collision outcomes in the following way: a higher-Type technique does not automatically "beat" a lower-Type technique of equivalent or greater power. A Basic-type Strike from a Core Solidification cultivator overwhelms an Elite-type Defense from a Xiantian Absolute God cultivator — because the power differential is greater than the Type difference. Type does not override power; it is independent of power.

However, Move Type does interact with Disruption: higher-Type techniques are harder to Disrupt because their energy patterns are more complex and more robustly structured. A Basic move can be Disrupted by any Disrupt technique at sufficient power. A Mythic technique's pattern is so deeply structured that only a Disrupt technique of equivalent or higher Type and equal or greater power can meaningfully affect it.

## Area 9 — Signature Move Identity Law

### 9.1 — What Makes a Move a Signature

A Signature move is not just the cultivator's most powerful technique. It is their most authentic technique — the technique that expresses, without compromise, who they fundamentally are. The following criteria all contribute to a technique's Signature status:

1. The technique was developed through the cultivator's personal journey — not inherited wholesale but grown from their own experience and understanding
2. The technique expresses a principle that is specific to this cultivator's cultivation philosophy, personal history, or inner identity
3. The technique evolves as the cultivator evolves — its expression today is deeper than its expression a decade ago
4. The technique cannot be fully replicated by another cultivator because its core derives from identity, not mechanics

A technique registered as Signature in cultivation society is recognized as identity-bound. Attempting to copy a Signature technique — observing it and developing an imitation — produces a Combat Observation-learned technique that is classified as Advanced at best (regardless of what it superficially resembles). The authentic Signature quality cannot be transferred without transferring identity, which is not possible.

> **Rule 9.1.1 — Scope of identity binding (Part 42).** Identity binding applies to a personal Signature: a Signature move that one cultivator developed through Experiential Discovery (Method L-4) and that is registered under that cultivator's `Signature Named Moves`. A personal Signature cannot be learned from observation (Method L-3) or from a transmission manual (Method L-2), and a copy is Advanced at best. A Signature, Fusion, Catastrophic or Mythic type move written into an institutional manual (Part 42) is a School Move: a Lineage Technique under Area 7.2, owned by the school or institution that holds the manual, teachable by Method L-1 and Method L-2, and not any character's personal Signature. A School Move keeps the Type it is classified as (Area 2) and the success rates of that Type; the depth levels of Area 9.2 belong to personal Signatures only. A school may codify a lineage-descended form of a founder's Signature only as a new Named Move under a new name (Rule 6.4); the founder's Signature stays identity-bound. The purpose of the binding is kept: a person's own Signature is never copied from a sight or a book.

### 9.2 — Signature Move Depth Levels

Signature moves develop through four depth levels as the cultivator's journey progresses:

**Depth Level 1 — Form:** The technique's physical and energetic structure is established. The cultivator can execute it consistently (at base 40% success rate). This is the birth of the Signature move.

**Depth Level 2 — Understanding:** The cultivator has lived enough with the technique's principle that their execution of it begins to reflect genuine comprehension rather than practiced form. Success rate approaches Expert level (60%). The technique produces secondary effects that were not part of the original design — expressions of the cultivator's deepening understanding.

**Depth Level 3 — Integration:** The technique and the cultivator's identity are no longer separate — the cultivator doesn't think about how to execute the Signature move; it expresses itself as a natural extension of who they are. Success rate at Master level (70%). Under ultimate pressure, Heart state determines how closely to this peak the cultivator can operate.

**Depth Level 4 — Transcendence:** The rarest state. The Signature move and the cultivator become synonymous in cultivation history — the technique is how they are remembered, and they are what the technique is remembered for. At this level, the technique may exceed its original Type classification in effect (not in classification — the Type does not change — but in the depth of its expression). A Transcendence-level Signature move produces effects that observers cannot attribute to the technique's Type alone; they attribute it to the cultivator themselves.

### 9.3 — Signature Move Type Upgrade (Evolution)

A Named Move — including Signature moves — can be elevated to the next Type through formal Type Upgrade. This is not a casual achievement; it is a milestone event in the cultivator's development. Requirements for Type Upgrade:

1. The technique must be at Master mastery level for its current Type
2. The cultivator must experience a breakthrough insight into the technique's underlying principle that reveals a deeper layer of its expression — typically occurring during a crisis, a significant combat event, or a moment of profound understanding (the same kinds of events that trigger Heart advancement in Part 4)
3. The technique's expression after the insight must demonstrably produce effects consistent with the new Type's definition — not by intent alone but by execution

**Upgrade paths:** Basic to Intermediate to Advanced to Elite; Elite to Signature, Fusion or Catastrophic by the move's nature; Signature or Catastrophic to Mythic; Mythic cannot be upgraded.

A Type Upgrade resets the technique's mastery to Novice for the new Type (base success rate of the new Type applies). This is the upgrade's cost — the cultivator has elevated the ceiling but must rebuild their reliability at the new level. The old form of the technique (pre-upgrade) is typically retained as a separate Named Move (often with "Lesser" or "Original" prefix) to preserve the lineage record.

**Signature Move Upgrade Specifics:**
When a Signature move undergoes Type Upgrade, it is one of the most significant events in the cultivator's arc. The old Signature form (Depth Levels 1–4 from the old Type) is preserved in cultivation history. The new form is recognized as the cultivator's evolved self — they are not the same person they were when they first developed the technique. The new Signature's name typically reflects the transformation.

## Area 10 — Fusion Move Protocol

### 10.1 — What Constitutes a Fusion Move

A Fusion move is formed when two or more distinct power streams are genuinely integrated into a single Named Move — not merely used simultaneously but unified into a technique whose expression is qualitatively different from either stream alone. Fusion moves meet the Type 6 (Fusion) classification criteria from Area 2.

The key requirement is **genuine integration**: the two streams must merge into a new expression, not simply appear together. Two techniques used simultaneously but independently are Combination-function moves (Convergence sub-type, Area 3.9.2). A Fusion move is one technique that could not exist without both streams — remove either stream and the technique ceases to exist as that technique.

### 10.2 — Fusion Formation Requirements

**Requirement F-1 — Dual Stream Mastery:**
The cultivator must have achieved at minimum Adept-level mastery of techniques from each of the two streams being fused. Attempting to fuse two streams while only Novice or Practiced in one produces immediate instability and forces the Backlash failure mode.

**Requirement F-2 — Energy Architecture Compatibility:**
The two streams must have compatible energy architecture — they must be able to occupy the same meridian space simultaneously without destruction. Some stream pairings are naturally compatible (Fire + Wind — both mobile, expansive forces). Some are naturally unstable (Fire + Ice — directly opposing principles). Naturally unstable pairings face an additional 5–10% penalty to success rate (Unstable Fusion, Area 10.3), even at Master mastery.

**Requirement F-3 — Integration Principle:**
The cultivator must understand the principle of integration — the underlying reason these two streams can combine. A cultivator who mechanically overlays two techniques without understanding why they can fuse will produce Partial Expression at best. True integration requires conceptual understanding of how the two principles relate to each other at a fundamental level.

### 10.3 — Fusion Stability Classification

Fusion moves are classified by their inherent stability:

**Stable Fusion:** The two streams are naturally compatible and the integration principle is well-understood. Success rate is at the Type 6 base/mastery levels without additional penalty. Example: Fire + Lightning (both energetic, outward-force principles).

**Unstable Fusion:** The two streams have natural opposition or the integration principle is complex. Success rate is reduced by an additional 5–10%. Example: Fire + Ice, Life + Death.

**Transcendent Fusion:** The two streams are from categorically different dimensions (physical qi + soul force, cultivation + technology). Success rate is reduced by an additional 10–15% but the potential output is significantly greater than same-dimension fusion. Example: **Soul-Flame Execution** (physical fire + soul strike).

### 10.4 — Fusion Move Failure Consequences

As established in Area 2.2 (Fusion type), failure produces one of four outcomes. In prose, the failure outcome is determined by the context: how far the fusion progressed before failure, how stable the streams are, and the cultivator's mastery level. The author selects from the four failure modes (Fizzle, Partial Expression, Distortion, Backlash) the one most consistent with the narrative moment and the cultivation mechanics.

**Backlash Rendering in Prose:** When a Fusion move fails with Backlash, the cultivator experiences internal energy conflict — the description should convey the violence of two power streams colliding inside the cultivator's meridians. The severity is proportional to the move's intended power level. A Basic-level Fusion Backlash is jarring and painful. A Catastrophic-level Fusion Backlash may be life-threatening.

## Area 11 — Move Narration Standards (DP-9 Named Move Architecture)

### 11.0 — DP-9 Named Move Architecture Integration

Area 11 governs the prose rendering of every Named Move. All Named Move narration must simultaneously satisfy **Part 28 Named Depth Pillar DP-9 — Named Move Architecture**, which mandates that every Named Move appearing in an Area is rendered with three-layer depth, told in plain words that show what the move does and how it looks (a first or special term gets its one-clause picture from the scene) (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06): (a) **physical execution layer** — the cultivator's body mechanics, qi direction, meridian activation, and technique trigger; (b) **sensory output layer** — what the technique looks, sounds, smells, feels, or tastes like at the point of expression, rendered through the POV character's senses; and (c) **consequence propagation layer** — what the technique changes in the environment, the target, or the deploying character's internal state in the immediate aftermath. A Named Move is normally rendered with all three layers; a compact later use carries them briefly (Rule 11.4). A rendering with fewer layers is reported as information only and is never an Audit Pass 1 FAIL. Named Move narration that satisfies DP-9 automatically satisfies the bold-italic mandate (Rule 11.1), the success/failure rendering standards (Rule 11.3), the soul dual-layer standard (Rule 11.6), and the affinity narration standard (Rule 11.7) — these rules are not superseded but are expressions of the same DP-9 depth requirement. The Area size range of Part 2 (1,280 to 2,400 words) gives Named Move sequences sufficient room to render all three DP-9 layers without compression.

### 11.1 — The Bold-Italic Mandate in Practice

Part 2, Area 3.2.1 establishes that all Named Moves are rendered in ***bold-italic*** in prose (`***text***`). Part 6 governs the naming conditions that make a technique a Named Move. The combined requirement is: every consciously executed, named cultivation technique is written in ***bold-italic*** at every point of use in prose. This includes:

- The first use of a technique in a scene
- Every subsequent use of the same technique in the same Area
- Uses in internal monologue when a character is thinking about a specific technique by name
- References in dialogue when characters name specific techniques

**What Is NOT Bold-Italicized:**
- General descriptions of force application without a specific technique name ("he sent qi forward," "she reinforced her body")
- Sensory descriptions of technique effects ("the fire consumed the wooden structure")
- Post-technique environmental descriptions
- Unnamed force applications

### 11.2 — Move Name First Appearance

When a Named Move appears for the first time in the novel — the first time the reader encounters this specific technique — the prose must provide sufficient descriptive context for the reader to understand what the technique does. This does not mean explaining it mechanically (which would break immersion) but rendering it experientially: what does it look, feel, and sound like? What is its immediate effect? Who deployed it and what was the intent?

Subsequent uses of the same technique in the same novel can be rendered more concisely — the reader has context. But they should never be purely mechanical ("he used **Crimson Tide Dispersal** again"). Each use should carry the scene's emotional and tactical charge.

### 11.3 — Move Success and Failure Narration

When a Named Move is deployed in prose, the outcome (success or failure, based on the Type's success rate and the cultivator's mastery) must be explicitly rendered through consequences. The prose cannot leave a Named Move's deployment ambiguous.

**Success Narration:** The technique's intended effect manifests at the appropriate level for the cultivator's power density and mastery. Environmental signature is consistent with the Heavenly Scale Grade (Part 3, Area 3.3). Physical and affinity effects are rendered with sensory precision.

**Failure Narration:** When a technique fails (the author decides based on type success rates, mastery, and narrative needs that this deployment does not succeed), the failure mode is rendered explicitly:
- For Type 2–5 (Intermediate through Signature): a "near-miss," incomplete formation, or partially expressed effect
- For Type 6 (Fusion): one of the four failure modes, rendered with appropriate intensity
- For Type 7 (Catastrophic): underpowered expression, premature release, or collapse — each with distinct physical description
- For Type 8 (Mythic): reality rejection, partial manifestation, uncontrolled manifestation, or backlash of reality — each rendered with the weight a Mythic failure deserves, in plain words (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06)

**High-Complexity Failure Narration Standards (Rule 11.3.1):**
The failure of a Catastrophic or Mythic move is a **major prose event** — it deserves the same narrative weight as a success. The following standards govern how each failure mode is rendered for high-complexity move types:

*Catastrophic Type Failures:*
- **Underpowered Expression:** The technique begins to form — the cultivator feels the energy moving, the environment responds partially (terrain shudders, a pressure wave starts) — and then the formation collapses before reaching critical threshold. The result is a visibly impressive but sub-Advanced effect. The narration must convey the cultivator's internal sensation of the technique "dying in their hands" and the opponent's recognition that the Catastrophic attempt failed. This is a turning point — the energy cost was paid; the opponent now knows the cultivator is depleted.
- **Premature Release:** The technique releases before complete formation, centering the explosion on or near the cultivator instead of the intended target. Narration must render the self-damage (meridian strain, knockback, disorientation), the cultivator's immediate recovery attempt, and any incidental environmental destruction that the release creates. The opponent may be partially affected if in close proximity but not at the intended scale.
- **Collapse:** The technique simply does not form. The energy expenditure vanishes into nothing. Narration renders the cultivator's moment of internal silence — the expected surge that never arrived — and then the sinking recognition that the reserves were depleted for nothing. This is the most psychologically devastating failure mode and should carry that weight.

*Mythic Type Failures:*
- **Reality Rejection:** The technique approaches formation — the cultivator may sense reality "bending around" the attempt, seeing the edges of the effect begin to manifest — and then the universe simply refuses. The energy disperses without creating any effect. Narration should convey the metaphysical resistance: not the technique failing, but reality actively declining to permit the interaction. The cultivator is left with the heavy weight of having reached for something the universe refused, told in plain words (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06).
- **Partial Manifestation:** Perhaps the most narratively complex outcome — the technique manifests at a fraction of its intended scope or in an altered form. The effect is real but incomplete: a technique intended to alter a battlefield creates only a localized, personal-scale reality disturbance. The prose must show what was intended versus what manifested, and the cultivator's attempt to determine if the partial result is salvageable.
- **Uncontrolled Manifestation:** The full technique activates but disconnected from the cultivator's intent. The effect unfolds according to the technique's principle rather than the cultivator's direction — a space-altering Mythic technique might create a spatial anomaly that affects friend and foe alike; a time-interaction Mythic might slow everything in a radius including the user. Narration renders the cultivator's horror as they watch something they cannot control unfold.
- **Backlash of Reality:** The most dangerous outcome — reality's resistance rebounds directly into the cultivator. Narration must render the physical sensation of cultivation damage (essence erosion, root strain, lifespan reduction), the visible signs visible to observers (the cultivator aging visibly, their qi signature destabilizing), and the profound assessment moment afterward: how much did this attempt cost, and is what remains sufficient to survive the current situation?

**Narrative Pacing of Failure Scenes:** A high-complexity move failure scene should not be rushed. The attempt, the moment of failure recognition, the immediate consequence, and the cultivator's next decision are each meaningful beats. Compressing all four into a single sentence tends to read as if the system's stakes do not actually apply; this is craft guidance reported as information only.

### 11.4 — Move Name in Combat Sequences

In extended combat sequences with multiple Named Move uses, the prose maintains the bold-italic standard throughout. The sequence creates a rhythm — each named technique a distinct beat in the combat's pulse. The author should vary the descriptive depth around each named technique to prevent repetition: some techniques are rendered in full sensory depth; others are rendered more briefly when pace demands it, within the limits set below. But the bold-italic itself never disappears.

**Compact later uses.** DP-9 stands (Part 28 Area 4.2): a move's first appearance, a Signature move, a Catastrophic or Mythic move and a turning-point move are rendered in full. A repeat use of the same move, or a similar use of it on a different type of target, may be rendered compactly, because explaining a move the reader already knows at full length kills the story. A compact rendering is one or two sentences and still shows all three layers of Rule 11.0: the physical act, one sensory detail and the consequence. A bare mention that leaves out any of the three falls short of DP-9 and is reported as information only.

**Move count per fight Area (reported, never a FAIL).** At the close of each fight Area the author is shown how many Named Moves the Area used; how many of them were rendered in full, how many compactly and how many only implied (used, but shown only through their effect, with no rendering of their own); and any three consecutive Named Moves rendered in the same sentence shape, with the three sentences quoted. These figures inform the author. They never pass, fail or block an Area.

### 11.5 — Multi-Cultivator Move Naming

When multiple cultivators are using Named Moves simultaneously (a common condition in large-scale battles at E3+), the prose must maintain clarity about which Named Move belongs to which cultivator. The attribution is handled through prose conventions (the cultivator is identified immediately before the bold-italic move name, or the move name's description identifies its source through its energy signature) rather than parenthetical annotations.

### 11.6 — Soul Move Special Narration

Soul moves require a dual-layer narration standard (per Part 4, Area 2.8). When a Named Move is a soul technique, the prose renders both the physical layer (what observers see: nothing, a faint shimmer, a visible soul manifestation) and the metaphysical layer (what the targeted cultivator experiences: the soul-force contact, the pressure against their soul structure, the specific effect the soul technique produces). Both layers are rendered in the same Area without breaking the POV standard of Part 2, Area 4.

### 11.7 — Affinity Move Narration

When a Named Move is affinity-typed (involves a cultivator's affinity resonance), the narration must incorporate the affinity's environmental response appropriate to the cultivator's affinity level:
- At Resonance/Attunement (Levels 4–5): secondary environmental response layered onto the move's direct effect
- At Harmony+ (Level 6+): the affinity's environmental response is pronounced and may dominate the visual description of the technique
- At Sovereignty/Origin Convergence (Levels 8–9): the affinity expression is so total that the technique's physical mechanics may be secondary in the prose to the affinity's manifestation

All affinity narration standards must simultaneously satisfy Part 4, Area 1.7 (affinity narration rules) and this Area's move narration standards.

### 11.8 — Worked Passages

These passages show Rules 11.0 to 11.4 at work. They are illustrations. Their scenes, figures and move Types establish nothing in canon, and the move names are the naming examples of Areas 3.2 and 6.2. Each setup line uses a Type that the Access Table (Area 4.1) allows for the realm the passage implies: Advanced is Possible for Champion and Ruler (11.8.1 and 11.8.2), Catastrophic is Possible from Xiantian Demigod (11.8.3), and 11.8.4 uses Core-realm fighters because Harmony (Level 6) needs Core Formation or above (Part 4, Area 1.4). ## Area 12 — Move Substrate Taxonomy Law

### 12.1 — Purpose and Domain of the Substrate Taxonomy

Areas 1 through 11 of this Part classify Named Moves along three primary axes: **Type** (complexity, Areas 2 and 4), **Function** (combat role, Area 3), and **Dimensional Influence** (cultivation-dimension shaping, Area 1). What those eleven Areas do not formally define is the **substrate** from which a Named Move draws its operative principle — the underlying carrier (energy architecture, anatomical architecture, bloodline resonance, relic channel, weapon channel, technology channel, formation mediation, or hybrid composite) through which the technique expresses. Area 6.3 requires an "energy substrate" field at registration, Area 7.2 describes bloodline resonance as a distinct technique property, and Area 1.6 distinguishes soul force from qi as differing substrates; Area 12 catalogues the full substrate space.

This Area sits beside Areas 1 through 11 and does not replace or override them. It introduces no new Move Types and does not alter the Access Table (Area 4). What it does is classify **what every Named Move rests on** — the operative substance the technique is made of — and defines the governance rules that follow from substrate category, including the critical category of Species-Innate moves (moves derived from species-anatomy rather than from channeled cultivation energy), which governs all species-innate Named Moves (the canonical reference case is the Minotaur-species moves **Chaprachup** and **Chalakudhaal** registered in the Bison ledger).

**Principle P6-6 (Substrate Classification Mandate):** Every Named Move has exactly one **primary substrate** drawn from the taxonomy in Area 12.2 and may have up to two **secondary substrates** where hybrid composition is established by Area 12.8. Primary substrate is an immutable property of the technique — a move whose primary substrate changes is a new technique requiring new registration under Area 6.

**Principle P6-7 (Substrate Governs Gating, Not Naming):** Substrate category governs *how a move's access, output, and suppression rules apply* (Realm gating, Firmament Lock interaction, cost scaling, collision rules). It does not govern *what a move may be named* — naming conventions (Area 6.2) remain substrate-agnostic, meaning a Species-Innate move may use any of the five naming patterns established in Area 6.2, plus the Species-Cultural Proper Name of Area 12.3.2.

**Principle P6-8 (Ledger Substrate Field Mandatory):** Every Named Move recorded in Character Ledger Section 3 (Power and Capability Snapshot) must declare its primary substrate. This is a Part 37 Area 2 ledger schema obligation and a Part 6 Area 6.3 registration-field obligation — both are simultaneously satisfied by a single Substrate Category entry in the move's ledger block.

### 12.2 — The Eight Primary Substrate Categories

The complete substrate space is partitioned into eight mutually exclusive primary categories. Every Named Move resolves to exactly one primary category.

**12.2.1 — Cultivation-Energy Substrate (Substrate Category S-1):**
The default substrate of the cultivation cosmos. The move's operative principle is **channeled cultivation energy** — qi, affinity-resonant force, soul force, heart-state projection, or the four combined. Almost every Named Move described in Areas 1–11 assumes this substrate: the Access Table (Area 4) is expressed in cultivation-realm terms because cultivation-energy substrate moves scale with realm; the energy-cost table (Area 5) is expressed in cultivation-reserve terms; the 3-Level Rule (Area 8 via Part 3 Area 7.2) is a cultivation-energy principle. **Firmament-Lock interaction:** fully suppressed by Firmament Lock (a Part 13 Special Zone) and by all cultivation-energy suppression mechanisms (Part 4 Area 2.7 soul suppression, suppression formations per Part 12 Area 7.2 and Part 14 Area 4.2). **Realm gating:** full Access Table applies. **Naming:** any of the five conventions; most commonly Principle + Action or Natural Force + Form.

**12.2.2 — Bloodline-Resonance Substrate (Substrate Category S-2):**
The move's operative principle is **cultivation energy amplified or modulated by heritable genetic resonance** (Area 7.2). The substrate is still cultivation energy, but the energy's pattern is bloodline-specific — it carries signatures that non-descendants cannot replicate at the same efficiency. A bloodline-resonance move used by a descendant has reduced cost and amplified output; the same move mechanically executed by a non-descendant performs at 40–70% depth even with identical technique understanding, because the bloodline signature is part of the principle. **Firmament-Lock interaction:** suppressed as cultivation-energy is suppressed (S-2 rides S-1's suppression profile); bloodline resonance does not grant immunity. **Realm gating:** Access Table applies. **Naming:** frequently Creator Reference or Clan-Ancestor naming (Area 6.2 patterns 3 and 5). **Inheritance:** Part 6 Area 7.2 governs transmission restrictions — bloodline moves are the most tightly held lineage techniques in cultivation society.

**12.2.3 — Species-Innate Substrate (Substrate Category S-3):**
The move's operative principle is **species-specific anatomical architecture rather than channeled cultivation energy**. A Minotaur's horn-force attack (**Chalakudhaal**), a Naga's coil-crush (hypothetical example), an Avian-sentient's wing-beat concussion — all are outputs the species produces *by existing as that species*, not by channeling qi. Species-Innate moves are the anatomical expression of a sentient being's biology as a combat vector.

*This substrate is governed in extreme breadth in Area 12.3.*

**12.2.4 — Relic-Channeled Substrate (Substrate Category S-4):**
The move's operative principle is **a cultivation relic or artifact acting as the energy channel or amplifier** (Part 12 domain). The move's identity binds to the relic — remove the relic, and the move cannot be executed, because the relic is not merely a tool but the substrate through which the technique expresses. This is distinct from a cultivator using a relic to amplify an otherwise-cultivation-energy move (that remains S-1 with relic amplification). Relic-Channeled moves are classified under Part 6 taxonomy even though the Part 12 object is load-bearing — Part 6 governs the move identity; Part 12 governs the relic itself. **Firmament-Lock interaction:** partial — the relic's channel may persist through some cultivation suppression, depending on relic Tier (Part 12 Area 2); a Tier 5+ relic can often project through a Firmament-class suppression that would nullify an S-1 equivalent. **Realm gating:** the relic's Tier provides the effective ceiling regardless of wielder's realm, but only within the relic's authorized wielder profile (Part 12 Area 11). **Naming:** frequently incorporates the relic's name.

**12.2.5 — Weapon-Bonded Substrate (Substrate Category S-5):**
The move's operative principle is **a weapon acting as structural component of the technique** (Part 33 domain). Distinct from S-4: a weapon is not a relic — it is a non-cultivation artifact whose properties (edge, mass, WS-class discharge capability, signature-weapon bond per Part 17/Part 33 cross-reference) are the vehicle of the move. Pure weapon strikes are not Named Moves; Weapon-Bonded Named Moves occur when a cultivator's codified technique incorporates the weapon's structural properties as an inseparable element (a signature sword form whose footwork and cut-pattern are inseparable from the blade's geometry; a Named Move that only exists because the specific WS-VII discharge delivery completes its principle). **Firmament-Lock interaction:** the cultivation-energy layer is suppressed; the weapon-substrate layer persists (a sword remains a sword). **Realm gating:** the cultivation-energy component is realm-gated; the weapon-mechanical component is not. **Naming:** often references the weapon.

**12.2.6 — Technology-Aided Substrate (Substrate Category S-6):**
The move's operative principle is **a cultivator-executed technique channeled through a Tier-rated technology system** (Part 8 Cultivation Integration Technology, with the synergy governed by Part 31). A Named Move delivered through a CI Tier 5 amplifier that could not exist without the technology layer is S-6. Pure technology weapon fire is not a Named Move — S-6 requires a cultivator-produced technique component. **Firmament-Lock interaction:** the cultivation-energy layer is suppressed; technology layer may or may not persist depending on integration depth (Part 31). **Realm gating:** Integration Resonance rolls operate in parallel with Part 6 base success rate; both must succeed for the S-6 output to manifest (Part 31 governance). **Naming:** any pattern; often references the technology designation or platform.

**12.2.7 — Formation-Mediated Substrate (Substrate Category S-7):**
The move's operative principle is **a Formation Array acting as the structural mediator of a Named Move** (Part 12 Area 7 domain). Formation Arrays are architecture, not technique (Part 12, Area 16.2); but a Named Move that requires a specific formation to express — where the formation is the scaffolding and the technique is the surge through it — is S-7. The formation is not a channel like a relic; it is a pre-structured domain that the move fills. **Firmament-Lock interaction:** varies by formation Tier; pre-built formations maintain structural integrity under cultivation suppression that would collapse cultivation-channeled equivalents. **Realm gating:** the formation's Tier provides the ceiling; the cultivator's realm provides the minimum keystone. **Naming:** often references the formation.

**12.2.8 — Hybrid-Composite Substrate (Substrate Category S-8):**
The move's operative principle is **two or more of the above substrates in inseparable combination**, where removing any substrate layer collapses the technique. S-8 is the substrate of Fusion Moves (Area 10) where the fusion is between substrate categories (not merely between affinity streams within S-1). A technique that fuses bloodline resonance with a species-innate anatomical component is S-2/S-3 composite; a technique that couples a relic channel with a technology amplifier is S-4/S-6 composite. The most common S-8 configurations are: S-1/S-2 (cultivation + bloodline), S-1/S-3 (cultivation + species-innate — the canonical form for hybrid sentient cultivators per Part 16 Area 17), S-1/S-4 (cultivation + relic), S-1/S-5 (cultivation + weapon), S-1/S-6 (cultivation + technology), S-1/S-7 (cultivation + formation), and rarer three-way composites. **Firmament-Lock interaction:** composite — each substrate layer's suppression profile applies to its component; a hybrid-composite move whose anchor is S-3 persists in reduced form when S-1 is suppressed, which is the structural reason hybrid sentients retain a minimum combat floor under cultivation-suppression environments. **Realm gating:** the most restrictive substrate's ceiling applies to the composite unless Part 28 Creative Flexibility authorization elevates the ceiling. **Naming:** composite often uses Principle + Action to reference the dominant substrate.

### 12.3 — Species-Innate Substrate — Extreme-Depth Governance

The Species-Innate Substrate (S-3) is the most consequential addition in Area 12 because it formalizes a category of Named Moves that the cultivation-centric Areas 1–11 structurally could not accommodate. This subsection establishes the complete governance envelope for S-3 moves in six sub-rules.

**12.3.1 — The Anatomical-Derivation Test.**
A Named Move qualifies as S-3 if and only if all four of the following conditions are met simultaneously:

*(a) The move's operative force vector is produced by species-specific anatomy* — a body part, physiological capability, or biological mechanism that is characteristic of the wielder's species and absent in species that lack the corresponding anatomy. A Minotaur's horn-force attack meets this test (horns are Minotaur anatomy); a human cultivator's bare-handed palm strike does not (palm strikes are available to all humanoid sentients and are governed by Mortal Tier 1 physical-architecture moves in Area 1.1).

*(b) The move is executable without conscious cultivation-energy channeling* — the wielder does not need to circulate qi, attune an affinity, mobilize soul force, or otherwise deploy cultivation reserves to produce the technique. The move is a refined biomechanical pattern plus species-anatomy, not a refined biomechanical pattern plus energy.

*(c) The move is not learnable by members of species that lack the underlying anatomy* — a non-Minotaur cannot learn **Chalakudhaal** by any Direct Transmission, Manual Study, Combat Observation, Experiential Discovery, or Void Root Absorption pathway (Area 7.1), because the pathway's endpoint requires anatomy the learner does not possess. Species-Innate moves are therefore exempt from Area 7's transmission taxonomy — they are inherited with the species, not transmitted through it.

*(d) The move expresses a culturally recognized codified pattern within the wielder's species* — idiosyncratic use of an anatomical feature (a random tail-slap) is not an S-3 Named Move; only codified species-cultural techniques with established naming, training traditions, and recognized execution forms qualify. This prevents S-3 from being a wildcard for any anatomical action and grounds the category in species-cultural canon (Part 16 Area 18 Bipedal Sentient Species Governance and Part 25 culture interaction). *(For a monster Kind, Part 40, see 12.3.7.)*

Moves failing any of (a)–(d) are not S-3. A move that meets (a) and (b) but fails (c) is typically S-1 with anatomical flavor (governed by Area 1 as a Mortal-tier physical-architecture move or higher-realm qi-physical variant). A move that meets (a)–(c) but fails (d) is raw biological capability, governed as environmental/biological description, not as a Named Move (no Part 6 registration required).

**12.3.2 — Naming Rules for S-3 Moves.**
All five naming conventions of Area 6.2 are permitted. A sixth convention is exclusive to S-3: **Species-Cultural Proper Name**. This pattern uses a word, phrase, or compound from the wielder species' own language or cultural lexicon that lacks direct translation into the standard Starward Saga common tongues. ***Chaprachup*** and ***Chalakudhaal*** are the canonical exemplars — both are Minotaur-language terms whose semantic content references the anatomical function (tail-snap / horn-force) in Minotaur cultural idiom. The Species-Cultural Proper Name convention is valid only for S-3 moves (and for S-8 composites with S-3 as a load-bearing substrate). Every Species-Cultural Proper Name registered under Part 6 Area 6.3 must be accompanied by a brief gloss in the ledger entry explaining the species-cultural referent — this preserves prose clarity without requiring in-prose translation (the bold-italic formatting mandate, Part 2 Rule 3.2.1 (Part 6 Area 11.1), applies unchanged).

**12.3.3 — Realm Access and the Anatomical-Maturity Scaling Rule.**
S-3 moves are **not gated by the Move Access Table (Area 4)**. Area 4's table expresses what cultivation realms permit what Move Types; S-3 moves are not bounded by cultivation realm because they are not cultivation-energy techniques. What governs S-3 output scaling is **anatomical maturity** — a species-specific developmental curve established in Part 16 Area 18.5 (Species Cultivation and Anatomical Profile) and operationalized here through the following scaling principle:

*S-3 moves scale with the wielder's anatomical development (species-age, physical conditioning, musculoskeletal integration, injury state), not with the wielder's cultivation realm.* A Mortal-tier Minotaur and a Champion-tier Minotaur execute **Chalakudhaal** with output differences driven by horn mass, horn density, neck musculature, and footwork — not by cultivation rung. This does not mean S-3 output is flat with realm; it means realm affects S-3 output only through the incidental channel that higher cultivation tends to correlate with better body conditioning (an indirect coupling, not a gating rule). A cultivator who advances from Core Realm to Divine Sea Realm sees no automatic S-3 uplift without corresponding anatomical improvement.

*Complexity Types still apply.* An S-3 move is still Type-classified per Area 2 (Basic through Mythic). **Chaprachup** is Basic-Type S-3; an Awakened-Beast-Sovereign's planet-shaking species-innate roar would be Catastrophic-Type S-3. Type governs the execution-complexity risk envelope (success rates, failure modes per Area 2.2); substrate governs what the move is made of. Both apply simultaneously.

*Cross-reference:* Part 16 Area 18.5 defines the Species Anatomical Development Scale (SADS) for each catalogued bipedal sentient species; S-3 output scaling uses SADS as its primary axis.

**12.3.4 — Firmament-Lock and Cultivation-Suppression Immunity (The S-3 Exemption Rule).**
This is the sub-rule with the largest operational implication. **S-3 moves are categorically immune to Firmament Lock and to all cultivation-energy suppression mechanisms that operate on S-1 substrate.** The reasoning is structural: Firmament Lock (a Part 13 Special Zone; Part 3 Rule 1.7.3 and Part 30 Area 1.3 state its ceiling), soul suppression (Part 4 Area 2.7 Suppression and Area 2.3 Bind Soul), and suppression formations (Part 12 Area 7.2, with Part 14 Area 4.2 Active Suppression and Interference) all operate on cultivation-energy channels. They cannot suppress anatomy. A Minotaur's horns exist whether the cultivator is under Firmament Lock or not; the charge that drives the horn-force is biomechanical, not qi-channeled; therefore **Chalakudhaal** remains executable.

*Scope of the exemption:*
- (a) **Full immunity** to cultivation-energy suppression (Firmament Lock, meridian seals, qi-suppression talismans, soul-sense sealing, affinity-dampening formations).
- (b) **Partial immunity** to environmental conditions that suppress both cultivation and biology — e.g., extreme cold immobilizes muscles regardless of substrate; toxin that paralyzes the wielder prevents all action regardless of S-3 status.
- (c) **No immunity** to anatomical damage — horn amputation disables **Chalakudhaal**; a severed tail disables **Chaprachup**. This is not suppression; it is literal destruction of the substrate. S-3 moves are more sensitive to anatomical damage than S-1 moves are to meridian damage, because the anatomy cannot regenerate on the cultivation-healing curve (Part 3 Area 8) — it heals on the species' biological curve per Part 16 Area 18.5.
- (d) **No immunity** to species-specific countermeasures — formations, substances, or techniques designed to specifically neutralize a species' anatomical capability (e.g., a Minotaur-specific horn-binding formation) can suppress S-3 moves that depend on that anatomy. These are classified as S-3-targeted suppression, a Part 11 / Part 12 design space.

*Narrative implication (captured in Area 12.3.6 narration rules):* the S-3 exemption is the structural reason hybrid sentient cultivators and bipedal-species cultivators remain threat-relevant under Firmament Lock and equivalent cultivation-suppression environments — a pure-human cultivator locked at Core Assimilation L3 Mid is cultivation-capped; a Minotaur locked at the same realm retains unrestricted S-3 access. This is a canon-level asymmetry that must be rendered truthfully in prose when Firmament Lock is a scene-active constraint.

**12.3.5 — Mandatory Ledger Registration Fields.**
Every S-3 Named Move must be recorded in Character Ledger Section 3 under the "Innate Moves (Part 6 Area 12)" subsection (see Part 37 Area 2) with the following required fields:

- **Move Name** — bold-italic in prose per Part 2 Rule 3.2.1 (Part 6 Area 11.1)
- **Substrate Category** — one of S-3 (pure species-innate) or S-8/S-3-composite (hybrid with S-3 as a load-bearing layer)
- **Anatomical Derivation** — specific species-anatomical feature producing the force vector (e.g., "Minotaur horns; forward-charging impact-delivery")
- **Firmament-Lock Interaction** — standard entry: "Immune per Part 6 Area 12.3.4(a). No suppression by Firmament Lock, meridian seals, qi-dampening arrays, or soul-sense sealing." — with any species-specific countermeasure notes per 12.3.4(d)
- **Power-Level Availability Profile** — SADS position plus realm-independent availability statement: "Functional at all realms; scaling driven by anatomical maturity per SADS"
- **Species-Cultural Naming Gloss** — required if the name uses Species-Cultural Proper Name convention (Area 12.3.2); a one-sentence gloss explaining the species-cultural referent
- **Move Type (Area 2)** — Basic through Mythic as normal
- **Move Function (Area 3)** — Attack / Defense / Mobility / etc., as normal
- **Mastery Grade (Area 2.1)** — Novice through Master as normal (S-3 moves undergo the same mastery progression; a juvenile Minotaur's **Chalakudhaal** at Novice differs mechanically from an adult warrior's Master execution even at identical anatomical maturity)

**12.3.6 — Collision and Interaction with S-1 Cultivation-Energy Moves.**
When an S-3 move collides with an S-1 cultivation-energy move of the same Combat Function (two Attack-function moves meeting in direct opposition), the Area 8 collision-outcome system applies with the following substrate-interaction modifiers:

- The 3-Level Rule (Area 8, via Part 3 Area 7.2) references cultivation realm differentials; for S-3 ↔ S-1 collisions, substitute the **Effective Force Rating** — a single scalar combining SADS maturity (for the S-3 side) and realm-plus-power-density (for the S-1 side), computed per the Part 3 Area 21 capability envelope cross-reference. This produces a substrate-neutral comparison.
- **Resonance collisions** (Area 8.2 outcome type) are rare at the S-3/S-1 boundary because resonance requires substrate compatibility; typical S-3/S-1 collisions resolve as Neutralization (CO-2), Deflection (CO-3) or Disruption (CO-5), with Resonance reserved for specific hybrid/bloodline interactions.
- **Under Firmament-Lock or equivalent suppression**, the S-1 side's Effective Force Rating is computed at the suppressed ceiling; the S-3 side's EFR is unchanged. This asymmetry is the mechanical expression of the structural narrative point in 12.3.4.
- **Environmental aftermath** of S-3 moves is anatomically grounded: a Catastrophic-Type S-3 horn-force attack leaves impact craters, splintered architecture, and biomechanical residue — not qi tremors, elemental discharge, or affinity environmental response.

Narration Standards N6-12 through N6-14 (below in Area 12.9) formalize the prose rendering of S-3 executions.

**12.3.7 — Monsters: the Kind as the Species, and the Register.**
A monster's abilities are named and entered in the single Named-Move register with the Kind or Individual as the user, body-based abilities as species-innate S-3 and qi-based abilities as S-1. Every distinct ability is named: it is distinct, not a regular punch, kick or bite, and it is memorable. Regular attacks stay plain description (Part 40 Area 4). S-3 is not a wildcard for any anatomical action (12.3.1).
- *(a) The Kind is the species.* For a monster (Part 40), the Kind entry in `ledger-character-monster/` is the species-level entry of Rule 6.5.2. A distinct body-based ability that the Kind entry records as the Kind's own meets condition 12.3.1(d), because the Kind's entry records it as its own, which is its codification. A monster has no school or training tradition, and (d) is not read to ask for one.
- *(b) Distinct, not regular.* Every distinct ability of a Kind or an Individual is named and registered, whether the whole Kind shows it or only one Individual. An ability is distinct when it is memorable: it does something a regular punch, kick, bite, claw, charge or other ordinary attack does not do, or does an ordinary thing in a way particular to this creature (Part 40 Rule 40.4.1.3 sets the test, with no count or threshold). A regular attack, however strong, is raw biological capability, described and not registered. A distinct body-based ability only one Individual shows is S-3 with that Individual as its user, and a distinct qi-based ability is S-1, named in the Individual's own entry or the Kind's.
- *(c) Scaling and registration.* S-3 output scales with the monster's anatomical development (12.3.3), with no Species Anatomical Development Scale (that scale belongs to bipedal species, Part 16 Area 18.5). The fields of 12.3.5 are recorded in Section 3 of the Kind's or Individual's entry, where `Species-Cultural Naming Gloss` applies only to a sentient monster whose name comes from its own language (Part 40 Rule 40.11.5.2). Each ability is also a row of the register, Rule 6.5.1, with the Kind or the Individual in its Users cell.

### 12.4 — Bloodline-Resonance Substrate — Formalization

Section 7.2 introduced bloodline techniques at the level of transmission (who may learn them). Area 12.4 formalizes the substrate-level governance:

**12.4.1 — Bloodline Signature Field.** Every S-2 Named Move must declare a Bloodline Signature — the specific heritable lineage (clan, family, awakened bloodline, or species-subtype) whose genetic-energy resonance is required for the move's full expression. This is a required ledger field under Part 37 Area 2 Section 3 "Signature Named Moves (Part 6)" subsection.

**12.4.2 — Descendant-Distance Scaling.** S-2 output scales by genetic proximity to the bloodline founder or canonical bloodline anchor: Direct Descendant (100%), First-Collateral Descendant (90%), Distant Collateral (70%), Adopted-into-Bloodline (ritual descendant, 50%), Non-Descendant-With-Technique-Knowledge (40–70% depth even with perfect mechanical replication). The specific numeric profile is per-bloodline-canonical and must be entered in the bloodline ledger (ledger-character-hero/ledger-character-villain/ledger-character-side Section 3).

**12.4.3 — S-2 Transmission Restrictions.** Bloodline moves are the most tightly held lineage techniques in cultivation society (Area 7.2). Formal transmission occurs only through bloodline rites recognized by the lineage authority (Part 22 faction governance / Part 25 cultural governance). Unauthorized transmission to non-descendants is a Part 24 information-security violation and a Part 25 cultural violation simultaneously.

**12.4.4 — S-2 Firmament-Lock Profile.** S-2 rides S-1's suppression profile; bloodline resonance does not grant suppression immunity. A bloodline cultivator under Firmament Lock loses S-2 access as they lose S-1 access. This is the critical contrast with S-3 (12.3.4) and must not be conflated — bloodline is heritable cultivation-energy modulation; species-innate is heritable anatomical capability.

### 12.5 — Hybrid-Composite Substrate — Extreme-Depth Governance

S-8 (Hybrid-Composite) is the substrate of techniques that cannot be reduced to any single primary category. This subsection governs the most common composites in the canon, with particular attention to the S-1/S-3 composite — the canonical form for hybrid sentient cultivators (Part 16 Area 17 Hybrid Sentient Character Governance).

**12.5.1 — Composite Registration.** An S-8 move's ledger entry must list every constituent substrate in descending load-bearing order (the first-listed substrate is the primary load-bearing layer; remaining substrates are contributing layers). Removing any load-bearing layer collapses the technique; removing a contributing layer degrades but does not eliminate expression. Registration under Part 6 Area 6.3 requires all constituent layers be enumerated.

**12.5.2 — The S-1/S-3 Composite (Hybrid Sentient Cultivator Composite).** The most common and narratively important composite. A hybrid sentient cultivator (Part 16 Area 17 definition) who possesses both cultivation-energy capability (S-1) and species-innate anatomy (S-3) may develop composite moves where cultivation energy channels *through* the species-innate anatomical feature. A Minotaur cultivator who channels qi through his horn during **Chalakudhaal** to amplify the impact with qi-pressure is executing an S-1/S-3 composite variant, not pure **Chalakudhaal** — composite execution is a separately registered Named Move per 12.5.1.

*Firmament-Lock behavior of S-1/S-3 composites:* under cultivation suppression, the S-1 layer is suppressed; the S-3 layer persists; the composite **degrades to the underlying S-3 base move at full S-3 capacity**. This preserves the hybrid cultivator's minimum combat floor even under Firmament Lock — a canonically important asymmetry that distinguishes hybrid sentient cultivators from pure-human cultivators in suppressed-environment encounters.

**12.5.3 — Other Common Composites.**
- *S-1/S-2 (Cultivation + Bloodline):* the substrate configuration of most high-register clan techniques. Firmament-Lock suppresses both layers together (S-2 rides S-1).
- *S-1/S-4 (Cultivation + Relic):* a technique requiring relic channeling to complete. Partial Firmament-Lock resistance per 12.2.4.
- *S-1/S-5 (Cultivation + Weapon):* a signature weapon-bound technique. The weapon layer persists under Firmament Lock.
- *S-1/S-6 (Cultivation + Technology):* a CI-Technology-integrated technique. Partial persistence depending on integration depth.
- *S-1/S-7 (Cultivation + Formation):* a formation-mediated technique. Formation layer persists per formation Tier.
- *Three-way composites* (S-1/S-3/S-5, etc.) are governed by compound rules; each layer's suppression profile applies to its component.

**12.5.4 — Composite-Specific Type Bump.** An S-8 composite is always at least one Type-tier above the highest-Type constituent — the coordination load of simultaneously managing two substrate layers is itself a complexity increase. A Basic S-1 move fused with a Basic S-3 move produces an Intermediate S-8 composite at minimum. This applies the same structural logic as Fusion Moves (Area 10) to substrate-composite coordination. A composite that meets Area 10.1 (genuine integration) is Type 6; any other composite takes the one-Type bump.

### 12.6 — Updates to Area 6.3 Registration Fields

This subsection extends Area 6.3. The mandatory Registration Fields for every Named Move include:

- **Substrate Category** — one of S-1 through S-8 per Area 12.2.
- **For S-2 moves:** Bloodline Signature field (per 12.4.1).
- **For S-3 moves:** Anatomical Derivation field plus Firmament-Lock Interaction field (per 12.3.5).
- **For S-8 moves:** full Constituent Substrate enumeration in descending load-bearing order (per 12.5.1).

A Named Move whose substrate is not declared defaults to S-1 until the next ledger audit corrects it (Part 37).

### 12.7 — S-3 Non-Transmission Principle (Exception to Area 7)

Area 7 defines five learning methods for Named Moves. Area 12.7 formalizes the S-3 exception: **S-3 moves are inherited with the species; they are not transmitted between non-conspecific individuals.** The implications:

- *Direct Transmission (Area 7.1.1)* — an S-3 move cannot be taught to a non-conspecific learner by any transmission method, because the learner lacks the substrate.
- *Manual Study (Area 7.1.2)* — an S-3 move's technique manual can be read and understood by non-conspecifics, but the understanding does not produce executable capability without the anatomy.
- *Combat Observation (Area 7.1.3)* — a non-conspecific observer can learn to *counter* an S-3 move but not to execute it.
- *Experiential Discovery (Area 7.1.4)* — a conspecific youth may experientially discover an S-3 move from species instinct or cultural exposure; this is the primary S-3 acquisition pathway for juvenile conspecifics.
- *Void Root Absorption (Area 7.1.5)* — a Void Root cultivator can absorb the *principle* of an S-3 move but cannot instantiate it without the substrate anatomy; the absorbed principle may inform a different Named Move the Void Root practitioner develops in a substrate they do possess.

*Intra-species transmission* of S-3 moves follows species-cultural protocols established in Part 16 Area 18 (Bipedal Sentient Species Governance) and Part 25 (culture); it is not universally Direct Transmission — some species treat innate moves as secret, others as communal, others as rite-of-maturity instruction.

### 12.8 — Substrate Interaction with Combat Function (Area 3) and Move Type (Area 2)

Substrate is orthogonal to Combat Function and Move Type. All eight Combat Functions (Attack, Defense, Mobility, Control, Trap, Support, Soul-Specific, Combination) can exist in any Substrate Category, with one constraint: **Soul-Specific Function moves are constrained to substrates that include a soul-force layer** — functionally, this means S-1 (when the cultivator has soul cultivation per Part 4 Area 2), S-2 (bloodline-inherited soul techniques), S-4 (soul-active relics), S-7 (soul-formation arrays), and S-8 composites containing a soul layer. S-3, S-5, and S-6 cannot host Soul-Specific moves because the substrates lack a soul-force channel. This is a definitional constraint, not a restriction — and it has a narrative upside: it establishes that species-innate capability does not include soul-attack vectors unless the species also possesses cultivation-soul architecture.

All eight Move Types (Basic through Mythic) are accessible in all substrate categories **subject to each substrate's native ceiling rules** (S-1 is Area 4 Access Table; S-3 is SADS; S-4 is relic Tier; etc.). Catastrophic- and Mythic-Type moves in S-3 require exceptional species anatomy, species-cultural canonical standing, and narrative-event gravitas per Part 27 consequence weight; they are rare but not structurally forbidden.

### 12.9 — S-3 Narration Standards (N6-12 through N6-14)

**N6-12 — Anatomical Grounding in S-3 Prose.**
Every S-3 Named Move rendering must ground the technique's force vector in species-anatomical description. Prose for **Chalakudhaal** must reference the horn-anatomy — curvature, density, the shoulder-into-horn force transfer, the horn's angle-of-impact — because those anatomical features are the technique. A rendering that describes only the impact effect without the anatomical mechanism is in violation of N6-12 and must be revised. This is parallel to the affinity narration requirement (Area 11.7) — substrate-truthful rendering is mandatory.

**N6-13 — Firmament-Lock Scene Truthfulness.**
When a scene is under Firmament Lock or cultivation-suppression per Part 3, prose must render the substrate-differential honestly. S-1 moves are suppressed; S-3 moves are not. A hybrid sentient character executing an S-3 move under Firmament Lock is executing a move the locked environment cannot dampen — the prose must convey this asymmetry without commentary (no "despite the Lock..." narration; the move simply functions), because the character's embodied experience is that their body works even when their cultivation does not. Breaking this rule produces a world-coherence violation that undermines the substrate taxonomy's narrative weight.

**N6-14 — Species-Cultural Name Rendering.**
S-3 moves named under the Species-Cultural Proper Name convention (12.3.2) appear in bold-italic in prose per Part 2 Rule 3.2.1 (Part 6 Area 11.1). The species-cultural gloss appears in the ledger, not in the prose; the prose treats the name as self-standing (***Chalakudhaal*** appears as ***Chalakudhaal***, not as "***Chalakudhaal*** [horn-force]"). If the narrative POV is a character who does not speak the wielder's species-language, the POV's internal recognition of the move may render non-linguistic (the observer recognizes the stance, the moment of commitment, the horn-angle, without knowing the name) — the name is still rendered in bold-italic when the narrating consciousness acquires it, either through subsequent recognition or through the wielder's own POV.

### 12.10 — Bison's Species-Innate Moves

Bison's Named Moves **Chaprachup** and **Chalakudhaal** are S-3 (Species-Innate Substrate) moves under this Area. They are recorded in the "Innate Moves (Part 6 Area 12)" subsection of the ledger, per Part 37 Area 2, with the Area 12.3.5 mandatory ledger fields (Substrate Category: S-3; Anatomical Derivation; Firmament-Lock Interaction; Power-Level Availability Profile; Species-Cultural Naming Gloss; other standard fields already present).

Future S-3 registrations (for any bipedal sentient, hybrid sentient, or other-sentient-being character entering the prose per Part 16 Areas 17–19) follow Area 12 registration requirements from first entry.
