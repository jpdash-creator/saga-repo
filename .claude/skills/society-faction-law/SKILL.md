## Overview

Part 22 is the Starward Saga Canon's Faction Law — the governing domain for every organized collective that stands between the individual character and the civilization. Part 22 defines what a faction *is*: its classification among the twenty-two faction categories, its Faction Power Tier (FPT), its internal governance, its territory and resources, and the governed events of its formation, transformation, and death. Part 23 (Affiliation Law) defines what a faction *is to every other faction*; Part 22 provides the institutional foundation on which that entire relational web is built — without Part 22's faction definitions, Part 23 has no entities to affiliate.

Ten foundational principles (Area 1) establish that a faction is a structured collective meeting four simultaneous criteria (coherent identity, internal hierarchy, shared purpose, coordinated action capacity); that factions exist within civilizations — or across them as Trans-Civilization Factions; that Faction Power Tier is distinct from civilizational CCT; that every faction type has a natural scale range; that faction identity is built on its Charter Principles; that hierarchy governs resource and authority allocation and is non-negotiable for faction status; that inter-faction relationships are governed law; that factions can be Hidden, Remnant, or Apex; that formation and dissolution are governed events; and that factions are the primary structural layer between individual and civilization.

From these foundations, Part 22 builds outward across 30 Areas: the twenty-two-category classification taxonomy, the Primary Function Rule for hybrid factions, and the SCL-1 through SCL-16 Faction Scale Code System (Area 2); the faction-type deep governance Areas — cultivator sects and Cultivation Alliance Networks (Area 3), government bodies and judicial factions (Area 4), military and security forces (Area 5), space forces and exploration factions (Area 6), academic and research factions (Area 7), alchemical and pharmaceutical factions (Area 8), science and technology factions and Synergy Integration Institutes (Area 9), mercantile and trading factions (Area 10), financial and banking factions (Area 11), artisan and craft factions (Area 12), intelligence and espionage factions (Area 13), assassination and bounty hunting factions (Area 14), criminal and underworld factions (Area 15), religious, ideological, and cultural factions (Area 16), noble houses and aristocratic factions (Area 17), and diplomatic and political factions (Area 18); the special categories of Apex, Hidden, Remnant, and Ancient factions (Area 19) and Trans-Civilization Factions (Area 20); the Faction Power Tier system, its gap dynamics, and the Faction Scale Transition Law (Area 21); internal governance, succession, and membership law (Area 22); territory and resource governance (Area 23); inter-faction alliance law (Area 24) and inter-faction conflict law (Area 25); absorption, merger, and dissolution (Area 26); faction formation law (Area 27); faction narration standards (Area 28); the faction-civilization boundary and reclassification governance (Area 29); and the full cross-Part dependency table (Area 30).

## Area 1 — Foundational Principles of Faction Law

### 1.1 — Core Principles

**P22-1 — A Faction Is a Structured Collective with Coherent Identity and Purpose**
A faction is any organized collective that meets four criteria simultaneously: (1) **Coherent Identity** — the faction has a name, recognized membership distinction, and self-defined purpose that its members recognize as binding; (2) **Internal Hierarchy** — the faction has a recognizable command or authority structure, however informal, with differentiated roles and decision-making authority; (3) **Shared Purpose** — the faction exists to pursue defined goals that are understood by its membership, whether those goals are cultivation advancement, political power, commercial profit, military conquest, scholarly discovery, or any other organized aim; (4) **Coordinated Action Capacity** — the faction can act as a unit, fielding its members, resources, and capabilities toward collective goals. An informal group of friends is not a faction. A cultivation sect with a master-disciple hierarchy, named membership, stated cultivation purpose, and the ability to mobilize its members collectively is a faction.

**P22-2 — Factions Exist Within Civilizations — or Across Them**
The default relationship between a faction and civilization is containment: factions exist within civilizations and are bounded by the cap of Rule 21.3.2, which follows the civilization's territory (its LA row). A village-level sect cannot indefinitely exceed the administrative and power parameters of the civilization that contains it. However, factions can become **Trans-Civilization Factions** — organizations that operate across multiple civilizations, maintain members in several polities, and answer to no single civilization's authority. Trans-Civilization Factions are not civilizations themselves; they are organizational structures that span civilizational boundaries. Their governance is more complex and is covered in Area 20.

**P22-3 — Faction Power Tier Is Distinct from Civilization CCT**
A faction's power level is expressed through its **Faction Power Tier (FPT)**, not directly through CCT. FPT uses CCT as a proxy reference (a faction is comparable in reach to the scale named in its FPT row of Area 21.2; a faction with FPT-7 is World scale) but is modified by faction-specific factors: internal cultivation peak, technology depth, territorial footprint, resource control, and internal cohesion. A faction can temporarily exceed its containing civilization's CCT in specific dimensions while remaining institutionally subordinate.

**P22-4 — Every Faction Type Has a Natural Scale Range**
Different faction types have characteristic scale ranges — the cosmological scales at which they naturally operate and can realistically be sustained. A cultivator sect naturally operates from Village to Galaxy scale. A galactic military force naturally operates from Star System to Galaxy scale. Authors do not casually depict faction types operating far outside their natural scale range without structural explanation. Every faction type definition in this Part includes its characteristic scale range. The range in each Area overview is typical; the Scale column of that Area's classification table governs an individual type.

**P22-5 — Faction Identity Is Built on Its Charter Principles**
Every faction has **Charter Principles** — the foundational rules, goals, and values that define the faction's identity and distinguish it from all other organizations. Charter Principles are not merely mission statements; they are structural governance rules that determine how the faction makes decisions, who can join, how power is distributed, and what the faction will or will not do. Charter Principles can evolve over time through formal faction governance processes, but sudden changes to Charter Principles constitute a faction identity crisis.

**P22-6 — Faction Hierarchy Governs Resource and Authority Allocation**
All factions distribute resources, authority, and responsibility through their internal hierarchy. Hierarchy can take many structural forms (linear chain-of-command, council governance, merit-based advancement, hereditary succession, election, trial by combat, etc.) but the existence of hierarchy is non-negotiable for faction status. Flat, egalitarian associations without meaningful authority differentiation do not qualify as factions — they are loose collectives.

**P22-7 — Inter-Faction Relationships Are Governed Law**
Relationships between factions — alliances, rivalries, trade agreements, non-aggression pacts, absorption, and armed conflict — are not informal social dynamics. They are formal governance events with recognized rules, obligations, and consequences. This Part governs the law of inter-faction relationships in Areas 24–26.

**P22-8 — Factions Can Be Hidden, Remnant, or Apex**
Beyond the standard taxonomy, three exceptional faction categories exist: **Hidden Factions** that conceal their existence from outsiders; **Remnant Factions** that once operated at higher FPT and retain knowledge, relics, or capability beyond their current apparent state; and **Apex Factions** that operate at FPT-15+ and require Part 28 authorization for direct narrative participation. These categories are governed in Area 19.

**P22-9 — Faction Formation and Dissolution Are Governed Events**
The birth and death of factions are canonical events, not casual narrative developments. Formation requires structural prerequisites; dissolution produces consequence events (Part 27). The lineage of a faction — who founded it, what circumstances forged it, how its principles evolved — is narratively and canonically significant.

**P22-10 — Factions Are the Primary Structural Layer Between Individual and Civilization**
In the Starward Saga, the individual character exists at the personal scale; the civilization exists at the macro scale; the faction is the intermediate structural layer where most story conflict, character development, loyalty, betrayal, and political intrigue occurs. Authors understand that faction dynamics are not background events — they are the primary social reality within which characters live their daily lives. Characters are defined in large part by their faction affiliations, positions within faction hierarchies, and histories of faction loyalty and conflict.

### 2.2 — The Primary Function Rule for Hybrid Factions

Most real-world factions blend multiple categories. A powerful cultivation sect may also operate a pharmaceutical arm, manage trade routes, and maintain an intelligence bureau. The Primary Function Rule states: **the category that defines the faction's founding Charter Purpose and controls the majority of its institutional investment is the governing classification.** Secondary functions are governed by the secondary-category rules as modifiers. A cultivation sect with a pharmaceutical arm is still a Cultivator Sect (CS-F) — the pharmaceutical function follows Alchemical Faction rules as a subsidiary arm, not as a primary classifier.

## Area 3 — Cultivator Sects and Cultivation Alliance Networks

### 3.1 — Overview: The Cultivator Sect

The cultivator sect is the oldest, most prevalent, and most narratively central faction type in the Starward Saga. It is the primary institutional mechanism by which cultivation knowledge is preserved, transmitted, advanced, and defended across generations. Sects exist at every cosmological scale from a single teacher-student pair in a remote village to galaxy-spanning cultivation empires that dwarf many political civilizations.

**Defining Characteristics of Cultivator Sects:**
- **Primary Purpose:** Cultivation advancement, knowledge transmission, and technique development
- **Core Resource:** Cultivation knowledge (manual traditions, formation arrays, techniques, sacred grounds)
- **Identity Anchor:** Lineage — every sect traces its knowledge heritage; the founding cultivator(s) define the sect's cultivation philosophy
- **Natural Scale Range:** Village (SCL-1) through Galaxy (SCL-11)
- **Characteristic Power Expression:** Peak individual cultivation power; Sacred Ground access; CPM resource generation

### 3.2 — Sect Hierarchy Archetypes

Cultivator sects organize themselves through one of several classic hierarchy models:

**The Master-Disciple Lineage Model (Traditional):**
The foundational model. Power, knowledge, and authority flow vertically from master to disciple through explicit transmission relationships. Inner Disciples receive direct personal teaching; Outer Disciples receive curriculum-based instruction; Honorary Disciples maintain formal affiliation without full transmission access; Core Disciples are groomed for leadership succession.
- Typical structure: Sect Master → Sect Elders → Core Disciples → Inner Disciples → Outer Disciples → Service Members/Handymen Disciples
- Decision-making: Sect Master has final authority; Elders have advisory input; Core Disciples may form an internal council
- Succession: Core Disciple designated by current Sect Master, or trial by vote of Senior Elders

**The Elder Council Model:**
Power is distributed among a council of senior cultivators. No single master has absolute authority; major decisions require council consensus.
- Typical structure: Grand Elder Council (5–13 members) → Sect Master (ceremonial or rotational) → Department Heads → Disciples
- Decision-making: Council vote; supermajority required for expulsion, war declarations, location changes, or inheritance decisions
- Common in large sects with multiple cultivation lineages where no single tradition dominates

**The Court-Institutional Model:**
The sect operates like a formal imperial court — positions are titled, bureaucratic, and may be held by individuals regardless of cultivation realm if they hold institutional authority.
- Typical structure: Sect Lord → Minister Positions (Arms, Treasury, Discipline, External Affairs, Internal Affairs) → Division Leaders → Disciples
- Decision-making: Formal administrative; Sect Lord holds executive authority; Ministers advise
- Common in large sects that govern territory and have non-cultivation members (support staff, city populations)

**The Meritocratic Tournament Model:**
Position and rank within the sect are determined by regular competitive assessment — cultivation examinations, sparring competitions, mission completion rates.
- All positions are subject to challenge; rank is not permanent but must be defended
- Promotes rapid advancement and high individual power density but generates internal conflict
- Common in martial-focused sects and competitive cultivation academies

**The Democratic Assembly Model (Rare):**
Full membership votes on all major decisions. Common only at small scale (village sects, fellowship groups).
- All members above a threshold cultivation stage have equal vote
- Extremely common early in a sect's formation; almost universally abandoned by the time a sect reaches Kingdom scale due to coordination cost

### 3.5 — Cultivation Alliance Networks (CAN)

A Cultivation Alliance Network is a formal federation of multiple independent sects that coordinate on shared governance, resource access, mutual defense, and talent exchange without merging into a single sect. CANs are not sects — each member sect retains its independent identity, Charter Principles, and internal governance. The CAN governs only the shared inter-sect domain.

**CAN Formation Requirements:**
- Minimum 3 independent sects at equivalent scale levels
- Formal Alliance Charter ratified by majority vote of member sect leaders
- Defined Alliance Territory — the geographic area subject to CAN governance
- Shared resource infrastructure (at minimum: joint Formation Array grid, CPM exchange protocols)
- Alliance Council with defined quorum and voting rules

**CAN Governance Structure:**
- Alliance Council: one senior representative from each member sect with equal formal vote
- Alliance Lord (optional): a rotational or elected position with executive authority during crises
- Shared Enforcement Branch: cross-sect disciplinary body for inter-sect disputes
- Joint Treasury: shared resource pool funded by member sect contributions

**CAN Scale Range:** Kingdom (SCL-5) through Universe (SCL-14)

**CAN Dissolution Triggers:** member sect withdrawal exceeding 50% of total membership; failure to agree on Alliance Charter amendments within a defined period; annexation of member sects by hostile powers; voluntary consensus dissolution.

## Area 4 — Government Bodies and Judicial Factions

### 4.1 — Overview: Government Bodies

Government Bodies are the formal political administrative structures that hold sovereign authority over a defined territory and its population. They are distinguished from other faction types by their claim to legitimate coercive authority — the right to enforce laws, collect taxes, maintain public order, and represent the territory in external relations. Government Bodies may coexist with cultivator sects, military factions, and commercial factions within the same territory, but they hold legal primacy over all other factions within their claimed jurisdiction.

**Defining Characteristics:**
- **Primary Purpose:** Political administration, law enforcement, public order, territorial governance
- **Authority Claim:** Sovereign authority over a defined territory and population
- **Core Resource:** Legal legitimacy, tax revenue, military alliance, population loyalty
- **Natural Scale Range:** Village (SCL-1) through Universe (SCL-14)

### 4.2 — Government Archetype Classifications

| Village Council / Elder Assembly | Local consensus governance | Elder wisdom + cultivation seniority | Eldest living elder or council vote |
| City-State Governance | Single-city political authority | Noble family or elected council | Hereditary or council rotation |
| Kingdom Administration | Regional territory, single royal authority | Royal bloodline + cultivation legitimacy | Hereditary; tested succession in cultivation kingdoms |
| Imperial Court | Multi-regional territory with central imperial authority | Emperor/Empress bloodline + Mandate of Cultivation | Hereditary; contest among princes/heirs common |
| Republic / Representative Assembly | Elected representative legislature | Popular mandate; noble representation | Election cycle; term limits |
| Theocratic Government | Religious or ideological authority governs | Divine mandate claimed from cultivation breakthrough or religious doctrine | Religious hierarchy succession |
| Military Government (Junta) | Military force controls political administration | Conquest or emergency authority | Senior military rank; succession contests |
| Cultivation Sovereignty (Sect-State) | Cultivator sect holds both cultivation and political authority | Peak cultivation power as political legitimacy | Sect succession law applies |
| Technology-Sovereign State | Technology development authority controls government | TA axis supremacy; technocrat governance | Technical merit; election within technocrat class |

### 4.3 — Government Scale Governance Rules

**Rule 4.3.1 — Government Authority Is Bounded by Structural Administration Capacity:**
A government body's effective authority does not automatically extend across its claimed territory. It extends as far as it can maintain physical presence, enforce law, collect taxes, and project military force. Government authority claims that significantly exceed administrative capacity are nominal claims — legally asserted but functionally hollow beyond a certain radius.

**Rule 4.3.2 — Legitimacy Requires Reproduction:**
Government legitimacy must be continuously reproduced through visible governance acts: law enforcement, dispute resolution, infrastructure maintenance, population service, and defense provision. Governments that fail to reproduce legitimacy through these acts gradually lose political authority regardless of legal claims.

**Rule 4.3.3 — CCT Sets the Administrative Ceiling:**
The CCT of the civilization within which a government operates sets the maximum administrative tier that government can reach. A government in a CCT-5 (Multi-World) civilization cannot administer at Star Cluster scale — its administrative infrastructure simply does not extend that far without first achieving the CCT requirements for Star Cluster administration.

**Rule 4.3.4 — Succession Crises Are Faction-Level Events:**
When government succession fails — the heir dies, the emperor is assassinated, the election produces a disputed result — the resulting succession crisis is a Faction-Level event that triggers inter-faction conflict between rival claimants and the factions aligned with each. Succession crises are among the primary drivers of political faction conflict in the Starward Saga.

### 4.4 — Judicial and Legal Factions (JLF)

Judicial and Legal Factions are specialized organizations that hold authority over dispute resolution, law interpretation, and legal enforcement. They may be arms of a government (state judiciary), independent arbitration bodies, or trans-civilization legal institutions.

**Types of Judicial Factions:**
- **State Judiciary:** An arm of government; governed by the Government Body it serves
- **Independent Arbitration Houses:** Neutral dispute resolution bodies trusted by multiple factions and governments; derive authority from reputation and multilateral recognition
- **Inter-Sect Tribunal Councils:** Bodies convened to resolve disputes between cultivator sects; often temporary but may become permanent at scale
- **Galactic Law Courts:** Universe-scale legal institutions with jurisdiction over inter-civilization trade, conflict, and rights violations
- **Martial Judgment Halls:** Cultivation-law courts that adjudicate disputes through regulated combat under Part 5 rules; common in cultivation-dominant civilizations

### 4.5 — Justice in a Kingdom

Below the level of war a kingdom keeps order through law. This section is the general model of who judges, what counts as a crime, how it is punished and who may appeal; a story that needs a hearing, an arrest or a sentence rests on it. It adds no law to any kingdom. The courts and laws of one kingdom are recorded in its Kingdom-State Ledger (Section 5, Law Enforcement / Internal Security) as the story establishes them; where nothing is recorded, the first Area that needs a court or a law sets it (OPEN-AREA) and the ledger update enters it. Part 5 owns what a use of force may lawfully be, Part 25 owns the culture the law rests on, and Part 27 owns the consequence of a verdict.

**Rule 4.5.1 — A Judge Has Standing:**
A hearing is held by someone with authority over this matter and these people. The usual judges are the ruler or a delegate in the ruler's name (the gravest matters, and appeals); a royal court or tribunal (State Judiciary, Area 4.4), often named for the matter it hears; a local authority such as a village council, an elder or the steward of a noble house (petty crimes and local disputes, bounded by its territory, Rule 4.3.1); a sect or house under its own charter (its members' internal matters, Area 22); and, by consent of both sides, a martial judgment hall or an arbitration house (Area 4.4). A judge who sits outside their standing is a story event and not an error to be repaired quietly.

**Rule 4.5.2 — A Crime Is What the Law Names:**
The usual families are harm to a person; taking or damaging what belongs to another; breaking an oath, a contract or a trust; defying lawful authority, which at its gravest is treason; and misuse of cultivation power, meaning force used outside what Part 5 allows, against someone who cannot answer it, or inside a protected zone (Part 5 Area 9.3). A kingdom's own law may name others, and what one kingdom punishes another may allow.

**Rule 4.5.3 — A Punishment Has a Reason and a Price:**
The usual punishments are restitution or a fine; labor or service; confinement; loss of rank, office or standing; exile; restraint of a cultivator's power by containment (Part 12); and death. The sentence follows the gravity of the crime, the offender's standing and the kingdom's custom. A sentence out of proportion, or one softened only because the offender is powerful, is shown with its reason and carries consequences (Part 27).

**Rule 4.5.4 — Appeal Runs Upward and Ends With the Ruler:**
A verdict may be appealed from a local authority to the kingdom's court, and from the court to the ruler, whose judgment is final inside the kingdom. A sect or house verdict is appealed within its own hierarchy. A dispute between kingdoms is not an appeal; it goes to diplomacy (Area 18; Part 23). An appeal costs something (time, standing, a fee, a favor), and a court that cannot enforce its verdict is losing the legitimacy that Rule 4.3.2 says must be reproduced.

### 5.5 — Mercenary and Bounty Hunter Organizations

Mercenary organizations occupy a distinct space in the MSF taxonomy: they are armed professional forces that sell their capability to any contracting party without permanent territorial or political loyalty.

**Governing Rules for Mercenary Factions:**
- **Contract Law (Rule 5.5.1):** All mercenary engagements are governed by formal contract. Contract breach by either party triggers consequence events (Part 27) — a mercenary organization that violates contract terms loses reputation; a client that fails to pay triggers enforcement clauses.
- **Neutral Status (Rule 5.5.2):** Mercenary organizations may maintain formal neutrality in political disputes, allowing them to accept contracts from multiple parties sequentially if not simultaneously.
- **Reputation Economy (Rule 5.5.3):** Mercenary faction power is measured as much by reputation as by raw capability. Organizations with long records of contract fulfillment command premium rates.
- **Scale Ceiling (Rule 5.5.4):** Mercenary organizations typically cap at SCL-10 (Star Quadrant) scale — above that scale, the political complexity of operating across multiple civilization boundaries requires trans-civilization governance structures.

### 14.3 — Assassination Faction Governance Rules

**Rule 14.3.1 — Contract Sanctity:**
Assassination faction contracts are binding on both contractor and organization. An assassination organization that fails to complete a contracted elimination loses guild reputation. A contractor who fails to pay upon completion triggers enforcement clauses — and assassination factions are uniquely positioned to enforce payment.

**Rule 14.3.2 — Target Restriction Clauses:**
Major assassination factions maintain Target Restriction Lists — categories of targets they will not accept contracts against regardless of payment. Common restrictions: children below cultivation threshold; neutral diplomatic personnel; members of the organization's own patron faction.

**Rule 14.3.3 — Counter-Contract:**
Any targeted individual aware of an active contract against them may issue a Counter-Contract — a contract with any willing faction to neutralize the original contractor or the original assassination organization itself. Counter-contracts are a recognized and common response at Kingdom scale and above.

## Area 19 — Special Faction Categories: Apex, Hidden, Remnant, and Ancient Factions

### 19.1 — Apex Factions (FPT-15+)

Apex Factions are organizations that operate at FPT-15 (Multiverse) or above. Their capabilities, territorial reach, and institutional depth place them in the same narrative weight class as CCT-15+ Apex Civilizations — they require **Part 28 authorization** for direct narrative participation. Apex Faction means FPT-15+ only; a ledger label such as Apex-tier for the strongest in its system is local standing and does not trigger Part 28.

**Governance Rules for Apex Factions:**
- **Rule 19.1.1 — Part 28 Authorization Required:** Any Apex Faction that directly interacts with the protagonist's world as an active narrative participant requires Part 28 Creative Flexibility Law authorization.
- **Rule 19.1.2 — Presence as Force:** Apex Factions may be present as background forces — their existence known, their shadow felt — without direct participation; this does not require Part 28 authorization.
- **Rule 19.1.3 — Apex Faction Representatives:** Individual representatives of Apex Factions may appear in narrative at scales below FPT-15 without triggering the Apex Faction rule, provided those representatives are not exercising the full institutional power of the Apex Faction.

### 19.2 — Hidden Factions

Hidden Factions are organized institutions that conceal their existence from all outside parties. Their public face (if any) provides no indication of the faction's true nature, extent, or power. Hidden Factions may be any category type — a Hidden Sect, a Hidden Government, a Hidden Criminal Syndicate.

**Governance Rules:**
- **Rule 19.2.1 — Identity Concealment Requires Active Investment:** Maintaining a hidden faction status requires continuous resource investment in counter-intelligence, cover operations, and member secrecy culture.
- **Rule 19.2.2 — Discovery Is a Narrative Event:** The discovery of a Hidden Faction's true identity is always a major narrative event — it triggers consequence events (Part 27) and typically reshapes the political landscape of the story arc.
- **Rule 19.2.3 — Hidden Factions May Operate at Any FPT:** A Hidden Faction at FPT-10 is organizationally as powerful as any other FPT-10 faction — its hiddenness is a strategic posture, not a power limitation.

### 19.3 — Remnant Factions

Remnant Factions are organizations that once operated at a significantly higher FPT and have since declined but retain knowledge, techniques, relics, or members that reflect their former status.

**Governance Rules:**
- **Rule 19.3.1 — Remnant FPT Distinction:** A Remnant Faction has a Current FPT (actual operational capability) and a Peak FPT (historical maximum). The distinction matters narratively — a Current FPT-4 faction with Peak FPT-11 may possess Tier 4 Formation Arrays and Divine Liquid reserves that a standard FPT-4 faction could never access.
- **Rule 19.3.2 — Remnant Factions as Story Treasure:** Remnant Factions are among the most narratively productive faction types — they hold treasures from a greater era in a diminished present, creating story opportunities for discovery, recovery, and restoration.
- **Rule 19.3.3 — Remnant Factions May Conceal Their True State:** A Remnant Faction may be a Hidden Faction — concealing not just its identity but its former peak status from outsiders.

### 19.4 — Ancient Factions

Ancient Factions are Remnant Factions that have existed for multiple civilization generations — centuries or millennia. Their institutional memory, internal tradition, and accumulated knowledge may exceed any contemporary faction of equivalent apparent scale.

## Area 20 — Trans-Civilization Factions

### 20.1 — Definition and Governance

A Trans-Civilization Faction is a faction that maintains organized presence and operational capability across two or more distinct civilizations, answering to none of them exclusively. Trans-Civilization Factions are not civilizations — they do not hold sovereign territory or claim government authority. They are organizational structures that operate in the spaces between civilizations and across their boundaries.

**Trans-Civilization Faction Formation Prerequisites:**
- Active membership and operational capacity in at minimum two distinct civilizations
- A Charter that explicitly asserts independence from any single civilization's authority
- Internal governance that is not dominated by any single civilization's political culture
- Resource base that is distributed across multiple civilizations — no single civilization can cut off the faction's resources by unilateral action

**Examples of Trans-Civilization Faction Types:**
- Ancient cultivation sects that predate the current civilization's founding
- Interstellar trade conglomerates with assets in multiple polities
- Intelligence organizations that sell to multiple governments simultaneously
- Religious/ideological movements that have spread beyond their originating civilization
- Criminal syndicates that exploit cross-civilization legal variation

### 20.2 — Trans-Civilization Faction Interaction Law

**Rule 20.2.1 — No Civilization Has Exclusive Authority:**
No single civilization can legally compel a Trans-Civilization Faction's full compliance — the faction's presence in other civilizations provides legal cover. Coordinated multi-civilization enforcement action is required to bring full legal pressure.

**Rule 20.2.2 — Trans-Civilization Factions Are Natural Intelligence Nodes:**
Because they move between civilizations, Trans-Civilization Factions are natural repositories of cross-civilization intelligence. Most major Intelligence Factions at scale are Trans-Civilization by necessity.

**Rule 20.2.3 — Trans-Civilization Factions May Have Their Own Territory:**
Sufficiently powerful Trans-Civilization Factions may control territory not claimed by any civilization — void stations, frontier worlds, unclaimed Sacred Grounds. This territory is governed by the faction directly.

## Area 21 — Faction Power Tier (FPT) System

### 21.1 — Faction Power Tier Definition

The **Faction Power Tier (FPT)** is the canonical measure of a faction's organizational strength. It uses the CCT numbering (1 through 20) as a proxy for power but is applied at the faction level with faction-specific modifiers, and each FPT is named by the scale in the FPT Reference Table (Area 21.2), not by a CTD name. A faction is comparable in reach to the scale named in its FPT row (a faction at FPT-7 is World scale) — but it is not a civilization.

**FPT is determined by five factors, three that set the base and two that modify it:**

| Factor | Role | Determination |
|---|---|---|
| Cultivation Peak | Base factor | Peak cultivation realm of the faction's top members; read the FPT row whose Cultivation Peak column (Area 21.2) contains it |
| Technology Depth | Base factor | Highest sustained technology tier of faction's infrastructure; read the FPT row whose Tech Tier column (Area 21.2) contains it, taking the higher row when a tier is shared by two rows |
| Territorial Footprint | Base factor | Faction's operational territory per Part 13 classification; read the FPT row whose Equivalent Scale (Area 21.2) matches the faction's Scale Code (Area 2.3) |
| Resource Control | Modifier: −1, 0 or +1 | CPM grade access, financial assets, strategic resource holdings: +1 if they exceed what its base tier normally commands, 0 if they match, −1 if they fall short or hang on a single route or patron |
| Internal Cohesion | Modifier: −1, 0 or +1 | Loyalty, succession stability, internal conflict rate: +1 for stable succession and no open internal conflict, 0 for ordinary friction, −1 for open schism, a disputed succession or active internal conflict |

**Modified Base FPT Formula:**
Base FPT = (Cultivation Peak equivalent + Technology Depth equivalent + Territorial Footprint equivalent) / 3, rounded to the nearest whole tier (a half rounds down), then modified by the Resource Control and Internal Cohesion modifiers (each −1, 0 or +1, so ±2 maximum in total).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| FPT-1 | Village | Village sect; local clan | Mortal Tier 1 | Tier 1–2 |
| FPT-2 | Town | Town sect; town militia | Mortal Tier 2 lower | Tier 2–3 |
| FPT-3 | City | City sect; city watch; regional trade guild | Mortal Tier 2 upper | Tier 3–4 |
| FPT-4 | Capital | Capital sect; royal guard; major trading house | Xiantian: Demigod–Void God | Tier 4–5 |
| FPT-5 | Kingdom | Kingdom-scale sect; kingdom army; banking house | Xiantian: True God–Absolute God | Tier 5–6 |
| FPT-6 | Empire | Empire sect; imperial military; interstellar trade corp | Core Formation | Tier 6–7 |
| FPT-7 | World | World sect; planetary defense force; planetary government | Core Assimilation | Tier 7–8 |
| FPT-8 | Star System | System sect; system fleet; system government | Core Solidification | Tier 8–9 |
| FPT-9 | Star Cluster | Cluster sect; sector patrol; interstellar merchant | Divine Transformation | Tier 9–10 |
| FPT-10 | Star Quadrant | Quadrant sect; FTL combat fleet; quadrant-scale trade cartel | Sea Formation | Tier 10–11 |
| FPT-11 | Galaxy | Galaxy sect; galactic armada; galactic merchant empire | Heaven Treading | Tier 11–12 |

**Reading "lower" and "upper" in the Cultivation Peak column:** Mortal Tier 2 lower = Senior and Warrior; upper = Champion, Ruler and Monarch. For a Divine realm, lower = Levels 1–5 and upper = Levels 6–9. Any faction with a Divine Sea Realm member needs Part 28 Tier 2 Authorization (Part 3).

### 21.3 — FPT Gap Dynamics

**Rule 21.3.1 — The Three-FPT-Tier Gap Rule:**
When two factions are separated by 3 or more FPT tiers, their interaction is inherently asymmetric. The higher-FPT faction can compel, absorb, or destroy the lower-FPT faction at will. The lower-FPT faction's resistance is narrative heroism or strategic cleverness — it is not a direct power contest. This mirrors the Three-Tier Gap Rule in Part 21 (Civilization Law).

**Rule 21.3.2 — FPT Within Civilization:**
No faction permanently operating within a civilization can maintain an FPT more than one FPT row above the row whose Equivalent Scale (Area 21.2) matches the civilization's territory (its Location Axis, LA, Part 21 Area 2.4) without becoming effectively independent of the civilization. A single faction three or more FPT rows above that row is functionally a state-within-a-state.

**Rule 21.3.3 — FPT Fluctuation:**
FPT is not static — it fluctuates with the faction's current operational condition. A sect that has suffered the death of its top three elders may drop from FPT-7 to FPT-4 instantly. A trading cartel that loses its primary trade route may drop from FPT-8 to FPT-5. FPT is a measure of current functional capability, not historical peak.

### 21.4 — Faction Scale Transition Law

Faction scale transitions — when a faction advances or declines across FPT tiers — are not administrative events. They are major narrative governance events with specific canonical requirements, consequence implications, and story-level significance. This Area governs how scale transitions occur and what they mean.

**Rule 21.4.1 — Scale Transitions Must Be Earned Through Narrative Events:** A faction cannot advance from FPT-3 to FPT-6 simply because "time has passed." Every FPT tier advancement requires canonical justification through one or more of the following event types:
- **Cultivation Peak Advancement:** A faction's top cultivators advance to the realm tier associated with the next FPT level
- **Territory Expansion:** The faction gains control of territory at the scale of the next FPT level through conflict, negotiation, or discovery
- **Resource Seizure or Development:** The faction acquires the CPM grade, technology tier, or resource base characteristic of the next FPT level
- **Institutional Integration:** The faction absorbs, allies with, or is recognized by the institutional structures characteristic of the next FPT level
- **Part 27 Consequence Cascade:** A major consequence event (CSS-7+) reorganizes the power landscape in a way that elevates the faction's relative position

**Rule 21.4.2 — Single-Tier Transitions Are Standard; Multi-Tier Transitions and Part 28 Authorization:** A faction advancing 1 FPT tier through a single narrative arc is standard governance. Advancing 2 FPT tiers usually combines several event types from the list above. Advancing 3 or more FPT tiers in a single narrative arc is a world-shaking event; the Chapter Spec states it and its justification, and Part 28 consultation is used when the author or the Chapter Spec raises it. The number of tiers is information only.

**Rule 21.4.3 — Scale Transition Has Internal Consequence:** When a faction advances a FPT tier, its internal structure is under pressure. The governance archetype, succession law, membership law, and resource distribution mechanisms that were appropriate at the previous FPT tier may not be suitable at the new tier. Scale transitions that are not accompanied by internal governance adaptation create **Post-Transition Instability** — a CSS-5 to CSS-6 ongoing consequence event where the faction's new scale exceeds its institutional capacity.

**Rule 21.4.4 — Downward Scale Transition (FPT Decline) Mechanics:** FPT decline is typically faster than FPT advancement and is usually triggered by sudden loss events:
- **Leadership Decapitation:** Loss of the top 1–2 cultivation peaks causes immediate FPT drop of 1–3 tiers depending on how concentrated the power was
- **Resource Severance:** Loss of a critical CPM source, trade route, or territory causes FPT drop of 1–2 tiers within the narrative Section
- **Alliance Collapse:** The loss of a faction alliance that contributed significantly to FPT can cause 1–2 tier drop
- **Information Exposure:** When a Hidden Faction's concealment is broken, their operational security FPT contribution is eliminated immediately

**Rule 21.4.5 — Faction Scale Transitions Are Part 27 Events:** Every FPT advancement or decline is a CSS-4 to CSS-7 consequence event for the changing faction (advancement produces positive CSS; decline produces negative CSS). Every FPT transition is also a consequence event for all factions within FPT-3 of the transitioning faction that share a territory, alliance, rivalry or Section with it — the power landscape has changed in a way that reorganizes the relational and strategic context for all proximate actors. Faction ledgers for all affected factions must be updated after a scale transition event.

**Rule 21.4.6 — Protagonist Faction Scale Transitions Are Hero Arc Events:** When a faction that one or more heroes are affiliated with (a hero is affiliated with a faction when the faction is listed under Faction Affiliation in the hero's Hero Ledger Section 7) transitions FPT tiers, this is an IE event for every affiliated hero. The faction's advancement is a growth milestone for affiliated heroes; the faction's decline is a potential Crucible Event. The faction scale transition must be recorded in every affiliated hero's Hero Ledger Section 7.

## Area 22 — Faction Internal Governance Law

### 22.1 — Internal Governance Archetypes

All factions use one of the following internal governance archetypes, or a hybrid of two or more:

| Archetype | Description | Best Suited For | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autocracy | Single leader holds absolute authority | Highly reactive; fast decisions | Succession crisis; single point of failure |
| Council Oligarchy | Small ruling council; consensus required for major decisions | Stability; balanced power | Slow decision-making; council deadlock |
| Meritocracy | Advancement through demonstrated achievement | High internal performance culture | Constant internal competition; burnout |
| Democracy (Internal) | Major decisions by member vote | High member loyalty; buy-in | Slow; susceptible to demagogues |
| Hereditary Succession | Authority passes by bloodline | Long-term stability; institutional memory | Incompetent successor risk |
| Electoral Appointment | Leader elected by designated electoral body | Legitimacy; regular succession | Electoral manipulation; faction politics |
| Contract-Based Authority | Authority based on mutual contract renewal | Commercially clear; member agency | Fragile; contract non-renewal risk |
| Theocratic Authority | Religious or ideological conviction as authority source | Deep member loyalty | Extremism risk; external conflict |

### 22.2 — Succession Law

**Rule 22.2.1 — Every Faction Must Have a Defined Succession Mechanism:**
Any faction operating at FPT-4 or above that lacks a defined succession mechanism is classified as succession-unstable. Succession instability is the single most common cause of faction collapse in the Starward Saga.

**Rule 22.2.2 — Contested Succession Is a Faction Crisis Event:**
When two or more claimants dispute a faction's leadership succession, the faction enters **Contested Succession State** — all major faction activities are suspended pending resolution; internal conflict may occur; external factions may attempt to exploit the instability.

**Rule 22.2.3 — External Appointment Is a Sovereignty Violation:**
Any outside faction or civilization attempting to appoint a leader to another faction without that faction's internal mechanism is committing a sovereignty violation — a formal justification for defensive military action.

### 22.3 — Membership Law

**Rule 22.3.1 — Membership Has Defined Tiers:**
Every faction with more than 100 members must have defined membership tiers with clear criteria for entry at each tier. Ambiguous membership creates legal disputes about who owes loyalty and who receives protection.

**Rule 22.3.2 — Expulsion Requires Process:**
Expelling a member from a faction at FPT-5 or above requires a formal internal process — charges, hearing, and decision by appropriate authority level. Summary expulsion without process is a governance violation that may be challenged by the expelled member and creates factional reputation damage.

**Rule 22.3.3 — Dual Membership Restrictions:**
Members of one faction may not hold membership in a directly rival faction without permission of both. Most factions above FPT-5 explicitly prohibit dual membership in competing factions in their charter. Exception: Trans-Civilization factions may maintain members from multiple political governments without restriction.

## Area 23 — Faction Territory and Resource Governance

### 23.1 — Territorial Control Law

**Rule 23.1.1 — Territorial Control Requires Active Administration:**
A faction's territory is defined by the space it actively administers and can defend, not by the space it claims. A sect may claim a mountain range but control only the central peak and three subsidiary ridges — its actual territory is those four locations.

**Rule 23.1.2 — Territory Is Expressed in Part 13 Terms:**
All faction territorial claims are expressed in Part 13 cosmological hierarchy terms with Part 14 CMS distance specifications. A faction's "home territory" includes: its primary base (classified per Part 13 location tier), its secondary outposts, and the contiguous area it can respond to within Part 15 operational travel time.

**Rule 23.1.3 — Sacred Ground Territorial Claims (Special):**
Sacred Grounds (Part 13 Area 9) within a faction's territory are classified as primary strategic assets. Control of a Sacred Ground by a faction is a special territorial claim that grants:
- Ambient energy access for faction cultivation advancement
- Formation Array ambient energy bonus (Part 12/Part 14 joint governance)
- Cultivation advancement acceleration for faction members in residence
- Social prestige and recruitment advantage

Disputes over Sacred Ground control are among the most common causes of inter-faction conflict.

### 23.2 — Resource Control and Distribution

**Rule 23.2.1 — Resource Allocation Is a Faction Governance Function:**
How a faction distributes its resources to members — CPM substances, technology equipment, financial compensation, Sacred Ground access time, cultivation manual access — is a primary internal governance function. Unfair or opaque resource distribution is the primary cause of internal faction rebellion and defection.

**Rule 23.2.2 — Strategic Resource Control Defines Faction Power:**
The resources a faction controls define its practical power more than its member count. A faction with five members and a Divine Liquid reserve can be more immediately dangerous than a faction with 50,000 members and Power Crystal access (Resource Control gives at most +1 FPT, Area 21.1). Resource assessment is a required component of FPT calculation.

**Rule 23.2.3 — Resource Transfer Governance:**
All transfers of strategic resources (Core substance or higher, Tier 4 relics, Class 3 Technology-Relic Hybrids, Divine Liquid) between factions are formal governance events requiring documentation and appropriate authority level approval on both sides.

## Area 24 — Inter-Faction Alliance Law

> **Governance boundary — Part 22 Area 24 vs. Part 23 Affiliation Law:**
> Part 22 Area 24 governs the *structural and legal requirements* for inter-faction alliances — the formal prerequisites, charter requirements, obligation frameworks, and violation consequences that make an alliance canonically valid. Part 23 (Affiliation Law) governs the *relational dynamics* of how alliances form, deepen, deteriorate, and end — including the full AFF-1 through AFF-14 spectrum, multi-axis relational profiles, and the cultural and emotional drivers of alliance formation. Both Parts are active simultaneously for any inter-faction alliance event. Part 22 answers "Is this a legally valid alliance?" Part 23 answers "What kind of relationship is this, how strong is it, and where is it going?" For a complete governance picture of any alliance, both Parts must be satisfied.

### 24.1 — Alliance Formation Requirements

A formal inter-faction alliance must satisfy five criteria:

1. **Bilateral Charter:** A written document specifying the alliance's scope, duration, obligations, and termination conditions, ratified by appropriate authority in both factions
2. **Defined Obligation Framework:** What each faction is obligated to provide to the other (military support, resource sharing, intelligence sharing, territorial access) must be explicitly stated
3. **Non-Aggression Clause:** All alliances include an implicit or explicit non-aggression clause — alliances without non-aggression are cooperation arrangements, not alliances
4. **Mutual Recognition of Sovereignty:** Each faction recognizes the other's independent governance and internal decision-making authority
5. **Termination Protocol:** The conditions under which the alliance may be terminated by either party, and the notice period required

### 24.2 — Alliance Categories

| Alliance Type | Scope | Duration | Obligation Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-Aggression Pact | Agreement not to attack each other | Fixed term or indefinite | Minimal — absence of hostility only |
| Mutual Defense Treaty | Obligation to defend each other against external aggression (spectrum position: Part 23, Area 4.3) | Fixed term or indefinite | High — military commitment |
| Resource Sharing Compact | Sharing of defined resources (CPM, territory, knowledge) | Fixed term | Medium — resource-specific |
| Full Alliance | Comprehensive political, military, economic alignment | Indefinite until terminated | Maximal — all domains |
| Subordinate Alliance | Smaller faction formally aligned under a larger faction's protection | Indefinite; power-asymmetric | Medium-High; protection for service |
| Grand Alliance (Multi-Faction) | Three or more factions in formal alliance framework | Fixed or indefinite | Variable by sub-treaty |

### 24.3 — Alliance Violation Governance

**Rule 24.3.1 — Alliance Violation Is a Formal Event:**
Any action by an allied faction that violates the terms of an alliance charter is a formal violation event — it creates an obligation for the violated party to formally declare whether the violation is an act of war, an error requiring compensation, or a trigger for alliance termination.

**Rule 24.3.2 — Alliance Betrayal Consequences:**
A faction that violates a formal alliance without provocation suffers severe reputation consequences in the broader faction ecosystem. Factions known for alliance betrayal find it increasingly difficult to form new alliances. Part 27 (Consequence Law) governs the long-term consequences of alliance betrayal.

### 24.4 — Natural Tensions Between Faction Categories

Some pairs of faction categories pull against each other by their nature, with no villain needed. A writer who needs a political scene can start from one of these pairs and let the pull carry the scene. The pairs are starting points, not laws: the same two categories may be allies in another story, and the Chapter Spec decides what a scene does with them.

| Pair | What each side wants | The pressure point | See |
|---|---|---|---|
| Government Body (GOV) and Cultivator Sect (CS-F) | The government wants its law obeyed and the sect's strength under its authority. The sect wants to teach, recruit and hold its Sacred Ground without leave. | Legitimacy: who may judge a cultivator, who may tax a sect, and whether peak cultivation power is itself a claim to rule (Rule 4.3.2; Cultivation Sovereignty in Area 4.2). | Areas 3, 4 |
| Noble House (NAH) and Government Body (GOV), over succession | The house wants its bloodline or its chosen heir confirmed. The government wants an orderly succession that it controls. | A failed or disputed succession (Rule 4.3.4). An outside appointment is a sovereignty violation (Rule 22.2.3). | Areas 4, 17, 22 |
| Mercantile Faction (MTF) and Criminal or Smuggling Faction (CRF, TSF), over smuggling | The traders want open, safe, taxed routes. The smugglers want secret routes and officials who look away. | One road carries both honest goods and contraband, and the smugglers' legal-face business competes with the traders (Area 15.4). | Areas 10, 15 |
| Religious Faction (RIF) and Academic Faction (ACR), over doctrine | The faith wants its account of the cultivation ladder and the world kept whole. The scholars want to test, publish and revise. | A finding that contradicts doctrine, or a doctrine that forbids a line of research (pathway purity in Cultivation Path Churches, Area 16.2). | Areas 7, 16 |

**Sample intrigue beat** (Government Body and Cultivator Sect; the same four parts fit any pair above). The beat is an illustration, not canon.

- **Goal:** the sect's second elder wants the district magistrate's written leave to hold the sect's autumn trials on the mountain road.
- **Leverage:** the sect's cultivators are the only force that can hold the pass open before the magistrate's tax convoy has to cross it.
- **Hidden card:** the magistrate has already promised the pass to a rival sect's patrons for a gift, and is using the meeting to learn how many cultivators this sect can field.
- **Turn:** the magistrate's clerk reads the draft leave aloud with the rival sect's name in it. The elder says nothing of the slip and asks that the pass be named in writing before witnesses. The magistrate must now grant it and lose his gift, or refuse it in the open and lose his convoy.

Keep the scene in speech and leverage until a hidden card is shown; open conflict needs provocation or declared intent (Rule 25.2.1). The politics must feel lived (N22-2), and each person at the table is loyal for a reason of their own (N22-5).

## Area 25 — Inter-Faction Conflict Law

> **Governance boundary — Part 22 Area 25 vs. Part 23 Affiliation Law:**
> Part 22 Area 25 governs the *formal mechanics and legal classification* of inter-faction conflict — the conflict type categories (Skirmish through Civilizational Conflict), escalation rules, and termination conditions. Part 23 governs the *relational trajectory* that produces conflict — the AFF-9 Cold War through AFF-14 Absorption Campaign spectrum, proxy conflict affiliation dynamics, and the affiliation cascade effects when conflict draws in allied factions. Part 5 (Combat Law) governs the actual combat mechanics. The three-way governance architecture for a faction war is: Part 22 Area 25 (political/legal classification and rules), Part 23 (relational context, alliance cascade, and how the conflict affects the entire affiliation web), Part 5 (individual combat and escalation tier mechanics). All three must be simultaneously satisfied.

### 25.1 — Conflict Categories

| Conflict Type | Trigger | Intensity | Governing Part |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skirmish | Localized territorial dispute; resource contest | E1–E2 (Part 5) | Part 5 for combat; Part 22 for context |
| Raid | Deliberate attack on a specific target for resource seizure | E2–E3 | Part 5 |
| Border War | Sustained territorial contest; repeated engagements | E3–E4 | Parts 5, 22 jointly |
| Formal War Declaration | One faction formally declares armed conflict against another | E6 | Parts 5, 22, 27 |
| Elimination Campaign | One faction declares intent to destroy another completely | E7 | Parts 5, 22, 27, 28 (if Apex involved) |
| Civilizational Conflict | Faction conflict that draws in their host civilizations | E6–E7 | Parts 5, 21, 22, 27 |

### 25.2 — Conflict Escalation Rules

**Rule 25.2.1 — Conflict Requires Provocation or Declared Intent:**
No faction can initiate open conflict without either (a) suffering a clear provocation that justifies defensive response, or (b) formally declaring hostile intent. Unprovoked silent attack without declaration is treated as assassination or criminal activity — not war.

**Rule 25.2.2 — Proxy Conflict Governance:**
When a faction uses a third party or criminal organization to conduct hostile action against another faction, the instigating faction bears the same legal responsibility as if it acted directly. Proxy warfare is a recognized conflict mode and does not shield the instigating faction from war-response.

**Rule 25.2.3 — Conflict Termination Conditions:**
Conflicts between factions end through one of: mutual ceasefire by negotiation; complete defeat and submission of one party; complete destruction of one party; arbitration judgment by recognized Judicial Faction; exhaustion of both parties.

## Area 26 — Faction Absorption, Merger, and Dissolution

### 26.1 — Faction Absorption (Hostile)

**Hostile absorption** occurs when a dominant faction forcibly incorporates another faction's territory, members, and resources.

**Rule 26.1.1 — Absorption Requires Military Defeat:**
Hostile absorption is not complete until the absorbing faction has defeated the absorbed faction's military capability, occupied its territory, and either secured loyalty declarations from or expelled the absorbed faction's leadership.

**Rule 26.1.2 — Absorbed Faction Identity:**
An absorbed faction's identity may be preserved as a sub-faction within the absorbing structure (vassal status) or eliminated entirely (full annexation). Vassal absorption is less stable — absorbed factions may seek to reassert independence at the first opportunity.

### 26.2 — Faction Merger (Voluntary)

**Voluntary merger** occurs when two or more factions agree to unify under a single new charter and identity.

**Rule 26.2.1 — Merger Creates Identity Crisis:**
Even voluntary mergers generate internal conflict because two or more charter traditions must be reconciled into one. Merger failures — where merged factions split again within a generation — are common at FPT-5 and below.

**Rule 26.2.2 — Merger Creates New Faction:**
A merged faction is canonically a **new faction** — not the continuation of either predecessor. This distinction matters for historical claims, inheritance of ancestral relics, and treaty obligations.

### 26.3 — Faction Dissolution

Factions dissolve through one of:

| Dissolution Type | Trigger | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary Dissolution | Charter vote to disband | Members disperse; assets distributed per charter rules |
| Collapse (Internal) | Internal conflict, resource exhaustion, succession failure | Rapid dissolution; members absorbed by other factions or go independent |
| Destruction (External) | Military defeat; total membership loss | May leave behind Remnant Faction |
| Legal Dissolution | Government authority revokes faction's legal charter | Forced; members must reaffiliate |
| Absorption Dissolution | Complete absorption into another faction | Predecessor identity extinguished |

**Rule 26.3.1 — Dissolution Creates Consequence Events:**
Every faction dissolution at FPT-5 or above is a Part 27 consequence event — it reshapes the territorial, resource, and alliance landscape in the dissolved faction's former operational area. Dissolution consequences can be more narratively significant than the faction's existence.

## Area 27 — Faction Formation Law

### 27.1 — Formation Requirements

For a new faction to achieve canonical status at the relevant scale level:

| FPT Target | Minimum at founding (membership grows to the Area 3.4 range) |
|---|---|
| FPT-1 to FPT-3 | Charter declaration; minimum 5 members; defined hierarchy; defined purpose |
| FPT-4 to FPT-5 | Charter declaration; minimum 200 members; physical headquarters; resource base |
| FPT-6 to FPT-7 | Charter; thousands of members; territorial control; government recognition (if within civilization) |
| FPT-8 to FPT-10 | Charter; tens of thousands of members; multi-location infrastructure; FTL capability from FPT-9; resource security |
| FPT-11+ | Charter; millions+ of members; galactic infrastructure; canonical political recognition |

### 27.2 — Formation Events as Narrative Catalysts

Faction formation is one of the most productive narrative events in the Starward Saga. The circumstances of a faction's founding — the need it arose to meet, the founding members' relationships, the charter principles written under the pressure of crisis or vision — define the faction's character for its entire existence.

**Common Formation Catalysts:**
- Sect founder achieves breakthrough to a new realm and gathers disciples
- Government failure or collapse forces former administrators to self-organize
- External threat requires rapid coalition of previously unaligned groups
- Commercial opportunity requires organized institutional structure to exploit
- Ideological revelation motivates group organization around shared belief
- Skilled individuals expelled from an existing faction form their own competing organization

## Area 28 — Faction Narration Standards

### 28.1 — Narration Principles for Faction Scenes

**N22-1 — Introduce Factions with the Usual Three Anchors:**
A first faction appearance usually establishes the following anchors as far as the scene needs them (the Chapter Spec and the scene decide which appear; a missing anchor is reported as information only): (1) **Identity** — the faction's name, type, and general purpose; (2) **Power Signal** — an immediately perceivable signal of the faction's scale and capability; (3) **Tone Character** — the faction's emotional and philosophical character as perceived by the viewpoint character.

**N22-2 — Faction Politics Must Feel Lived:**
Faction political dynamics — internal hierarchy tensions, resource competition, succession anxiety, alliance negotiations, rival faction assessments — must feel like organic features of the world rather than exposition dumps. Characters within factions are products of their faction culture; their assumptions, fears, and ambitions are shaped by their position in the faction hierarchy.

**N22-3 — Scale Must Be Perceptible:**
When depicting a faction at scale, authors make the scale perceptible through specific details — the number of disciples visible in the training hall, the length of a fleet convoy, the breadth of a trade network's storage facility, the depth of an intelligence organization's information network. Abstract scale statements ("the most powerful sect in the galaxy") are less effective than concrete scale signals ("six hundred ships in the harbor, and these were just the defensive garrison").

**N22-4 — Hierarchy Must Be Felt:**
When a character interacts with a faction hierarchy, the hierarchy must be felt — through how people address each other, who defers to whom, who speaks first, who controls the space. Hierarchy is not just institutional structure; it is a constantly performed social reality.

**N22-5 — Faction Loyalty Is Earned, Not Assumed:**
Authors never assume that faction membership equals loyalty. Members of the same faction may be loyal for entirely different reasons — belief in the mission, fear of the leadership, personal loyalty to a specific individual within the faction, commercial interest, or simple inertia. A faction's most important narrative question is often: what holds these people together?

**N22-6 — Faction Conflict Reveals Character:**
How a character navigates faction conflict — internal disputes, rival faction provocations, betrayal by fellow members, loyalty to faction versus loyalty to personal relationships — is one of the primary character revelation engines in the Starward Saga.

**N22-7 — Faction Type Determines Cultural Norms:**
Each faction category carries characteristic cultural norms that govern daily life within the faction. A cultivator sect has a different social texture than a mercantile trading house or a criminal syndicate. Authors ensure the daily texture of faction life is category-appropriate.

**N22-8 — FPT Gap Is Usually Dramatized:**
When characters from factions with a large FPT gap interact, the power asymmetry is usually dramatized (guidance: a gap of about three or more FPT tiers); the Chapter Spec decides how, and the gap is information only. The representative of a FPT-10 faction visiting a FPT-3 faction is an event that the FPT-3 faction's members feel existentially — not because they are told the gap, but because they can see, sense, and feel the gap in the visitor's casual exercise of power.

## Area 29 — Faction-Civilization Boundary Governance

### 29.1 — When Does a Faction Become a Civilization?

The boundary between a very powerful faction and a small civilization is not absolute — it is a governance threshold determined by four criteria:

**The Four Civilization Threshold Criteria:**
1. **Sovereign Authority Claim:** The entity claims legal sovereignty over a defined territory and enforces it
2. **Self-Sufficient Governance:** The entity administers law, collects taxes, provides public services, and maintains internal order without reference to external authority
3. **Recognized Independence:** Other civilizations or major factions recognize the entity as an independent political unit
4. **Self-Reproducing Structure:** The entity has mechanisms for reproducing its membership, infrastructure, and governance across generations without external dependency

A faction that satisfies all four criteria is functionally a civilization and should be classified using Part 21 CCT assessment in addition to Part 22 FPT classification.

**Rule 29.1.1 — Dual Classification:**
Some entities are legitimately both — a faction that is also a civilization in its own right. The Starward Saga treats these as dual-classified entities: Part 21 CCT for civilization-layer governance; Part 22 FPT for faction-layer governance.

**Rule 29.1.2 — FPT-to-CCT Reclassification Threshold:**
The quantitative threshold that typically accompanies the four qualitative criteria is an FPT-CCT equivalence trigger: when a faction's FPT equals or exceeds the CCT level that would be assigned to the territory it administers if that territory were assessed as an independent civilization, the reclassification pressure is complete. In practical terms, this means:

| Transition Type | Trigger Condition |
|---|---|
| Faction → Civilization (formal reclassification) | All four qualitative criteria met AND FPT ≥ the CCT equivalent of the governed territory |
| Faction operating as de facto civilization (dual-classified) | All four qualitative criteria met but FPT < the CCT equivalent — entity governs like a civilization but lacks the resource/cultivation depth to be assessed as one |
| State-within-a-state (Rule 21.3.2) | Faction FPT more than one FPT row above the row whose scale matches the civilization's territory (its LA) — faction has outgrown the containing civilization but not formally separated (pre-reclassification tension state) |

The "state-within-a-state" tension state (Rule 21.3.2) is the canonical pre-reclassification condition. A faction in this state will either: (a) formalize as an independent civilization; (b) fragment the containing civilization; or (c) be suppressed back into compliance. All three outcomes are Part 27 Consequence Law events.

> **Governance boundary clarification:** The CCT assessment that determines a reclassifying faction's civilization tier is governed entirely by Part 21 Areas 2 to 5. Part 22 governs only whether the four qualitative criteria are met and whether FPT ≥ CCT equivalent threshold. Part 21 governs what CCT tier is assigned once reclassification occurs.

### 29.2 — When a Civilization Fragments Into Factions

Civilizational collapse, political fragmentation, and civil war often produce the reverse transition: what was a single civilization becomes multiple competing factions. This fragmentation event is governed by Part 27 (Consequence Law) for the civilizational collapse event and by Part 22 for the resulting faction dynamics.
