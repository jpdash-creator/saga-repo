## Overview

Part 33 is the Starward Saga Canon's Technology Weapon Law — the governing domain for every technology weapon in the setting: what a weapon is, how it is classified, who may operate it, what it can destroy, what powers it, what it costs, and how its use intersects combat law, civilization governance, and narrative standards. Part 8's Type 8 Military & Defense Technology domain is the authoritative source for weapon tier assignments; Part 33 builds the complete weapon governance architecture on top of that source — from a Tier 1 Shard-Thrower to a Tier 20 Existence Hammer.

From its classification foundations, Part 33 builds outward across 18 Areas: the classification system of paradigm bands, scale classes, configuration codes, and tier bands (Area 1); the seven-type weapon taxonomy with named sub-types from Tier 1 through Tier 20 (Area 2); the six weapon user classes and civilization-tier accessibility rules (Area 3); the master weapon tier reference table mapping every tier to its Part 8 source level and cultivation defense threshold (Area 4); the five damage vectors and target-type efficiency multipliers (Area 5); the three modes of weapon-cultivation interaction and cultivation weapon augmentation (Area 6); weapon energy classes, CPM fuel grades, and ammunition governance (Area 7); multi-domain construction prerequisites and prototype governance (Area 8); vehicle-mounted weapon systems under joint Part 32/33 governance including CHV soul-interface weapons (Area 9); deployment authorization hierarchy, maintenance doctrine, and arms transfer law (Area 10); the interface with Part 5 combat and escalation law (Area 11); autonomous weapons governance (Area 12); Absolute Prohibition and Restricted weapon categories (Area 13); weapon market tiers, valuation, and stockpile-as-advancement-indicator rules (Area 14); weapon culture and the prestige scale (Area 15); weapon narration standards (Area 16); the full cross-Part dependency master table (Area 17); and domain ownership disambiguation (Area 18).

## Area 1 — Weapon Classification System

### 1.1 — The Five Weapon Deployment Paradigm Bands

All weapons in the Starward Saga are classified within one of five weapon deployment paradigm bands that correspond to the five technology transport paradigm bands defined in Part 32, Area 1.1, and the five technology travel paradigms in Part 15, Area 8.1. The weapon paradigm band is the primary organizational category — it establishes the scale of operation, theater of deployment, and physics context for the weapon.

| Paradigm Band | Designation | Technology Tier Range | Operational Theater |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface Warfare Band | TW-Band | Tiers 1–5 | Planetary surface, atmosphere, ocean floor; no sustained orbital or space operation |
| Orbital and Inner-System Warfare Band | OW-Band | Tiers 5–7 | Orbit to stellar inner system; subluminal inter-planetary conflict; no FTL deployment |
| Interstellar Warfare Band | IW-Band | Tiers 8–10 | Between star systems; FTL-delivered or FTL-range weapons; gate-launched ordnance |
| Galactic Warfare Band | GW-Band | Tiers 11–14 | Galaxy-scale and intergalactic theaters; high-FTL and gate-network weapon delivery |
| Cosmological Warfare Band | CW-Band | Tiers 15–20 | Universe-scale and beyond; partially deferred scope; existential-class instruments |

**Cross-Band Note:** Some weapons are designed to be deployed *from* one band against targets in another band. A Tier 8 interstellar-range weapon deployed from an orbital platform is still classified in the IW-Band by construction tier; its deployment context falls under OW-Band operational rules. The construction tier determines band classification; operational employment determines which band's deployment rules apply.

### 1.2 — Weapon Scale Class

Within each paradigm band, weapons are further classified by their **Weapon Scale Class** — an independent dimension from tier that defines the spatial footprint of the weapon's destructive effect and the target magnitude it is designed to affect. Weapon Scale Class uses a universal seven-level designation system (WS-I through WS-VII) applicable across all bands.

| Scale Class | Designation | Effect Footprint | Primary Target Magnitude | Escalation Tier Implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WS-I | Precision Strike | Centimeter to meter radius | Individual combatant or single object | See Area 11.2 |
| WS-II | Squad Suppression | Meter to 10-meter radius | Small group (2–20 individuals) or light structure | See Area 11.2 |
| WS-III | Area Denial | 10 m to 1 km radius | Military unit (20–500 combatants), fortifications, vehicles | See Area 11.2 |
| WS-IV | Siege Class | 1 km to 100 km radius | City-level infrastructure, fleet action zones, mountain-scale terrain | See Area 11.2 |
| WS-V | Continental Class | 100 km to planetary radius | Continent-level destruction, ecological disruption, planetary atmosphere | See Area 11.2 |
| WS-VI | Planetary Class | Planet to stellar system radius | Full planet sterilization, moon destruction, planetary orbit disruption | See Area 11.2; Absolute Authorization Protocol required |
| WS-VII | Existential Class | Stellar scale and beyond | Star system annihilation, galactic topological disruption, multi-galactic events | See Area 11.2; Absolute Authorization Protocol required |

**Important:** WS class is independent of weapon tier. A Tier 3 hand-grenade equivalent is WS-II; a Tier 3 strategic aerial carpet-bomb payload is WS-IV. A Tier 11 precision soul-targeting weapon may be WS-I if designed as a single-target instrument. WS classification follows the weapon's designed effect footprint, not the civilization tier that built it.

## Area 2 — Weapon Type Taxonomy

### 2.1 — Primary Weapon Type Categories

All weapons are classified into one of seven primary weapon type categories. Type determines the weapon's operational mechanism, energy requirement, cultivation interaction properties, and countermeasure profile. Types are mutually exclusive at the primary classification level; a weapon with multiple delivery mechanisms receives a primary type and one or more secondary type modifiers (e.g., *Lancefire-6/ShieldPierce* for a directed-energy weapon with a penetrating formation element).

| Type Code | Type Name | Mechanism | Cultivation Interaction Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-KIN | Kinetic Strike Type | Physical mass delivery — ballistic, railgun, mass driver, orbital kinetic | CIC-I (Physical) |
| T-RAY | Directed Ray Type | Focused energy beam delivery — laser, particle beam, plasma jet, maser | CIC-II (Energetic) |
| T-BURST | Detonation Burst Type | Explosive energy release at or near target — chemical, nuclear, antimatter, vacuum collapse | CIC-III (Area Energy) |
| T-FORM | Formation Array Type | Cultivation formation-matrix-derived weapon effect — qi distortion, field suppression, void collapse | CIC-IV (Spiritual Field) |
| T-SYN | Synergy Strike Type | Cultivation-technology hybrid weapon; requires SYN configuration; integrated practitioner activation | CIC-V (Dual Track) |
| T-PSI | Consciousness Targeting Type | Weapons that target soul architecture, mental continuity, or spiritual substrate rather than physical body | CIC-VI (Soul) |
| T-VEC | Vector Payload Type | Weapons that deliver a payload agent (biological vector, nanite swarm, CPM contaminator, ecosystem disruptor) | CIC-VII (Systemic) |

**Type Note:** Weapons with no cultivation interaction capability are CIC-I (physical) by default, as the weapon delivers purely physical force that cultivation defense must resist through raw physical blocking.

### 2.2 — Kinetic Strike Type (T-KIN) — Full Taxonomy

Kinetic Strike weapons deliver destructive force through the physical impact of mass. All weapons of this type obey the same underlying physics: kinetic energy scales with mass and the square of velocity. At technology Tier 7+, kinetic weapons begin to enter relativistic velocity regimes where relativistic mass increase becomes significant.

**Named Kinetic Strike Sub-Types (by tier range):**

|---|---|---|---|
| Shard-Thrower | Tier 1 | Stone, bone, or early metal projectile propelled by muscle power; slings, bows | WS-I |
| Iron-Lance | Tier 2 | Metal crossbow bolt, arrow, early trebuchet stone, spear-thrower | WS-I–II |
| Powder-Shot | Tier 3 | Gunpowder-propelled solid or fragmenting round; cannon, musket, rifle, artillery shell | WS-I–III |
| Hardstrike Round | Tier 4 | Modern firearms round; tank penetrator; guided kinetic munition; bunker-buster | WS-I–III |
| Orbital Impactor | Tier 5–6 | Tungsten rod or mass-driver projectile deployed from orbital altitude; early rods-from-god equivalent | WS-III–IV |
| Void Hammer | Tier 7 | Relativistic kinetic bombardment — mass accelerated to 0.1–0.5c, targeted from stellar system distances | WS-IV–V |
| Star-Lance | Tier 8–9 | Multi-stellar relativistic payload; mass accelerated to 0.5–0.99c; capable of planetary core penetration | WS-V–VI |
| Event Spike | Tier 10 | Near-lightspeed compressed mass payload with gravitational focus; pierces planetary magnetic fields; effective against stellar targets | WS-VI |

Directed Ray weapons deliver focused energy in a directional beam — laser light, charged particle stream, superheated plasma jet, or similar directed-energy mechanism. They are characterized by light-speed or near-light-speed delivery (for laser/EM weapons), high precision, and the ability to penetrate soft targets without explosive area effect.

Detonation Burst weapons release large quantities of energy at or near a target through controlled or uncontrolled energetic reactions. They are characterized by area effect, omnidirectional energy release, and thermal/concussive/radiation delivery. This type includes chemical explosives, nuclear and thermonuclear devices, antimatter detonation, and exotic matter collapse events.

### 2.5 — Formation Array Type (T-FORM) — Full Taxonomy

Formation Array weapons operate through cultivation formation matrices embedded in technology platforms. The weapon effect is spiritual-physical rather than purely energetic — it manipulates qi fields, disrupts spiritual substrate, creates vacuum voids in ambient qi, or projects formation-defined force shapes at targets. T-FORM weapons are jointly governed by Part 12 (formation array rules) and Part 33.

| Void-Field Projector | Tier 5–6 | Creates localized void of qi; all qi-dependent techniques fail inside field boundary; suppression weapon | WS-II–IV |

| Primordium Splitter | Tier 11–14 | Formation weapon targeting the Primordium-level substrate; reality distortion in targeted zones | WS-VI–VII |
| Absolute Formation Weapon | Tier 15–20 | Deferred — formation weapons at cosmological tier; absolute-field disruption | WS-VII |

**Cultivation Interaction Note:** Formation Array weapons interact directly with cultivation at every tier. They are the primary weapon class that can harm cultivators through spiritual rather than physical mechanisms. Higher-tier cultivation defense trained specifically for qi-disruption is more effective against T-FORM than against T-KIN weapons of the same tier.

### 2.7 — Consciousness Targeting Type (T-PSI) — Full Taxonomy

Consciousness Targeting weapons are designed to attack the soul, mental continuity, or spiritual substrate of a target rather than its physical body. They do not necessarily produce physical destruction — a perfectly executed T-PSI weapon hit may leave the target's body physically unharmed while inflicting soul damage, memory erasure, spiritual fragmentation, or consciousness severance. These weapons are among the most tightly governed in the Starward Saga Canon and require special authorization above WS-II.

**Named Consciousness Targeting Sub-Types:**

| Sub-Type Name | Tier Range | Mechanism | Governance Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memory-Wipe Pulse | Tier 4–5 | Electrical/magnetic disruption of neural architecture; temporary to permanent memory loss at close range | Standard (WS-I) |
| Soul-Sting Array | Tier 5–6 | Formation array component tuned to spiritual substrate resonance; causes pain-state or confusion in cultivators without physical injury | Restricted (WS-I–II) |
| Spirit-Fracture Beam | Tier 6–7 | Targeted soul-layer disruption; does not damage Core but disrupts soul-body connection; cultivation techniques disabled for duration | Restricted (WS-I–II) |
| Core-Crack Projector | Tier 8–9 | Weapon capable of directly attacking cultivation Core structure via combined spiritual-technology interface; extreme range with precision targeting | High Restriction (WS-I) |
| Soul-Sever Lance | Tier 9–10 | Weapon designed to sever soul from physical body without killing the body; most feared weapon class among cultivators; use classified as extreme war crime under major faction codes | Absolute Restriction |
| Consciousness Erase Engine | Tier 11–14 | Mass-scale soul architecture disruption; entire planetary population can be consciousness-targeted simultaneously; civilizational-scale soul weapon | Part 21 / E7 governance only |

**Critical Governance Rule (Rule 33.2.1 — T-PSI Deployment Authorization):** All T-PSI weapons above WS-I require special authorization from the deploying faction's governing body (Part 22 governance). Soul-Sever Lance class weapons are considered an extreme war crime and will trigger E6+ escalation response from third-party civilizations under Part 21 civilization protection law regardless of faction alignment. Their use is a Part 21 civilization-level political event, not merely a battlefield one.

### 2.8 — Vector Payload Type (T-VEC) — Full Taxonomy

Vector Payload weapons deliver a payload agent rather than direct energy. The destructive mechanism is the agent — a biological organism, a nanite swarm, a CPM contaminant, an ecosystem disruptor, or an engineered cognitive vector. T-VEC weapons are the most heavily restricted class under Part 33 governance due to their uncontrolled propagation potential.

**Named Vector Payload Sub-Types:**

| Sub-Type Name | Tier Range | Mechanism | Governance Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plague Vial | Tier 3–4 | Biological pathogen delivery weapon; early battlefield disease vector; targeted or area-deployed | Special Authorization (WS-III+) |
| Cultivator-Bane Toxin | Tier 5–6 | Engineered biological agent specifically targeting meridian/qi-flow disruption; blocks cultivation temporarily or permanently | Faction High Authorization |
| Nano-Corrosive Swarm | Tier 5–7 | Nanite swarm delivered to target area; breaks down specific materials, blocks cultivation infrastructure, corrodes technology | Technology Authorization |
| CPM Poisoner | Tier 6–8 | Delivers CPM-contaminating agent into target CPM stockpiles or fuel systems; corrupts or destabilizes CPM at grade-level | Special Authorization |
| Ecosystem Eradicator | Tier 7–8 | Engineered biological vector targeting planetary biosphere; mass extinction-rate ecosystem disruption | Strategic Ordnance (WS-V+) |
| Cognitive Vector | Tier 8–10 | Engineered vector delivering artificial spiritual-substrate modification to target cultivator population; alters cultivation trajectory, loyalty architecture, or soul-alignment | Absolute Restriction |
| Reality Corruptor | Tier 11+ | Agents that corrupt the local physical substrate; alters local constants in target area; permanent area denial | Deferred — Absolute Restriction |

**Governance Rule (Rule 33.2.2 — T-VEC Propagation Liability):** T-VEC weapons that spread beyond their intended deployment zone create unlimited escalation liability for the deploying party. If a biological, nanite, or cognitive vector spreads to neutral parties or protected zones, the deployer assumes full escalation consequences under Part 5's E5+ escalation law regardless of intent. There is no "accidental deployment" defense for T-VEC weapon use above WS-III.

## Area 3 — Weapon User Classification

### 3.1 — The Six Weapon User Classes

All weapon users are classified into one of six Weapon User Classes that determine which weapons they may legally operate, what training prerequisites apply, and how their weapon use is classified under Part 5's combat framework.

| User Class | Designation | Who Qualifies | Maximum Authorized Weapon Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| WU-I | Individual Operator | Single cultivator or technology practitioner; unaugmented individual | WS-I–II standard; WS-III with Individual Heavy Weapon certification |
| WU-II | Crew-Operated Platform | Multi-operator team (2–20 operators); crewed vehicles at SC-I through SC-III; emplacement crews | WS-II–IV |

## Area 5 — Weapon Damage Architecture

### 5.1 — The Five Damage Vectors

Every weapon delivers destructive effect through one or more of five Damage Vectors. The damage vector determines what type of damage the weapon inflicts, what defensive countermeasures are relevant, and how the damage interacts with cultivation healing and technology repair.

| Vector | Designation | Damage Nature | Cultivation Healing Applicable? | Technology Repair Applicable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DV-I | Kinetic-Structural | Physical mass displacement; bone fracture, organ disruption, structural collapse | Yes (for biological targets); dependent on realm for speed | No (for biological); yes for technology objects |
| DV-II | Thermal-Radiant | Heat and radiation delivery; burns, cellular disruption, material fusion | Yes (partial — internal qi circulation needed for radiation); yes for technology shielding repair | Yes |
| DV-III | Electromagnetic | Electrical, magnetic, or EM-spectrum disruption; neural disruption, technology system damage | Limited (nerve damage); yes for technology systems | Yes — specialized EM shielding repair |
| DV-IV | Spiritual-Field | Qi field disruption, soul architecture damage, formation disassembly | Cultivation healing only; technology repair inapplicable | No — requires cultivation restoration |
| DV-V | Systemic | Propagating damage through host vector; biological, nanite, cognitive — spreads beyond initial delivery | Specialized cultivation healing (meridian purge required); specialized antidote technology | Limited (containment possible but rarely reversal) |

### 5.3 — Stacking Damage Vector Rules

A weapon may deliver multiple damage vectors simultaneously. When multiple vectors stack on a target:

1. Each vector is assessed independently against the target's defenses.
2. Secondary vectors may find gaps in defenses that the primary vector cannot exploit (e.g., EM damage to a cultivator whose formation defense blocks DV-I but not DV-III).
3. Spiritual-field damage (DV-IV) from T-FORM or T-PSI weapons stacks additively with physical damage and cannot be blocked by the same defense that blocks DV-I.
4. Systemic damage (DV-V) once initiated propagates independently and cannot be stopped by destroying the weapon after delivery.

## Area 6 — Weapon-Cultivation Interaction Law

### 6.1 — The Cultivation Defense Threshold System

**Rule 33.6.1 — The Three Modes:** Technology weapons interact with cultivation in two fundamentally different modes depending on the weapon tier relative to the defender's realm, and a third mode (Mode C) applies from Tier 9 upward. At Tier 8, where Table 4.1 shows cultivation defense failing at WS-II and above, the defender is in Mode B.

**Mode A — Cultivation Defense Superior:** When the weapon tier's force category equivalent is below the cultivator's realm (as per Area 4.1 table), the cultivator's cultivation defense provides meaningful protection. Cultivation body hardening, defensive qi circulation, formation shields, and aura barriers all reduce damage. The cultivator may be able to withstand WS-I and WS-II impacts at this weapon tier through cultivation defense alone.

**Mode B — Technology Weapon Superior:** When the weapon tier's force category equivalent meets or exceeds the cultivator's realm, cultivation defense is insufficient to meaningfully resist the weapon's output. Individual defensive techniques slow the damage but cannot prevent it. At WS-III and above in Mode B, cultivation defense is largely irrelevant — the weapon delivers energy that exceeds what the cultivation system can process, circulate away, or transform into defensive force.

**Mode C — Complete Defense Failure:** From Tier 9 upward (Low Core Force Category equivalent and higher), all cultivation defensive capacity fails entirely regardless of realm. No cultivator in the existing realm ladder can defensively survive a direct hit from a Tier 9+ weapon in Mode C. The only viable defense is dedicated technology shielding at an appropriate tier level.

**Rule 33.6.2 — The Tier 3 Fight Table:** The table below says what a hit from a Tier 3 weapon does to a defender of each group. It does not say who wins a fight: the confirmed Chapter Spec decides that (Part 5 Area 7.7). It is a guide post for how the hit is written.

**Reading the words.** *Shrugs off*: the hit leaves no lasting harm and the defender keeps fighting. *Wounded*: the hit harms the defender (a vital hit still kills an unprotected body) and the defender can still act or withdraw, at a cost. *Overrun*: the defender cannot hold against the weapon at that scale; the defender is killed, disabled or forced to break.

**Reading the rows.** WS-I and WS-II rows describe one hit from one weapon (a ball, a grenade). The WS-III row describes a field gun or a large mine, and a massed volley of WS-I weapons is read at this row because it denies an area. The WS-IV row describes a siege barrage or an aerial bombing run (Part 33 Area 1.2).

| Weapon class (Tier 3) | Mortal Tier 1 | Mortal Tier 2 Senior to Warrior | Champion to Ruler |
|---|---|---|---|
| WS-I and WS-II (musket, rifle, grenade, small mine) | Wounded | Wounded | Shrugs off |
| WS-III (field gun, large mine; a massed volley is read at this row) | Overrun | Overrun | Wounded |
| WS-IV (siege barrage; a bombing run only if a Chapter Spec places aircraft) | Overrun | Overrun | Overrun |

**Tier 4 reading.** For a Tier 4 weapon read each column one group to the left: Champion to Ruler takes the Senior-to-Warrior result, Senior to Warrior takes the Mortal Tier 1 result, and Mortal Tier 1 stays as it is.

### 6.3 — Cultivation Weapon Augmentation

Cultivators can augment technology weapons through cultivation energy injection, improving weapon performance above its baseline. This falls under joint governance with Part 31 (Synergy) and Part 11 (CPM energy).

**Rule 33.6.3 — Augmentation Ceiling:** A cultivator may augment a technology weapon's output up to a maximum of +1 weapon tier equivalent through direct qi injection, provided the cultivator's realm Force Category equivalent meets or exceeds the weapon's current tier. A Tier 5 weapon augmented by a cultivator whose Force Category equivalent meets Tier 5 functions as a Tier 6 weapon for output purposes but remains a Tier 5 weapon for all classification purposes.

**Rule 33.10.2 — Maintenance Competency Requirement:** Maintaining a weapon requires technology practitioners whose individual skill tier meets or exceeds the weapon's construction tier. A Tier 8 weapon cannot be maintained by a civilization operating at Tier 5 technology — the maintenance will be inadequate regardless of effort.

### 11.3 — Weapon vs. Cultivation Technique — Part 5 Priority Adjudication

When a cultivator is struck by a technology weapon and simultaneously uses a defensive cultivation technique, Part 5 adjudication occurs in the following order:

1. **Cultivation defense force category comparison first:** Is the weapon's force category below, equal to, or above the cultivator's realm?
2. **Damage vector assessment:** Which DV vectors does the weapon deliver, and which is the cultivator's defense aligned against?
3. **WS class vs. cultivation aura spatial footprint:** If the weapon's WS radius exceeds the cultivator's aura radius (Part 3, Area 20 table), the cultivator cannot defend using aura alone.
4. **Outcome adjudication:** If weapon tier force category exceeds cultivation realm AND WS class exceeds aura footprint → cultivation defense provides no meaningful protection. Otherwise, cultivation defense reduces damage proportionally.

## Area 12 — Autonomous Weapons Governance

### 12.1 — Autonomous Weapon Classification

Autonomous weapons are technology weapons that operate with self-directed targeting and firing capability without real-time operator control. They are classified by their autonomy tier and governed jointly by Part 33 and Part 8's Type 4 AI domain.

| Autonomy Tier | Designation | Control Architecture | Maximum Authorized Deployment |
|---|---|---|---|
| AT-1 | Semi-Autonomous | Operator defines target envelope; weapon selects specific targets within envelope | WS-I–II; all escalation tiers |
| AT-2 | Mission-Autonomous | Operator defines mission parameters; weapon autonomously executes including target selection | WS-I–III; E3 maximum without special authorization |
| AT-3 | Theater-Autonomous | Weapon system autonomously defines its own target priorities within theater | WS-I–IV; E4 maximum; faction authorization required |
| AT-4 | Strategic-Autonomous | Fully independent strategic decision-making; no real-time human control | WS-IV+; E5 minimum; civilization authorization required |

**Rule 33.12.1 — Autonomous Weapon Accountability:** All damage caused by autonomous weapons is attributed to the deploying faction regardless of operator intent or weapon malfunction. There is no "rogue weapon" defense under Part 33 / Part 22 governance. Deploying faction bears full accountability for all autonomous weapon actions.

**Rule 33.12.2 — AT-4 Strategic Autonomous Weapons:** Strategic-autonomous weapons capable of self-directed strategic decision-making are classified as existential-risk objects under Part 21 civilization governance. Their creation, deployment, and storage are civilization-level governance events. A faction that creates AT-4 weapons above WS-V without civilization authorization has committed a civilization-level violation.

## Area 13 — Restricted Weapons Governance

### 13.1 — Absolute Prohibition Weapons

The following weapon categories are classified as Absolute Prohibition weapons across all major factions and civilizations in the Starward Saga. Their production, storage, trade, or deployment constitutes a civilization-level war crime regardless of context:

1. **Soul-Sever Class weapons** (T-PSI, WS-I+) — any weapon designed to sever soul from physical body while leaving the body alive.
2. **Cognitive Vector weapons** (T-VEC Cognitive Vector sub-type) — weapons designed to permanently alter spiritual alignment, cultivation trajectory, or soul-level loyalty architecture.
3. **Consciousness Erase Engine** (T-PSI WS-VI) — mass-scale consciousness destruction weapons.
4. **Reality Corruptors** (T-VEC Tier 11+) — weapons that permanently alter local physical constants.
5. **Stellar Igniter class** (T-BURST Tier 9–10) — weapons specifically designed to trigger stellar collapse, deployed without evacuation protocol in inhabited systems.

**Rule 33.13.1 — Absolute Prohibition Enforcement:** Evidence of production, storage, or use of Absolute Prohibition weapons triggers mandatory civilization-level response under Part 21's collective security framework. Any faction possessing evidence of an Absolute Prohibition weapon is legally authorized to notify the relevant civilization authority and request E6-level military response, regardless of peacetime treaties.

### 13.2 — Restricted Weapons

The following weapon categories are Restricted — permissible only with special multi-level authorization and only in defined conflict contexts:

1. T-PSI weapons above WS-I (all soul-targeting weapons except Memory-Wipe Pulse in non-combat contexts).
2. T-VEC Plague Vial and Cultivator-Bane Toxin above WS-II.
3. T-BURST Stellar Igniter class — permissible only with civilization authorization and mandatory affected-party notification protocol.
4. T-VEC Ecosystem Eradicator — permissible only in uninhabited planetary targets with written Part 21 authorization.
5. AT-4 Strategic-Autonomous weapons — faction-level restriction; civilization authorization required.

## Area 14 — Weapon Economic Law

### 14.1 — Weapon Market Classification

Technology weapons are classified into three weapon market tiers that define their economic governance:

| Market Tier | Designation | Available To | Governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| WM-I | Open Market | Any individual, faction, or civilization meeting technology tier prerequisites | Commerce law; standard Part 22 trade rules |
| WM-II | Faction Market | Military-authorized buyers only; requires faction-level purchase authorization | Part 22 military procurement law; classified transaction records |
| WM-III | Civilization Strategic Market | Civilization-level buyers only; multi-civilization treaties governing stockpile limits | Part 21 arms control governance; treaty-bound |

**WM-I Open Market Weapons:** Tier 1–4 weapons across all non-restricted types are open market items in most civilizations. Personal defense weapons, hunting arms, and light commercial security systems operate in open markets.

**WM-II Faction Market Weapons:** Tier 5+ weapons and all vehicle-hardpoint systems (VHM configuration) require faction-level authorization and enter classified procurement channels. Unauthorized purchase of WM-II weapons is a Part 22 faction violation.

**WM-III Civilization Strategic Market:** WS-V+ strategic ordnance, Absolute Prohibition weapons (when illegally traded), and all WS-VII systems operate (legally) only within civilization-to-civilization treaty frameworks. Weapons in this tier are not traded — they are manufactured, stored, and deployed under civilization-sovereign authority.

### 14.2 — Weapon Valuation Framework

**Rule 33.14.1 — Weapon Value Scaling:** Technology weapon value scales geometrically with tier, following the same geometric law as civilization tier advancement. A Tier 7 weapon is not simply twice the value of a Tier 6 weapon — it represents the entire industrial and scientific civilization infrastructure that makes it possible.

**Rule 33.14.2 — Arms Trade and Faction Economics:** Arms trade between factions of different technology tiers is a major economic event under Part 22. A lower-tier faction purchasing Tier 7 weapons from a higher-tier faction acquires a strategic asset it cannot reproduce or maintain — this creates permanent dependency on the supplier faction and is a recognized form of political leverage.

### 14.3 — Weapon Stockpile as Advancement Indicator

A faction or civilization's weapon stockpile composition is a Part 9 Technology Growth Law advancement indicator. The presence of:

- Tier 5+ weapons in a faction arsenal signals TB-Gamma civilization minimum.
- Tier 8 IW-Band weapons signal IW-Band capability milestone (Part 9 reference cross-point with Part 32's IS-Band fleet indicator).
- WS-VI civilization-class weapons signal Tier 8+ civilization floor.
- Operational WS-VII weapons signal Tier 9+ floor (Stellar Igniter) or extraordinary artifact possession.

## Area 16 — Weapon Narration Standards

### N33-1 — Weapon Tier Grounding

Every technology weapon in prose must be grounded in its tier band. A Tier 3 firearm does not operate with Tier 7 precision targeting. A Tier 6 orbital cannon does not appear on a primitive planet without establishing how it arrived and who maintains it. Weapon presence always implies weapon ecosystem (Area 7 — supply, maintenance, operator training).

### N33-2 — Damage Vector Consistency

When a weapon inflicts damage in prose, the description must be consistent with the weapon's damage vector profile. A T-KIN kinetic weapon creates physical displacement damage — not radiant burns unless the kinetic energy converts to heat (which happens at high velocity and requires explicit acknowledgment). A T-PSI weapon does not create physical wounds unless a DV-I secondary vector is present. Prose damage must match weapon type.

### N33-3 — Cultivation Defense Rendering

When a cultivator resists a technology weapon, the prose must reflect the weapon tier's force category relative to the cultivator's realm (Area 6.1 — Modes A, B, C). If the weapon is in Mode B (weapon superior), the cultivator should be visibly struggling against overwhelming technological force even if their cultivation is formidable. If in Mode C (complete defense failure), cultivation defense attempting to block a Tier 9+ weapon should read as futile.

*Technique note:* show the futility through what the defense does and does not do. The guard rises on the right line at the right moment, and the weapon's output goes through the place where it stood as if nothing were there. Take the image from the character's own craft (their school's shield form, the pattern of their affinity) so that the failure belongs to that character, and do not reach for a stock comparison.

**Worked example (Mode A and Mode B, about 120 words, model only; the wording is not to be reused):**

> The first volley came from the ridge, and the captain let it come. Rounds struck his chest and shoulder and flattened against the qi hardening on his skin, and he walked on through the dust with his jaw set, hurt only by the noise. Then the beam found him. It did not strike; it stayed, a white line on his raised forearm, and the hardening there went red, then raw, and began to peel back in strips. His guard slowed the burn but did not stop it; the heat went on through the hardening and into the meat of the arm. He threw himself behind the broken wall, one sleeve smoking, and for the first time that day he stopped walking forward.

The captain is a Mortal Tier 2 Ruler. The first volley is rifle fire, a Tier 3 weapon below his realm, so the hardening holds and the cost is small (Mode A). The beam is a Tier 5 directed-energy weapon, above his realm, so the guard only slows the burn (Mode B). A scene chooses its own weapons, realm and words.

### N33-4 — Weapon Scale Class Environmental Description

Weapon WS class determines the environmental description scope. WS-I impacts are personal in scale — a hit on one person or one object. WS-V impacts restructure landscapes — authors must describe continental-scale environmental consequences, not merely individual casualties. Each WS class requires description at its actual scale or the weapon's power is narratively undermined.

### N33-5 — Weapon Depletion Realism

Magazine-governed weapons deplete ammunition. Authors must track weapon shots in combat sequences if the weapon's finite magazine is narratively relevant. Characters should experience ammunition limits as genuine tactical constraints, not forgotten inconveniences. Energy-beam weapons (WE-IV) sourced from continuous power plants have operational endurance determined by power plant capacity — not unlimited fire.

### N33-6 — Autonomous Weapon Narration

Autonomous weapons (AT-1 through AT-4) should be narratively distinguished from operator-controlled weapons. AT-3 and AT-4 weapons make their own decisions — their targeting choices should sometimes be surprising, suboptimal, or chillingly efficient in ways that human operators would not replicate. The autonomous nature of the weapon should be narratively present, not invisible.

### N33-7 — Weapon-Cultivation Interaction as Narrative Tension

The threshold where cultivation defense shifts from Mode A (cultivation superior) to Mode B (technology superior) to Mode C (complete failure) is a primary source of narrative tension in the Starward Saga. Authors should exploit this threshold deliberately. A cultivator facing a Tier 6 weapon for the first time and discovering their realm cannot fully block it is a major narrative moment — it represents the boundary where cultivation's individual supremacy ends and civilization-scale technology begins to win.

### N33-8 — Restricted and Prohibited Weapons as Narrative Weight

When Restricted or Absolute Prohibition weapons appear in prose, they carry narrative weight beyond their mechanical effect. The Soul-Sever Lance is not merely a powerful weapon — it is a war crime, a civilizational taboo, a political detonator. Authors must frame such weapons with the full gravity of their governance classification. A character deploying or threatening such a weapon has committed an act with civilizational-level consequences, not merely tactical ones.
