# Cultivation Battle Plausibility Law

**Authority Tier:** 1 (Domain Authority, Cultivation; see `.claude/guardrails/authority/guardrail-authority-tier-system.md`). Part 44 reads Part 3 and Part 5 and does not override either: Part 3 owns the Relative Power Table, the minor step, the 3-Level Rule, the Categorical Impossibility Law and the damage thresholds; Part 5 owns who may begin, escalate and end a fight. Part 44 owns the question neither asks: whether a whole engagement, with both sides, their numbers, ground and weapons, makes sense at the levels written.

## Overview

Part 44 is the Starward Saga Canon's Battle Plausibility Law. Before this Part, Part 3 Area 7.2 judged one pairing (the 3-Level Rule, counted in minor steps) and Part 5 judged who may start and end a fight. Nothing asked whether a whole engagement made sense by cultivation level. Chapter 3 showed the gap: a raiding band led by a Warrior at Level 9 Peak, with line fighters at Warrior Levels 3 to 5, met a patrol of one Warrior at Level 2 Peak and eight Seniors, and the draft had the patrol hold. By the levels it could not.

Part 44 asks, for every fight, from the levels alone: does this engagement make sense at all? It also tells a writer how a lower power can lawfully hold, delay or beat a higher one, so that a good story shape is never lost to a level table. It uses no formula. A group is judged in words, by who anchors each side, which pairings actually meet, which offset explains any held gap, what it costs and whether the outcome matches.

Part 44 adds no command. It runs in two places that already exist: the Chapter Spec and build plan, before any drafting, and Gate 1 Check #23, on every Area with a fight (and, for its prose element, on every Area). The arithmetic belongs to the spec and the gate; the page shows only what a person in the scene would see, hear and feel (Area 5.3).

Across eleven Areas, Part 44 defines: the principles (Area 1); reading the two sides (Area 2); the lawful offsets (Area 3); registered gifted fighters (Area 4); the plausibility checklist and the Engagement Ledger (Area 5); scales of engagement, ambush and open field, retreat and pursuit (Area 6); people on the field (Area 7); how a lower power wins (Area 8); where the law runs and Check #23 (Area 9); a worked example (Area 10); and the cross-Part dependency table with the record of what this Part amended (Area 11).

## Area 1: Foundational Principles

### 1.1: The Principles

**Rule 44.1.1.1: P44-1: A Fight Is Checked as a Whole.** The 3-Level Rule compares two fighters. A fight is also a place, a time, two or more sides, their numbers and their tools. Part 44 checks the whole engagement, then the pairings inside it.

**Rule 44.1.1.2: P44-2: Levels Decide What Is Possible; the Story Decides What Happens.** Part 44 never picks an outcome. It says whether an outcome the story wants is lawful, and if it is not, what lawful shape could carry the same story.

**Rule 44.1.1.3: P44-3: No Math for a Group.** Part 44 sets no sum, average, ratio or score for a side. The only figure it uses is the one Part 3 already uses: the gap in minor steps between two named fighters in one pairing. A side is judged by its anchors (Area 2) and the checklist (Area 5), in words.

**Rule 44.1.1.4: P44-4: A Lower Side Needs a Named Offset.** A lower side holds, delays or beats a higher one only through a lawful offset (Area 3), named in the plan and shown in the text, with its price. "They fought hard" and "they were many" are not offsets by themselves.

**Rule 44.1.1.5: P44-5: Nothing Above Is Weakened.** The 3-Level Rule, the Categorical Impossibility Law and every Part 5 rule stand exactly as written. Where Part 44 and Part 3 differ on a single pairing, Part 3 rules.

**Rule 44.1.1.6: P44-6: Heroes Keep Their Own Advantage.** A hero's Combat Level Advantage (Part 16 Rule 16.16.4.2, Part 3 Area 7.2) is unchanged and is counted before the comparison exactly as Part 3 says. Registered gifted fighters (Area 4) are a separate and smaller allowance for named non-hero people.

**Rule 44.1.1.7: P44-7: Strain Is the Author's Call.** A result the levels forbid is a FAIL. A result the levels allow only through offsets, or only just, is reported to the author as a ruling point (Area 9). The author decides whether the story wants it.

**Rule 44.1.1.8: P44-8: The Story Is Not Gated.** Part 44 sets no count, threshold or quota that a plot must meet, and no fight is owed to anyone. It does not decide which fights are shown, how long they run or who wins them; the Chapter Spec and the macro story (Part 30) decide that.

**Rule 44.1.1.9: P44-9: The Spec Counts, the Page Senses.** Gaps, steps, percentages and bands are working tools of the spec and the gate. They never appear as measurement in narration or dialogue (Area 5.3).

## Area 2: Reading the Two Sides

### 2.1: Where a Level Comes From

**Rule 44.2.1.1: Ledger First.** A fighter's realm, Level and sub-level come from the ledger (or, for a debut, from the Chapter Spec's level table, Part 29 Rule 29.12.1.3). The Relative Power Table (Part 3 Area 1.6, with `powerlevels.csv` at the repo root, whose Early and Medium are Low and Mid) gives the row. A fighter with no known level is an open item for the author and the engagement cannot be checked until it is closed.

**Rule 44.2.1.2: A Sealed Fighter Fights at the Current Level.** A sealed or hidden fighter is compared at the current level that actually fights, and the true level counts again the moment the seal opens.

### 2.2: The Anchors

**Rule 44.2.2.1: A Side Is Anchored by Its Strongest Few.** The anchors of a side are its top fighter and the few fighters near the top who are present, able to fight and willing. The side reads at its anchors' level, never at an average of its ranks. A crowd of low fighters does not raise its anchors.

**Rule 44.2.2.2: Commanders Anchor Armies.** In a war (Area 6) the commander and the strongest officers are the anchors. The mass below them is read by its typical level, in words.

**Rule 44.2.2.3: The Top Fighter Is One Body.** The strongest fighter on a field is in one place at a time. This is the fact most plausible lower-side holds rest on (Area 3).

### 2.3: Counting the Gap

**Rule 44.2.3.1: Count Minor Steps as Part 3 Does.** Inside one realm a Level is four minor steps (Low, Mid, High, Peak). Number a position as four times (Level minus one), plus 0 for Low, 1 for Mid, 2 for High or 3 for Peak, and the gap is the difference. The Level 9 Peak of one realm to the next realm's Level 1 Low is one step, and so the boundary counts as one. Example: Warrior Level 2 Peak is position 7 and Warrior Level 9 Peak is position 35, so the gap is 28 steps. Counting positions is counting, not a power formula; the table values are not used.

**Rule 44.2.3.2: A Major Realm Is Not a Count.** The barriers Mortal to Xiantian, Xiantian to Core and Core to Divine are laws of kind (Part 3 Rule 17.3.1), not a number of steps. Mortal Tier 1 to Tier 2 is not a Major Realm barrier (Part 3 Area 7.4).

### 2.4: The Three Bands

Every pairing that actually meets falls in one band.

| Band | Gap | The lower fighter in a fair contest | What can change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** | 0 to 2 minor steps | May win. Skill, ground, numbers, weapons and exhaustion can decide. | Any lawful offset. |
| **Shut** | 3 or more minor steps inside one Major Realm | Cannot win. Part 3 closes a gap of 3 to skill and ground, and above 3 nothing lets the lower side win. The lower fighter can survive, delay, flee or be saved. | The offsets of Area 3 change the engagement, not the fair contest (Rule 44.3.1.2). A registered gifted fighter (Area 4) lifts the fighter's own level by up to 3 steps before the count. |
| **Barred** | Across a Major Realm | Cannot harm the higher fighter. The lower fights to survive, escape or buy time (Part 3 Rule 17.3.2). | Only the exceptions of Part 3 Rule 17.3.3. |

**Rule 44.2.4.1: A Fair Contest.** A fair contest is one where both fighters are ready, in the open, one against one or in like numbers, with no trap, surprise, prepared ground or weapon the other cannot meet. Part 3 Area 7.2 speaks of a fair contest, and so does the Shut column above.

**Rule 44.2.4.2: Mortal Tier 1.** A Mortal Tier 1 fighter has no qi of their own (Part 3 Area 7.4). Tier 1 fighters compare by Stage, and a Senior beats any Tier 1 fighter unless a weapon or the terrain changes the contest.

## Area 3: The Lawful Offsets

### 3.1: How an Offset Works

**Rule 44.3.1.1: An Offset Changes the Engagement.** An offset changes who meets whom, what each has, how long it lasts and what each side pays. It does not change any fighter's level, with one exception: the registered gifted fighter (Area 4), whose own lift is a fact of the person.

**Rule 44.3.1.2: What an Offset Can Reach.** Three reaches are named.

- **Delay:** the stronger side loses time, from minutes to hours. The weaker side pays and is then broken, withdraws or is relieved.
- **Hold:** the weaker side keeps a place for as long as the story needs, against the stronger side's line, or against the stronger side's anchor when he is not there. It is paid for in wounded, rotation and exhaustion, and it ends when the price runs out or the anchor arrives.
- **Beat:** the weaker side wins the pairing or the engagement.

In an Open pairing any reach is lawful. In a Shut pairing a lower fighter cannot beat the higher fighter in a fair contest, so the lawful reaches are Delay and Hold at a price. A lower side beats a Shut pairing only in an unfair contest (a trap, an ambush on the unready, a weapon the stronger fighter cannot meet, a stronger fighter spent by exhaustion), only for as long as that unfairness lasts, and always as a strain item reported to the author (Rule 44.9.2.2). In a Barred pairing the lower side may reach Delay, and may reach Hold only against the anchor's absence. It harms the higher fighter only through Part 3 Rule 17.3.3.

**Rule 44.3.1.3: Stacking.** One offset in the explanation is plain. Two together are normal. An outcome that leans on several offsets at once, or on one offset to carry a Shut pairing by itself, is a strain item.

### 3.2: The Six Offsets

**(a) Numbers and formation.**
- *What it does:* many fighters occupy ground, take the stronger side's rank and file, keep the anchor busy for a time and rotate the tired out of the front. A held line, a locked formation or a narrow front that only a few can attack at once lets a weaker file stand against a stronger one for a while.
- *Bound:* numbers never raise the weaker side's anchor, and no number of low fighters lawfully beats a Shut anchor in the open. A formation helps in the way skill and ground help in Part 3: it can decide an Open pairing and stiffen a Shut one, and it never turns a Shut pairing into a fair win.
- *Price:* each held minute costs wounded and killed; the line has to be fed.
- *Strain if:* the numbers alone carry a Shut anchor.

**(b) Ground, preparation, ambush, surprise and shielding.**
- *What it does:* a narrow, a ford, a wall, a wood, a prepared trap, a first strike on an unready force, or shielding (walls, arrays, relic shields of Part 12, cover) turns the contest unfair for the stronger side. Part 3 Area 7.2 lets ground add up to two minor steps to an Open pairing. An ambush on the unready can wound or kill a Shut target once.
- *Bound:* an ambush needs information, time and a place (the leak, the scouts, the road; Part 24). After the first strike the engagement returns to its bands. Shielding delays and does not lift a Barred pairing.
- *Price:* the trap is spent, the ambushers are known, and the stronger side that lives returns angrier and warned.
- *Strain if:* the ambush kills a Shut anchor and is never shown to have been prepared.

**(c) Technology and weapons.**
- *What it does:* a weapon of Part 33, a siege weapon, a volley, a mine or a machine of Part 32 hurts what a body cannot meet, breaks a formation, drives a force off a road or kills the unready.
- *Bound:* a weapon helps only as far as its class can wound the target's body under Part 3 Area 8.2; it is read from Parts 8 and 33, not from the wielder's level. It cannot cross a Major Realm (Part 3 Rule 17.3.1; ordinary siege weapons cannot damage a Demigod). A weapon is a resource and does not turn a Shut fair contest into a win. It makes the contest unfair, as (b) does.
- *Price:* ammunition, charge, crew, set-up time, and the operator, who can be killed.
- *Strain if:* a weapon of a class that cannot wound the target is made to wound it.

**(d) Exhaustion and cost.**
- *What it does:* the stronger side tires, runs low on qi (Part 3 Area 12), pays for its big moves and carries its wounds; a long fight narrows the practical gap. A stronger fighter set on by many in turn is worn faster than one who fights once.
- *Bound:* exhaustion shifts a result only after the stronger fighter has spent effort the scene shows (many opponents, big moves, no rest, a wound). It never works from a standing start, and it never crosses a Major Realm.
- *Price:* the weaker side must be able to keep fighting through it, and pays in the lives the long fight costs.
- *Strain if:* the stronger fighter tires with no spend shown.

**(e) Registered gifted fighters.** Area 4.

**(f) Limit break and striving.** A fighter who strives, breaks their limit or does something chaotic fights above their level for a burst. Defined in Part 3 Area 7.5; checked here in Section 3.4.

### 3.3: What No Offset Does

**Rule 44.3.3.1.** No offset lifts a Barred pairing without a Part 3 Rule 17.3.3 exception. No offset lets a lower fighter win a fair contest at a Shut gap that remains after every lift. No offset lets a Mortal Tier 1 fighter with no qi stand as a line against fighters who would flatten them (Area 7). No offset exists because a scene needs one.

### 3.4: Limit Break and Striving (Offset f)

**Rule 44.3.4.1: Defined in Part 3.** A limit break (a striving, limit-breaking or chaotic burst) is defined in Part 3 Area 7.5, which holds the lift by affinity, the three cost tiers, the hard caps (10 minor steps for an ordinary person, 20 for a hero), the rule that it never crosses a Major Realm and is a burst, and its interaction with the 3-Level Rule, the lethal threshold, Qi Deviation and breakthrough surge. This Part restates none of it. Part 5 Area 7.8 says how it shows in a combat.

**Rule 44.3.4.2: What Part 44 Checks.** In an engagement a limit break is plausible when (a) the fighter can use it (Part 3 Rule 7.5.2) and the lift is within their affinity reach and the hard cap; (b) the remaining gap after the lift is read by the bands of Area 2.4 and is not Barred; (c) it is a single burst, not a standing level; (d) it stacks with a gifted lift or a hero's advantage only within the cap of Part 3 Rule 7.5.6; (e) it is planned in the Engagement Ledger (fighter, tier, planned cost) and is priced on the page afterwards, with the cost carrying across Areas as any wound does (Part 27).

**Rule 44.3.4.3: Reporting.** A limit break with no price on the page FAILs Check #23 (23-4). A Tier 2 or Tier 3 burst is also a strain item (23-6) reported to the author, and a hero's burst is ruled by the author.

**Rule 44.3.4.4: Not a Rescue.** A limit break does not turn a Barred pairing into a fair one and is not a way to win a fight the levels forbid.

## Area 4: Registered Gifted Fighters

**Rule 44.4.1.1: Who.** A registered gifted fighter is a rare, named, non-hero person (a side character or a villain) with a recorded reason: talent (Part 3 Area 5), a rare technique (Part 6), or long hard practice. A monster keeps the rules of Part 40. A hero keeps the Combat Level Advantage of Part 16 Rule 16.16.4.2, which this Part does not change.

**Rule 44.4.1.2: What the Registration Gives.** The person fights as if up to 3 minor steps above their level, never more. The lift is counted before the comparison, the way Part 3 Area 7.2 counts a hero's advantage. It is a fact about the person's fighting, not a change to their realm, Level or sub-level.

**Rule 44.4.1.3: Never Across a Major Realm.** The lift never carries the person over a Major Realm barrier. A Mortal Tier 2 gifted fighter is still Barred from a Xiantian one.

**Rule 44.4.1.4: May Tide, May Not Win Alone.** A gifted fighter may tide a battle (hold a flank, turn an exchange, delay an anchor, carry a retreat). One gifted fighter does not win an engagement or a war alone, and does not beat an anchor the lifted level still leaves Shut.

**Rule 44.4.1.5: Registered Before the Scene.** The registration is made in the ledger before the scene. The author rules each registration (asked in the Chapter Spec build). For a Tier A or Tier B side character or a villain with a full file it is the Combat Level Advantage line of Section 2 (Part 37 Area 2.1), written in minor steps (1, 2 or 3) with one line beside it giving the kind (talent, rare technique or long practice) and the reason. For a Tier C or D person it is a note on the registry row in `ledger-character-side/_index.md` (or the villain index). An unregistered person who fights above their level is a FAIL of Check #23.

**Rule 44.4.1.6: Rare Means Rare.** Gifted is a mark of one person among their fellows. A side whose fighters are mostly registered gifted is reported to the author as strain. Changing or retiring a registration is a ruling of the same kind and is logged as any ledger change is.

**Rule 44.4.1.7: One Record, Not Two.** Where Part 16 Rule 16.16.4.2 already records a rating for a side character, that rating is the registration, within the cap of 3; the two are not added together.

## Area 5: The Plausibility Checklist and the Engagement Ledger

### 5.1: The Seven-Step Checklist

**Rule 44.5.1.1.** For every fight, in this order:

1. **Anchors.** Who anchors each side (name, realm, Level, sub-level, source).
2. **Gaps.** The gap in minor steps between the two anchors, and for each pairing that actually meets, with its band (Open, Shut, Barred).
3. **Presence.** Can each fighter be there and survive at the stated levels (aura, lethal threshold, speed, the qi to last the length of the fight; Area 7).
4. **Offset.** Which offset of Area 3 explains each Shut pairing that holds, and any lower side that does better than its levels say; is its bound kept.
5. **Price.** What it cost, and who paid (lives, qi, a spent trap, spent weapons, wounded).
6. **Outcome.** Does the result match the levels: who is wounded, who is killed, who flees, who holds and for how long (Area 7), and does it fit the end class of Part 5 Area 12.
7. **Strain.** Is it lawful but strained. If so, list it for the author.

### 5.2: The Engagement Ledger (Required Format)

**Rule 44.5.2.1: Every Fight Gets One.** Every Area of the Chapter Spec with a fight carries an Engagement Ledger under its Fight line (Part 38 Rule 2.4.10), made at the spec build and fixed before drafting. It is a plan record. It establishes nothing, and the text is what Gate 1 measures.

**Rule 44.5.2.2: The Header.**

- **Engagement:** Area reference; escalation tier E1 to E7 and duel type if any (Part 5 Area 5); shape (ambush, open field, siege, escort, duel, pursuit, retreat); the ground and its one feature.
- **Anchors:** Side A, name and realm, Level, sub-level, source; Side B, the same. **Anchor gap:** a number of minor steps, or Barred.

**Rule 44.5.2.3: The Table.** One row for each fighter or group of equal level (a group may be one row, with its size in plain words).

| Side | Fighter or group | Realm and Level | Role | Faces | Gap (steps or Barred) | Band | Offset used (a to e, or none) | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Role is one of anchor, line, flank, reserve, rear, non-fighter. Expected outcome is one of win, hold, delay, lose, wounded, killed, flees.

**Rule 44.5.2.4: The Closing Lines.** After the table: **Price paid** (who pays what); **Level made known** (how the reader learns each level, Part 29 Rule 29.12.1.3); **Registered gifted fighters** (name, steps, ledger reference); **Strain notes for the author**; **Outcome of the engagement** (the end class, Part 5 Area 12).

**Rule 44.5.2.5: Where It Lives.** In the Fight line of the Area block of the Chapter Spec, and in the build plan of a whole-built Chapter. A battle that runs over several Areas has one ledger for each Area. At Gate 1 the checker builds a ledger from the draft and the ledgers and compares it with the spec's.

### 5.3: Story and Spec Are Kept Apart

**Rule 44.5.3.1: The Arithmetic Lives in the Spec.** The power-gap arithmetic (minor-step counts, gaps, percentages, the bands Open, Shut and Barred, anchors, the terms of the Engagement Ledger, the tier of a limit break) lives only in the Chapter Spec, the build plan, the ledgers and the Gate 1 and Gate 2 checks. It never appears as measurement in narration or dialogue.

**Rule 44.5.3.2: What the Page Does Show.** A battle on the page shows soldier counts, formations, ground and battle strategy. Commanders may talk strategy, headcounts, holding a line and flanks. Power is sensed and fought with: pressure, weight, slowed qi, aching knees, a blade that will not move. Level strings (bold-italic, Part 29 Rule 29.12.1.3) are allowed and still required once for each person in a Chapter. Sensed comparison in words is allowed in narration or dialogue as the situation calls: a sensing character may say someone is far above, a little above, about equal, or no match.

**Rule 44.5.3.3: What the Page Never Shows.** Numbers, minor steps, percentages, multipliers, "N levels above", a computed gap, the band names, "anchor" as a measure, or any ledger term used as a measurement. This reads with Part 3 Rule 3.4.1 (descriptors, not numbers) and Part 29 Rule 29.12.1.3 (the lawful ways to make a level known) and weakens neither: a level string names a place on the ladder and is not a computed gap, and a character who senses or compares uses words, not a count.

**Rule 44.5.3.4: Gate 1.** Element 23-7 of Check #23 FAILs gap arithmetic in prose (Area 9).

### 5.4: How a Battle Reads on the Page

- Open on the field: ground, banners, headcounts in the world's own units (two dozen, eight spears, a file of carters).
- Give orders and formations in the words commanders use: hold the line, close the narrow, flank left, fall back to the ford.
- Show power through the body and the air: pressure on the chest, weight in the air, qi that slows, knees that ache, a blade that will not move. The lower body gives way before the higher.
- Show an offset as something people do: the narrow held, the trap sprung, the volley loosed, the tired arm.
- Show a limit break as a person straining past themselves, and show its price in the beats after: shaking hands, a faint, blood, qi that will not settle.
- Make every level known by the lawful ways of Part 29 Rule 29.12.1.3; a level string once for each person in a Chapter.
- The spec says "28 steps, Shut". The page says that Rask's stroke came down and the shields buckled like wet bark, and that Tregear's knees ached before the blade arrived.

## Area 6: Scales of Engagement

### 6.1: Skirmish, Battle, Siege, War and Campaign

**Rule 44.6.1.1: Aggregate Forces.** A force is read as its commander and strongest officers (its anchors) and its mass, whose typical level is stated in words. The mass is not summed. Its number sets what ground the force can take, hold and feed, and how long it can fight; its level sets who it can stand against.

**Rule 44.6.1.2: Anchor Against Anchor.** If one side's anchors are Shut above the other's, the other side's anchors cannot win the centre in a fair field. They survive by ground, distance, other fighters or withdrawal. The stronger anchor is one body, so a wide front can be held or won by the weaker side where he is not.

**Rule 44.6.1.3: Mass Against Mass.** Where the lines are Open to each other, numbers, discipline, ground, supply, command and weapons decide, in words and by the story. Where the lines are Shut, the higher line wins its pairings and the weaker side's numbers buy time at a cost in lives.

**Rule 44.6.1.4: A War Is a Chain of Engagements.** Each engagement is checked on its own. A campaign result may be set by supply, season, politics or a lost anchor even when a field is lost (Parts 21 to 24 and 30). Part 44 forbids only a war result that needs an engagement to break the law. Leader death and command succession stay with Part 5 Area 11.

### 6.2: Ambush and Open Field

**Rule 44.6.2.1: Who Is Caught.** In an ambush the ambushed side is the one in the unfair contest. The ambusher's first strike can reach above its bands, against the unready only. After the first strike the engagement returns to its bands, and an ambushed stronger side regroups and its anchors act. An ambush can reach Delay and Hold, and Beat only for the first strike on the unready.

**Rule 44.6.2.2: Information.** An ambush needs information (a leak, a scout, a copy of the route; Part 24), time to set it and a place. The plan names where the information came from.

**Rule 44.6.2.3: Open Field.** In an open field with both sides ready, the bands apply in full and offsets reach only what Area 3 allows.

### 6.3: Retreat and Pursuit

**Rule 44.6.3.1.** Retreat and pursuit follow Part 5 Area 14. The stronger side can run down the weaker unless ground, a rearguard that delays at a cost, the pursuer's choice, the distance limit or a protected zone changes it. A rearguard that holds a narrow is Delay with a price. The faster side is the one the levels favour unless a mount, a machine of Part 32 or an obstacle changes it.

## Area 7: People on the Field

### 7.1: Non-Cultivators and Mortal Tier 1

**Rule 44.7.1.1: Present and Surviving.** A non-cultivator can hold a weapon and can be hit and killed by anything. They are not a line against Senior and above. Passive aura is a combat fact (Part 3 Areas 17.2 and 17.4): a Ruler's aura collapses Tier 1 beings in range, a Demigod's aura harms them, a True God's aura damages the organs of non-cultivators. A Tier 1 fighter or a civilian cannot stand in a fight beside fighters who would flatten or kill them by presence. A non-cultivator on the field is a carter, a clerk, a crew member or a prize, not a combatant, unless the fighters near them are low enough.

**Rule 44.7.1.2: What a Mortal Tier 1 Fighter Can Do.** They fight other Tier 1 fighters by Stage. Against a Senior or above they can act only through a weapon, the terrain, a trap or a crowd, as Area 3 allows, and they are not credited with a hit that a Senior's body would shrug off (Part 3 Area 8.2).

### 7.2: Tech-Scale Weapons

**Rule 44.7.2.1.** A weapon is read from its Part 33 class and its Part 8 scale, and set against the target's body under Part 3 Area 8.2. Heavy weapons break walls, formations and the unready and do not duel. A barrage can hurt a Warrior and cannot touch a Demigod. Hybrid use follows Part 31. The price is ammunition, crew, set-up time and the operator's life.

### 7.3: Healing

**Rule 44.7.3.1.** A healer is a person on the field, with qi that runs out, who can be targeted. Healing in the fight follows Part 3 Area 8.2 recovery times and Part 6 Area 3.7. A wounded fighter does not return to the line in the next Area unless the recovery time allows, and the wound is a consequence (Part 27).

### 7.4: Who May Be Wounded or Killed by Whom

**Rule 44.7.4.1.** Part 3 Area 8.2 says whether a strike can kill a body. Part 3 Area 7.2 says who wins a fair contest. They are read together.

- In an Open pairing either side may wound, and may kill if the Area 8.2 lethal threshold allows.
- A higher fighter in a Shut pairing wounds or kills the lower at will, within the legitimacy limits of Part 5 (pursuit limits, protected status, surrender).
- A lower fighter in a Shut pairing wounds or kills the higher only through an offset: a strike that can kill and lands on the unready, through a trap, an unfair weapon or a spent opponent. It is a strain item.
- A lower fighter in a Barred pairing harms the higher one only through Part 3 Rule 17.3.3.
- The stronger side's anchors wound and kill only those they reach in the time they have. A stronger side's casualties are as likely to be its line as its anchors.

## Area 8: How a Lower Power Wins

**Rule 44.8.1.1: The Menu.** These are the lawful story shapes. Each is paid for in the story.

1. **The held narrow.** Ground, a locked formation and rotation hold a place for a time (a, b). Cost: the line's wounded and dead.
2. **The ambush that breaks the head.** A prepared trap, once (b). Cost: the trap is spent and the survivors are warned.
3. **The long fight.** The stronger side tires or runs dry (d). Cost: the weaker side must last that long.
4. **The weapon that changes the field.** A volley, siege weapon or machine (c). Cost: crew, charge, setup.
5. **The gifted fighter who tides it.** A registered person turns an exchange or holds a flank (e). Cost: the person.
6. **Win the objective, not the fight.** Burn the supply, close the pass, reach the gate, hold until relief. The stronger side wins fields and loses the war.
7. **Divide the stronger side.** Draw the anchor away; strike where he is not; make him choose (the top fighter is one body, Rule 44.2.2.3).
8. **Make the stronger side stop.** It leaves because staying costs too much: command, politics, honour, an end class of Part 5 Area 12.
9. **Borrow a stronger power.** An ally, a hero's arrival, a Part 12 relic made to cross barriers (Part 3 Rule 17.3.3). Cost: debt, and the relic's own rules.
10. **The stronger side's own mistake.** Overreach, a split force, a misreading. It must come from the character, not from convenience.
11. **The striving burst.** A fighter breaks their limit for a few exchanges (f, Rule 44.3.4). Cost: the burst's tier, shown on the page afterwards.

**Rule 44.8.1.2: Not on the Menu.** A lower side wins by skill alone against a Shut anchor. A crowd beats an anchor in the open. A sudden rise in level that the ledger and Part 3 breakthrough rules never recorded. The stronger side forgets to use its power.

## Area 9: Where the Law Runs

### 9.1: Before Drafting: Chapter Spec and Build Plan

**Rule 44.9.1.1.** At the Chapter Spec build (and the build plan of a whole-built Chapter), the planner fills an Engagement Ledger (Area 5) for every Area with a fight. Both sides' levels are fixed from the ledgers, the offsets are named, the checklist is run, and every strained item is put to the author in the build. A registered gifted fighter is ruled and written to the ledger at that point (Rule 44.4.1.5). The light continuity audit (Part 38 Rule 3.3.2) lists, report-only, any fight Area without an Engagement Ledger and any Shut pairing that the plan lets the lower side win without an offset.

### 9.2: Gate 1 Check #23

**Rule 44.9.2.1: Scope.** Check #23 runs at Gate 1 on every Area whose text holds a fight (any exchange of blows or weapon fire between named or counted sides, and any battle, siege, ambush, pursuit or war event shown or reported). For an Area with no fight, elements 23-1 to 23-6 read N/A; element 23-7 reads every Area. In a whole-built Chapter it runs once for each fight Area block. The guardrail is `.claude/guardrails/quality/guardrail-battle-plausibility.md`.

**Rule 44.9.2.2: Severity.** Six elements block and one reports.

| Element | Reads | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 23-1 Anchors and levels | Both sides' anchors and every combatant's level agree with the ledgers and the Chapter Spec | FAIL if unstated, unknown or contradicted |
| 23-2 Pairings | Each pairing that meets is in its band; the lower side wins no Shut pairing in a fair contest and harms no Barred pairing without Rule 17.3.3 | FAIL on a break |
| 23-3 Presence and survival | Each fighter can be there and survive at the stated levels | FAIL if a side cannot |
| 23-4 Offsets | Each offset used is lawful, within its bound and priced in the text; a gifted fighter is registered before the scene; a limit break (Rule 44.3.4) is within its reach and caps and its cost is shown on the page afterwards | FAIL on an unlawful, unbounded, unpriced or unregistered offset, or an unpriced limit break |
| 23-5 Outcome | The result is not one the levels forbid (wounds, kills, holds, ends) and fits Area 7 and the end class | FAIL if forbidden |
| 23-6 Strain | A result reached only through offsets, through several at once, through one offset carrying a Shut pairing, or through a Tier 2 or Tier 3 limit break | Reported to the author as a ruling point; never a FAIL |
| 23-7 No gap arithmetic in prose | Narration and dialogue state no minor steps, computed gap, percentage, multiplier or "N levels above", and use no band name or ledger term as a measurement. Level strings and sensed comparison in words (far above, about equal) are lawful | FAIL, quoted from the text |

### 9.3: Send-Back

**Rule 44.9.3.1.** A FAIL goes back through the normal Gate 1 loop (`[REVISE AREA]`, or the Whole-Chapter FAIL loop). The lawful fixes are: change the plan through a revised Chapter Spec (the levels in the ledger do not change to fit a scene), add a lawful offset with its price, change the outcome, or ask the author to register a gifted fighter. A strain item is put to the author at `[CONFIRM WRITE]` with the offset, the reason it is strained and the options.

### 9.4: Scope in Time

**Rule 44.9.4.1.** Part 44 is not retroactive. Chapters 1 and 2 hold no fight and are not touched. A written Area with a fight is not re-audited unless it is revised, and then Check #23 runs on the revision.

## Area 10: Worked Example

**Rule 44.10.1.1: The Failure.** In the Chapter 3 plan the raiders are led by Rask, Warrior Level 9 Peak, with line fighters at Warrior Levels 3 to 5. The patrol is Lieutenant Tregear, Warrior Level 2 Peak, with eight Seniors at Levels 5 to 8. The draft had the patrol hold the road against them.

| Pairing | Gap | Band |
|---|---|---|
| Tregear (Warrior 2 Peak, position 7) against Rask (Warrior 9 Peak, position 35) | 28 steps | Shut |
| Tregear against the lowest line raider (Warrior 3 Low, position 8) | 1 step | Open |
| Tregear against a line raider at Warrior 5 Low (position 16) | 9 steps | Shut |
| A Senior at Level 8 Low against a Warrior 3 Low raider | 16 steps | Shut |

Anchors are Rask and Tregear. Every Senior is Shut to every raider, and Tregear is Open only to the two lowest raiders. No offset is named. Checklist steps 2 and 4 fail. Check #23 FAILs (23-2 and 23-4).

**Rule 44.10.1.2: The Lawful Correction.** The patrol delays; it does not hold. It reaches the narrow first (b), forms a locked file (a), loses ground pair by pair, and buys minutes for the caravan to scatter. Tregear beats the two low raiders she is Open to (the one pairing the levels allow), and the eight Seniors go to ground under Rask's stroke. The price is wounded and dead. Outcome: delay, the caravan breaks apart and the head wagons are lost.

**Rule 44.10.1.3: If the Story Needs the Patrol to Hold.** The plan, not the scene, changes, through the Chapter Spec: Rask absent from that stretch of road, the line raiders no higher than the patrol's best, a prepared position with a weapon the raiders must respect, or a registered gifted fighter. A gifted lift alone does not rescue it: Tregear lifted by 3 steps stands at position 10, still 6 steps below a Warrior 5 Low raider and 25 below Rask, so the lift tides but does not win. A limit break does not rescue it either: even a burst of 6 steps for an ordinary person (Tier 2, injury or fainting afterwards) leaves her 22 below Rask and 3 below the Warrior 5 Low raider, still Shut, and she pays for it. On the page none of this is counted: Tregear's knees ache, the raiders' line does not give, and Rask's stroke buckles the shields.
