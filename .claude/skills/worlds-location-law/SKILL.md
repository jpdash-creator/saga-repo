## Overview

Part 13 is the Starward Saga Canon's Location Law — the governing domain for every place in which the saga's events occur, from a single frontier compound to the existential substrate beyond the Multiverse. Location in the Starward Saga is not backdrop: it is a mandatory, active governance layer that modifies every system operating within it — cultivation recovery and advancement, dimensional technique function, substance formation, technology integration, and combat conditions.

Ten foundational principles (Area 1) establish that every event requires a defined, resolvable location; that location is a governance layer, not decoration; that location identity is immutable without a traceable governing cause; that the cosmological hierarchy is strictly ordered from Village through Multiverse to the Primordium; that classification is self-contained within each tier (a Class 10 Village is not thereby cosmically significant); that Sacred Grounds are the highest special zone status; that cross-location interaction requires defined mechanisms; that spatial stability is the prerequisite for habitation; that world type describes character, not destiny; and that the Primordium / Absolute Chaos is beyond classification.

From these foundations, Part 13 builds outward across 27 Areas: the complete 16-tier cosmological architecture with its Rogue Location exceptions, eight-class Ambient Energy System, fifty-two-entry Affinity Profile System, and Special Zone Status table (Area 2); the settlement classification systems for Village, Town, City, and Capital (Areas 3–6); the political territory classification systems for Kingdom and Empire (Areas 7–8); the special zone governance regimes — Sacred Grounds (Area 9), Energy Formation Zones (Area 10), Void Zones (Area 11), Dimensional Structures (Area 12), Resonance Planes (Area 13), and Ruins Zones (Area 14); the Cosmic Tier classification systems for World, Star System, Star Cluster, Star Quadrant, and Galaxy (Areas 15–19), with the identical 10-class framework extended to Galaxy Cluster, Galaxy Supercluster, Universe, and Multiverse (Area 20); the Primordium / Absolute Chaos and its Part 28 authorization requirement (Area 21); how a location's properties interact with the cultivation, technology, and combat systems operating within it (Area 22); the mechanisms by which perception, energy, and consequence cross location boundaries (Area 23); the ten mandatory Location Narration Standards N13-1 through N13-10 (Area 24); the full cross-Part dependency master table (Area 25); the disambiguation boundaries against Parts 21, 22, 14, 15, and 11, plus the Classification vs. Occupation rule (Area 26); and the climate model, weather law and seasons of Saha with their use in scenes (Area 27).

The "Cross-References" table shared by 23 Areas and the "Governing Authority" paragraph shared by all Areas are given once in Appendix A and Appendix B; the Areas whose Cross-References tables differ from the shared table (Areas 7, 8, 21 and 27) have their Area-specific tables in Appendix A. ## Area 1 — Foundational Principles of Location Law

### 1.1 — Core Principles

**P13-1 — Every Event Requires a Location**
Every entity, object, event, effect, perception, and consequence in the Starward Saga must occur at a defined, resolvable location. A cultivator does not simply "advance" — they advance at a specific location with specific properties. A battle does not simply "happen" — it happens at a defined spatial address with defined boundaries. An energy formation does not simply "exist" — it exists at a specific Formation Zone with specific spatial characteristics. Location is the mandatory context of all existence. Events without defined locations are spatially invalid and cannot produce canonically valid consequences.

**P13-2 — Location Is a Governance Layer**
Location is not merely backdrop or setting. Location is an active governance layer that modifies every system operating within it. The ambient energy class of a location modifies cultivation recovery rates and advancement potential (Part 3, Area 14). The spatial stability of a location governs what dimensional techniques function within it. The world type of a location determines what systems are endemic to it. The Formation Zone status of a location determines what substances form there. Authors must treat location as a mechanical variable, not a decorative one.

**P13-3 — Location Identity Is Immutable Without Cause**
A location's classification, properties, and status are immutable without a defined governing cause. Sacred Grounds do not spontaneously degrade. Energy Formation Zones do not appear randomly. A Void Zone does not form without a specific sealing event. All location state transitions require a governing cause that is traceable through the canon. This prevents arbitrary location rewrites and ensures the spatial layer remains consistent across books.

**P13-4 — Cosmological Hierarchy Is Strictly Ordered**
The Starward Saga universe is organized into a strict cosmological hierarchy from smallest to largest: Village → Town → City → Capital → Kingdom → Empire → World → Star System → Star Cluster → Star Quadrant → Galaxy → Galaxy Cluster → Galaxy Supercluster → Universe → Multiverse → The Primordium / Absolute Chaos. Containment is strict and hierarchical: Galaxies contain Star Quadrants; Star Quadrants contain Star Clusters; Star Clusters contain Star Systems; Star Systems contain Worlds. Galaxy Clusters contain Galaxies. Galaxy Superclusters contain Galaxy Clusters. Universes contain Galaxy Superclusters. Rogue Worlds, rogue Star Systems, and rogue Clusters exist outside normal containment as classified exceptions.

**P13-5 — Classification Is Self-Contained Within Each Tier**
Every location tier (Village, Town, City, etc.) has its own internal classification system. The classes within any tier describe only that tier's internal variation — they do not link or escalate to higher tiers. A Village of Class 10 is the most exceptional possible Village; it is not a Village that has become cosmically significant. Class designations reflect the intrinsic properties of the location itself: ambient energy class, affinity profile, special zone status, territorial scale, historical depth, and dominant character. They do not designate who lives there, what cultivation realm is present, or what technology tier has been achieved. The story governs those facts.

**P13-6 — Sacred Grounds Are the Highest Special Zone Status**
Within any spatial scale, Sacred Ground status is the highest achievable special zone classification. Sacred Grounds form through extended Divine Sea Realm cultivation presence and represent locations where local reality has been permanently altered. Sacred Grounds cannot be manufactured by technology — only approximated. Their existence permanently changes the political and military landscape of their region regardless of the political entities currently controlling them.

**P13-7 — Cross-Location Interaction Requires Defined Mechanisms**
Effects do not cross location boundaries without a defined mechanism. Ambient energy does not leak from one Star System to another without a specific energy conduit. Sacred Ground effects do not extend infinitely without governance rules. Combat effects do not propagate infinitely into adjacent spatial zones. Every cross-location interaction — whether perception, energy exchange, physical travel, or consequence propagation — requires a defined mechanism that Part 13 governs or directs to the appropriate Part.

**P13-8 — Spatial Stability Is the Prerequisite for Habitation**
A location is habitable if and only if its spatial stability conditions are satisfied. Spatially unstable locations — collapsing dimensional pockets, Void Zones, shattered World surfaces, cosmic event boundaries — are not habitable regardless of their other properties. Spatial stability is the first condition assessed for any location in narrative.

**P13-9 — World Type Describes Character, Not Destiny**
A world's type classification describes its current dominant characteristic, not a fixed fate. A Beast World can develop cultivation traditions. A Technology World can discover cultivation energy. A Green World can be industrialized. World type transitions require time, cause, and institutional change. Authors must not treat world type as a permanent label that prevents narratively motivated transitions.

**P13-10 — The Primordium / Absolute Chaos Is Beyond Classification**
The Primordium / Absolute Chaos exists beyond the Multiverse. It does not conform to the classification systems of Part 13 for normal locations. Its sub-regions are governed by their own internal principles, which are discoverable but not mappable from within ordinary universe structure. Narrative deployment of the Primordium requires Part 28 (Creative Flexibility Law) authorization.

## Area 2 — Cosmological Architecture

### 2.1 — The Complete Cosmological Hierarchy

The Starward Saga universe is organized into the following hierarchy from smallest to largest. Each tier strictly contains only the tier immediately below it within the standard hierarchy.

**Tier 1 — Village:** The smallest defined inhabited location unit. A single inhabited structure, compound, hall, or small settlement cluster. The fundamental unit of settlement.

**Tier 2 — Town:** An organized inhabited cluster larger than a single compound. A small-to-medium settlement with social organization, basic commerce, and defined territory.

**Tier 3 — City:** A major urban center with complex governance, multiple administrative districts, a self-sustaining economy, and major cultivation or technology infrastructure.

**Tier 4 — Capital:** The political, administrative, and cultivation apex of a governance structure — the center from which authority radiates. Carries symbolic, historical, and power-concentration functions.

**Tier Is Decided by Governance:** The tier of a settlement (Village, Town, City or Capital) is decided by its governance structure, as the tier definitions above describe it. The population bands in Areas 3 to 6 are guides: where bands overlap, the governance test wins and the ledger records the reason.

**Tier 5 — Kingdom:** An organized political territory with defined borders, a recognized governing structure, military capability, and a cultivation/technology character that defines the political unit. Scale ranges from minor principalities to powers spanning several Worlds (classes Km-9 and Km-10 are political overlays; see the Overlay Rule below Tier 6).

**Tier 6 — Empire:** A major multi-territory political entity spanning multiple Kingdoms or equivalent governance zones under unified imperial authority.

**Overlay Rule for Large Polities:** Kingdom classes Km-9 and Km-10 and Empire classes E-4 to E-10 reach beyond a single World, so they are political overlays, not places that sit inside a World. The Kingdom-State Ledger records each one as spanning the places it covers (Worlds, Star Systems, Star Clusters, Star Quadrants or a Galaxy), and those places keep the strict containment of P13-4. Only the polity's Capital (Area 6) sits inside a World, and its location file gives the Containing World and Containing Star System.

**Tier 7 — World:** A self-contained planetary body — complete or capable of supporting civilization. Contains all sub-World tiers (Villages through Empires, except the overlay classes Km-9, Km-10 and E-4 to E-10) within it.

**Tier 8 — Star System:** A complete stellar unit — a star or stars and all bodies orbiting within their gravitational influence. Contains Worlds and all sub-World tiers.

**Tier 9 — Star Cluster:** A gravitationally associated group of Star Systems sharing a regional spatial neighborhood. Contains Star Systems and all below.

**Tier 10 — Star Quadrant:** Multiple Star Clusters forming a navigable region of a Galaxy. Contains Star Clusters and all below. A Star Quadrant exists within a Galaxy only.

**Tier 11 — Galaxy:** A gravitationally bound collection of Star Quadrants — the fundamental large-scale structure of the physical universe. Contains Star Quadrants and all below.

**Tier 12 — Galaxy Cluster:** Multiple Galaxies gravitationally associated. Contains Galaxies and all below. Does NOT directly contain Star Quadrants or Star Clusters.

**Tier 13 — Galaxy Supercluster:** Multiple Galaxy Clusters forming cosmic web structures. Contains Galaxy Clusters and Galaxies. Does NOT directly contain Star Quadrants, Star Clusters, or Star Systems.

**Tier 14 — Universe:** All observable physical reality in a single spacetime manifold. Contains Galaxy Superclusters and all below.

**Tier 15 — Multiverse:** Multiple Universes. Each Universe within the Multiverse may have different physical constants, dimensional properties, and cultivation energy profiles.

**Tier 16 — The Primordium / Absolute Chaos:** The medium containing the Multiverse. Not a "place" in the normal sense but the existential substrate within which multiverses float. Beyond classification.

### 2.2 — Rogue Locations (Exceptions to Containment)

Not all locations conform to standard containment. Recognized exceptions are classified normally by their tier but carry a "Rogue" governance annotation:

**Rogue World:** A world unbound from any star — drifting in inter-system space or dimensional anomaly pockets.

**Rogue Star System:** A star system not gravitationally associated with any Star Cluster, drifting in inter-cluster void.

**Rogue Star Cluster:** A cluster not contained within any Star Quadrant, occupying inter-quadrant void within a Galaxy.

**Rogue Star Quadrant:** A quadrant-equivalent region that is not part of any recognized Galaxy — an intergalactic formation or anomalous structure. Extremely rare.

Rogue locations are classified by their type and class normally. The Rogue designation is a governance annotation, not a separate classification tier.

### 2.3 — Ambient Energy Class System

All locations carry an Ambient Energy Class from 0 to 7, as defined in Part 3 Area 14.2. This class is an intrinsic property of the location — determined by the density of natural cultivation energy in the environment. It is NOT determined by who lives there.

### 2.6 — Landforms and Sub-Locations

The tiers of Area 2.1 classify settlements, polities and cosmic bodies. The ground between them (continents, seas, rivers, ranges, passes, roads) and the parts of a settlement (halls, gates, streets, chambers) are not tiers. Each is a Sub-World location. It lies inside one physical parent, which is the place it sits in, and its address under P13-1 is its chain of parents. Each carries its own Ambient Energy Class (Area 2.3), affinity profile (Area 2.4) and Special Zone status (Area 2.5), recorded for that place; a pass can be Class 4 inside a Class 2 range. The Location Ledger records these places under the labels below, which are the labels the registry already uses.

| Landform | Registry label | Physical parent |
|---|---|---|
| Continent | Sub-World Continental | A World (Tier 7) |
| Region | Sub-World Continental Region, Sub-World Coastal Region, Sub-World Kingdom Sub-Region | A Continent, or a larger Region |
| Ocean or Sea | Sub-World Ocean (frozen seas included) | A World (Tier 7) |
| River | Sub-World River / Inland Waterway | A Continent or a Region |
| Mountain Range | Sub-World Mountain Range | A Continent or a Region |
| Plateau | Sub-World Plateau | A Continent or a Region |
| Pass | Sub-World Mountain Pass | A Mountain Range |
| Road or Waypoint | Sub-World Road Junction, Sub-World Waypoint | The Region, Pass or Range the road runs through |
| Structure | Sub-World Royal Seat, Sub-World Palace Audience Hall | The settlement it stands in, or a larger Structure |
| Sub-Location | Sub-World Sub-Location, with its kind in brackets: (gate), (thoroughfare), (hall), (chamber), (bridge), (waypoint) | The settlement or Structure it lies in |

A landform not listed (a lake, an island, a desert, a forest, a valley, a cave) is recorded as a Sub-World Region and names its kind in its Classification cell. A feature shared by two or more polities has one physical parent and a list of controlling polities (Area 23.4).

## Area 9 — Sacred Ground Governance

Sacred Grounds are the most powerful special zone classification in Part 13. They represent locations where local reality has been permanently altered by extended exposure to Divine Sea Realm cultivation presence. Sacred Grounds are the pinnacle of location-level governance.

### 9.1 — Formation Law

**Rule 9.1.1 — Sacred Ground Formation Requires Divine Sea Realm Presence**
A Sacred Ground cannot form through any process short of extended Divine Sea Realm cultivation presence. No quantity of lower-realm cultivation activity can accumulate to produce a Sacred Ground. No technology can manufacture a natural Sacred Ground. This is absolute.

**Rule 9.1.2 — Formation Is Gradual and Permanent**
Sacred Ground formation occurs over extended time — not through a single event but through sustained presence that permanently alters the spatial-energetic structure of the location. Once formed, the Sacred Ground designation is permanent unless specifically destroyed (see Rule 9.1.4).

**Rule 9.1.3 — Sacred Ground Grade Reflects Formation Depth**
The grade of a Sacred Ground (S1 through S8) reflects how deeply reality has been altered and how long the formation process has been operating. Higher grades require more powerful Divine Sea Realm cultivators and longer formation periods.

**Rule 9.1.4 — Sacred Ground Destruction**
A Sacred Ground can be destroyed or degraded by a CSS-7+ consequence event of appropriate scale — typically requiring either the deliberate destruction by a comparable-realm cultivator, or a catastrophic spatial event (collapsing star, dimensional tear of sufficient scale). Destruction of a Sacred Ground is a narrative event of enormous weight (CSS-8+) that reshapes regional politics and cultivation geography permanently.

## Area 10 — Energy Formation Zone Governance

Energy Formation Zones are locations where cultivation energy naturally concentrates, accumulates, and produces CPM substances over time. They are distinct from Sacred Grounds — Formation Zones are naturally occurring energy features; Sacred Grounds are reality-altering legacies of Divine Sea Realm cultivation.

### 10.1 — Formation Zone Types

**Passive Formation Zone**
The most common Formation Zone type. Energy accumulates gradually from natural environmental factors — geological formations, underground energy rivers, convergence of affinity-aligned geographic features. Produces lower-grade CPM substances over long periods. Stable and predictable. Common targets for settlement and faction control. No dramatic environmental effects — perceptible only to practitioners with cultivated qi sense.

**Active Formation Zone**
A Formation Zone with ongoing energy flow — ley lines, volcanic activity, or an established cultivation site carrying centuries of Embodiment Resonance. Gems form at a steady, meaningful rate and Shards in small amounts (rates in Part 11 Area 3.3.1). Ambient energy sits at Class 5 (Saturated). The backbone of regional cultivation economies, and a standing object of faction contest (Part 22).

**Vortex Formation Zone**
An active, high-energy Formation Zone where multiple energy streams converge with significant force. The convergence point produces visibly elevated qi density — perceptible even to Mortal Tier 2 cultivators in the vicinity. Produces mid-to-high grade CPM substances at meaningful rates. Environmentally dramatic — qi mists, unusual weather, spontaneous energy discharges. Dangerous to the uninitiated. Competition for Vortex Formation Zones is fierce.

**Celestial Formation Zone (Cosmic-Scale)**
At Star System scale and above, Formation Zones exist on a cosmic scale — stellar cultivation energy convergence points, galactic energy river confluences, inter-cluster energy density peaks. These celestial-scale Formation Zones follow the same principles as their planetary counterparts but operate at cosmic spatial scales (governed by Part 14 CMS units).

### 10.2 — Formation Zone Spatial Rules

**Rule 10.2.1 — Boundary Integrity:** Formation Zone boundaries are defined by the natural energy gradient. A Formation Zone does not simply end at a wall — its energy density decreases gradually toward the boundary. The "zone" designation applies to the region where density meaningfully exceeds the surrounding environment.

**Rule 10.2.2 — Overlap Principle:** Two Formation Zones that share spatial overlap produce combined effects in the overlap region. The combined affinity of overlapping zones may be Mixed (Area 2.4) if the zones carry different affinities. Combined output may exceed what either zone produces independently.

**Rule 10.2.3 — Zone Priority:** Sacred Ground designation supersedes all Formation Zone designations. A Sacred Ground absorbs any Formation Zone within its influence zone — the Formation Zone's properties are incorporated into the Sacred Ground's profile.

**Rule 10.2.4 — Collapse Conditions:** A Formation Zone can collapse if: (a) the underlying geographic or energetic feature is destroyed; (b) the energy is deliberately extracted faster than it replenishes; (c) a Void Zone forms within the Formation Zone's core. Collapse of a major Formation Zone is a narrative event with CSS-5+ consequences for any faction dependent on it.

## Area 11 — Void Zone Governance

Void Zones are regions of spatial emptiness — areas where no ambient cultivation energy exists, where the qi dimension is absent or sealed, and where cultivators relying on qi exchange with the environment find themselves cut off from environmental support.

### 11.1 — Void Zone Types

**Natural Void Zone:** Regions of genuine spatial absence — deep inter-galactic voids, certain cosmological anomaly zones, regions where dimensional collapse has exhausted energy. Natural Void Zones are features of the cosmological landscape, not the result of any action.

**Artificial Void Zone:** Sealing formations or deliberately constructed spatial suppression fields that create Void Zone conditions within their boundaries. Requires significant formation array capability (Part 12) and typically significant CPM expenditure to maintain.

### 11.2 — Void Zone Properties

Within a Void Zone:
- Ambient energy Class is 0 (Void). The environment provides no cultivation support.
- Cultivators cannot exchange energy with the environment. They are limited to internally stored qi only.
- Soul sense range is compressed — qi-based perception cannot extend normally.
- Formation arrays that rely on ambient energy draw fail unless carrying their own internal CPM supply.
- Technology systems that rely on cultivation energy integration (Model B–D per Part 31) are impaired.
- Divine Sea Realm cultivators and above are not fully suppressed by Void Zones — their internalized divine energy is not ambient-dependent — but they lose certain environmentally interactive techniques.

### 11.3 — Void Zone Narration Rule

A character entering a Void Zone must experience the transition — the sudden compression of environmental qi, the silence of the qi dimension, the increased weight of solitary internal cultivation. Void Zone entry and exit are mandatory narration events that cannot be skipped over. The narration is owed by characters who feel the zone (cultivators, open-air travelers). A vessel or fold chain crossing a Natural Void Zone is narrated once per journey, and only where the crossing changes what the characters can do.

## Area 12 — Dimensional Structure Governance

Beyond physical space, the Starward Saga universe contains dimensional layers that interact with physical space at specific locations.

### 12.1 — Dimensional Layers

**Physical Dimension:** The standard three spatial dimensions plus time. Where all normal existence occurs. The domain of Part 13's standard location classification system.

**Qi Dimension / Cultivation Space:** The dimensional substrate through which cultivation energy flows. Not physically traversable by ordinary beings but perceptible to cultivators through qi sense. At very high cultivation development, physical-and-qi-dimensional overlap becomes achievable.

**Soul Dimension:** The dimensional substrate where soul constructs, spiritual entities, and soul techniques operate. Soul sense operates directly in the soul dimension. Soul attacks traverse the soul dimension. Part 4 (Affinity, Heart and Soul Law) governs soul dimension mechanics from the cultivation side.

**Extradimensional Pockets:** Small stable dimensional spaces detached from standard physical coordinates. Created by artifact construction (Part 12, Area 9), dimensional technique users, or natural spatial anomalies.

**Stable Dimensional Rifts:** Persistent connections between two non-adjacent physical locations or between dimensions. Of great strategic value. Formation, governance, and closure rules follow Rule 12.2 below.

**Unstable Dimensional Tears:** Dangerous openings in spacetime caused by extreme energy events, cultivation technique misuse, or dimensional pressure. Unlike stable rifts, tears are uncontrolled and may expand, collapse, or shift.

**Void Zones:** See Area 11.

**Resonance Planes:** Cultivation-adjacent dimensional zones associated with specific affinities. Fire Plane, Ice Plane, Soul Plane — dimensional spaces where the corresponding cultivation energy is the dominant medium. Access to Resonance Planes is governed jointly by Part 4 (affinity mechanics) and Part 13 (spatial properties).

### 12.2 — Dimensional Rift Rules

**Rule 12.2.1 — Stable Rift Formation:** A stable dimensional rift requires a specific formation event — either extreme energy convergence at a cosmologically significant location, deliberate dimensional formation by an appropriately capable practitioner, or an artifact of Part 12 Tier 4+ classification.

**Rule 12.2.2 — Rift Permanence:** Once formed, a stable rift persists until deliberately closed or until a disrupting event of sufficient scale. It cannot be accidentally opened or closed.

**Rule 12.2.3 — Rift Strategic Value:** Stable rifts connect two fixed points in space. They cannot be moved. Controlling both endpoints grants strategic access that no other mechanism replicates. Rifts are among the most contested spatial features in the Starward Saga.

**Rule 12.2.4 — Unstable Tear Hazard:** An unstable tear is a spatial emergency. It does not connect two known points — it opens into uncertain dimensional territory. Entry is potentially fatal. Any faction with capability will attempt to seal an unstable tear, as its expansion can damage surrounding physical space.

## Area 13 — Resonance Plane Governance

Resonance Planes are dimensional zones adjacent to physical space where a specific affinity type dominates. They are accessible at specific physical locations where the boundary between the physical dimension and the Resonance Plane is thin.

### 13.1 — Resonance Plane Properties

- Each major affinity type (Fire, Ice/Frost, Thunder, Earth, Wind, Life, Death, Soul, Space, Time) has a corresponding Resonance Plane.
- Cultivators with strong alignment to the plane's affinity experience enhanced cultivation states within it.
- Opposing affinities are suppressed within the plane.
- Non-aligned cultivators may enter if capable of navigating the extreme environmental conditions.
- Physical laws within a Resonance Plane reflect the dominant affinity — a Fire Plane has no cold; a Space Plane has non-standard spatial geometry.

### 13.2 — Access Points

Physical locations where Resonance Plane access is possible:
- Resonance Plane Access Points are typically also high-class Formation Zones or Sacred Grounds of the corresponding affinity.
- Access Point locations are immutable (P13-3) — they do not move unless a catastrophic spatial event occurs at that location.
- Controlling an Access Point is a major political and military asset.

## Area 14 — Ruins Zone Governance

A Ruins Zone is any location that contains significant physical, energetic, or informational remnants of a prior civilization or cultivation tradition at a higher development level than what currently occupies the location.

### 14.1 — Ruins Zone Properties

- Ruins Zones may contain dormant formation arrays (Part 12) that were constructed by prior occupants.
- They may contain residual ambient cultivation energy from prior occupants — ancient cultivation residue that slowly dissipates over millennia.
- They may contain artifacts, relics, or CPM deposits that have accumulated since the prior civilization's departure or collapse.
- The Location Ledger records the prior civilization's Part 21 CCT tier as the ruin's depth, not the current occupants' tier. The depth sets what the ruin can hold (Part 12 relic tier, Part 33 weapon class).

### 14.2 — Ruins Zone Narrative Rules

**Rule 14.2.1 — Ruins Are Discovery Zones:** A Ruins Zone is always a potential narrative discovery site. What remains is not always immediately visible. Excavation, research, and high-level formation sense are required to fully assess a Ruins Zone's contents.

**Rule 14.2.2 — Dormant Formations May Reactivate:** Ancient formation arrays in a Ruins Zone may reactivate if triggered — by qi exposure of the right affinity, by deliberate activation, or by external energy events. Reactivation of a major ancient formation is a CSS-5+ consequence event.

**Rule 14.2.3 — Ruins Cannot Be Fully Depleted:** A Ruins Zone's informational content (cultural records, historical inscriptions, architectural knowledge) cannot be exhausted — only lost if the physical ruins are destroyed.

## Area 20 — Galaxy Cluster, Galaxy Supercluster, Universe, and Multiverse Classification

At the scale of Galaxy Clusters and above, the classification system follows the same 10-class framework established for lower cosmic tiers. ## Area 23 — Cross-Location Interaction Law

Effects, perceptions, and energies do not cross location boundaries without defined mechanisms. This Area governs those mechanisms.

### 23.1 — Perception Across Location Boundaries

- Soul sense range by cultivation realm is governed by Part 14 (Distance Law, Area 4.1). It does not automatically cross World or Star System boundaries — perception is bounded by range.
- Technology sensor range by tier is governed by Part 14 (Area 5.1). Technology sensors at sufficient tier can perceive across Star System and Star Cluster scales.
- Sacred Ground ambient energy does not extend beyond its influence zone (Area 9.2). Sacred Ground presence cannot be perceived at arbitrary distance.

### 23.2 — Energy Exchange Across Location Boundaries

- Ambient energy does not "leak" from one Star System to another without a specific energy conduit — a Formation Zone river, a dimensional rift, or a deliberately constructed cultivation infrastructure.
- Formation Zone substance formation is bounded by the Formation Zone's spatial extent. Resources do not flow across boundaries without extraction.

### 23.3 — Cross-Location Combat and Consequence

- Combat effects are bounded by range (Part 14) and technique scope (Part 5).
- Cultivation aura effects extend within the active range of the cultivator's soul sense — they do not propagate beyond that range unless a specific technique governs the extension.
- Consequence of events at one location can affect other locations only through defined causal chains — political consequence, economic consequence, cultivation energy distribution change.

### 23.4 — Features Shared by Two Polities

A feature shared by two or more polities, such as a range, a pass or a river, has one physical parent, the place it lies in, so the containment of P13-4 stays strict, and a list of controlling polities; a controlling polity is not a second parent.

## Area 24 — Location Narration Standards

### 24.1 — Narration Standards (N13-1 through N13-10)

**N13-1 — Location Introduction Standard:** When a named location appears for the first time in prose, it must be introduced with the following, scaled to the place's weight in the scene: (a) its classification tier and class, held in the ledger and shown only through what the viewpoint character perceives; (b) its ambient energy class; (c) its affinity profile; (d) its special zone status if any; (e) at minimum one sensory descriptor that makes the location tangible. A place passed through needs (e) one sensory descriptor and whichever of (a) to (d) the scene uses; the rest is recorded in the ledger and delivered when a scene needs it. The ledger records (a) to (d) before the place is written. Prose introduces a place through what the viewpoint character perceives: sensory detail plus only the governance facts the scene uses; codes never appear in prose; a passing mention of a minor place needs only its name and one telling detail.

**N13-2 — Class Is Not Description:** Stating a location's class code (e.g. "a W-6 World") in prose is not description; codes stay in the ledger (N13-1). The class must be expressed through the narration of the location's actual properties — energy density, sensory environment, special zone presence, historical depth. The code is for governance; the narration is for the reader.

**N13-3 — Special Zones Are Active Governance:** A Sacred Ground, Vortex Formation Zone, Void Zone, or Dimensional Rift in a location is not background detail. It must be felt — sensory presence, effect on characters, political weight, narrative implication. A Sacred Ground that a character enters without noticing is a narration failure.

**N13-4 — Affinity Is Environmental Character:** A location's affinity is not a static label — it manifests in the environment. A Fire affinity world has the heat, the volcanic character, the amber-tinged sky. An Ice affinity world has the cold, the crystal formations, the white compression of ambient energy. The affinity must be expressed in prose texture.

**N13-5 — Historical Depth Is Narrated:** An Ancient Village or Ancient City does not simply say it is old. The antiquity is in the architecture, the accumulated formation residue, the layered tradition, the elder memory. Historical depth is shown through accumulated detail, not stated.

**N13-6 — Kingdom and Empire Character Is Narrated:** When a Kingdom or Empire appears, its dominant character — whether cultivation, technology, hybrid, or military — is expressed in the governance structures, the military, the daily life, the hierarchy characters interact with. Character is shown, not labeled.

**N13-7 — Void Zone Entry and Exit Are Mandatory Events:** A character entering or exiting a Void Zone must experience it explicitly in prose. The compression, the silence, the change in cultivation environment, the adjustment required. This cannot be elided for a character who feels the zone; Rule 11.3 sets who owes the narration and how a vessel or fold chain crossing is treated.

**N13-8 — Sacred Ground Grade Determines Prose Weight:** Higher-grade Sacred Grounds deserve proportionally more page in their introduction. As guidance for how much page a Sacred Ground of a given grade deserves, not a limit: S1 to S2, one paragraph of sensory establishment; S3 to S4, up to about 300 words; S5 to S6, up to about 600 words; S7 to S8, up to about 900 words of environmental, philosophical, and physical rendering. The confirmed Chapter Spec decides how much page a Sacred Ground gets in each Area, and a different length is reported as information, never as a FAIL.

**N13-9 — Dimensional Features Are Not Routine:** A Dimensional Rift, Resonance Plane access point, or Extradimensional Pocket is never a routine element of a scene. Its existence is always significant — it shapes what characters can do, what they notice, what risks they face.

**N13-10 — Location Changes Trigger Ledger Update:** Every change to a named location's classification, special zone status, or access conditions triggers a mandatory Post-Write Ledger Update to ledger-worlds-location (`guardrails/ledger/guardrail-ledger-update-law.md`).

### 24.2 — Worked Introductions

Three short examples show N13-1 and N13-2 at work. In each, the first version carries the place through what the viewpoint character perceives. The second states the code and lists the properties, which is the pattern N13-2 warns against (see also Part 36 Sin 7, Mechanical Inventory Writing). They show a pattern; they are not text to copy.

**A V-3 Common Village** (Class 2, Neutral affinity, no special zone, about two centuries old, a few hundred people).

Works: "Past the pear trees the road bent once and the village was simply there: thirty roofs of gray thatch leaning toward a well, smoke from every third chimney. The air asked nothing of her qi and gave nothing back; it was the plain air of any farming country, and the hens did not trouble to look up. At the well a woman watched her walk the whole length of the lane, then returned to her bucket as if strangers were weather. The doorframes were worn pale at hip height by a great many hands."

Fails: "This was a V-3 Common Village, Class 2 ambient energy, Neutral affinity, no special zone, established two centuries ago, with a population of about three hundred."

The first version carries the age and size of the village in the thatch and the worn doorframes, the ambient class and neutral affinity in air that neither helps nor hinders, and no special zone because there is nothing to feel.

**A C-8 Vortex City** (Class 5 to 6, Thunder affinity aligned with its Vortex, a Vortex Formation Zone, several hundred thousand people).

Works: "Long before the gate his tongue found metal in the air and the hair rose along his arms. Inside the wall, voices reached him a half-beat late, as if the street were pouring sound through water, and every iron rail wore a thin blue edge that crawled whenever the light failed. Porters in felt-soled boots crossed the square without looking up. The Vortex itself lay somewhere behind the Exchange Hall, and no one pointed toward it; they only stopped talking when the wind turned that way."

Fails: "The city was a C-8 Vortex City, Class 6 ambient energy, Thunder affinity, with a Vortex Formation Zone at its center and about four hundred thousand people."

The first version carries the size and standing of the city in the wall, the porters and the Exchange Hall, the Class 5 to 6 energy and Thunder affinity in the taste of metal, the raised hair, the late sound and the creeping blue light, and the Vortex Formation Zone in the way people avoid speaking of it.

**An S3 Sacred Ground** (Resonant Ground, Class 7, aligned Life/Wood affinity).

Works: "The fringe announced itself a day out: the grass leaned toward a point beyond the ridge, and the pack animal would not be led past a split boulder. By the time the ridge fell behind them the qi pressed on the chest like a hand held flat, steady and unhurried, and the lesser cultivators in the party walked with their jaws set. Moss furred the standing stones thicker than any damp could explain. Lines of pale light formed between them, hung for a breath, and let go. At the first stone an old marker had been cut with the names of those who had broken through here, the oldest almost worn smooth, and a green shoot had split its base."

Fails: "They entered an S3 Resonant Ground, a Class 7 zone with aligned Life/Wood affinity, where lesser cultivators feel qi pressure and energy formations occur spontaneously."

The first version carries the grade in the pressure the lesser cultivators feel and the lines of light that form on their own, the aligned affinity in the growth no damp explains, and the history of breakthroughs in the marker of names. A Sacred Ground must be felt (N13-3); at this grade a short passage is enough (N13-8).

## Area 26 — Disambiguation

**Part 13 vs. Part 21 (Civilization Law):** Part 13 classifies LOCATIONS by their intrinsic properties — energy class, affinity, special zones, dimensional features, territorial scale. Part 21 classifies CIVILIZATIONS by five axes including location quality. A World-class location (Part 13) is the spatial fact. The civilization that occupies it (Part 21 CCT) is the political and developmental fact. These are different layers of the same reality.

**Part 13 vs. Part 22 (Faction Law):** Part 13 defines what a location IS (its class, properties, zone status). Part 22 defines who CONTROLS it (faction type, FPT, territorial claim). A V-10 Sacred Village exists independently of which faction controls access to it.

**Part 13 vs. Part 14 (Distance Law):** Part 13 governs WHAT locations are and HOW they are classified. Part 14 governs HOW FAR APART they are and in what units distances between them are expressed.

**Part 13 vs. Part 15 (Travel Law):** Part 13 governs WHERE things are. Part 15 governs HOW characters move between them.

**Part 13 vs. Part 11 (Energy Substance Law):** Part 13 governs the SPATIAL PROPERTIES of Formation Zones — their extent, stability, type, and zone interaction rules. Part 11 governs WHAT SUBSTANCES form within those zones and the mechanics of extraction and use.

**Classification vs. Occupation:** A location's class is determined by its intrinsic properties. A V-1 Frontier Village does not become a V-10 Sacred Village because a Divine Sea Realm cultivator camps there for a week. Sacred Ground formation requires the sustained permanent alteration of the location's spatial-energetic structure (Area 9.1). Classification reflects the location's nature, not its current occupants.

## Area 27 — Climate, Weather and Seasons

### 27.1 — Governing Principle

Climate is the pattern a place's weather follows, and weather is that place's state at a moment. Both are facts of the location and bind once established (P13-3). This Area owns the climate facts of Saha and the rules for using weather in a scene. How weather is rendered belongs to Part 36 (Area 9, and CT-3 in Area 6.2), which chooses the form; nothing here is waived. Energy weather, the qi mists and unusual weather of Sacred Grounds and Formation Zones (Areas 9 and 10), has its source in the zone and is not ordinary weather. Ordinary weather is not a cultivation effect unless a source is on record (P13-3, P13-7).

### 27.3 — Weather in Scenes

**Rule 27.3.1 — Established Weather Holds.**
A scene's weather agrees with the region's climate, with the season and time of day for its date (Part 30 §1.3.2a for the Section window: the date itself and its plan season word are read from the calendar, Part 41 Area 2, Epoch, year and months, and Area 4, Phase Day conversion and Section windows, and from the scene's date line, Part 41 Area 5, The date line), and with every fact already established for the location. Weather changes inside an Area only by a change the scene shows.

**Rule 27.3.2 — Weather Is a Condition.**
Weather changes what people can do: what they can see, where they can stand, how long a road takes (Part 15), how a fight goes (Area 22.4), what the harvest brings, who can gather. It is a fact the story uses, not a mood painted over the scene.

**Rule 27.3.3 — Weather and Light Mark Time.**
Season, light and weather make elapsed time visible and place a scene in its date without a label. A time skip or a change of season is shown by what the weather has done to the place and its people.

**Rule 27.3.4 — No Weather Floor.**
No Area is required to carry weather. An Area carries it where the Area block's Place, in-story time, weather and light field names it, where it changes what people do, or where the scene earns it. This Area adds no sensory floor to any unit (Part 36 Area 9.1; Part 38 Rule 2.3.5a).

**Rule 27.3.5 — Anything Beyond the Ordinary Needs a Source.**
Weather outside a region's climate, such as an unseasonal storm or a fog that affects qi, needs a source: a zone (Areas 9 and 10), a technique, a relic or an event. Without one it is a change of location state with no governing cause (P13-3).

### 27.4 — What Fails

- Weather that contradicts the region, the season or an established fact.
- Weather as backdrop only: nothing anyone does depends on it.
- An unseasonal or energy-bearing weather event with no source.
- A paragraph of sky standing in for a scene's setting.
- The same weather rendered the same way in every scene of a place.

### 27.5 — Boundaries

**Rule 27.5.1 — Who Owns What.**
Part 3 (Area 14) owns ambient energy and cultivation. Part 14 owns distance and Part 15 owns travel time and conditions. Part 25 owns seasonal customs, festivals and harvest rites. Part 41 owns the calendar, Part 30 the Section durations. Part 36 owns how weather feels on the page and chooses the form; nothing here is waived. Part 38 owns the World, setting and people fields that name weather in the plan (Rule 2.4.10).
