## Overview

Part 20 is the Starward Saga Canon's Ideology Law — the governing domain for every character's organized system of beliefs about reality, value, correct action, and self. Ideology is not personality (how a character engages the world), not culture (Part 25's inherited collective value patterns), not the Cultivation Heart (Part 4's will architecture), and not psychology (Part 26's interior experience) — it is the conceptual framework through which a character interprets all of these, and it is the governance center of action: every significant action a character takes must be traceable to their current ideological state or recorded as a governed deviation.

Area 1 establishes the foundations: the Three Layers of Character Ideology (Core, Operational, Surface) whose gaps drive hypocrisy, self-deception, and revelation; the four action-driver rules (action-ideology coherence, unconscious ideology, ideology-determines-enemy, ideology under pressure); and the Five Moral Orientations (Altruistic, Self-Directed, Communal, Transcendent, Nihilistic). Area 2 supplies the twelve foundational ideology categories with their variations, the Compound Ideology Profile structure (Primary/Secondary/Tertiary), and the five-level Polarity scale from Fluid to Absolutist. Area 3 defines the Ideology Ledger — the mandatory governance document recording every named character's ideology profile, layer mapping, event history, Active Contradictions, and Blindspots — together with the ten IE event types, change-threshold mechanics, rupture governance, blindspot law, and contradiction resolution law.

From these foundations, Part 20 builds outward across the remaining Areas: how local environment, faction doctrine, information and misinformation, cultivation advancement, and technology exposure shape and pressure ideology (Area 4); the ideological architecture of good, evil, neutral, and holy characters, of deception, and of hypocrisy (Area 5); cultivation-specific dynamics — path affinities, realm-adequacy thresholds, ideological lag, and generational conflict (Area 6); technology-specific dynamics and the series' central technology-versus-cultivation ideological engine (Area 7); the five Ideological Stability States, the Ideological Void, and the five Reconstruction Pathways (Area 8); ideological compatibility, transmission, and conflict between characters (Area 9); the ideology interfaces with combat, consequence, faction, culture, psychology, and information law (Area 10); the eight narration standards N20-1 through N20-8 (Area 11); the special formations of Post-Cataclysm Ideology, Ideological Extremism, and Absolute-Tier transcendent ideology (Area 12); and the master reference tables — the Ideology Category Quick Reference and the full Cross-Part Dependency Master Table (Area 13).

This file is the single authority for the whole of Part 20; there is no layer beneath it.

## Area 1 — The Nature of Ideology

### 1.1 — What Ideology Is

**Ideology** is a character's organized system of beliefs about:
- The nature of reality and what is true
- The nature of value and what matters
- The nature of correct action and what should be done
- The nature of self and who the character is in relation to all of the above

Ideology is not personality. Personality describes *how* a character engages with the world — their style, emotional tendencies, social approach. Ideology describes *what the character believes the world is* and *what place they have in it*.

Ideology is not culture. Culture (Part 25) describes the collective value patterns, norms, and conventions a character inherits. Ideology is the personal belief system a character builds from — and sometimes against — their cultural inheritance.

Ideology is not the Cultivation Heart (Part 4). The Cultivation Heart is the will architecture that governs how much of a cultivator's potential they can reach at the limit (Part 4 Area 3). Ideology is the conceptual framework that gives the will's direction meaning — the explanatory architecture that surrounds and justifies it.

Ideology is not psychology (Part 26). Psychology governs the interior experience of a character — trauma, grief, moral injury, identity crisis, the felt quality of being alive. Ideology governs the belief framework through which the character interprets and gives meaning to that experience. Psychological wounds generate ideological shifts; ideological frameworks shape psychological interpretation. They are mutually shaping but governed by separate Parts.

**The Three Layers of Character Ideology:**

| Layer | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Core Ideology** | The foundational belief about reality, value, and self that rarely shifts except through catastrophic experience | "Strength is the only true currency — all else is temporary" |
| **Operational Ideology** | The day-to-day beliefs and principles that guide decisions and interactions | "I ally with those stronger than my enemies; I discard them when the calculus changes" |
| **Surface Ideology** | The expressed beliefs — what the character claims to believe and projects publicly | "I serve my sect and honor its traditions" |

These three layers may align completely, partially, or not at all. The gap between Surface and Core ideology is the engine of hypocrisy, self-deception, and narrative revelation.

### 1.2 — Ideology as Action Driver

Ideology is the governance center of action. Every significant action a character takes is ultimately ideologically driven — even when the character does not recognize their ideology as such. This means:

**Rule 20.1.2.1 — Action-Ideology Coherence:** Every significant action in the narrative must be traceable to the character's current ideological state. If the action is not coherent with the character's recorded ideology, one of two things is true: (1) the ideology has changed and the Ideology Ledger must be updated; (2) the character is acting against their ideology under duress, deception, or psychological crisis — which itself must be recorded.

**Rule 20.1.2.2 — Ideology Is Unconscious as Often as Conscious:** Characters do not always know their own ideology. They may say they believe one thing and act according to another. They may hold ideological contradictions simultaneously without awareness. Ideology operating below conscious articulation is among the most powerful narrative forces in the series.

**Rule 20.1.2.3 — Ideology Determines Enemy:** Who a character considers an enemy — not a target but a *genuine* enemy — is a direct expression of their ideology. Enemies are not merely dangerous; they are ideologically threatening. The most profound conflicts in the series arise when two characters with irreconcilable ideologies inhabit the same story.

**Rule 20.1.2.4 — Ideology Under Pressure:** Pressure does not eliminate ideology — it reveals which layer is real. A character who claims to believe in mercy will reveal, under genuine pressure, whether that belief is Core (genuinely structural), Operational (practically maintained), or Surface (socially performed). Pressure is the diagnostic of ideological depth.

### 1.3 — Ideology vs. Morality

Ideology contains moral frameworks but is not reducible to morality. The distinction matters narratively:

- **Morality** asks: Is this action right or wrong?
- **Ideology** provides the framework that answers: By what standard? For whom? Evaluated by what authority?

A character with a Strength-Dominant ideology may murder an innocent without moral crisis because their ideology does not grant the innocent any particular moral status. The act is not wrong by their ideology's standards — the innocent was simply weaker and therefore expendable. The narrative must render this ideological coherence rather than imposing external moral judgment onto the character's decision.

At the same time, Part 20 does not endorse any ideology. The SSC Canon renders ideologies with coherence and internal consistency while maintaining narrative space for other characters to challenge, oppose, and sometimes defeat ideologically coherent antagonists.

**The Five Moral Orientations of Ideology:**

| Orientation | Description | Example Ideological Position |
|------------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **Altruistic** | The good of others is primary; self-interest is subordinate or tools | "I exist to serve those weaker than myself — my cultivation is not mine, it is theirs" |
| **Self-Directed** | The self is primary; others matter instrumentally | "My advancement is the only real purpose — all else is means" |
| **Communal** | The group (sect, family, faction, civilization) is primary; individual serves group | "My sect's name must endure beyond me — I am nothing outside it" |
| **Transcendent** | A principle beyond self, others, or group is primary | "The Dao cares nothing for us — I pursue only its truth, regardless of consequence" |
| **Nihilistic** | No axis is primary; all value is constructed, temporary, or absent | "Nothing matters beyond the moment — so I take what is in front of me" |

Most characters hold hybrid moral orientations. The dominant orientation is the one that prevails when all five axes come into direct conflict.

## Area 2 — The Ideology Taxonomy

### 2.1 — Foundational Ideology Categories

All character ideologies in the Starward Saga belong to one or more of twelve foundational categories. These categories are not mutually exclusive — most characters hold compound ideologies drawn from two or three foundational categories.

**Category I — Strength-Dominant Ideology (SDI)**

The foundational belief that power (cultivation power, military power, political power, informational power) is the real currency of all existence. Everything that appears to operate on different terms — compassion, justice, loyalty — is ultimately a power calculation in disguise. The SDI character does not respect morality; they respect what morality is able to accomplish. When morality can be leveraged to accumulate power, they use it. When it cannot, it is discarded.

*SDI Variations:*
- **Pure-Force SDI:** Raw combat power is the only measure. Tactical intelligence matters only insofar as it amplifies physical force output.
- **Network-Power SDI:** Social and informational power are the real currencies. Cultivation strength is a tool; the real power is in who owes you.
- **Structural-Power SDI:** Institutional power (sect leadership, governmental authority, legal authority) is the highest form. The SDI practitioner who controls the rules controls the outcome of every contest conducted by those rules.
- **Potential-Power SDI:** Future power is real power. The SDI practitioner who correctly reads cultivation potential invests in future power even when present power is negligible.

*SDI characters frequently:* justify exploitation through productivity ("the strong must use the weak or the weak are wasted"), maintain surface ideologies of loyalty while calculating defection windows, experience ideological crisis when they encounter an opponent whose power exceeds their own without exploiting them (which their ideology cannot account for).

---

**Category II — Justice-Centered Ideology (JCI)**

The foundational belief that reality operates according to principles of fairness, desert, and accountability — and that one of the character's primary responsibilities is to enforce or restore that alignment. JCI characters do not experience harm as pain first; they experience it as *wrong* first. Their primary question when observing the world is not "what happened?" but "was this right?"

*JCI Variations:*
- **Retributive-JCI:** Those who do harm must suffer equivalent harm. Justice is balance. The scales must be balanced personally, regardless of institutional authority.
- **Restorative-JCI:** Justice is achieved when those harmed are made whole and those who harmed are accountable. Punishment matters less than repair.
- **Structural-JCI:** Individual actions are less important than structural conditions. Justice requires changing the institutions that produce unjust outcomes.
- **Cultivator-JCI:** The strong have a special obligation to protect the weak — because their power creates an asymmetry, they bear disproportionate moral responsibility. Strength without service is corruption.
- **Cosmic-JCI:** The Dao or cosmos has a justice built into it. What is truly wrong will be destroyed eventually. The practitioner's role is to accelerate that justice or align with it.

*JCI characters frequently:* experience profound conflict when the path of justice requires actions that appear unjust (torturing one to save many); accumulate personal enemies by applying their justice standard consistently without regard for power differentials; experience ideological crisis when they are confronted with their own unjust actions.

---

**Category III — Loyalty-Bound Ideology (LBI)**

The foundational belief that relationships — to persons, to lineages, to institutions, to principles — create binding obligations that supersede abstract reasoning. The LBI character does not ask "is this action right?" first; they ask "does this honor or betray the bond?" Loyalty is the organizing principle from which all other values are evaluated.

*LBI Variations:*
- **Personal-Loyalty LBI:** Specific relationships (master, sworn brother, beloved) are the binding units. The LBI character will violate any principle to protect those specific persons.
- **Lineage-Loyalty LBI:** Blood and cultivation lineage are the binding units. Sect, clan, family — these come before any individual. Individuals are expressions of lineage; the lineage is what matters.
- **Institutional-Loyalty LBI:** The institution (sect, government, organization) is the primary loyalty object regardless of who leads it. The institution's survival is worth more than any member's principles.
- **Principled-Loyalty LBI:** Loyalty is owed to the principle the group embodies — and withdrawn when the group betrays the principle. "I am loyal to what this sect stands for; if it stops standing for that, my loyalty ends."
- **Code-Loyalty LBI:** A personal code (honor code, warrior's oath, merchant's pledge) is the organizing loyalty unit. The code may mandate loyalty to enemies when they follow the same code.

*LBI characters frequently:* discover that their multiple loyalties are in irreconcilable conflict; experience profound identity crisis when a loyalty object betrays them (because their identity is organized around the loyalty, not the self); take actions that appear irrational to outsiders because loyalty logic is opaque to those who don't share the structure.

---

**Category IV — Freedom-Sovereign Ideology (FSI)**

The foundational belief that self-determination — the right to choose one's own path without constraint from external authority — is the highest value. FSI characters resist all institutional authority, social expectation, and conventional moral framework that they did not choose. This is not nihilism; the FSI character has values. Their values simply originate entirely from within rather than being inherited or imposed.

*FSI Variations:*
- **Personal-Sovereignty FSI:** No one has authority over me. I will determine my cultivation path, my alliances, my moral code, my life's purpose — and no sect, government, or cosmic force has standing to override that.
- **Anarchic-Political FSI:** No institution has legitimate authority over any individual. The FSI practitioner opposes all hierarchy actively, not just personally. This can generate revolutionary politics.
- **Dao-Sovereignty FSI:** The Dao itself is freedom — the ultimate state is cultivation beyond all constraints, including the constraints of self, relationship, and identity. This FSI variant often becomes transcendent or ascetic.
- **Selective-Authority FSI:** Authority is legitimate only when chosen. The FSI practitioner consents to specific hierarchical relationships (master, guild, temporary alliance) while rejecting imposed ones.

*FSI characters frequently:* make powerful allies uncomfortable because the alliance is always conditional; have difficulty maintaining long-term cultivation relationships because relationships carry mutual obligation that FSI experience as constraint; occasionally discover that their commitment to freedom was itself a constraint.

---

**Category V — Transcendence-Seeking Ideology (TSI)**

The foundational belief that ordinary existence — personal survival, social standing, material accumulation, even cultivation advancement — is not the real point. There is something beyond. The TSI character is organized around reaching, understanding, or becoming that something beyond — and ordinary concerns are evaluated entirely by how much they advance or impede that goal.

*TSI Variations:*
- **Cultivation-Transcendence TSI:** The highest objective is the cultivation ceiling — whatever lies beyond the current known limit of the Heavenly Scale. Nothing else genuinely matters by comparison.
- **Dao-Comprehension TSI:** The real point is understanding the Dao itself — not accumulating power derived from it, but comprehending its nature. Power may or may not be a byproduct.
- **Cosmic-Role TSI:** The character believes they have a cosmically significant function. Finding and fulfilling that function is the transcendent goal. Personal desire, social approval, even survival are secondary to role fulfillment.
- **Enlightenment-TSI:** The transcendent goal is a state of being — freedom from suffering, complete understanding, liberation from the cycle of karma or cultivation striving. This TSI variant often appears in elder cultivators who have exhausted ordinary ambitions.
- **Void-TSI:** The transcendent goal is dissolution of self into the void, the absolute, or the undifferentiated. This is rare and unsettling to most other characters — it is indistinguishable from suicidal ideology at first glance but operates according to a completely different metaphysical logic.

*TSI characters frequently:* seem detached or indifferent to events that galvanize other characters; perform startling acts of self-sacrifice that appear irrational without the transcendent framework; occasionally reveal that their transcendence framework is a defense mechanism against ordinary human vulnerability.

---

**Category VI — Order-Maintaining Ideology (OMI)**

The foundational belief that stability, structure, hierarchy, and institutional continuity are primary goods. Chaos is the enemy. Disruption — even disruptive change aimed at improvement — must be evaluated carefully against the value of what it may destroy. The OMI character may be a powerful force for stability or a rigid enforcer of unjust structures — both are possible within this ideology.

*OMI Variations:*
- **Cultivation-Hierarchy OMI:** The cultivation hierarchy is the natural order. Realm determines worth. All social structures that reflect this hierarchy are legitimate; those that challenge it are dangerous.
- **Institutional-Continuity OMI:** Existing institutions (sects, governments, guilds) are themselves valuable because they represent accumulated wisdom and stability. Reform should be incremental and internal, not revolutionary.
- **Cosmic-Order OMI:** The cosmos has a designed order — cultivation tiers, faction hierarchies, species relationships — and human attempts to reshape that order produce catastrophe. The practitioner serves the cosmic order.
- **Pragmatic-Stability OMI:** The OMI practitioner supports order not from belief in its intrinsic rightness but from conviction that chaos costs more than structure costs. Even an unjust order may be preferable to disorder.
- **Meritocratic-Hierarchy OMI:** Hierarchy is right, but it must be based on actual merit (cultivation ability, skill, service), not birth or lineage. This variant actively polices the hierarchy to ensure it reflects true worth.

*OMI characters frequently:* become villains when the order they maintain is revealed to be built on exploitation; experience profound crisis when a figure they consider their legitimate superior acts in ways the OMI character cannot reconcile with order-values; defend positions that from outside appear as complicity with oppression.

---

**Category VII — Ambition-Driven Ideology (ADI)**

The foundational belief that advancement — in cultivation, in social standing, in power, in wealth, in legacy — is the organizing purpose of existence. The ADI character's guiding question is always: *does this advance my position?* Not advancement for what purpose — the advancement itself is the purpose. This distinguishes ADI from SDI: SDI treats power as a means of existing safely; ADI treats advancement as the meaning of existing at all.

*ADI Variations:*
- **Cultivation-Advancement ADI:** Higher realm is the goal. Every resource, relationship, and decision is evaluated by its contribution to cultivation speed and depth.
- **Legacy-Advancement ADI:** What I leave behind is the real ambition — sect founding, knowledge preservation, bloodline continuation, the name that lasts beyond my lifespan.
- **Dominion-Advancement ADI:** The goal is the largest domain under control — territory, followers, influence. This ADI variant produces empire builders and sect patriarchs who cannot stop expanding.
- **Knowledge-Advancement ADI:** The goal is the widest and deepest knowledge — techniques, relics, universal mechanics, historical secrets. The accumulation of understanding is the meaning of the ADI practitioner's existence.
- **Status-Advancement ADI:** Social recognition and prestige are the core measurement. The ADI practitioner wants to be *acknowledged* as great, not merely to be great.

*ADI characters frequently:* sacrifice relationships that cannot be converted into advancement; experience the ADI plateau — reaching a goal only to find it immediately unsatisfying and requiring a new, larger goal; have profound difficulty with characters who do not share the advancement framework (those who are *content*).

---

**Category VIII — Vengeance-Anchored Ideology (VAI)**

The foundational belief that a specific wrong — a harm done to self, to loved ones, to a group — must be rectified through the suffering of the responsible party. VAI is not justice (JCI) because it is personal and specific rather than principled and universal. The VAI character does not seek justice; they seek this specific person or group to suffer *this* specific consequence. Everything else is provisional — relationships, cultivation path, survival itself — organized around the central drive.

*VAI Variations:*
- **Targeted-Personal VAI:** A specific individual or small group is the target. All other concerns are subordinated to reaching, defeating, and destroying this target.
- **Collective-VAI:** An entire faction, civilization, or category of person bears the guilt. The VAI practitioner cannot distinguish between the original perpetrators and all members of the responsible group.
- **Proxy-VAI:** The original perpetrators are dead, inaccessible, or already destroyed. The VAI practitioner redirects to proxies — successors, inheritors, symbolic representatives of the original harm.
- **Expanding-VAI:** What began as specific vengeance metastasizes into a broader ideological program. The original wound becomes a lens through which all perceived injustice toward the VAI practitioner's group is filtered, making the target list perpetually expand.
- **Self-Directed VAI:** The VAI practitioner holds themselves as the object of vengeance — for failures, betrayals, or acts they cannot forgive. This variant drives self-destruction patterns that appear externally directed.

*VAI characters frequently:* experience the VAI completion crisis — when the target is destroyed, the organizing purpose disappears, leaving a void; discover that their VAI has expanded to include their own allies or even themselves; struggle with the moment the target shows vulnerability or genuine remorse, which threatens the VAI's moral foundation.

---

**Category IX — Truth-Seeking Ideology (TRI)**

The foundational belief that reality has a structure that can be understood, and that understanding it correctly is a primary obligation. The TRI character does not experience comfort of false belief as a trade-off worth making — truth, even devastating truth, is preferable to comforting falsehood. This ideology can produce scientists, philosophers, archaeologists of the Dao, or characters so committed to accuracy that they become socially destructive.

*TRI Variations:*
- **Empirical-TRI:** Truth is found through observation, systematic investigation, and experimentation. The cultivator who approaches the Dao scientifically. Technology-aligned characters frequently hold Empirical-TRI.
- **Philosophical-TRI:** Truth is found through rigorous reasoning and conceptual analysis. The TRI practitioner who debates cultivation principles for decades before touching qi.
- **Dao-Navigation TRI:** The Dao itself is the truth. The TRI practitioner reads the Dao directly as a navigational act — the Dao shows what is real; all social truths are provisional by comparison.
- **Historical-TRI:** What actually happened — recovered from falsification, legend inflation, and motivated distortion — is the truth that matters most. This TRI variant produces historians who are dangerous to establishments built on false history.
- **Self-Knowing TRI:** The truth most worth finding is the truth about the self. The TRI practitioner's entire project is radical self-knowledge, including knowledge of their own ideological contradictions.

*TRI characters frequently:* cause harm by telling the truth at the wrong moment; experience profound conflicts with LBI characters (who prioritize loyalty over accuracy) and Surface-ideology performers (who experience TRI revelation as attack); occasionally discover that their commitment to truth-seeking was itself organized around an unexamined assumption.

---

**Category X — Protection-Centered Ideology (PCI)**

The foundational belief that those one is responsible for — loved ones, the weak, one's sect members, one's civilization, life in general — must be protected. The PCI character's primary motivation is not their own advancement but the shielding of their protection domain. This ideology is among the most powerful in the series because it is both deeply sympathetic and capable of producing atrocities when the protection domain is defined broadly and the means used become extreme.

*PCI Variations:*
- **Family-Anchored PCI:** The protection domain is specific loved ones. Everything else is instrumental to their safety.
- **Sect-Protective PCI:** The sect and its members are the protection domain. The elder who will do anything — including terrible things — to protect the younger generation.
- **Universal-Protective PCI:** All sentient life (or all human life, or all cultivation-capable life) is the protection domain. This variant resembles altruistic-JCI but differs because the PCI practitioner is not primarily concerned with justice — they are primarily concerned with shielding.
- **Civilization-Protective PCI:** A civilization or cultural tradition is the protection domain. Its survival justifies compromises that would otherwise be unconscionable.
- **Future-Protective PCI:** The protection domain is those who do not yet exist — future generations, potential cultivators who will be born, the civilization a thousand years hence. This variant produces characters willing to sacrifice the present for an imagined future.

*PCI characters frequently:* discover that protecting one group requires harming another; experience the PCI paradox — the actions taken in the name of protection become the thing that most harms the protection domain; have difficulty distinguishing protection from control.

---

**Category XI — Hedonistic-Libertine Ideology (HLI)**

The foundational belief that pleasure, beauty, sensation, and the full experience of existence are primary goods. Not merely the pleasure of the flesh — this ideology encompasses the pleasure of cultivation itself, of beautiful technique, of aesthetic experience in combat, of the sensory richness of a world full of cultivators and technology. The HLI practitioner is not irresponsible — they may be disciplined and strategic — but what they are disciplined *toward* is experience, not advancement or justice.

*HLI Variations:*
- **Sensory-HLI:** Physical pleasure and aesthetic experience are the primary goods. The cultivator who cares more about how a technique *feels* to execute than what it achieves.
- **Aesthetic-Discipline HLI:** The cultivation of beauty and refinement in all things — technique, appearance, social performance, speech — is the organizing principle. This variant produces master artists, legendary courtesans, and the most dangerous social manipulators.
- **Experience-Maximizing HLI:** The goal is to experience as many different states of existence as possible. This variant drives exploration, experimentation, and a tendency to take risks that purely advancement-focused cultivators consider irrational.
- **Dao-of-Joy HLI:** The deepest insight into the Dao is available through uninhibited experience rather than ascetic restriction. This philosophical HLI produces controversial cultivation methods that may be more or less genuine.
- **Epicurean-Moderate HLI:** Pleasure is the primary good, but excess destroys pleasure's value. The sophisticated HLI practitioner cultivates the conditions that enable sustained pleasure: health, social warmth, aesthetic environment, moderate indulgence.

*HLI characters frequently:* appear lightweight until the moment their genuine capability is revealed; collect enemies among ascetic and discipline-obsessed characters who mistake their pleasure orientation for weakness; experience the HLI crisis when the depth of their hedonism comes into direct conflict with loss, suffering, or catastrophic consequence.

---

**Category XII — Sacred-Covenant Ideology (SCI)**

The foundational belief that a sacred relationship — to a deity, to a cosmic principle, to an ancestor, to a cultivated lineage — is binding and constitutes the highest authority. The SCI character does not evaluate actions by self-interest, by loyalty to persons, or by abstract principle — they evaluate them by the covenant's terms. What does the covenant require? That is the question. The covenant's authority transcends all else.

*SCI Variations:*
- **Deity-Covenant SCI:** A specific divine entity (or what the character takes to be divine) has issued commands or established terms. The SCI practitioner fulfills those terms regardless of personal cost.
- **Ancestor-Covenant SCI:** The cultivation lineage's founders made promises and established standards. The SCI practitioner is their living embodiment — to deviate is to betray not just the past but the covenant itself.
- **Dao-Covenant SCI:** The practitioner has entered into a relationship with the Dao itself — a personal covenant of alignment. Specific behaviors violate that alignment; the SCI practitioner avoids them categorically.
- **Oath-Covenant SCI:** A specific oath taken at a specific moment constitutes the covenant. The SCI practitioner may or may not believe in the cosmic framework into which the oath was embedded — but the oath itself is irrevocable.
- **Destiny-Covenant SCI:** The practitioner believes they are cosmically assigned to a specific role or outcome. The covenant is not with a person or deity but with the structure of fate itself. This variant produces characters who appear fearless because they believe their survival is guaranteed until their destined purpose is fulfilled.

*SCI characters frequently:* experience devastating ideological rupture when the covenant's source is revealed to be morally compromised (the deity is cruel, the ancestor was a murderer, the Dao-alignment was a manipulation); produce intractable conflicts because they cannot be argued or bought — only the covenant terms can motivate them; occasionally find their covenant-following produces outcomes that appear miraculously aligned.

### 2.2 — Compound Ideology Profiles

Most significant characters hold compound ideologies — two or three categories in a specific hierarchy. The hierarchy determines which ideology governs when the categories come into conflict.

**Compound Ideology Profile Structure:**
- **Primary Category** — governs by default; provides the foundational framework for all evaluation
- **Secondary Category** — governs when the primary category does not directly address the situation, or modifies how the primary category is expressed
- **Tertiary Category** — provides the aesthetic or emotional coloring of ideology without directly governing decisions

**Example Compound Profiles:**

| Profile Name | Primary | Secondary | Tertiary | Character Type |
|-------------|---------|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Righteous Warrior | JCI | LBI | PCI | A cultivator who pursues justice within the framework of personal loyalty; will ultimately choose justice when the two conflict |
| Calculating Patriarch | SDI | OMI | LBI | A sect leader who maintains order through power while using loyalty language — power is real; order is the method; loyalty is the performance |
| Driven Avenger | VAI | ADI | SDI | A revenge-seeker who has organized their entire cultivation advancement around the VAI goal; strength is the tool |
| Transcendent Scholar | TSI | TRI | HLI | A cultivator who pursues cosmic truth with intellectual discipline but takes genuine pleasure in the process |
| Loyal Guardian | LBI | PCI | OMI | An elder whose loyalty to their master organizes their protective drive; they will protect their ward even against their ward's explicit wishes |
| Revolutionary Truth-Seeker | TRI | FSI | JCI | A character who finds truth regardless of institutional authority; their freedom is in service to accuracy |
| Holy Avenger | SCI | VAI | JCI | A covenant-bound character whose sacred terms include vengeance against specific enemies; they experience their violence as divine service |

### 2.3 — Ideological Polarity

Every ideological position has a **polarity**: the degree to which it is held as absolute versus provisional.

| Polarity Level | Description | Narrative Characteristic |
|---------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Polarity-1 (Fluid)** | The ideology is held as the best current framework, consciously provisional | Character frequently questions and examines their own positions |
| **Polarity-2 (Settled)** | The ideology is held with conviction but can be updated through sufficient evidence or experience | Character holds their ground but is genuinely persuadable |
| **Polarity-3 (Anchored)** | The ideology is held as fundamental; updating it requires significant narrative events (PIS-6+) | Character resists ideological change; it takes major experiences to shift them |
| **Polarity-4 (Crystallized)** | The ideology is held as absolute within specific domains; those domains cannot be entered by contrary argument | Character has ideological no-go zones that produce categorical responses regardless of argument quality |
| **Polarity-5 (Absolutist)** | The entire ideology is held as categorically true; no evidence or argument can enter | Character is doctrinaire; ideological challenge produces hostility or violence |

High polarity is not inherently villainous. A Polarity-5 SCI character who has covenanted with a genuinely moral cosmic principle may be the most reliably virtuous character in the narrative. A Polarity-1 SDI character may be the most adaptable and survivable. Polarity describes *rigidity of belief*, not quality of belief.

## Area 3 — The Ideology Ledger

### 3.1 — What the Ideology Ledger Is

The **Ideology Ledger** is the canonical record of every character's ideological state and its history of change. It is not a narrative document — it is a governance document. Authors consult the Ideology Ledger before writing significant character decisions to ensure those decisions are ideologically coherent or ideologically justified deviations.

**The Ideology Ledger Contains:**
1. **Current Ideology Profile** — the character's present ideological position: Primary Category, Secondary Category, Tertiary Category, and Polarity level for each
2. **Core / Operational / Surface Ideology Mapping** — what the character's deepest beliefs are versus what they act from day to day versus what they publicly express
3. **Ideology Event History** — the chronological record of every ideological shift, challenge, and confirmation
4. **Active Contradictions** — unresolved tensions between layers or categories that the character has not yet integrated
5. **Ideological Blindspots** — beliefs the character holds without awareness; things the character cannot see because their ideology does not give them the tools to see it

**Rule 20.3.1.1 — Ledger for Named Characters:** A named character who appears in about three or more scenes (a scene is one Area, as in Parts 18 and 19) typically has an Ideology Ledger entry. The count is a guide, never a gate: the story and the Chapter Spec decide when an entry is built, and Part 37 Threshold C-1 stays the file rule.

**Rule 20.3.1.2 — Ledger Primacy Over Momentary Inspiration:** If an authorial impulse to have a character act in a certain way conflicts with the Ideology Ledger, the Ledger governs. Either (a) the action must be grounded in the Ideology Ledger's current state, (b) the action triggers an Ideology Ledger update (the character is changing), or (c) the action is an ideological rupture event (the character is acting against their ideology under pressure, which has its own governance rules in Area 3.5).

**Rule 20.3.1.3 — Protagonist Ledgers Are Not Protected:** The protagonist's ideology is not exempt from challenge, contradiction, or change. Protagonist narrative immunity in ideological matters violates the same principle as protagonist narrative immunity in consequence matters (Part 27 Area 9.1, Standard N27-6). The protagonist's ideology must be genuinely tested, not merely affirmed.

### 3.2 — Ideology Ledger Entry Format

**Character Name:** [Character's full name and aliases]

### 3.3 — Ideology Event Types

Every event recorded in the Ideology Ledger is classified by type:

| Event Type | Description | Minimum Impact Level |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **IE-1 — Confirmation Event** | An experience that confirms the character's current ideology | PIS-2 |
| **IE-2 — Challenge Event** | An experience that challenges but does not change the current ideology | PIS-3 |
| **IE-3 — Expansion Event** | An experience that adds nuance, new categories, or depth without replacing core beliefs | PIS-4 |
| **IE-4 — Partial Shift Event** | An experience that modifies Operational Ideology without touching Core Ideology | PIS-5 |
| **IE-5 — Core Shift Event** | An experience that modifies Core Ideology | PIS-7 |
| **IE-6 — Ideology Crisis Event** | An experience that destabilizes the entire ideology structure simultaneously | PIS-8 |
| **IE-7 — Ideology Collapse Event** | An experience that destroys the character's operating ideology, requiring complete reconstruction | PIS-9 |
| **IE-8 — Ideology Crystallization Event** | An experience that permanently fixes a specific belief domain at Polarity-4 | PIS-6 |
| **IE-9 — Ideology Reconciliation Event** | An experience that resolves a recorded Active Contradiction | PIS-5 |
| **IE-10 — Ideological Revelation Event** | The character becomes consciously aware of a previously unconscious ideological structure | PIS-4 |

The IE levels that Area 4.4 lists for cultivation events are guidance posts (see Area 4.4); the minimums above do not make those listings a gate.

### 3.4 — Ideology Change Mechanics

Ideology does not change arbitrarily. It changes through specific types of experience that meet threshold criteria.

**Ideology Change Threshold Table:**

| Layer | Minimum Event | Minimum Experience Intensity |
|-------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Surface Ideology | IE-1 | PIS-2 (any perception that surface performance is being challenged) |
| Operational Ideology | IE-3 or IE-4 | IE-3 at PIS-4, IE-4 at PIS-5 (significant repeated experiences or one powerful experience) |
| Core Ideology | IE-5 | PIS-7 (experiences that strike the foundational identity layer) |
| Polarity Increase | IE-8 | PIS-6 (experiences that produce ideological commitment hardening) |
| Polarity Decrease | IE-3 + IE-10 | PIS-4 + self-reflection (difficult to achieve; requires both challenge and awareness) |

For a position held at Polarity-3, the PIS-6 or more named in Area 2.3 applies on top of the row for its layer, not in place of it. The whole table is guidance, not a gate, as with the IE levels in Area 4.4.

**Rule 20.3.4.1 — Core Ideology Cannot Be Changed by Argument Alone:** Intellectual counter-argument, however brilliant, cannot change Core Ideology. Core Ideology is changed only by lived experience of sufficient PIS intensity. Characters who appear to change their Core Ideology through argument alone are (a) changing only their Surface Ideology, (b) experiencing an IE-10 revelation facilitated by the argument, or (c) in mid-crisis already (IE-6 or IE-7 in progress).

**Rule 20.3.4.2 — Repeated Small Events Accumulate:** Multiple IE-2 (Challenge) events in the same ideological domain can build up until they amount to an IE-3 (Expansion) or IE-4 (Partial Shift) event. As an illustration, five IE-2 events in the same domain can amount to one IE-4 event. This is an illustration, not a count: no counter triggers anything, and the story and the Chapter Spec decide whether and when the buildup happens.

**Rule 20.3.4.3 — Ideology Does Not Flip:** Even IE-7 (Ideology Collapse) does not produce an immediate opposite ideology. A character who held SDI (Strength-Dominant) through Polarity-4 and experiences an IE-7 collapses into ideological disorientation — they may trend toward JCI or TSI in reconstruction, but the reconstruction process takes narrative time and passes through an ideology-void phase (governed jointly by Part 26 Area 2.4 — Identity Crisis, and Part 20 Area 8 — Ideological Void).

### 3.5 — Ideological Rupture Events

An **Ideological Rupture Event** is when a character acts against their own recorded ideology under extreme pressure, deception, or psychological crisis. This is not a Ledger error — it is a canonical event type.

**Ideological Rupture Conditions:**
- Psychological state at PIS-8 or higher overrides ideological processing (Part 26 Area 10 — combat psychology override)
- Complete information deprivation: the character acts on false information that, had it been correct, would have been ideologically coherent
- Extreme duress where the character's survival instinct overrides all ideological processing
- Soul or mind control technique (Part 4 Area 2.3 — Weapon Soul or Bind Soul) that temporarily replaces ideological processing

**Rule 20.3.5.1 — Rupture Events Have Consequences:** An Ideological Rupture Event is recorded in the Ideology Ledger and produces either (a) an IE-5 or IE-6 based on the character's retrospective confrontation with the rupture, or (b) a Core Ideology rationalization — the character retroactively reconstructs an ideological explanation for the ruptured behavior. Rationalizations are recorded as Active Contradictions.

**Rule 20.3.5.2 — Rupture Cannot Be Forgotten:** Part 20 prohibits authorial erasure of Ideological Rupture Events. The character may choose to forget, suppress, or rationalize — but the Ideology Ledger retains the event, and it is available for later narrative activation.

### 3.6 — Ideological Blindspot Revelation and Removal Law

An **Ideological Blindspot** (Ideology Ledger entry type 5 per Area 3.1) is a belief or assumption the character holds without awareness — something their ideology structurally prevents them from seeing. Blindspots are among the most powerful narrative assets in the series. This Area governs how blindspots are revealed, challenged, and removed.

**Rule 20.3.6.1 — Blindspots Are Structurally Generated:** A blindspot is not a failure of intelligence or perception — it is a failure of ideological framing. The character cannot see what their ideology has no category for. A Strength-Dominant character cannot perceive relational obligation as a real force because their ideology has no framework for obligation that is not ultimately reducible to power. Blindspots arise from the internal logic of the character's Core Ideology and must be consistent with that logic.

**Rule 20.3.6.2 — Blindspot Revelation Triggers an IE Event:** When a character becomes consciously aware of a blindspot — either through confrontation, lived experience, or the specific IE-10 Revelation Event — this is always a Part 20 IE event:
- **Confrontation-Triggered Revelation:** Another character explicitly exposes the blindspot in dialogue or action; IE-4 minimum (Partial Shift Event)
- **Experience-Triggered Revelation:** The blindspot causes a consequence so significant the character cannot rationalize it away; IE-5 to IE-6 depending on severity
- **Crisis-Triggered Revelation:** The blindspot causes the character to fail at a fundamental level (Phase 4 arc context); IE-7 or higher
- **IE-10 Ideological Revelation Event:** The purest form — the character achieves conscious awareness of a previously unconscious structure; always accompanied by PIS-4+ (Significant Impact) from the psychological impact of self-recognition

**Rule 20.3.6.3 — Revelation Does Not Equal Removal:** Becoming aware of a blindspot does not remove it. A character who now knows they have a blindspot around relational obligation may still be unable to act on that knowledge because their Core Ideology has not changed. Revelation creates cognitive dissonance (an Active Contradiction per Area 3.1 entry type 4) that must be resolved through further IE events. The gap between revelation and genuine removal is a productive narrative space.

**Rule 20.3.6.4 — Blindspot Removal Requires Core Ideology Shift:** A blindspot is fully removed only when the Core Ideology shifts to include the category the blindspot excluded. This normally follows a large ideology event (guidance post: around IE-5, a Core Shift, at about PIS-7 — the Core Ideology row of the Ideology Change Threshold Table); the character's own traits and the Chapter Spec decide the event and its size. Partial blindspot removal (the character can now sometimes see what they previously couldn't, under favorable conditions) is a valid intermediate state and should be tracked in the Ideology Ledger as a "Partially Removed" blindspot entry.

**Rule 20.3.6.5 — Blindspot Replacement:** When a blindspot is removed, it is often replaced by a new blindspot generated by the character's new ideological position. An ideologically evolving character does not simply accumulate wisdom — they trade one set of blindspots for another. The new blindspot must be tracked. A character who has overcome SDI blindness to relational obligation may now have a blindspot around the costs of their new relational priority.

**Rule 20.3.6.6 — Narration Standard for Blindspot Revelation:** The narrative must not editorialize when a blindspot is exposed. The prose renders the consequence of the blindspot operating — the character's failure, confusion, surprise, or damage — without imposing authorial judgment that they "should have known." The reader perceives the blindspot through the character's behavior and its consequences, not through narrative pronouncement.

### 3.7 — Active Contradiction Resolution Law

An **Active Contradiction** (Ideology Ledger entry type 4 per Area 3.1) is an unresolved tension between two layers or positions within a character's ideology that the character has not yet integrated. Active Contradictions are canonical — they are not authorial errors; they are the structured governance of internal ideological complexity. This Area governs how contradictions are resolved, sustained, or weaponized.

**Rule 20.3.7.1 — Active Contradictions Must Be Recorded:** Every named character whose Ideology Ledger contains an Active Contradiction must have that contradiction specifically documented: what the two positions are, which layers they occupy (Core/Operational/Surface), when they entered the Ledger as a contradiction, and what triggered the contradiction's emergence. Undocumented contradictions are authorial convenience, not governed ideology.

**Rule 20.3.7.2 — Contradictions Under Pressure Are Story Engines:** An Active Contradiction is most useful when it is placed under narrative pressure — when the story forces the character into a situation where both sides of the contradiction cannot simultaneously be honored. The character must choose. That choice is both a character definition moment and an IE event. This pressure should not be arbitrary — the best contradiction pressure emerges organically from the narrative circumstances the character's own actions have created.

**Rule 20.3.7.3 — Contradiction Resolution Pathways:** Active Contradictions can resolve through four canonical pathways:

| Resolution Pathway | Description | IE Event |
|-------------------|-------------|---------|
| **Integration** | The character finds a framework that genuinely reconciles both positions; neither is discarded | IE-9 Ideology Reconciliation Event; hardest and most significant resolution |
| **Dominance** | One side of the contradiction overwhelms the other; the dominated position is discarded or suppressed into a new blindspot | IE-5 to IE-6; the character becomes more coherent but loses something |
| **Sublimation** | The contradiction is redirected — rather than resolving the tension, the character directs its energy into action, cultivation, or creativity that channels the unresolved tension productively | IE-4; tension remains but is managed |
| **Collapse** | The contradiction becomes so acute it collapses the character's ideology toward IS-4 (Fractured) or IS-5 (Collapsed) | IE-6 to IE-7; the contradiction was not resolvable and the ideology fractures |

**Rule 20.3.7.4 — Unresolved Contradictions Accumulate:** A character who carries multiple unresolved Active Contradictions without any IE events processing them is building ideological pressure. This is a governed state — the character may appear fine on the surface while the contradiction count climbs. When the pressure is released (through a sufficiently significant narrative event), the release is proportional to the accumulated weight. As an illustration, not a count: no counter triggers anything; the story and the Chapter Spec decide. A character carrying 4–5 unresolved Active Contradictions who faces a genuine Phase 4 Unmaking event may experience ideology collapse (IS-4 or IS-5) rather than manageable crisis.

**Rule 20.3.7.5 — Contradiction Cannot Be Administratively Removed:** An Active Contradiction cannot be removed from the Ideology Ledger without a corresponding IE event of sufficient magnitude. Authors cannot simply write a contradiction out of existence because it has become inconvenient. The contradiction must be resolved through the narrative — through lived experience, choice under pressure, or consequence — and the resolution event must be logged in the Ideology Ledger.

**Rule 20.3.7.6 — Hero-Villain Contradictions Are Structurally Related:** When a hero and villain share a structural ideological contradiction (the hero's Active Contradiction involves the same tension that the villain has resolved into their Core Ideology, or vice versa), this creates the highest-value ideological confrontation structure in the series. The ideological confrontation between them is the hero confronting what their own contradiction could become. This relationship must be tracked in both the Hero Ledger Section 4 and the Villain Ledger Section 4.

## Area 4 — Ideological Governance by Context

### 4.1 — Local Context and Ideology

A character's ideology does not exist in a vacuum — it is shaped by the local context in which they were formed and in which they currently operate. Local context is the immediate lived environment: the village, sect, region, civilization level, and physical conditions of existence.

**Local Ideology Shapers:**

| Local Factor | Ideological Effect |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Survival-Margin Environment** (constant threat, scarce resources) | Amplifies SDI, LBI, and PCI; suppresses TSI, TRI, and HLI |
| **Abundance Environment** (safety, resource surplus, stable social order) | Enables TSI, TRI, HLI, and JCI; SDI becomes aspirational rather than survival-based |
| **Isolated Community** (limited exposure to other ideological frameworks) | Amplifies Polarity across all categories; reduces IE-2 events (fewer challenges to existing ideology) |
| **High-Mobility Environment** (trade cities, spaceport cultures, gate network hubs) | Reduces Polarity naturally; multiplies IE-3 events; compounds are more common |
| **Power-Asymmetric Environment** (extreme stratification) | Amplifies SDI in the powerful; amplifies JCI and VAI in the suppressed; creates LBI-SCI compound in the compliant |
| **Existential Threat Environment** (civilization under attack, realm-scale threat) | Amplifies PCI, LBI, and OMI; temporarily suppresses ADI and HLI |

**Rule 20.4.1.1 — Local Context Is Formative, Not Deterministic:** Local context shapes probability of ideological development but does not determine it. A character formed in a Survival-Margin Environment who develops TSI is possible — and often narratively significant precisely because of that deviation.

### 4.2 — Faction Ideology and Character Ideology

Faction doctrine (Part 22) produces ideological pressure on member characters. The faction's institutional ideology is a continuous IE-1 (Confirmation) environment for matching characters and a continuous IE-2 (Challenge) environment for non-matching characters.

**Faction-Character Ideology Alignment Types:**

| Alignment Type | Description | Narrative Dynamic |
|---------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Full Alignment** | Character's Core, Operational, and Surface ideologies align with faction doctrine | Character is ideologically comfortable; narrative tension comes from external pressure |
| **Surface Alignment** | Character's Surface ideology matches faction; Core diverges | The character performs faction ideology while holding a private Core — classical double consciousness |
| **Operational Alignment** | Character follows faction doctrine in practice without believing it at Core | The loyalist who acts from LBI rather than genuine ideological conviction |
| **Core Conflict** | Character's Core ideology directly contradicts faction doctrine | Character is on a trajectory toward either changing (IE-5 event required) or defection |
| **Ideological Exile** | Character has left the faction but carries its ideological imprint — positive or negative | Former sect member who either continues the teaching or builds their identity around its opposition |

**Rule 20.4.2.1 — Faction Indoctrination:** Factions actively engage in ideological formation of their members. Cultivation sects transmit not only technique but cultivator worldview. The longer and deeper the faction membership, the more significant its ideological imprint — even after departure. A character who spent thirty years in a SDI-dominant sect will carry SDI imprints even after a Core Ideology shift.

**Rule 20.4.2.2 — Faction Ideology Does Not Override Individual Ideology:** Individual character ideology is always the governing reality for character action. Faction doctrine describes what the faction believes and pressures members toward; the Ideology Ledger describes what the character actually believes and acts from. These may differ.

### 4.3 — Information, Misinformation, and Ideology

Ideology is always built on a specific set of information. Misinformation, secrecy, and information deprivation (Part 24) shape ideological development in specific, governable ways.

**Information-Ideology Interface Rules:**

**Rule 20.4.3.1 — Misinformation-Founded Ideology:** When a character's ideology is built substantially on false information, the ideology can appear internally coherent while being externally incoherent. When the misinformation is revealed (an IE-10 or IE-6 event), the ideology faces a structural crisis regardless of its Polarity level — because the factual foundation has been removed.

**Rule 20.4.3.2 — Information Deprivation as Ideology Stabilizer:** Controlled information environments (sect isolation, government censorship, cult secrecy doctrine) stabilize ideology by preventing IE-2 (Challenge) events. A character in a high-secrecy environment may hold Polarity-5 beliefs not because of the quality of their ideology but because the challenge mechanism is structurally absent.

**Rule 20.4.3.3 — Propaganda and Ideological Manufacture:** Any faction, group or person may manufacture ideology in others, by building a false history or factual framework, by propaganda, or by controlling what may be known, for gain, for power, for an ideology of their own, or for any other reason, including immoral or evil ones. Living people act for the future they want, and the canon does not bar them from it. No scale, tier or class of faction is required. The Chapter Spec decides whether it happens: the spec names who manufactures, why, and by what means (the methods of Part 24 Area 17: disinformation, propaganda and narrative control, and selective amnesia under Law IA-2). A manufacture that an Area adds and the spec did not plan is listed at Check #18 like any other difference from the plan. A villain keeps the reasons that are their own (Rule 20.5.2.1); the spec records them and does not replace them. This rule is about systematic manufacture; one person's lie is deception (Part 24; Part 39 Layer L09). What is manufactured is still ideology: its holders carry it at the three layers of Area 1, the credibility ceiling of Part 24 Area 17.1 limits what can be believed, and Rules 20.4.3.1, 20.4.3.2 and 20.4.3.4 govern a belief built on it and its exposure. Consequences follow Part 27: an exposed false history is a consequence event for the manufacturer, for those who believed it and for those it harmed. This is a joint Part 24 and Part 20 domain.

**Rule 20.4.3.4 — The Revelation Cascade:** When a character whose ideology was built on substantial misinformation receives accurate information, the first response is rarely immediate ideological revision. Instead, the character typically: (1) rejects the new information as the misinformation (the false is the familiar), (2) attempts to integrate the new information without changing the Core ideology, (3) if (2) fails, enters an IE-6 (Ideology Crisis) state. The Revelation Cascade governs the narrative pacing of information-driven ideological change.

### 4.4 — Cultivation and Ideology

Cultivation advancement is one of the most powerful ideological transformation vectors in the Starward Saga universe. The experience of genuine breakthrough, of contact with higher levels of reality, of surviving what should have killed them — these experiences create ideological events that no external input can replicate.

**The IE levels in this table are guidance posts, not requirements or ceilings.** A character's own traits decide how they react to a transformation, and they may react above or below the level listed. More power does not by itself mean a drastic change in psychology; that would erode individuality and the unique formation of each character. For this reason the Area 3.3 PIS minimums do not make an ordinary breakthrough a Severe Trauma event (PIS-7).

**Cultivation-Ideology Interface:**

| Cultivation Event | Ideological Effect |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| **First Qi Manifestation** | IE-3 (Expansion) — the world now contains something the character's previous framework did not account for |
| **First Realm Breakthrough** | IE-4 (Partial Shift) — the experience of physical and metaphysical transformation produces operational ideology changes |
| **Major Realm Breakthrough** (Xiantian Demigod, Core Formation, Divine Transformation, etc.) | IE-5 or IE-6 (Core Shift or Crisis) — the boundary between pre-breakthrough and post-breakthrough identity creates ideological reassessment |
| **Near-Death in Cultivation** | IE-5 or IE-8 (Core Shift or Crystallization) — depending on the character, the survival experience either deepens existing ideology (crystallization) or restructures it |
| **Qi Deviation** | IE-6 or IE-7 (Crisis or Collapse) — Qi Deviation shatters the cultivator's relationship with their own cultivation; ideological reconstruction follows |
| **First Killing** | IE-4 or IE-5 — the first time a character kills, their ideology must account for that fact in a way it may not have before |
| **Confronting Absolute-tier Existence** | IE-5 through IE-7 — direct encounter with Heavenly Scale Grade 17 or cosmological powers produces ideological events; the character's prior framework may simply be insufficient to contain what they witnessed |
| **Cultivation Plateau** | IE-2 (Challenge) accumulated — extended failure to advance produces ideological pressure in ADI characters; TSI characters may reinterpret the plateau |
| **Heart Awakening** (Part 4) | IE-5 (Core Shift potential) — Heart awakening involves a genuine encounter with the character's will and moral identity; it may confirm or restructure existing ideology |

**Repeated Triggers (guidance):** As guidance, a trigger type that has already produced an IE event for this character produces one level lower the next time, unless the ledger records a new stake. This keeps a character who meets the same trigger again and again (near-death, Qi Deviation) from hardening into Absolutist by repetition alone.

**Rule 20.4.4.1 — Realm Does Not Determine Ideology Quality:** Higher cultivation realm does not produce better ideology. A Divine Sea Realm cultivator may hold SDI at Polarity-5 in its most exploitative form. Cultivation produces ideological *events* — it does not determine ideological *direction*.

**Rule 20.4.4.2 — Cultivation Path Colors Ideology Without Governing It:** A fire-affinity cultivator develops a relationship with intensity, force, and transformative energy that shapes how they experience ideological content. A void-affinity cultivator develops a relationship with stillness, absence, and dissolution. These cultivation-path colorings are Part 4 territory; the ideological frameworks built on those colorings are Part 20 territory.

### 4.5 — Technology and Ideology

Technology exposure (Part 8, Part 31) produces distinct ideological events depending on the character's existing ideology and the nature of the technology encountered.

**Technology-Ideology Interface:**

| Technology Event | Ideological Effect |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| **First Technology Encounter** (cultivator encountering Model A integration for the first time) | IE-3 (Expansion) or IE-2 (Challenge) depending on the character's existing OMI/SDI orientation toward hierarchy |
| **Model B Integration Adoption** | IE-4 (Partial Shift) — the physical integration of technology into the body produces operational ideology updates about the nature of self and power |
| **Model D Adoption** | IE-5 or IE-6 — Model D identity drift (Part 26 Area 4.2, Part 31 Area 5.4) produces ideological crisis about the boundaries of self; the character's ideology must account for the ambiguity of their own identity |
| **Equalizer Effect Experience** | IE-4 — experiencing the power equalization that technology enables forces ideological recalculation in SDI/OMI characters who valued hierarchy |
| **Technology Failure Catastrophe** | IE-5 (for TSI characters who believed in technology as transcendence) or IE-2 (for TRI characters who anticipated failure probability) |
| **Information Technology Mastery** | IE-3 (for TRI characters — expanded truth-access); IE-2 (for SCI characters — the covenant's information restrictions may conflict) |

**Rule 20.4.5.1 — Technology-Cultivation Tension as Ideological Battleground:** The core ideological debate of the Starward Saga — whether cultivation-first or technology-first paths represent the superior approach to existence — is expressed through character ideology. This debate is not settled by the narrative. Characters on both sides must be rendered with full ideological coherence. The technology-cultivation tension is one of the primary ideological engines of the entire series.

## Area 5 — Specific Ideology Dynamics

### 5.1 — The Ideology of Good Characters

"Good" in the Starward Saga is not a single ideology — it is a quality that can be achieved through multiple ideological frameworks. Good character behavior is characterized by:
- Genuine consideration for the wellbeing of others (not just their usefulness)
- Willingness to be accountable for harm caused
- Operating from a moral orientation that includes others in its circle of concern
- The capacity to restrain their own power in recognition of its cost

JCI, PCI, LBI (Principled variant), and TSI (with sufficient expansiveness of the transcendent goal) can all generate genuinely good characters. The same categories can produce terrible characters under different context conditions.

**Rule 20.5.1.1 — Good Is Not Simple:** Good characters in the Starward Saga are not morally uncomplicated. A genuinely good character has ideological blind spots, has caused harm, has rationalized actions that their ideology did not fully support. The difference between good and evil characters in this series is not purity of record but quality of orientation and willingness to account.

### 5.2 — The Ideology of Evil Characters

"Evil" in the Starward Saga emerges when an ideological framework achieves the following specific configuration:
- Others outside the character's circle of concern are without moral status — they do not count
- The character's goals justify any means required to achieve them — consequence to others is irrelevant
- The character has no genuine accountability mechanism — they neither acknowledge harm nor allow its acknowledgment to affect their behavior

SDI (Pure-Force), ADI (Dominion variant), VAI (Collective), and OMI (Cultivation-Hierarchy in its most exploitative expression) most frequently produce the ideological substrate for genuinely evil behavior.

**Rule 20.5.2.1 — Evil Characters Believe Their Ideology Is Right:** The SSC Canon does not permit cartoonishly self-aware evil. An evil character does not experience themselves as evil — they experience their ideology as correct and their actions as justified. The narrative renders their internal coherence while making the cost to others visible.

**Rule 20.5.2.2 — Evil Has a Formative History:** Every evil character has an Ideology Ledger that traces how they arrived at their current ideological position. That position was not inevitable. There were pivot points at which a different ideological event could have produced a different outcome. The presence of those pivot points is not an excuse — it is a complexity that makes the evil character fully human.

### 5.3 — Neutral Characters and Moral Ambiguity

Neutral characters are not ideologically empty — they hold ideologies that produce outcomes across the moral spectrum depending on context. Common neutral ideological profiles:

- **Pragmatic-Stability OMI**: produces order that can be just or unjust depending on what order is being maintained
- **Network-Power SDI**: produces effective strategic actors who support or undermine larger causes based entirely on self-interest calculation
- **Code-Loyalty LBI**: produces honor-bound characters whose honor code may or may not align with wider moral frameworks
- **Empirical-TRI**: produces truth-seekers whose discoveries may help or harm depending on what is done with them

**Rule 20.5.3.1 — Neutral Characters Are Ideologically Active:** Neutral does not mean passive or undecided. A neutral character has a clear ideology — that ideology simply does not produce consistently good or consistently evil outcomes. The moral weight of a neutral character's actions is context-dependent, not ideologically neutral.

### 5.4 — The Ideology of Holy and Sacred Characters

The SSC Canon uses "holy" and "sacred" as ideological orientations rather than metaphysical states. A holy character is one whose ideology and behavior are aligned with a principle recognized as transcendently valuable — and whose alignment is genuine rather than performed.

Holy character ideological profiles frequently combine:
- SCI (Sacred-Covenant) at Polarity-4 or 5 — genuine covenant relationship
- JCI (Justice-Centered, Cosmic variant) — alignment with a cosmic moral order
- TSI (Transcendence, Enlightenment variant) — a transcendent goal that encompass the wellbeing of others

**Rule 20.5.4.1 — Holy Is Earned Through Consistency:** Holiness as a narrative quality is demonstrated through ideological consistency under pressure. A character who proclaims holy values and maintains them when those values cost them everything has established genuine holiness. A character who proclaims holy values and abandons them under pressure has established performed holiness — a form of Surface ideology fraud.

### 5.5 — Lying, Cheating, and Stealing as Ideological Expression

Characters who lie, cheat, and steal are not necessarily evil — they are expressing specific ideological frameworks in which honesty, rule-following, and property rights either have no status or have reduced status under specific conditions.

**Ideological Roots of Deception:**

| Behavior | Most Common Ideological Roots | Potential Justification Within Framework |
|----------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Systematic lying** | SDI (Network-Power), ADI (Status-Advancement), SCI (Destiny-Covenant where truth is subordinate to covenant fulfillment) | "Truth is a tool; its value is situational" |
| **Tactical deception** | JCI (Retributive, Structural), PCI (any variant), LBI (Institutional, Code) | "Deception of the enemy to protect the innocent is not lying — it is strategy" |
| **Cheating systems** | FSI (Anarchic-Political), TRI (Historical — the system's rules are false), JCI (Structural — unjust rules have no legitimacy) | "The rules are designed to maintain unjust outcomes; refusing them is correct" |
| **Theft** | PCI (survival of protection domain justifies it), VAI (taking from the enemy is restitution), HLI (experience-maximizing) | "What belongs to my enemy can belong to me" |

**Rule 20.5.5.1 — Lying As Character Revelation:** A character who lies does not simply lie — the way they lie, what they lie about, and what they will not lie about reveals their Core ideology more reliably than their honest speech. The lies that feel natural to a character are expressions of their deepest beliefs.

### 5.6 — Ideological Hypocrisy

Ideological hypocrisy — the gap between professed ideology and enacted ideology — is one of the most narratively productive character structures in the series.

**Types of Ideological Hypocrisy:**

| Type | Description | Narrative Potential |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Innocent Hypocrisy** | Character genuinely does not know the gap exists; Surface and Core are authentically disconnected | Revelation scenes; crisis when gap is exposed |
| **Convenient Hypocrisy** | Character knows the gap but rationalizes it case by case | The escalating rationalization arc; the moment the rationalization fails |
| **Structural Hypocrisy** | The ideology itself is internally contradictory — the character cannot be consistent | Philosophical crisis; the ideology must evolve or collapse |
| **Strategic Hypocrisy** | The Surface ideology is consciously performed while Core ideology differs — this is strategic positioning | The long con; the deep-cover character |
| **Recovering Hypocrisy** | The character has identified the gap and is working to close it — either by changing behavior or by genuinely updating the ideology | The redemption arc; the honest reckoning |

## Area 6 — Cultivation-Specific Ideology Dynamics

### 6.1 — The Ideology of Cultivation Paths

Different cultivation paths (Part 3, Part 4) are associated with ideological affinities — not determinate correlations but structural tendencies arising from what each path requires of the cultivator.

**Cultivation Path Ideological Tendencies:**

| Cultivation Path Type | Ideological Affinity | Structural Reason |
|----------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Body Cultivation** | LBI, SDI (Pure-Force), PCI | The body path builds ideology through physical experience — proximity, sensation, the immediate reality of force and protection |
| **Qi Cultivation** | TSI, OMI, JCI | Qi cultivation abstracts power into an invisible layer; cultivators who work with qi often develop frameworks that accommodate invisible structure |
| **Soul Cultivation** | TRI, TSI, SCI | Soul cultivation gives direct access to deeper reality layers — truth-seeking ideology is amplified by what soul practitioners actually perceive |
| **Heart Cultivation** | JCI, PCI, TSI | Heart cultivation requires genuine confrontation with one's will architecture — it naturally amplifies whatever moral orientation the cultivator genuinely holds |
| **Affinity Specialization** | Varies by affinity type | Fire affinity → SDI/VAI/ADI color; Water affinity → TSI/TRI color; Void affinity → FSI/TSI/HLI color; etc. |
| **Fusion Cultivation** | TRI, FSI, TSI | The synthesis nature of fusion cultivation tends to attract or produce ideological synthesis orientations |

**Rule 20.6.1.1 — Cultivation Path Does Not Lock Ideology:** These are tendencies, not determinations. A soul cultivator with SDI is possible and narratively interesting. A body cultivator with TSI is possible and narratively interesting. The deviation from tendency is often the character's most defining feature.

### 6.2 — Realm Advancement and Ideological Pressure

As cultivators advance through Heavenly Scale Grades, they encounter increasing pressure to develop ideologically adequate frameworks for the power they wield and the reality they inhabit.

**Realm-Ideological Adequacy Thresholds:**

| Realm Range | Ideological Adequacy Challenge |
|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Mortal Tier 1 (Unawakened — Soul)** | Managing the gap between their power and mortal-scale social norms; first awakening to the cultivation world; first major consequence events |
| **Mortal Tier 2 (Senior — Monarch)** | First kills; ideology of discipline and restraint as power exceeds ordinary human scale; managing longer lifespan against a non-cultivating family's ordinary one |
| **Xiantian (Demigod — Absolute God)** | Developing an ideology adequate to extended lifespan — what does loyalty mean across centuries? What does justice mean when they will outlive every contemporary? |
| **Core Realm (Core Formation — Core Solidification)** | The ideology of power that makes ordinary human lives look fragile; the responsibility architecture for power that can destroy civilizations |
| **Divine Realm (Divine Transformation — Divine Sea Realm)** | The ideology of cosmological-scale existence; the question of what humanity, justice, and care mean at scales that dwarf civilization; the Divine Sea Realm in particular demands ideology beyond the human scale entirely — these characters require Part 28 consultation for narrative rendering of their ideological architecture |

**Rule 20.6.2.1 — Ideological Lag Is a Narrative Resource:** Characters who have accumulated power faster than they have developed ideological frameworks adequate to that power are among the most dangerous and narratively interesting characters in the series. The powerful with inadequate ideology are structurally similar to the powerful with no ideology — unpredictable, often destructive, and in genuine internal conflict.

### 6.3 — Ideological Conflict Between Cultivation Generations

In long-lived cultivation communities, ideological conflicts frequently align with cultivation generation gaps. The founder's ideology shaped the sect's doctrine; later generations experience that doctrine as their cultural inheritance. This creates:

- **Doctrine-Holder vs. Doctrine-Inheritor tension:** Those who hold original ideological certainty versus those who have only been told about it
- **Advancement-Epoch tension:** Ideology formed in the previous cultivation epoch may be inadequate to challenges of the current epoch (new enemies, new technologies, new cultivation discoveries)
- **Prolonged Exposure Effects:** A cultivator who has held the same ideology for five hundred years without significant IE events becomes brittle — their ideology at Polarity-5 across all domains may look like wisdom but is structural rigidity

## Area 7 — Technology-Specific Ideology Dynamics

### 7.1 — Technology Operator Ideology

Characters who operate advanced technology (Part 8, Part 32, Part 33) develop specific ideological formations arising from their technological relationship to power.

**Technology-Generated Ideological Formations:**

| Technology Role | Ideological Effect | Common Resulting Profile |
|----------------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **Weapons Operator (WS-IV+)** | Remote lethality dissociation creates ideological challenge to PCI and JCI; the emotional distance of ranged/remote killing affects how the ideology processes moral cost | TRI/JCI tension; SDI rationalization through efficiency language |
| **Integration Adopter (Model B)** | Self-boundary ideology revision; the "where do I end and the machine begin?" question forces operational ideology updates | FSI (personal sovereignty under pressure), TSI (transcendence through integration potential) |
| **Model D Integration** | Identity dissolution pressure; the ideology must address what "I" means when cognitive architecture is partially technological | TRI (self-knowing), SCI (is the covenant still valid for this self?), FSI crisis |
| **Technology Commander (fleet, weapons systems)** | Scale of consequence creates ideological events; commanding technology that can sterilize a planet forces ideological confrontation with power at civilizational scale | OMI, PCI (protecting those under command), SDI at its most institutional |
| **Technology Inventor/Designer** | The ideology of creation; what one builds represents one's ideology in physical form | TRI, ADI (knowledge), TSI (technology as transcendence path) |

### 7.2 — Technology vs. Cultivation Ideology

The structural ideological divide between technology-primary and cultivation-primary characters is one of the series' central ideological engines.

**Technology-Primary Ideological Position:**
- Power is a function of intelligence, design, and accumulated knowledge — not innate spiritual talent
- The universe's laws can be understood, engineered, and redirected rather than merely aligned with
- Advancement is not limited by spiritual talent (affinity, soul capacity, Heart potential) — it is limited only by knowledge and resource
- Knowledge accumulated in technology can be transmitted without the intimate master-student relationship cultivation requires — it scales democratically

**Cultivation-Primary Ideological Position:**
- Power at its deepest derives from cultivator self-cultivation — the transformation of the self through alignment with the Dao
- Technology operates on fixed principles; cultivation operates on the boundary between the fixed and the transcendent
- There are dimensions of reality (soul, Heart, Dao-resonance) that technology can approach but never fully access
- The most significant forms of power in the universe are available only to cultivators — and specifically cultivators who have undertaken the full cultivation journey of self-transformation

**Rule 20.7.2.1 — Neither Position Is Authoritatively Correct:** The SSC Canon does not settle this ideological debate. Both frameworks have genuine evidence within the universe's mechanics. Both produce characters capable of extraordinary achievement and terrible harm. The ideological debate is a permanent engine of the series, not a question awaiting narrative resolution.

## Area 8 — Ideological States and Transitions

### 8.1 — Ideological Stability States

Every character's ideology occupies one of five stability states at any narrative moment:

| Stability State | Description | Narrative Indicators |
|----------------|-------------|----------------------|
| **IS-1 — Secure** | The character's ideology is functioning coherently; no active contradictions; Ledger is current | Consistent behavior; clear motivation; ability to state their position when challenged |
| **IS-2 — Pressured** | The character's ideology is under challenge; active IE-2 events occurring | Defensiveness; over-explanation of positions; inconsistent response to challenge |
| **IS-3 — Strained** | One or more Active Contradictions are unresolved; IE-4 or IE-5 potential | Avoidance of specific situations that would force the contradiction to surface |
| **IS-4 — Fractured** | Core Ideology is under active attack; IE-6 potential present | Emotional volatility around specific ideological topics; uncharacteristic behavior |
| **IS-5 — Collapsed** | IE-7 has occurred; the character is in ideological void | Absence of characteristic motivation; inability to make decisions; loss of the usual self-explanatory framework |

### 8.2 — Ideological Void

An **Ideological Void** is the state following IE-7 (Ideology Collapse). The character's organizing framework has been destroyed, and no new framework has yet emerged.

**Characteristics of the Ideological Void:**
- Actions become reactive and immediate — driven by basic survival, basic attachment, basic sensation — rather than principled
- The character experiences the world as radically unstable, because their interpretation framework has collapsed
- Other characters' values appear arbitrary and incomprehensible, because the void character has lost their own framework for evaluating values
- The character may appear to adopt multiple new ideological frameworks temporarily and discard each — they are trying on new structures to see if any fit
- The character is highly susceptible to ideological adoption from authority figures during this period — cults, mentors with strong ideological frameworks, and charismatic leaders are the primary risks

**Rule 20.8.2.1 — Void Duration Is Proportional to Polarity:** A character who collapsed from Polarity-5 ideology faces a longer and more severe void than one who collapsed from Polarity-2. Higher polarity means more of the character's identity was organized around the ideology; more of the self must be reconstructed.

**Rule 20.8.2.2 — Void Characters Are Not Neutral:** The Ideological Void is not ideological neutrality — it is ideological devastation. These characters are not free from ideology; they are ideology-homeless. The difference is between a person who has no strong opinions (Polarity-1 fluid) and a person who has lost every framework that gave their life meaning.

### 8.3 — Ideological Reconstruction

Following IE-7, the character undergoes ideological reconstruction — a narrative process by which they develop a new organizing framework. This process is governed jointly by Part 20 (ideology) and Part 26 (psychological) because reconstruction is both an intellectual and an emotional process.

**Reconstruction Pathways:**

| Pathway | Description | Resulting Ideology Profile |
|---------|-------------|---------------------------|
| **Integration Pathway** | The character integrates the experiences that destroyed their old ideology into a more complex framework that can contain them | Modified version of prior ideology at lower Polarity with new depth |
| **Opposition Pathway** | The character builds their new ideology in direct opposition to what destroyed the old one | Often produces high-Polarity new ideology that is the mirror image of the collapsed framework |
| **Transplant Pathway** | The character adopts a ready-made ideology from a significant figure or institution they encounter in the void | Rapid ideological stabilization at high risk of producing dependence on the ideological source |
| **Minimalist Pathway** | The character reduces to the barest possible ideological commitments — a small number of principles they can verify through direct experience | Low-Polarity, PCI or LBI (personal, minimal) framework; vulnerability to later IE-6 from accumulated complexity |
| **Transcendence Pathway** | The character uses the void as the cultivation Heart encounter it is, and emerges with TSI or Dao-navigation TRI that is genuinely new | Rare; requires specific cultivation conditions; produces the most transformed characters in the series |

## Area 9 — Ideology and Interpersonal Dynamics

### 9.1 — Ideological Alignment in Relationships

Relationships in the Starward Saga are shaped by ideological alignment — not just by power dynamics, history, or affection. Ideologically aligned characters form deeper bonds; ideologically opposed characters experience structural friction regardless of personal warmth.

**Ideological Compatibility Patterns:**

| Combination | Dynamic | Narrative Potential |
|-------------|---------|---------------------|
| **Matching Category + Matching Polarity** | Deep identification; mutual reinforcement | Risk of echo chamber; combined vulnerability to shared ideology's blind spots |
| **Matching Category + Different Polarity** | Agreement on values; friction on conviction intensity | One character appears more committed; the other appears more flexible; productive tension |
| **Adjacent Category + Compatible Moral Orientation** | Natural alliance with philosophical texture | Two characters who agree on ends but differ on means; productive conflict about method |
| **Opposed Category + Compatible Moral Orientation** | The productive ideological opposition that drives the best narrative debates | Each makes the other more honest; each challenges the other's blindspots; genuine friendship is possible |
| **Opposed Category + Opposed Moral Orientation** | Irreconcilable conflict | Enemies; or one must change significantly for genuine relationship |

### 9.2 — Ideological Transmission

Ideology can be transmitted — through teaching, through social influence, through cult formation, through cultural saturation. The most powerful ideological transmitters in the Starward Saga are:

- **Masters** — the cultivation master-student relationship is among the most powerful ideological transmission vectors. Masters transmit ideology alongside technique; the student frequently cannot separate the two.
- **Catastrophic Events Shared** — characters who survive the same catastrophic event together often develop synchronized ideological orientations. The shared survival produces a shared interpretive framework.
- **Longterm Proximity** — prolonged inhabitation of the same environment produces ideological convergence. This is the mechanism behind faction ideological homogeneity.
- **Sacred Text and Oral Tradition** — ideologically organized knowledge (scriptures, cultivation manuals with philosophical content, historical records with ideological framing) transmits its organizational framework to those who absorb it deeply.

**Rule 20.9.2.1 — Manipulation vs. Transmission:** Ideology transmission becomes manipulation when the transmitter conceals the ideological content of what they are transmitting, when the receiver does not have access to alternative frameworks for evaluation, or when the transmission is paired with psychological dependency or threat. Part 24 governs the information dimension of this manipulation; Part 20 governs the ideological dimension.

### 9.3 — Ideological Conflict in Narrative

When two characters with incompatible ideologies interact, the interaction generates one of four outcomes:

| Outcome | Description | Required Conditions |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Evasion** | Both characters avoid the direct ideological confrontation | High political stakes; mutual need; insufficient pressure |
| **Argumentation** | Both characters engage in direct ideological debate | Intellectual safety; genuine curiosity; relative safety from immediate consequence |
| **Crisis** | The ideological incompatibility produces an IE-6 event in one or both characters | Extreme stakes; a narrative moment where the ideological incompatibility cannot be contained |
| **Resolution** | One or both characters update their ideology | IE-4 or IE-5 event; sufficient narrative weight; genuine engagement rather than strategic performance |

**Rule 20.9.3.1 — Evasion Is Temporary:** In the SSC Canon, ideological incompatibility cannot be permanently evaded between characters who occupy the same narrative space for extended periods. Evasion builds pressure; the longer it is maintained, the more significant the eventual confrontation when it cannot be avoided.

## Area 10 — Ideological Alignment with All Other Parts

### 10.1 — Ideology and Combat (Part 5)

Every significant combat encounter is a simultaneous ideological event. The decision to fight, the intensity deployed, the choice to kill or spare — these are all expressions of the character's current ideological state.

- **Why a character fights** is an ideological question answered by their Ideology Ledger
- **How a character fights** (mercilessly, with restraint, with artistic investment, with pure function) expresses their ideology through combat behavior
- **The aftermath of killing** generates an IE-4 or IE-5 for characters whose ideology has not previously required them to account for killing as a fact of their life (joint governance with Part 26 Area 2.1.1 — Kill Impact)
- **The decision to spare** an enemy is often the most ideologically complex act in combat — it reveals which of the character's ideological layers actually governs in extremis

### 10.2 — Ideology and Consequences (Part 27)

The character's ideology determines which consequences they are capable of genuinely registering. An SDI character cannot be genuinely moved by social consequence unless it threatens their power base. A JCI character is more affected by moral consequence than physical consequence.

- **Ideological Blindspot to Consequence:** Characters whose ideology cannot process a specific consequence type will appear narratively callous — not because they lack psychology but because their ideology does not assign weight to that consequence type
- **Consequence-Driven Ideology Change:** CSS-8 or higher consequence events (Part 27) frequently generate IE-5 or IE-6 ideology events when the consequence type is one the character's ideology does not have adequate framework for

### 10.3 — Ideology and Faction (Part 22)

- Every faction has a dominant institutional ideology — the collective expression of the faction's governing beliefs
- Character ideology interacts with faction ideology through alignment, performance, resistance, and transformation
- Faction leadership succession is frequently ideological — who inherits leadership may depend on who most convincingly embodies the faction's institutional ideology
- Cross-faction relationships carry the ideological tensions of their respective institutions

### 10.4 — Ideology and Culture (Part 25)

- Cultural paradigm types (CPT-1 through CPT-7) are the collective cultural expression of dominant ideological orientations — culture is the social precipitation of ideology
- Individual characters may hold ideologies that deviate significantly from their cultural context — these deviations are among the most productive narrative characterization tools
- Cultural conflict (Part 25) always has ideological content — cultures clash because their governing ideological orientations are incompatible
- The cultivation sect cultural axis (CuA-C) is particularly ideology-dense because cultivation philosophy is simultaneously an ideological framework

### 10.5 — Ideology and Psychology (Part 26)

- Psychological events (Part 26) generate Ideology Ledger events — trauma, grief, moral injury, identity crisis are all IE sources
- Ideological frameworks shape psychological interpretation — the same event (a first kill) produces different psychological experiences depending on whether the character holds SDI (functional, possibly confirmatory), JCI (profound moral-psychological event), or PCI (desperate psychological justification)
- Psychological healing (Part 26 Areas 7-9 — Cultivation-Based Coping and Healing, Technology-Based Coping and Healing, and Psychological Recovery and Resolution Pathways) is also ideological reconstruction — the character who heals from trauma must also address the ideological framework that either produced the trauma or was shattered by it
- Maladaptive coping mechanisms (Part 26 Area 6.3 — Maladaptive Coping Mechanisms) are frequently ideological performances — the character's ideology does not permit them to acknowledge the psychological wound, so it is managed through ideological channels instead

### 10.6 — Ideology and Information (Part 24)

- Information asymmetry produces ideological asymmetry — those with more accurate information have ideological advantages over those operating on misinformation
- Secrecy maintenance (Part 24) often requires characters to maintain Surface ideologies that contradict their Core — the spy, the double agent, the strategically performing sect elder
- Information revelation events (Part 24 revelation and exposure events) are Ideology Ledger events by definition — learning something true about the world always carries ideological weight

## Area 11 — Narration Standards for Ideology

### 11.1 — Core Narration Rules

**N20-1 — Show Ideology Through Action, Not Proclamation:** Character ideology is expressed through the choices characters make under pressure, not through speeches about what they believe. A character who proclaims JCI but never acts in accordance with it has only demonstrated their Surface ideology. Ideology is narrated through behavior.

**N20-2 — Render Internal Consistency Even for Repugnant Ideologies:** If a character holds SDI at Polarity-5, the narrative renders their decision-making as internally coherent from that ideological position. The reader may find the ideology repugnant — that is permitted and intended. The narrative does not apply external moral judgment through narratorial condemnation; it renders the consequences through the story.

**N20-3 — Ideological Change Is Not Announced:** A character does not say "I used to believe X but now I believe Y." Their behavior changes. Their responses to challenge change. Their blind spots shift. The Ideology Ledger is updated. The reader perceives the change through the accumulation of these shifts. One exception is lawful: a costly spoken admission, once per arc, made after the behavior has already shown the change. The admission costs the speaker something on the page, and it confirms what the reader has already seen; it is not how the reader first learns of the change. Authorial announcement of ideological change is a violation of N20-1.

**N20-4 — Never Collapse Ideology to Backstory:** A character's ideology is not explained by their backstory — it is shaped by it. The same backstory (murdered family) can produce JCI, VAI, SDI, or PCI depending on dozens of other developmental factors. Authors must not use backstory as a shortcut to ideology declaration. The Ideology Ledger contains the developmental history; the narrative contains the behavioral expression.

**N20-5 — Layer Contradictions Are Narrative Assets, Not Errors:** When a character's Surface ideology contradicts their Core ideology, this is not a character inconsistency — it is a character complexity. The author maintains awareness of both layers and allows the gap to surface at narratively appropriate moments. The revelation of the gap is often among the most significant moments in the character's arc.

**N20-6 — Compound Ideologies Require Sequential Resolution:** When a character's Primary and Secondary ideologies come into conflict (the LBI character whose loyalty commands them to protect someone their SDI assessment says is too weak to save), the hierarchy in the Ideology Ledger determines the outcome — unless the situation is sufficiently extreme to trigger an IE-5 or IE-6 that restructures the hierarchy.

**N20-7 — The Ideology Ledger Is Always Current:** Authors must update the Ideology Ledger after every IE-3 or higher event, and after any IE-1 or IE-2 that changes the Stability State (Area 8.1). An out-of-date Ledger produces characters who behave inconsistently without narrative justification.

**N20-8 — Ideological Debate Has No Author Proxy:** In scenes where characters debate ideology, no character speaks for the author's position. Both/all positions are rendered at their most coherent and most challenging. The reader is not told which character is right. They are shown what each position costs and what it makes possible.

**Worked example (illustration only).** A Strength-Dominant character of the Network-Power kind ("the real power is in who owes you", Area 2.1), held at Polarity-4 in the matter of debt (Area 2.3), is offered a favour that asks nothing. The figures are unnamed and the scene is invented. The scene shows only action and speech (N20-1); the ideology is never stated, and nothing is announced (N20-3).

> The ox pulled the cart out of the mud before the caravan master had finished cursing his mule. The boy who owned the ox wiped his hands on his shirt and turned the beast about.
>
> *"Wait."* The caravan master's hand was already in his purse. *"What do I owe you?"*
>
> *"Nothing, sir. My father says a stuck cart is everyone's trouble."*
>
> *"Nothing is never the price."* He held out three coins. *"Take them."*
>
> *"I cannot."* The boy's eyes went to the road, where his father stood watching.
>
> The caravan master laid the coins on the yoke, then took a fourth from his purse and laid it beside them, though no one had asked for more. He rode on without looking back and did not speak for a kilometre.

The scene writes one line in the Ideology Ledger's Event History (Area 3.2):

| Event | Type | Impact | Result |
|-------|------|--------|--------|
| The ox at the ford | IE-2 Challenge | PIS-3 | Core held (Polarity-4 in the matter of debt). Operational response: paid at once, then paid again. The unease is shown and not named. |

What the example shows: a favour with no price is a challenge to a belief that every favour is a chain, so the Polarity-4 response is categorical and does not depend on how kind the offer was (Area 2.3). The Surface line he speaks ("Nothing is never the price") and the Core he acts from agree here, which is why no contradiction is recorded; the silence at the end is the only sign that the belief was pressed.

## Area 12 — Special Ideology Types

### 12.1 — Post-Cataclysm Ideology

Characters who have survived civilization-scale catastrophe (Part 21 — civilizational collapse; Part 27 — CSS-8 or higher events) develop Post-Cataclysm ideological formations with specific characteristics:

- **Survivor-Primary reordering:** Survival becomes an organizing principle that may temporarily override all previous ideological categories
- **Before/After framework:** The cataclysm becomes the organizing temporal event; all values are evaluated by whether they contributed to survival or caused the catastrophe
- **Foundational distrust:** Institutions and categories of person who failed during the cataclysm are assigned permanent ideological zero-status
- **Hyper-protectionism:** The failure to protect (in the cataclysm) produces PCI at extreme Polarity as the dominant reconstruction ideology
- **Nihilism risk:** If the cataclysm destroyed everything of value, the reconstruction ideology may fail entirely — producing the most extreme form of IS-5 (Collapsed) possible

### 12.2 — Ideological Extremism

When any ideological category reaches Polarity-5 across all domains, with a Moral Orientation of Self-Directed or an Other-Orientation that excludes large categories of person, the character has developed **Ideological Extremism** — the belief that their ideology justifies any action in its service.

**Ideological Extremism Properties:**
- IE-1 (Confirmation) events are amplified in significance; IE-2 (Challenge) events are reinterpreted as attacks rather than arguments
- The character becomes organized around the ideology's enforcement or defense, not just its expression
- Other characters who hold incompatible ideologies become threats rather than differences
- The character loses the capacity for IE-10 (Ideological Revelation) because self-examination would threaten the Polarity-5 structure

**Rule 20.12.2.1 — Extremism Is Reversible Only Through IE-6 or IE-7:** Polarity-5 extremist ideology cannot be reduced through argument, social pressure, or ordinary experience. Only an IE-6 (Crisis) or IE-7 (Collapse) event can break the Polarity-5 structure. This makes extremist characters among the most narratively inert — they cannot develop without catastrophic ideology-level intervention.

### 12.3 — Transcendent Ideology in Absolute-Tier Characters

Characters approaching or having achieved absolute cultivation tiers (Part 28 domain) face ideological challenges that ordinary ideological frameworks cannot address:
- The complete absence of peer-level relationships (TSI at cosmic scale)
- The relationship to mortality when one may be effectively immortal at a cosmological timescale
- The ideological framework adequate to possessing power that can restructure civilizations or end them

These characters require Part 28 creative flexibility consultation for their full ideological rendering. Part 20 governs the ideological structure; Part 28 governs the narrative rendering of what transcendent-scale ideology actually looks like in practice.
