# Cultivation Situational Combat Law

**Authority Tier:** 1 (Domain Authority, Cultivation; see `.claude/guardrails/authority/guardrail-authority-tier-system.md`). Part 45 reads Part 3, Part 5, Part 6, Part 42, Part 44 and Part 40 and does not override any of them: Part 3 owns the levels, the damage thresholds, the Depletion States and the 3-Level Rule; Part 5 owns who may begin, escalate and end a fight; Part 6 owns the move types, functions, energy cost classes and success rates; Part 42 owns the manuals, the Access Table reading, the Seal rule and the Named Move forms; Part 44 owns whether a whole engagement makes sense at the levels written; Part 40 owns the monsters. Part 45 owns the question none of them asks: given this moment, which of the moves this fighter may lawfully use is the best one, and which are impossible or foolish here.
**Domain:** Cultivation, Situational Combat Mechanics: Foundational Principles, the Situation Frame, Ground and Space, Bodies and Posture, Reach and Anatomy, Tempo and Counter-Windows, Numbers and Protection, Seeing and Being Seen, Hands and Weapons, Cost and Fatigue, Chaining Finishing and Mercy, the Move Selection Test with the Worked Table and the Anti-Patterns, Where the Law Runs and Gate 1 Check #24, and the Cross-Part Dependency Table
**Governing Canon Parts Cross-Reference:** Parts 3, 5, 6, 16, 17, 27, 29, 36, 38, 39, 40, 42, 44
**Added:** 2026-10-19 (author directive; Part 45 is the next free Part number, Parts 2 to 44 existing). Applies to future writing only, starting with Chapter 006 (Area 12, Rule 45.12.4.1).

## Overview

Part 45 is the Starward Saga Canon's Situational Combat Mechanics Law. Before this Part the canon said which moves exist (Part 6, Part 42), which a person may use (the Access Table, the Seal), what they cost (Part 6 Area 5) and whether a whole fight is plausible (Part 44). It did not say how a writer chooses among the moves a fighter may use. A planner could read a rule that never existed, that an Intermediate or Advanced move must follow a Basic move, and so write a fight as a ladder: a Basic move, then an Intermediate move, then an Advanced move, whatever was happening in the scene.

The author removed that rule on 2026-10-19. There is no order rule. Anyone can use any move they have access to, at any point. A Basic move can open and end a fight, an Advanced move can open it, and a couple of Advanced moves can finish it. What limits a fighter is effort: higher-grade moves cost more energy and tire the user. What chooses the move is the situation. A person who is crouching cannot be kicked in the head. A broad cleave cannot be swung in a lane between two walls. A bare-handed fighter has no blade arc. The writer builds the fight around the moment, and the moment decides.

Part 45 turns that into a decision model. It has no formula. A writer reads the Situation Frame (Area 2), runs the Move Selection Test (Area 10) and checks the choice against the worked table and the anti-patterns. It runs in two places that already exist: the Chapter Spec, in the Fight line's Situation line, and Gate 1 Check #24 on every Area with a fight. Part 45 adds no command.

Across twelve Areas Part 45 defines: the principles (Area 1); the Situation Frame (Area 2); ground, footing, cover, space and crowding (Area 3); bodies and posture (Area 4); reach, range, height, size and anatomy (Area 5); tempo, telegraph and counter-windows (Area 6); numbers, lines and protecting others (Area 7); seeing and being seen (Area 8); hands and weapons (Area 9); cost, fatigue and the energy budget (Area 10); chaining, finishing and mercy, the Move Selection Test, the worked table and the anti-patterns (Area 11); and where the law runs, Check #24 and the cross-Part table (Area 12).

## Area 1: Foundational Principles

### 1.1: The Principles

**Rule 45.1.1.1: P45-1: There Is No Order Rule.** No canon text requires a move of a higher type to follow, or be paired with, a move of a lower type in a scene. An Intermediate, Advanced, Elite or Signature move may be the first move, the only move or the last move of a fighter in a fight. The lower-grade moves of a person's manual do not have to be shown before a higher-grade move of the same or another manual is used. A reading that says otherwise is void (Area 12, Rule 45.12.5.1, the record of what was removed).

**Rule 45.1.1.2: P45-2: Eligibility Is Not Order.** The link codes of Part 42 (LINK-PRIOR, LINK-STEP, LINK-GUARD) say what a person must already hold, at Practiced mastery, to hold a manual. They are about the person's kit and training, not about the order of moves in a scene. They stand unchanged. A scene never has to show the lower manual for a link code to count as met; a Tier C or Tier D person with no kit row is eligible by the ledger reading of Part 42 Rule 42.11.5.3 and is not asked to show earlier moves first.

**Rule 45.1.1.3: P45-3: Anyone Can Use Any Move They Have Access To.** A fighter may use any move that is Usable for them (Part 42 Rule 42.3.3.1: the effective realm, the Access Table and the Seal). A move that is Latent is known and never executed (Part 42 Rule 42.13.1.3). Within the Usable moves, the fighter is free.

**Rule 45.1.1.4: P45-4: Effort Is the Limit, Not Sequence.** A higher-grade move costs more energy and tires the user more (Part 6 Area 5.3). That is the price of choosing it. A fighter who uses several costly moves in a row is spent sooner. There is no rule that forces a cheap move first and no rule that forbids a costly move early; there is a price, and the price is shown (Area 10).

**Rule 45.1.1.5: P45-5: The Situation Chooses the Move.** The best move is the one the moment allows and calls for: the posture of the target, the ground, the space, the reach, the light, the people to protect, the fighter's own state and the energy left. A move that the moment makes impossible is not used. A move that is possible but poor is a weak choice the text must explain or avoid.

**Rule 45.1.1.6: P45-6: Build the Fight Around the Situation.** A fight is built from what is in front of the fighters, not from a list of moves to be shown. The spec lists the moves a scene may use as a menu with the situation, never as a fixed sequence.

**Rule 45.1.1.7: P45-7: The Author Names a Move When the Author Wants One.** For a generic scene the planner and writer choose by this Part. Where the author names a move or a type (Elite, Signature), the author's word rules, within the Access Table and the Seal; where the author wants options, the planner offers options with the situation reading for each (Rule 45.10.3.3).

**Rule 45.1.1.8: P45-8: Nothing Above Is Weakened.** The Access Table, the Seal, the 3-Level Rule, the Depletion States, the Move-Naming Law, the bold-italic Named Move form, Part 44, Part 29 Rule 29.12.1.3 and every Part 5 rule stand as written. Part 45 chooses among what they allow.

**Rule 45.1.1.9: P45-9: Arithmetic Stays Off the Page.** Level gaps, steps, percentages and energy figures live in the spec, the ledgers and the gate report. Prose shows what a person in the scene sees, hears and feels: breath, heat, a slowing hand, a blow that finds only air (Part 44 Rule 44.5.3 and Area 5.3 apply unchanged; Check #23 element 23-7 stands).

### 1.2: What Part 45 Owns and What It Does Not

**Rule 45.1.2.1: What Part 45 Owns.** The choice of a move for a moment; whether a chosen move is possible in the posture, ground, space and light of the scene; the fit of a move to the hands, the weapon and the opponent's body; the energy budget and the shown price of a fight; the ordering question (there is none); and Gate 1 Check #24.

**Rule 45.1.2.2: What It Does Not Own.** The move types and their success rates (Part 6); the manuals, access and Named Move forms (Part 42); the levels and the damage thresholds (Part 3); who may fight and how a fight ends (Part 5); whether an engagement is plausible by level (Part 44); a monster's Kind and abilities (Part 40); the Seal (Part 42 Rule 42.3.4.2 and the hero ledgers).

## Area 2: The Situation Frame

### 2.1: The Fourteen Readings

**Rule 45.2.1.1: The Frame.** Before a move is chosen the writer reads the moment through fourteen readings. They are a checklist and not a score. Each is answered in a word or a short phrase in the spec's Situation line (Area 12) and held in mind when writing.

| # | Reading | Asks | Area |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Ground and footing | Mud, thatch, stone, ice, a ladder, a roof, a slope, water, a crowd underfoot | 3 |
| S2 | Space and cover | Lane, yard, doorway, room, open field; walls, carts, trees, shields | 3 |
| S3 | Posture and body state of the target | Standing, crouching, kneeling, prone, grounded, airborne, mid-stride, guard up or down, off balance, exposed flank, seized | 4 |
| S4 | Posture and body state of the user | The same list, plus carrying, holding someone, one hand busy | 4 |
| S5 | Reach, range and height | Melee, mid, long; the reach and height difference between the two | 5 |
| S6 | Size and anatomy | Person or beast; quadruped, winged, small swarm, armoured, horned, long-bodied | 5 |
| S7 | Speed and tempo | Who is faster, who is committed, who telegraphs | 6 |
| S8 | Numbers and lines | How many on each side; a line to hold; who can reach whom | 7 |
| S9 | People to protect | Anyone behind the user; a shield or guard obligation | 7 |
| S10 | Light, weather and ground condition | Dusk, night, lamplight, rain, wind, smoke, mud, frost | 8 |
| S11 | Concealment and witnesses | Who watches; what the Seal or the story keeps hidden | 8 |
| S12 | Hands and weapon | Bare-handed, armed, one weapon lost, a borrowed tool; what the hands can do | 9 |
| S13 | Condition: injury, fatigue, energy left | Wounds, breath, the Depletion State (Part 3 Area 12.4), the budget of this fight | 10 |
| S14 | Intent and style | Kill, stop, drive off, hold, spare; the fighter's own style and affinity | 11 |

**Rule 45.2.1.2: Readings Interact.** A reading rarely decides alone. Thatch underfoot (S1) and a crouching target (S3) together close a high kick and a wide sweep and open a short downward strike. The writer reads the readings together and does not stop at the first that allows a move.

**Rule 45.2.1.3: The Frame Moves.** A fight is not one situation. The frame changes with each exchange: the target stands up, the ground gives, the light goes, a protectee steps behind the user. The writer re-reads the frame at each change and the next move follows the new frame, not the old.

## Area 3: Ground, Footing, Cover, Space and Crowding

### 3.1: Ground and Footing

**Rule 45.3.1.1: Footing Limits the Body Before the Move.** Every move that needs a planted foot, a turn or a push off the ground needs ground that gives that. A wide stance, a spin, a leap or a heavy lunge on mud, wet thatch, loose gravel, ice or a rolling log is slow, wide or ends in a slip. On such ground the lawful choices are the short, low, planted ones (a close strike, a brace, a short step, a grip), or a move that carries its own footing (a Step-function move, Part 6 Area 3.4.1, which is built to move on bad ground at the cost of energy).

**Rule 45.3.1.2: The Ground Is Also a Tool.** Mud takes a heel and drops an advancing foot; a lane's wet clay holds a fallen man; a roof's thatch gives under weight and is a hazard to the heavy; a step or a rail is a lever. The writer uses the ground for the fighter who knows it, and against the fighter who does not.

**Rule 45.3.1.3: Weight and Surface.** A heavy fighter on thatch, an upper floor, a thin ice crust or a footbridge risks the surface. A heavy move (a stamp, a drop, a charge) there either breaks the surface or is held back, and the text shows which.

### 3.2: Space, Cover and Crowding

**Rule 45.3.2.1: Space Sets the Size of the Move.** A move needs room for its arc. A sweeping, whirling or long-reach move in a lane, a doorway, a stair or a crowded yard strikes the wall, the cart or an ally. In tight space the lawful choices are straight, short and close: thrusts, short strikes, grips, shoulder and elbow work, a brace. In open ground the long, wide and running moves open.

**Rule 45.3.2.2: Cover Is Used or Respected.** A fighter behind a wall, cart, post or shield is hit only where the cover leaves them open. A move aimed at a covered part either goes around (a wide step, a high arc), goes through (a move built to break or bypass, Part 6 Area 3.2.4), or is not used. A fighter seeking cover uses it and does not stand in the open for a move that a short step would make safe.

**Rule 45.3.2.3: Crowding Cuts Both Ways.** In a press of bodies a fighter cannot back away, cannot swing wide and often cannot see; a grip, a shoulder, a short strike and a braced hold fit. A crowd also shelters a smaller fighter and hinders a larger one. Crowded space is a reason to choose control and close moves and a reason that big area moves harm friends (Area 7).

**Rule 45.3.2.4: Village Ground.** For the saga's village scenes the writer reads these defaults unless the ledger says otherwise. Lanes are narrow, with walls, fences and carts on both sides. Thatch roofs take light weight and burn. Mud after rain or at the water's edge is slick. Yards and squares are open but littered (benches, carts, tools). Doorways and gates are choke points. Fences and woodpiles are cover that breaks. These are readings of the Situation Frame, not new canon about any location; a Location Ledger entry rules where it speaks.

## Area 4: Bodies and Posture

### 4.1: Posture Governs What Can Reach What

**Rule 45.4.1.1: The Rule of the Target's Body.** A move must be able to reach a part of the target that is, at that moment, within reach and open. A strike aimed at where the target's head, chest or legs are not is not used. A target that crouches has no head at kick height; a target that is prone has no standing guard to push through; a target that is airborne cannot be braced against or swept at the feet.

**Rule 45.4.1.2: Posture Table.** The posture of the target (S3) and of the user (S4) close or open families of moves.

| Posture | Closed | Open |
|---|---|---|
| Standing, guard up | A plain frontal strike to the guarded part | Feints, low line, grip, trip, a move that bypasses a guard (Part 6 Area 3.2.4), the flank |
| Standing, guard down or opened by a missed blow | Nothing is closed by posture | A quick direct strike to the open part; a finishing blow |
| Crouching or kneeling | High kicks, high wide cuts, a head-height strike, a standing throw | Downward strikes, knee or low kicks, a push over, a grip on the neck or shoulder, a low sweep |
| Prone or grounded | Standing stamps beyond the user's balance, a high kick, a rising strike from above their reach | A pin, a downward strike, a stamp, a drag, a bind (Part 6 Area 3.5.1), stepping out of reach |
| Airborne (a leap, a throw, a fall) | A sweep at the feet, a guard of the legs; a frontal brace of a body with no footing | A counter-strike to the open body, a catch or redirect, a stepped-aside miss |
| Mid-stride or charging | A stand-and-wait wide move that needs time | A braced hold, a sidestep with a turn, a trip, a shoulder to the line of travel |
| Off balance | A heavy committed move that needs balance | A short strike, a push in the direction already leaning, a trip |
| Exposed flank or turned back | A move at the guarded front | A strike at the flank or back, a grip from behind |
| Seized or held | A free swing | Moves that work from a hold: elbow, head, stamp, a grip break, a throw |
| User carrying or holding someone | Any move needing two hands or a full turn | One-handed short moves, a guard with the body, retreat |
| User on one knee or slipping | A leap, a run-up, a kick above waist height | A rising strike, a low sweep, a guard |

**Rule 45.4.1.3: The Reach of the Body Is Real.** The user's own body limits the move: a kick cannot be thrown while both hands are gripping a rail and the feet are not set; a strike from the ground has the reach of a seated arm; a fighter whose shoulder is hurt does not throw that arm.

**Rule 45.4.1.4: Posture Changes Within a Move and Between Moves.** A move that drops, lifts, turns or leaps changes the user's posture, and the next move follows the posture the last one left. A user left airborne, low or turned is open until the posture is recovered. This is a cost of the move and a counter-window (Area 6).

**Rule 45.4.1.5: Body States of Defence.** A guard up is a closed front. A guard down is an opened front. A person who has just blocked a blow with a braced arm has that arm busy. A person who has just struck is committed. The writer lets the last exchange decide the next opening.

**Rule 45.4.1.6: A Weakness Is a Choice, Not a Gift.** A target's open part is a chance only to a fighter with the position, reach and speed to use it. A fighter who sees an opening and cannot reach it does not take it.

## Area 5: Reach, Range, Height, Size and Anatomy

### 5.1: Reach and Range

**Rule 45.5.1.1: Three Ranges.** Melee (a body's length or less: grips, short strikes, elbows, a short blade at its best), mid (an arm's or weapon's reach: most strikes, thrusts, the long weapon at its best) and long (beyond the weapon: projectiles and thrown things, a charge, a Step, a Projectile move, Part 6 Area 3.2.2). A move has a natural range; used outside it, it falls short or lands without force.

**Rule 45.5.1.2: Who Holds the Range Holds the Fight.** The fighter with the longer reach keeps the range and the shorter fighter must close it or be hit; the shorter fighter who closes is strong at melee and weak in between. The writer shows the closing as a cost (a blow taken, a gap crossed, a Step spent).

**Rule 45.5.1.3: Height and Reach Differential.** A taller fighter strikes down and hits the shorter one's head and shoulders with little effort; a shorter fighter strikes up and reaches the taller one's body and legs more easily. A short fighter rarely lands a clean head-height kick on a tall one at melee; a tall one rarely gets a low sweep easily past a short one. Against a mounted or raised opponent the same holds.

### 5.2: Size and Anatomy

**Rule 45.5.2.1: A Beast's Body Chooses the Move.** A beast is read by its body (Part 40 Area 5, the twelve traits, and the Kind entry). The posture table of Area 4 applies to the beast's own stance.

| Body | Typical consequences |
|---|---|
| Quadruped, low (a wolf, a fox) | Head and spine at waist or knee height; high strikes miss; downward strikes, kicks to the ribs, a grip on the scruff or flank; it bites low and circles |
| Quadruped, large (a buck, a bear) | The mass carries a charge; a head-on brace is costly; a sidestep and flank strike, a trip, a strike at the legs or jaw; a bear stands and reaches |
| Horned or tusked | The head is the weapon and the danger; the body behind the head is the opening; a hold at the horn base or neck only by a stronger fighter |
| Winged (a hawk, a raven) | Attacks from above and breaks off; the user fights the dive, not the bird: a guard overhead, a pull-down move, a Projectile; a bird on the ground or roof is slower |
| Small swarm (many small beasts) | A single heavy move wastes itself on one; area moves (Part 6 Area 3.2.3), sweeping guards, a cleared circle, fire, noise; a Step to move away from the mass |
| Armoured (hide, scale, shell, bone plate) | An ordinary strike has little effect; the gaps (eye, throat, joint, belly), a move that bypasses armour (Part 6 Area 3.2.4), or a blow that unbalances rather than harms |
| Long-bodied or coiled | Reach is longer than it looks; the user fights the strike's line and the grip; a pin at the head or tail |

**Rule 45.5.2.2: A Person's Anatomy.** A person has two arms, two legs, one head and a centre of weight. A move that needs a third limb, a joint to bend the wrong way, or a reach the body does not have is not available. Fighters of unusual build are read from the ledgers (a very tall or broad person, a child, an old person).

**Rule 45.5.2.3: Size Changes What Works.** A small fighter does not stop a large charge with a bare brace; a large one does not easily follow a small one through a gap. Lawful offsets of size are skill, position and a move built for it (Part 44 Area 3).

## Area 6: Tempo, Telegraph and Counter-Windows

**Rule 45.6.1.1: Speed Sets Who Chooses.** The faster fighter chooses the exchange and the slower answers. A faster fighter may use a quick short move or take a long wind-up; a slower fighter must use a move whose start is hidden, short or already in motion.

**Rule 45.6.1.2: Telegraph.** A move with a long or visible start (a wide swing, a leap, a charged strike, a Cast deployment, Part 6 Area 5.2) is seen and answered by a fighter who is awake to it. It is used when the opponent cannot answer (held, occupied, blind, stunned, boxed in, turned away) or when its start is covered. A move with a short start (a jab, a short thrust, a flicked wrist, a step) is used when time is short.

**Rule 45.6.1.3: Commitment and Recovery.** A move commits the user. After a heavy strike, a leap or a lunge, the user needs a beat to recover posture (Rule 45.4.1.4). That beat is the counter-window of the exchange. A cautious move leaves a short window and a bold one leaves a long window.

**Rule 45.6.1.4: Counter-Windows.** An opponent's committed move, miss, wide step or recovery is an opening. The best answer to an opening is a move the user can reach and finish inside the window: a short, fast, direct move when the window is short, a heavier move when the window is long. A heavy move into a short window is late and is itself exposed.

**Rule 45.6.1.5: Cast and Channeled Moves Need Cover.** A move that must be built before release (Cast) or held (Channeled, Part 6 Area 5.2) is used with space, time or a guard (a protector, a wall, a distance). Using one with an enemy on the user's chest is a mistake; a disruption (Part 6 Area 8) forces the failure table.

**Rule 45.6.1.6: A Single Decisive Move Is Lawful.** One well-chosen move at a window can end an exchange or a fight. This is not a shortcut; it is the best case of the Move Selection Test. The fight is short because the situation, not the number of moves, decided it.

## Area 7: Numbers, Lines and Protecting Others

**Rule 45.7.1.1: Many Against One.** A fighter outnumbered chooses moves that deal with several at once or that keep the number who can reach them small: a back to a wall, a doorway held, an area or sweeping move where space allows (Rule 45.3.2.1), a quick succession of short moves down a line, moving so that the foes block each other. A single-target heavy move on one of many costs the fighter a window the others use.

**Rule 45.7.1.2: One Against Few in a Line.** A fighter holding a line (a lane, a gate, a doorway, a ward-line) chooses moves that hold ground and not ones that leave it. A move that carries the fighter forward, back or aside from the line opens the line unless the line is held by others behind.

**Rule 45.7.1.3: Protecting Others.** A fighter with a person behind them (a child, a wounded person, a shield-bearer, a ward) has an obligation the move must honour. The moves open to them are guards, holds, braces, redirects and strikes that keep the protectee out of the line; a wide area move, a leap away or a strike that draws the enemy onto the protectee is not. If the story wants the protector to fail, the failure comes from the foe, the ground or the numbers (Area 3 and Area 7) and not from a move chosen carelessly.

**Rule 45.7.1.4: Friends in the Arc.** A move whose force or arc covers an area (Part 6 Area 3.2.3 and Area 3.10 radius) harms whoever is in it. In a crowd or a mixed line the area move is used only when the friends are out of it, or the text shows the cost to them.

**Rule 45.7.1.5: Support.** In a group the fighter chooses for the group: a guard that lets another strike, a bind that holds a foe for an ally, a Support function (Part 6 Area 3.7) when a wounded person needs it. The best move is sometimes not an attack.

**Rule 45.7.1.6: Pairs.** Two allies fight better than apart when they use the other's openings: one draws, one finishes; one guards, one strikes. The move of one is chosen with the position of the other in view.

## Area 8: Seeing and Being Seen

### 8.1: Light, Weather and Ground Condition

**Rule 45.8.1.1: Light.** In dusk, night and lamplight a fighter fights what they can see. Precise strikes at a small target, long-range throws and a move that needs a sight of the foe's hands are poor in the dark; moves that rely on contact, feel and sound (a grip, a bind, a push, a brace) are better. A cultivator may sense by qi (Part 3), by sound or by touch, and the text shows which sense stands in for sight. Lamplight makes shadows and glare; a lamp can be struck out or turned into a hazard.

**Rule 45.8.1.2: Weather and Wetness.** Rain and mud spoil footing (Rule 45.3.1.1), dampen thatch, slip grips and blur sight. Wind turns thrown and light things and carries sound. Fog hides range. Heat and cold tire the body and stiffen hands. Smoke blinds and chokes. Each is a reading, and each is used by the fighter who knows it.

### 8.2: Concealment, Witnesses and the Seal

**Rule 45.8.2.1: Who Is Watching Is a Reading.** A fighter who must not show their power (a hidden rank, a secret, a sealed level) chooses moves that fit the surface they are showing: a plain strike for a plain man. Using a move that reveals more than the fighter can afford before witnesses is a choice with a price in the story (Part 27).

**Rule 45.8.2.2: The Seal Is Unchanged.** Under Part 42 Rule 42.3.4.2 Hercules and Bison fight at an effective Champion Level 8 High with their Signature and Catastrophic types blocked by the Seal; Elite, Fusion and Mythic are Latent through the effective realm. Part 45 changes none of it. Their selection is made among Basic, Intermediate and Advanced moves of manuals they hold and may use (Part 42 Rule 42.3.3.1). Bison's transformation and the horn and tail innate moves stay sheathed before witnesses unless the author rules otherwise (the hero ledgers). Under the Seal the choice among the Usable moves is wide and Part 45 uses all of it: a Basic move to close, an Advanced move to end, in any order the moment calls for.

**Rule 45.8.2.3: A Latent Move Is Not a Choice.** A move the fighter knows but may not execute is not an option in the Move Selection Test. It may be shown as knowledge (a stance drilled slowly, the order of the movements recalled) and never executed (Part 42 Rule 42.13.1.3).

## Area 9: Hands and Weapons

**Rule 45.9.1.1: Bare-Handed Fighters Use Bare-Handed Geometry.** A fighter with no weapon has the reach of an arm or a leg, the force of a hand, a shoulder, an elbow, a knee, a foot or the head, and the grips of a hand. The moves are strikes, grips, locks, throws, sweeps, braces and holds of the unarmed families. Blade arcs, cutting edges, thrusts of a point, a haft's leverage and a weapon's extra reach are not theirs. A bare-handed fighter who wants reach finds it in a thrown object, a stolen tool, a Step or a charge, and the text shows that.

**Rule 45.9.1.2: Armed Fighters Use the Weapon's Geometry.** A move of a weapon type is built for that weapon: a saber cuts and slashes, a short blade thrusts and works close, a halberd or polearm needs room and keeps range, a staff swings and levers, a shield or a defence weapon holds a line. The move is not used without the weapon, or with a weapon of another family, unless the manual says it works so.

**Rule 45.9.1.3: Weapon and Space.** A weapon needs room for its arc and its reach (Rule 45.3.2.1). A long weapon in a lane or a crowd is a handicap; a short one in an open field against a long weapon is the same. The fighter chooses the grip, the length and the move to fit.

**Rule 45.9.1.4: Weapon Lost, Broken or Borrowed.** A fighter whose weapon is lost or broken can use only the moves that the remaining tool or the hands allow. The fighters of the exile, whose weapons were lost, fight bare-handed from Chapter 006 (Canon Amendment 2026-10-19). A borrowed tool is used for what it is: a plank is a club, a pole a staff.

**Rule 45.9.1.5: Type and Function Fit.** A move's Function (Part 6 Area 3) must fit the job: a Strike for a blow at hand, a Projectile for a distance, a Shield or Deflect for a guard, a Bind or Suppress for control, a Step for position. A Defense move does not kill; a Strike does not guard. A move is chosen for the job the moment needs.

**Rule 45.9.1.6: Type Is Not Function.** The type (Basic to Mythic) says how hard the move is to do and how much it costs. A Basic move can be a decisive blow; an Advanced move can be a plain guard. The type does not say that a move is more fitting. Fit comes from the readings of the Frame.

## Area 10: Cost, Fatigue and the Energy Budget

### 10.1: What a Move Costs

**Rule 45.10.1.1: The Cost Classes.** Part 6 Area 5.3 sets the cost of a move by its type: Basic minimal, Intermediate low, Advanced moderate, Elite high, Signature variable, Fusion very high, Catastrophic extreme, Mythic reality-level. The reserve impact of a single use runs from negligible (Basic) through minor (Intermediate) and noticeable (Advanced) to significant (Elite) and beyond. Part 45 uses those classes unchanged. The figures stay in the spec and the gate; the page shows signs (Rule 45.1.1.9).

**Rule 45.10.1.2: Mortal Tier 1.** A fighter without qi of their own pays in stamina, not qi (Part 6 Area 5.1): breath, strength and the hands. The same budget logic holds in words.

**Rule 45.10.1.3: Effort Accrues.** Each costly move adds fatigue: the reserve falls, the Depletion State of Part 3 Area 12.4 moves down (Optimal, Taxed, Strained, Depleted, Critical, Exhausted), the success rate of later moves falls and the heaviest types become unavailable at the lower states. This is the mechanism the removed order rule was wrongly standing in for. A fighter who goes straight to a costly move pays for it; a fighter who saves it pays later.

**Rule 45.10.1.4: Cooldown, Recovery and Commitment.** A costly move leaves a short recovery in the body (Area 6): the breath, the hands, the stance. A Cast or Channeled move occupies the user (Part 6 Area 5.2). Heavy moves are not strung together with no beat between unless the text shows the cost.

**Rule 45.10.1.5: Wounds and Fatigue Lower Success.** Injury and energy depletion change success rates (Part 6 Area 2.1) and may close a move (Part 3 Area 16.4). A hurt arm closes the arm's moves. A fighter at a low state uses the cheap moves.

### 10.2: The Energy Budget of a Fight

**Rule 45.10.2.1: A Fight Has a Budget.** For each fight the spec states, for each named fighter, how many costly moves the fight lets them spend and what it leaves them. The budget follows the fighter's reserve and the fight's length: a short fight against a weak side spends little; a long one against many spends a great deal.

**Rule 45.10.2.2: A Couple of Advanced Moves Can Finish a Fight.** A fighter at a healthy reserve may open and end a fight with a handful of Basic and Intermediate moves and a couple of Advanced moves. Two or three Advanced moves (each noticeable) leave the fighter in Taxed or Strained at worst; that is the lawful, ordinary shape of a short decisive fight. A fighter who uses Advanced moves again and again will reach Strained and Depleted, and the text shows it (Rule 45.10.1.3).

**Rule 45.10.2.3: Spend Where It Counts.** The best use of the budget is a costly move where the situation needs it (a heavy foe, an armoured body, a line to break, a protectee to save) and a cheap move elsewhere. A costly move on a small matter (an Advanced move on a straggler a plain strike would stop) is a poor choice; a cheap move against a charging mass is a worse one.

**Rule 45.10.2.4: Cost Is Not Shame.** Spending is lawful. It is priced, not forbidden, and a hero who spends is not weakened by being shown to spend. Naming a lawful move is never presented as a cost of the move (Part 28 Rule 20.9).

### 10.3: Cause and Options

**Rule 45.10.3.1: A Costly Move Has a Cause in the Moment.** An Advanced or higher move is used because the Situation Frame calls for it: the target is armoured, the line is about to break, the protectee is in the path, the window is the only one. The cause is shown by the scene (not announced). A costly move used with no cause in the moment, and shown with no price, is a Check #24 finding (Area 12).

**Rule 45.10.3.2: The Price Is Shown.** After a costly move the text shows a sign sized to it: a held breath after a Basic or Intermediate move, a shaking hand or a burning chest after an Advanced move, a staggered stance after a heavier one. The price is proportionate; it is not a melodrama.

**Rule 45.10.3.3: Options for the Author.** When the author wants options (a named type, or "show me what he could do here"), the planner gives two or three moves of the author's chosen type or any type, each with its Frame reading, its fit, its cost class and its risk, and the author picks. The planner never sets the choice aside.

## Area 11: Chaining, Finishing and Mercy; the Move Selection Test, the Worked Table and the Anti-Patterns

### 11.1: Chaining and Finishing

**Rule 45.11.1.1: A Chain Follows the Situation.** Moves join because the first one leaves the second possible, not because of their grades. A strike that drops the foe opens a downward finish; a guard that takes a blow opens a counter; a Step that closes range opens a close strike. A chain is two or three links; a long unbroken chain of named moves is the move-name spam of Rule 45.11.4.2.

**Rule 45.11.1.2: Finishing.** A fight ends on the move that the end class (Part 5 Area 12) and the Frame call for. It may be any type. A Basic move on a foe already beaten is a finish; an Advanced move on a foe who will not fall is a finish. The finisher is chosen for the result: stopped, driven off, held, killed or spared.

**Rule 45.11.1.3: One Hard Move or Many Light Ones.** Against one tough foe, one hard move at a window may be best. Against many weak foes, light moves one after another are best. The count of moves follows the foes and the ground.

### 11.2: Mercy and Non-Lethal Choices

**Rule 45.11.2.1: Intent Chooses the Move.** A fighter who means to stop, hold or spare chooses moves that stop, hold or spare: a bind, a brace, a push, a strike to the limb or the body that does not kill, a move kept short of its lethal threshold (Part 3 Area 8.2). A fighter who means to kill chooses accordingly. A killing move used on someone the scene says is spared, or a spare used where the scene says the stake is life, is a contradiction.

**Rule 45.11.2.2: Control Without Harm.** Bind, Suppress and Deflect (Part 6 Areas 3.3 and 3.5) are the moves of restraint. A strong fighter with a weak opponent may use control moves to end a fight without hurt, and the text shows the restraint as skill.

**Rule 45.11.2.3: Mercy Costs Something.** A spared foe may rise, a restrained foe may break the hold; mercy leaves an opening the story can use (Part 27).

### 11.3: The Move Selection Test

**Rule 45.11.3.1: The Test.** For each move of a fight the writer asks these questions in this order. A move that fails a question is dropped and the writer returns to the list of Usable moves.

1. **Is the move Usable?** It is in a manual the fighter holds, it is Usable for their effective realm, and the Seal does not block its type (Part 42 Rule 42.3.3.1). A Latent move is out (Rule 45.8.2.3).
2. **Can the body do it now?** The user's posture, holds, wounds and weapon allow it (S4, S12, Area 4 and Area 9).
3. **Does the move reach the target as it now is?** The target's posture, height, size and anatomy leave the part open and in reach (S3, S5, S6, Area 4 and Area 5).
4. **Does the ground and space allow it?** Footing, room for the arc, cover, crowding (S1, S2, Area 3).
5. **Can the user see to do it?** Light, weather, smoke, the senses that stand in (S10, Area 8).
6. **Is the timing right?** The move's start fits the opponent's window and speed; the user is not left exposed past what the story means (S7, Area 6).
7. **Does it keep faith with others?** The protectee, the line, the friends in the arc (S8, S9, Area 7).
8. **Does it fit the intent?** Kill, stop, drive off, hold or spare (S14, Area 11).
9. **Is the cost right?** The type's cost, the fighter's state and the fight's budget allow it, and the moment calls for it; the price will be shown (S13, Area 10).
10. **Is it the best of what is left?** Among the moves that pass, prefer the one that fits the moment most simply, spends least for the result and suits the fighter's style and affinity.

**Rule 45.11.3.2: Style and Affinity.** The fighter's style (Part 17 combat signature, Part 39 behavioural code) and affinity (Part 4) rank the moves that pass the Test: a patient fighter prefers the counter, a fierce one the close; an earth-affinity fighter uses ground and weight, a wind-affinity fighter speed and line. Style breaks ties and never overrides a failed question.

### 11.4: The Worked Table

**Rule 45.11.4.1: Fifteen Situations.** The table shows viable and non-viable choices in generic terms. The move types are used in the spec and gate reading; the page names moves only by their Named Move name.

| # | Situation | Viable | Not viable (and why) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Target crouching, user standing | Downward strike, low or knee kick, a push-over, a grip on the shoulder (any type) | High kick or head-height cut: no head at that height |
| 2 | Target prone, user standing, space clear | Pin, stamp, downward strike, a bind | A sweep at the feet: no feet in the line; a rising blow from below |
| 3 | Target leaps over a fence at the user | A step aside and a strike at the open body; a catch | A guard braced at the legs: the legs are over it |
| 4 | Narrow village lane, one foe, thatch above | Straight thrust, short strike, shoulder, brace | A whirling or wide sweeping move: walls, thatch and ally hit |
| 5 | Mud, user's wide stance would slip | A short planted strike; a Step that carries its own footing | A leap, a spin, a heavy lunge: slip, late |
| 6 | Crowd of weak foes around the user in a yard | A sweeping area move with the friends out of its arc, or short moves down a line | A slow single heavy strike on one foe: the rest hit the user's back |
| 7 | Hawk-like beast diving from above | A guard overhead, a pull-down at the dive, a throw at the bird | A low sweep: the beast is not there |
| 8 | Small swarm of beasts at the knee | A sweeping guard, a circle cleared with an area move, a Step away | One heavy punch at one beast: wasted |
| 9 | Armoured beast with a horned head, charging | A sidestep and a strike at flank or legs, a trip; a bypass move at a gap | A frontal brace at the horn; a plain blow on the plate |
| 10 | Bare-handed user, foe with a blade | A close grip or lock inside the arc, a Step to reach, a thrown object | A blade-geometry cut or thrust: the user has no blade |
| 11 | Dusk, small target far across a yard | A closing Step and a contact move | A precise long throw at a small target in poor light |
| 12 | User holds a child behind them at a gate | A guard, brace or redirect from the gate; a short strike that does not draw the foe past | A leap away, a wide area move, any strike that leaves the gate |
| 13 | Foe recovering from a missed lunge, window short | A quick short direct strike, a trip | A Cast or charged move: late and exposed |
| 14 | User at Strained after two Advanced moves, foes remain | A Basic or Intermediate move, a guard, a retreat; an Advanced move only if the window needs it and the price is shown | Elite or heavier moves at Depleted; a string of Advanced moves with no sign of tiring |
| 15 | Scene says the foe is to be spared; hero strong | A bind, a brace, a push, a strike to the limb | A killing blow; a heavy move that cannot be held back |

**Rule 45.11.4.2: Viable Is Wide.** A viable column lists many moves at many types. The writer picks among them by the Test; the writer is not told which. Any viable move may be of any type, in any order.

### 11.5: The Anti-Patterns

**Rule 45.11.5.1: The Fixed Ladder.** A fight in which a person's moves rise Basic, then Intermediate, then Advanced, in step, with the situation unchanged and no cause given, is a ladder. The ladder is the removed order rule showing through. A fight has no required next grade. The writer breaks the ladder: opens with the move the moment calls for, repeats a Basic move if it is still best, and ends on any type.

**Rule 45.11.5.2: Move-Name Spam.** A run of named moves, a name every sentence, a menu shown for its own sake. A fight shows the moves the moment needs, each with its picture, and fills the space between with plain action, ground and breath. A fight Area shows its moves in proportion to its length and the budget; a named move is a beat of the fight, not its whole substance.

**Rule 45.11.5.3: Ignoring Posture.** A kick to a head that is not at kick height, a strike at a part the target has turned away, a sweep at an airborne target. The posture table (Rule 45.4.1.2) decides.

**Rule 45.11.5.4: Ignoring Ground and Space.** A wide move in a lane, a leap on mud, a whirl in a crowd.

**Rule 45.11.5.5: Free Spending.** A costly move with no cause, or a run of costly moves with no sign of tiring.

**Rule 45.11.5.6: Wrong Hands.** A bare-handed fighter with a blade's arc, or a blade fighter with an unarmed grip they do not have.

**Rule 45.11.5.7: The Protector Who Leaves.** A guard who abandons the line or the protectee for a showy move.

**Rule 45.11.5.8: Name Without the Move's Picture.** A Named Move is bold-italic at every mention (Part 42 Rule 42.13.1.1) and is shown by its picture in the same breath, as the Move-Naming and naming rules already require. The name is never used as a label for what the text did not show.

**Rule 45.11.5.9: Narrating the Test.** The prose never lists the readings, the costs or the budget. The reader sees a person who looks at the ground, the foe, the people behind them, and does the thing that fits.

## Area 12: Where the Law Runs, Gate 1 Check #24 and the Cross-Part Table

### 12.1: Before Drafting: the Situation Line

**Rule 45.12.1.1.** At the Chapter Spec build (and the build plan of a whole-built Chapter), every Area with a fight carries, under the Fight line and its Engagement Ledger (Part 44 Area 5), a **Situation line**: the ground and space, the light and weather, the postures that matter, the people to protect, the intent (kill, stop, drive off, hold, spare), the energy budget of each named fighter, and the moves the fight may use as a menu with the situation each fits. The menu is not a sequence. Where the author named a move or a type, the line says so. Where the author wanted options, the line carries them (Rule 45.10.3.3). The light continuity audit (Part 38 Rule 3.3.2) lists, report-only, any fight Area without a Situation line.

**Rule 45.12.1.2: Menu, Not Order.** The Situation line never states that a move of one type follows a move of another, and never states that link codes are met in the scene.

### 12.2: Gate 1 Check #24

**Rule 45.12.2.1: Scope.** Check #24 runs at Gate 1 on every Area whose text holds a fight (the same definition as Check #23: any exchange of blows or weapon fire between named or counted sides, and any battle, siege, ambush, pursuit or war event shown or reported). For an Area with no fight, every element reads N/A. In a whole-built Chapter it runs once for each fight Area block. The guardrail is `.claude/guardrails/quality/guardrail-situational-combat-selection.md`.

**Rule 45.12.2.2: Severity.** Seven elements block and one reports.

| Element | Reads | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 24-1 Body and posture | Each move is possible in the posture and body state of the user and reaches a part of the target that is open and in reach at that moment | FAIL, quoted from the text |
| 24-2 Ground, space, light and weather | Each move fits the footing, the room for its arc, the cover, the crowding, the light and the weather the text has set | FAIL |
| 24-3 Reach, size and anatomy | Each move fits the range, the height and reach difference, and the body of the opponent, a beast included | FAIL |
| 24-4 Hands and weapons | A bare-handed fighter uses no weapon geometry; an armed fighter uses the weapon's; a move is used only with the tool it needs; no Latent or Seal-blocked move is executed | FAIL |
| 24-5 No fixed ladder; no move-name spam | No ordering of moves by type is stated or followed as a rule; no run of moves rises in grade step by step with no cause in the situation; no more moves are named than the fight and the budget bear | FAIL where the order is stated as a rule or the ladder is shown; move-name spam FAILs when the named moves crowd out the action |
| 24-6 Cost, cause and fatigue | An Advanced or higher move has a cause in the moment and shows a price in proportion; a run of costly moves shows fatigue and respects the Depletion State | FAIL |
| 24-7 Faith and intent | A protector keeps their line and protectee; the move fits the stated intent (spare, stop, kill); friends are not in the arc without a cost shown | FAIL |
| 24-8 Strain | A move that is lawful but a poor fit by the Move Selection Test, with no reason in the text, or a result that rests on a perfect window | Reported to the author as information; never a FAIL |

**Rule 45.12.2.3: Builds On.** Check #23 reads whether the engagement is plausible by level. Check #24 reads whether each move fits its moment. Check #17(b) reads the Named Move name against the Move-Naming Law. Check #12 reads the bold-italic form. Check #8 element 8-V reads the level made known. Check #24 repeats none of them.

### 12.3: Send-Back

**Rule 45.12.3.1.** A FAIL goes back through the normal Gate 1 loop (`[REVISE AREA]`, or the Whole-Chapter FAIL loop). The lawful fixes are: change the move to one the Frame allows, change the Frame by showing what changed the posture, ground or light, add the price of a costly move, or revise the Situation line through a revised Chapter Spec. A 24-8 item is put to the author at `[CONFIRM WRITE]` with the move, why it is a poor fit and the better options.

### 12.4: Scope in Time

**Rule 45.12.4.1.** Part 45 is not retroactive. It applies to future writing only, starting with Chapter 006, by author ruling of 2026-10-19. Chapters 001 to 005 are left as written: Check #24 is not run on them, no redraft or revision of them is implied, and a written Area is re-checked against Check #24 only if the author orders its revision later, and then the check runs on the revision.

### 12.5: Record of What Was Removed

**Rule 45.12.5.1: The Removed Scene-Ordering Rule.** Before 2026-10-19 working text (the Chapter 006 proposal and spec draft, and a sentence of the Manual Ledger's `eligibility-rules.md` E3 that counted link codes as met only if a scene showed the earlier manual) carried a requirement that each Intermediate or Advanced move follow a Basic move of its own manual in the same Area, so that link codes were met in scene. That requirement was invented, is not canon and is removed. The link codes of Part 42 stay as eligibility prerequisites to hold a manual; they are never an ordering rule of a scene.
