## Overview

Part 28 (Novel Creative Flexibility Law) is the bridge between the SSC Canon's locked mechanical law and the act of authorship. Parts 2 to 27, 29 to 33 and 35 govern what is mechanically, structurally, and consequentially true about the Starward Saga universe: cultivation realms, technology tiers, faction taxonomies, consequence architecture, dialogue voice, content density. None of those Parts specify what a given character says in a given scene, what a given sect is named, or how a given breakthrough feels from inside a character's body. Part 28 governs that remainder — the author's creative latitude — and makes it a governed category within the Canon rather than an unregulated gap.

Part 28's twenty Areas fall into five functional clusters:

- **The Locked/Negotiable boundary (Areas 1, 2, 12):** the twelve Foundational Principles (P28-1 through P28-12) that establish freedom-in-instances-not-categories; the full enumeration of what is Locked Canon versus Negotiable Canon; and the absolute Prohibited Creative Acts (causally unsupported jumps, information miracles, consequence evasion, the Forbidden Realm Vocabulary hard-block, and the Content Limits of Area 12.3).
- **The four domains of creative freedom in practice (Areas 3, 5, 6, 7, 8):** Naming Freedom, Tactical Variation, Behavioral Authenticity, Emotion-From-Experience, and Cultural/Event Expansion — each governing one concrete axis along which the author invents specific instances within Canon-governed categories.
- **Depth, compliance, and lore mechanics (Areas 4, 9, 10, 11, 15, 17):** the Prose Depth and Immersion Law (the depth standard set per Area by its pacing and the Chapter Spec, and its Ten Named Depth Pillars); the Authorization Protocol for Tier 2 high-magnitude creative acts; the Simultaneous Multi-Part Compliance Law (the No-Local-Decisioning Mandate); Lore Depth and Retroactive Enrichment; the Contextual Earning Law (the Three-Beat Test governing when canon information may be delivered in prose); and the Lore Creation Law (the mandatory ledger lore capture for every named entity).
- **Cross-cutting standards and audit apparatus (Areas 13, 14, 16):** the seventeen master Narration Standards (N28-1 through N28-17, including the em-dash prohibition and the contraction rule (barred in narration and in dialogue), the dialogue format standard, and the craft standards N28-15 through N28-17); the five-question Flexibility Audit Protocol; and the full Cross-Part Dependency Master Table mapping Part 28's relationship to every other Canon Part.
- **Structural and procedural law (Areas 18, 19, 20):** the Structural Naming Mandate governing Novel-Part/Chapter/Area names; and the Decision Consultation Gate (Author-as-Rudder Law) — the mandatory sequence-step-0 AskUserQuestion protocol by which the author steers major narrative-direction decisions before any prose is drafted, including the Exhaustive Clarity Mandate and the rule that pacing is decided in the Chapter Spec for every Area and carried at the Gate (Rule 19.3a); and the Entity Naming Law — the narrative-significance test that converts Area 3's naming freedom into a naming obligation at Gate 1 Check #17, with its thematic naming methodology and major/minor consultation split, and the named-item rule that sets the name of a made or used thing in italic and enters it in the named-item register at Gate 2 (Area 20, Rule 20.8). Naming a person, thing, place or move is never presented as a cost, risk or downside in any consultation, plan or recommendation, and the named version is the recommended default (Area 20, Rule 20.9).

**Relationship to other Parts.** Part 28 does not invent new mechanics — every creative act it authorizes must still satisfy the full, simultaneous compliance of every other governing Part (Area 10's No-Local-Decisioning Mandate). Part 28 also does not govern how required content *feels* in prose — that thematic layer belongs to Part 36 (Narration Novel Thematic Law). Part 28 and Part 36 are both Authority Tier 0 — coequal narrative authorities with a defined division of labor: Part 28 governs the **structural presence** of what must be in prose, Part 36 governs the **thematic quality** of how it reads. Both must be satisfied simultaneously; neither substitutes for the other (see Area 19.7 and Part 36 Area 15.1 for the full sovereignty delineation).

## Area 1 — Foundational Principles of Creative Flexibility

### 1.1 — The Twelve Foundational Principles

**P28-1 — Part 28 Is the Bridge Between Law and Authorship.**
The SSC Canon governs the laws of the Starward Saga universe. Part 28 governs how those laws are inhabited by the act of authorship. Without Part 28, every invented name, every unexpected tactical reversal, every emotional choice made in the moment of composition would require explicit Canon authorization. With Part 28, the boundary is clear: the Canon's locked content is inviolable, and everything else is the author's creative domain. P28-1 establishes that the author's creative latitude is not a gap in the Canon — it is a governed category within the Canon.

**P28-2 — Creative Flexibility Must Adhere to All SSC Canon Parts and Areas Simultaneously.**
No creative decision — no matter how small — is exempt from simultaneous compliance with the full Canon. Inventing a faction name does not exempt the faction from Part 22's governance. Naming a cultivation technique does not exempt it from Part 6's taxonomy and Part 3's realm ceiling. Describing a cultural custom does not exempt it from Part 25's CPT architecture. Creative freedom operates *within* the Canon, not *beside* it. A creative act that satisfies Part 22 but violates Part 16 is a Canon violation. A name that satisfies Part 6 but contradicts the character's Part 3 realm is a Canon violation. Simultaneous compliance with all Parts is not optional — it is the condition that makes creative expansion possible at all.

**P28-3 — Creative Freedom Exists in Instances, Not in Categories.**
The SSC Canon governs categories, not instances. It defines the category "cultivation sect" but does not name every sect. It defines "Named Moves" but does not list every technique. It defines "cultural tournament" as a valid cultural event type but does not script every tournament. Creative freedom is the freedom to invent instances within governed categories. The category is locked; the instance is free. A Creative act that invents a new instance of an established category — a new sect, a new Named Move, a new tournament, a new cultural custom — is free creative authorship. A creative act that invents a new category not authorized by the Canon — a new type of cultivation system, a new structure of consequence that bypasses Part 27, a new ideology architecture that bypasses Part 20 — is a Canon violation.

**P28-4 — All Flexibility Is Negotiable Canon, Not Override.**
Creative expansion produces Negotiable Canon — content that is original, invented, and not pre-specified by the SSC Canon, but that is fully consistent with it. Negotiable Canon adds lore, depth, richness, and immersion. It never overrides, contradicts, or silently replaces Locked Canon. When Negotiable Canon content is established in the prose — a location is named, a faction is introduced, a custom is described — that content becomes a fixed reference point going forward. Negotiable Canon, once written and established in the novel, cannot be retroactively contradicted.

**P28-5 — Prose Depth Is Governed, Not Optional.**
The Starward Saga is written at a pacing-appropriate prose depth — an immersion standard that renders every combat exchange, every conversation thread, every cultivation principle, every sensory environment, every internal psychological state, every power-system interaction, every cultural register, every consequence cascade, every Named Move deployment, and every continuity thread with complete, scene-appropriate specificity. This is not a stylistic preference. It is a governance mandate under Part 28. Thin prose — summary, functional description, expository dialogue, flat action, any compressed rendering of content that sacrifices depth — is a Part 28 violation in the same way that a realm continuity error is a Part 3 violation. The immersion mandate is a law. The immersion a scene calls for is set by the pacing classification of its Area (Part 2 Area 1 section 1.6.2.1, Pacing Vocabulary): Fast, Medium or Slow, decided for the Area in the Chapter Spec. The size of an Area is a separate matter, set by Part 2; **Part 2 owns those figures and its reasoning is not restated here.** The ten Named Depth Pillars (DP-1 through DP-10) defined in Area 4 are simultaneously mandatory in every scene regardless of pacing category, weighed on the Area (for a scene continued across consecutive Areas, on those Areas read in order) and not paragraph by paragraph (Area 4.2).

**P28-6 — Creative Freedom Includes Names, Behavior, Emotion, and Tactics.**
The four primary domains of creative freedom are: (1) **Naming** — all proper nouns that the Canon does not pre-specify are free creative territory; (2) **Behavior** — characters, factions, civilizations, and institutions may behave in any way that is consistent with their governed identity and does not violate Canon law; (3) **Emotion** — all emotional states and emotional expressions are free creative territory, subject to the requirement that they are grounded in lived prose experience and consistent with Part 26 psychological governance; (4) **Tactics** — tactical choices in combat, negotiation, information warfare, and social interaction are free creative territory, subject to Part 5 force legitimacy and Part 3 power ceiling governance. None of these domains are governed by canon pre-specification. All are governed by consistency with canon law.

**P28-7 — No Local Decisioning.**
A local creative decision is a decision made by reference to only the immediate scene, without checking the decision against the full Canon. Local decisioning is prohibited. When a character advances in cultivation, that advancement must be checked against Part 3 breakthrough requirements, Part 4 affinity implications, Part 16 classification update requirements, Part 26 psychological impact, Part 27 consequence implications, and Part 17 (or 18 or 19) Hero/Villain/Side Character Ledger update triggers — simultaneously, not sequentially. The prohibition on local decisioning applies to every creative act: naming, behavior, emotion, tactics, cultural invention, faction interaction, and consequence rendering. Every creative act is checked against all Parts at once.

**P28-8 — Creative Freedom May Add Depth Retroactively, May Clarify Ambiguity, May Enrich Scenes.**
The Starward Saga is written across multiple volumes. Creative acts in later volumes may enrich, deepen, and clarify content established in earlier volumes. A location named in Volume 1 may receive its full cultural history in Volume 3. A faction introduced as an unnamed background element in Volume 1 may become a named, fully characterized faction in Volume 2. A custom mentioned briefly in early prose may be given its full ritual architecture later. Retroactive enrichment is permitted. What is not permitted: retroactive contradiction. Earlier established Negotiable Canon content cannot be contradicted by later creative choices. Retroactive enrichment deepens; it does not rewrite.

**P28-9 — Creative Expansion May Never Invalidate or Contradict Locked Canon.**
Locked Canon content — the governance laws of all SSC Canon Parts — is inviolable. Creative expansion may never produce a result that contradicts Locked Canon. A character may not advance cultivation in a way that bypasses Part 3's breakthrough requirements. A faction may not possess technology in a way that exceeds Part 8's civilization ceiling. A named relic may not function in a way that contradicts Part 12's material ceiling. A dialogue scene may not involve a character speaking in a register that contradicts Part 29's voice architecture for that character's classification. No creative freedom — no matter how narratively convenient — overrides Locked Canon.

**P28-10 — Surprise Is Governed, Not Prohibited.**
Narrative surprise — the reversal of reader expectation, the unexpected tactical choice, the emotional turn that reframes everything that came before — is one of the primary tools of the author and one of the primary sources of reader investment. Surprise is not prohibited by the Canon. What the Canon prohibits is *causally unsupported surprise* — the reversal that contradicts what has been established, the tactical choice that ignores power ceiling governance, the emotional turn that is not traceable to established experience in the prose. Governed surprise is surprise that the reader could not predict but that they recognize, on reflection, as fully consistent with what they knew. Ungoverned surprise is surprise that the reader experiences as a Canon violation — and rightly so.

**P28-11 — Part 28 Authorization Is Required for Specific High-Stakes Creative Expansions.**
Most creative acts do not require explicit Part 28 authorization — they are simply free, subject to the standard simultaneous-compliance mandate. However, certain creative expansions involve elements of such narrative magnitude or such potential for Canon disruption that they require explicit acknowledgment under Part 28 before execution. The single list of these elements is Area 9.2; any narrative element designated as requiring Part 28 authorization in any other Part is added to it there. Part 28 authorization means explicit narrative preparation — these elements must be foreshadowed, structurally grounded, and consequentially integrated before they appear.

**P28-12 — Every Creative Act Is Bound by the SSC Canon — This Is the Source of Its Power.**
Creative freedom without constraint is not power — it is noise. The immovability of Locked Canon is what makes creative invention within it meaningful. A faction name invented in a universe with no governance can be anything — and therefore means nothing. A faction name invented within Part 22's complete faction classification framework means something specific: it has a type, a scale code, a power tier, a cultural profile, a place in the civilizational hierarchy, and a governance relationship with every other faction the reader has already met. The Canon does not constrain the author's creative vision — it gives the author's creative vision weight.

## Area 2 — The Negotiable Canon Framework

### 2.1 — What Negotiable Canon Is

Negotiable Canon is the category of content that is: (a) original to the author's creative act; (b) not pre-specified by any SSC Canon Part; (c) fully consistent with all SSC Canon Parts; and (d) established as fixed within the novel once written.

Negotiable Canon is the living body of the Starward Saga's specific world — the particular names, histories, customs, techniques, rivalries, and textures that make the governed universe feel inhabited. Without Negotiable Canon, the Starward Saga would be a governance document. With it, the governance document becomes a world.

### 2.3 — The Negotiable Canon Establishment Rule

Once Negotiable Canon content is established in the prose — a name is given, a custom is described, a history is told — that content is fixed. It becomes a reference point for all subsequent prose and may not be contradicted. The author who names a faction's headquarters in Chapter 3 cannot rename it in Chapter 11. The author who describes a cultural marriage ceremony in one volume cannot describe the same culture as having no marriage ceremony in a later volume. Negotiable Canon, once established, acquires the same inviolability as Locked Canon.

**The Establishment Threshold:** Content is established when: (a) it is given a proper name, (b) it is described with specific detail that creates expectation of consistency, or (c) a character-level consequence (IE event, CSS event, PIS event) is attached to it. Content that is vague, unnamed, or consequentially unattached is not yet established and may be refined.

### 2.4 — The Retroactive Enrichment Rule

Later prose may enrich, deepen, and expand upon earlier Negotiable Canon content without contradiction. A faction named in Volume 1 may have its full internal governance structure, cultural traditions, and political history revealed in Volume 3 — provided the revelation does not contradict anything established earlier. A Named Move first used in Volume 1 may have its cultivation technique architecture, affinity requirements, and historical origin fully elaborated in Volume 2 — provided the elaboration is consistent with the move's established effects. Retroactive enrichment is a powerful narrative tool: the world deepens as the reader progresses, without becoming inconsistent.

## Area 3 — Naming Freedom Law

### 3.1 — The Naming Freedom Principle

All proper nouns that the SSC Canon does not pre-specify are free creative territory. The author may invent any name for any instance within any governed category. The name is governed only by two constraints: (a) it must be consistent with the cultural and linguistic register established by the relevant Part 25 CPT for the naming culture; and (b) once given, it is fixed.

### 3.2 — Nameable Categories

The following categories of proper nouns are freely nameable by the author:

**Geographic and Spatial Names:**
All locations — cities, villages, kingdoms, empires, star systems, star clusters, galaxies, sacred grounds, mountain ranges, rivers, seas, deserts, forests, wilderness areas, dimensional zones, underground caverns, void-spaces, and any other spatial designation within Part 13's cosmological hierarchy — may be named freely. Infrastructure elements — roads, gates, towers, bridges, ports, markets, administrative districts, academies, prisons, arenas, training grounds, and all other built-environment elements — may be named freely.

**Faction and Organizational Names:**
All cultivator sects, military organizations, governmental bodies, commercial enterprises, criminal organizations, academic institutions, intelligence agencies, religious orders, noble houses, political factions, syndicates, alliances, guilds, schools, networks, and all other faction-category instances within Part 22's taxonomy may be named freely.

**Character Names:**
All characters — heroes, villains, side characters, background characters, historical figures, named ancestors, named successors, named enemies, named supporters — may be given any name consistent with their cultural origin per Part 25 CPT.

**Named Moves and Cultivation Techniques:**
All cultivation techniques, Named Moves, affinity-typed techniques, fusion techniques, body-cultivation forms, soul-cultivation methods, heart-cultivation practices, pill-cultivation processes, formation inscription sequences, and relic-activation protocols within Part 6's taxonomy may be named freely. The name must be consistent with the cultivation world's aesthetic register — it must not use contemporary Earth terminology that is anachronistic to the world.

**Cultural Events and Ceremonies:**
All tournaments, festivals, examinations, ceremonies, rites of passage, political gatherings, trade fairs, cultivation competitions, military exercises, academic convocations, mourning rituals, marriage ceremonies, naming ceremonies, death rites, seasonal celebrations, and all other cultural event types within Part 25's framework may be named and detailed freely.

**Technology Types and Applications:**
All specific technology systems, devices, platforms, programs, installations, weapons, vehicles, relics, and operational forms within Part 8's fourteen domain types may be given specific names and operational descriptions, provided they are consistent with the technology tier of the civilization producing them.

**Vehicles and Weapons:**
All vehicles, weapon systems, relic-weapons, and formation arrays that the Canon does not pre-specify as named objects may be given specific names and identifying characteristics within their Part 32 and Part 33 governed classification.

### 3.3 — Naming Register Law

All names must be consistent with the linguistic and aesthetic register of the culture that produces them, governed by Part 25 CPT. A CPT-1 Hierarchical-Sacral civilization produces names that reflect hierarchy and sacred designation — terms of power, rank, and cosmological reference. A CPT-3 Mercantile-Exchange civilization produces names that reflect transaction, value, and practical function. A CPT-5 Syncretic-Adaptive civilization produces hybrid names that blend cultivation and technology registers. Names that impose contemporary Earth linguistic patterns on cultures for whom those patterns are historically impossible are a Part 29 register violation.

### 3.4 — Name Immutability

Once a name is given and established in the prose, it is immutable. The author may not rename established locations, factions, characters, techniques, or cultural events. If a narrative reason for a name change arises — a city is renamed by its conquerors, a sect is renamed after a governance restructuring, a character adopts a new name as a consequence of arc transformation — the name change must be (a) consequentially motivated, (b) explicitly rendered in the prose, and (c) consistent with the cultural norms governing renaming in the relevant Part 25 CPT.

## Area 4 — Prose Depth and Immersion Law

### 4.1 — The Prose Depth and Pacing Mandate

The Starward Saga is written at the immersion depth that the Area's pacing calls for, as the confirmed Chapter Spec sets it — meaning that the depth of immersion, sensory specificity, psychological rendering, combat granularity, dialogue threading, world-texture, power-system precision, consequence propagation, cultural register fidelity, Named Move architecture, and continuity integration is that of a fully rendered scene and not that of functional genre prose. The depth is a quality of the writing and carries no percentage. This is a governance mandate, not an aspiration.

**This standard exists because** the Starward Saga is primarily a fighting novel — cultivation, technology, or both — and every scene, whether combat or not, must be rendered at the density appropriate to its pacing that makes the reader feel every blow, hear every word in full, understand every technique, all in plain, clear wording (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06), live inside every character's psychology, and carry the world's lore naturally. An Area that merely conveys what happened has failed. An Area at pacing-appropriate depth makes the reader feel, understand, and remember — and leaves no scene gap that a careful reader could identify.

**Area Size and Pacing Are Separate (Authoritative Source: Part 2 Area 1 section 1.6.2.1, Pacing Vocabulary, and Part 2's Area size rule):** Pacing (Fast, Medium, Slow) is set per Area in the Chapter Spec. It says how the scene moves. The size of an Area is a separate matter: an Area is written to one of three targets, 1,600, 1,800 or 2,000 words, decided in the Chapter Spec, with a passing range of 1,280 to 2,400 words (Part 2 Rule P2-Area-7). Every combination of pacing and Area size is lawful: a Fast battle may run long and a Slow scene of world-building may run short. A count outside 1,280 to 2,400 words is a blocking finding: the write waits for the author to confirm the Area or send it back. The finding gives the word count and the counting method, and no agent pads or trims an Area to move the figure (`quality/guardrail-threshold-consultation.md`). The pacing category is carried from the Chapter Spec into the Gate 1 Position Header Block (Rule P2-Pacing-2).

### 4.2 — The Ten Named Depth Pillars (DP-1 through DP-10)

All ten Depth Pillars are **simultaneously mandatory** in every scene, weighed on the Area (Part 2 Area 1, Tiers 4 and 5; Part 36 Area 2.4) and, where a scene continues across consecutive Areas, on those Areas read in order, and not paragraph by paragraph. A Pillar the Area does not carry is reported at Gate 1 under the Depth Pillars dimension of Part 36 Check #16 (Dimension 16-DP) for the author's ruling; it is a craft report and does not block. A Pillar a continued scene has not yet carried is the exception: a Pillar a continued scene has not yet carried is reported open at a Gate 1 before the scene's last Area and judged at that last Area (Part 36 Area 2.4). A paragraph may be quiet as Area 4.5 allows, because the Area carries every Pillar. The Pillars apply equally to combat prose, dialogue prose, training prose, transition prose, lore prose, and every other prose type. No prose type is exempt from any Pillar.

---

**DP-1 — Combat Sequence Density**

Every combat scene narrates every exchange at full resolution. No exchange is skipped and none is summarized. An **exchange** is one clash that changes the positions or states of the fighters, rendered once with its outcome and cost; a flurry of blows that ends with the positions or states of the fighters changed is one exchange, rendered as one clash. For every combat beat: the attacker's cultivation state and intent at the moment of deployment, the Named Move or technique mechanics, the physical trajectory and energy signature, the defender's detection and response, the collision outcome and power differential rendering, the environmental change produced by the exchange, the psychological state of both parties during and after the exchange, and the consequence to story state are all rendered explicitly. A combat scene that summarizes "they exchanged dozens of blows" without narrating the exchanges in it has committed a DP-1 violation. DP-1 is weighed on the whole fight Area, as Part 2 Rule 3.3 weighs its four combat elements: an exchange may run on across consecutive Areas of the fight, each Area of the fight serves the fight, and no exchange of the fight is skipped or summarized. **At battle scale** (above small-group scale: Part 5 tier E3, more than 50 fighters) DP-1 applies to the focal engagement, the part of the battle that the POV character takes part in or sees whole; the rest of the field is shown by what that character perceives, carried by specific, sensory, consequence-bearing detail (a named beat, a count, a change of ground), never by summary judgement, and no writer is asked to narrate every exchange of every fighter on the field. Part 36 Area 6.4 sets out the craft of a battle. Combat is the spine of the Starward Saga — it must be narrated with every exchange fully resolved and clear, in plain words a reader can follow blow by blow, and with the visceral weight of lived experience simultaneously. (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06)

**DP-2 — Dialogue Threading**

Every conversation renders the complete exchange — no line is compressed into summary, no subtext is omitted. For each exchange: the spoken words in full; the physical behavior of the speaker during and between lines (gesture, posture, eye contact, breath, micro-expression); the unspoken content the speaker is carrying but not saying; the listener's internal response to what they heard (as distinguished from what was meant); the cumulative shift in relationship state from the opening line to the closing exchange; and any silence, pause, or physical act that carries meaning between words. Dialogue is never merely information delivery. Every conversation is a contest, a performance, a negotiation, or a confession — and it must read as such at every level. The speech is natural and flowing (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.7, Canon Amendment 2026-10-08): sentence length varies, the moment sets the manner, and clipped, formulaic, tally-like or report-form speech is a fault. The speech is built from plain words (a ceremonial speaker keeps the tone and the fixed phrases of the occasion, in plain words); the depth stays in full. **DP-3 — Lore and World-Building Integration**

Every scene, weighed on the Area, weaves cultivation system lore, world history, faction dynamics, geographical texture, cultural customs, and institutional behavior into the experience of the scene — not as expository blocks inserted for the reader's benefit, but as the natural texture of how characters think, speak, and move through their world. A cultivator on a battlefield thinks in cultivation concepts, in plain words, and the first use of a special term carries its one-clause picture from the scene; one new special term at a time, never a wall of them in one sentence. (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06) A diplomat from a technology-dominant civilization notices what a cultivation palace lacks. Lore is not explained to the reader; it is lived by characters and absorbed by the reader through that living. Every new named entity, location, technique, or faction that appears in prose must have its lore grounded in the Ledger (per Part 28 Area 17) and delivered through the Contextual Earning Law (Area 15).

**DP-4 — Sensory Environment**

The environment of a scene is a place the reader can stand in, and it answers to what happens in it. A full sensory anchor is required only where the plan sets it: an Area the Chapter Spec tags `[SENSORY ANCHOR REQUIRED]` leaves the reader at least one concrete, specific sensory anchor (Part 38 Rule 2.3.5a; Part 36 Area 9.3), and every other Area deploys zero, one or all five sensory channels as the scene requires (Part 36 Area 9.1). This Pillar sets no sensory floor of its own beyond the tag. The elements the environment draws on are: spatial geometry (how the space is bounded and how much of it is visible to the POV character), dominant ambient sounds and their sources, the quality of light and its movement, the ambient cultivation or technological energy level and its flavor, temperature and weather where outdoors, smell and texture where relevant to the scene's content, and the ways in which the environment changes in response to narrative events (energy discharge reshaping terrain, weather responding to powerful cultivation, technology altering the acoustic environment). The sensory environment is not static backdrop — it is an active participant in every scene.

**DP-5 — Internal POV Architecture**

Every POV character's internal state is rendered across four channels simultaneously, drawing its content from the Part 26 psychological architecture. **Cognitive channel:** what the character is thinking, analyzing, and concluding — their interpretation of the scene's events, their tactical assessment, their ideological framing of what they observe. **Emotional channel:** the specific emotional coloring of this moment, traced to its causal roots in established prior-prose experience — not labeled ("she was afraid") but rendered through the specific texture of this character's fear or anger or grief. **Somatic channel:** what the body is doing with the emotional state — the physical symptoms that the character experiences from inside (not the symptoms an observer would see, but what the character feels in their chest, hands, gut, throat). **Behavioral channel:** how the internal state shapes what the character does, says, withholds, or prevents themselves from doing. All four channels are present for each POV character of the scene, weighed on the Area; an Area may give one channel its full weight and leave the others quiet.

**DP-6 — Power System Precision**

Every cultivation technique deployment, every Named Move execution, every power-level differential, every energy state transition, every soul technique, every affinity expression, every CI integration event, every weapon discharge, and every formation array interaction must be rendered with exact compliance to the governing power-system Parts (Parts 3, 4, 6, 11, 31, 32, 33). Imprecise power rendering — using a technique at a level inconsistent with the character's established realm, invoking an affinity the character has not established, deploying a CI device with capabilities that exceed its established specifications — is a DP-6 violation and an audit FAIL. Power is the physics of this world. It must be as consistent as physics.

**DP-7 — Consequence and State Propagation**

Every scene changes the story's state, and every change is made visible in prose; the Area carries the change, and an Area that continues a scene need not close it. Changes must propagate through all applicable state dimensions: IE event declaration where a significant interpersonal event occurs (Part 20); CSS event declaration where a consequence-severity event occurs (Part 27); relationship state update visible in behavior and speech; faction alignment update where applicable (Part 23); information state update where new knowledge is revealed or concealed (Part 24); world-location state update where the scene materially alters a location (Part 13). Nothing may happen in prose without observable consequence. A fight ends and someone is changed — in power, in belief, in relationship, in position. A conversation ends and something is known or decided that was not before. The state propagation must be implicit in the prose (the reader feels it) and explicit in the Gate 2 Ledger updates.

**DP-8 — Cultural and Social Register Fidelity**

All characters speak, move, and behave in exact accordance with their cultural register, social hierarchy position, faction ideology, institutional role, and psychological state — simultaneously. Speech patterns, terms of address, body language conventions, and emotional display rules are all culturally governed: a Sky Throne cultivator does not speak to a peer the way a Sapphire Basin technologist does; a Gale Kingdom aristocrat's deference rituals are not those of a sect elder. Social interactions carry their full hierarchy weight — power differentials are visible in how bodies orient, how silences are filled, how requests are framed. Cultural customs are observed without authorial explanation — the reader infers from behavior, as a character present in the scene would; a custom is shown by what people do and see, in plain words, and one word carries one meaning in the Area. (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06)

**DP-9 — Named Move Architecture**

Every Named Move deployed in combat receives its Part 6 narration treatment. A move's first appearance, a Signature move, a Catastrophic or Mythic move and a turning-point move receive it in full, without compression. Mandatory elements of the full treatment: the pre-activation state (cultivator's cultivation surge, stance shift, weapon orientation, energy gathering); the activation announcement (bold-italic move name, carried in full dramatic weight in prose); the technique's physical and energy mechanics during execution; the opponent's detection, assessment, and response; the collision or resolution outcome including power differential rendering; the immediate aftermath (energy expenditure, body state, positional change); and the consequence to the combat's tactical state. A repeat use of the same move, or a similar use of it on a different type of target, may be rendered compactly (Part 6 Area 11.4): one or two sentences that still show the physical act, one sensory detail and the consequence. Over-explaining a move the reader already knows kills the story. A Named Move mentioned in a bare single sentence that leaves out the physical act, the sensory detail or the consequence — "He activated Storm Cleave and the enemy fell back" — is a DP-9 violation. Named Moves are architectural events in combat. They must be treated as such.

**DP-10 — Continuity Integration**

Every Area explicitly demonstrates its connection to all established prior prose. Every fact that was established in prior prose and is relevant to this scene is reflected in character behavior, speech, and awareness. No orphaned facts — a wound established two Areas ago is still a wound; a promise made in Chapter 1 colors the behavior of the character who made it in Chapter 4; a Named Move learned in a training arc is referenced with the character's developed familiarity when deployed in a war arc. The Pre-Area Read Mandate (the Area before it, and for a continued scene the Areas of that scene, in full; older history through the ledgers; Area 4.6) exists to enforce this Pillar. Continuity is not a fact-check at the end. It is the texture that makes the world feel real.

### 4.3 — The Prohibition on Thin Prose

Thin prose — summary narration, functional description, expository dialogue, flat action, emotional assertion without grounding, compressed combat, or any other rendering that sacrifices a Depth Pillar — is reported under Dimension 16-DP for the author's ruling (nothing is blocked). The depth standard of this Area names these thin forms:

- **Summary in place of scene:** Writing "The battle lasted three hours and ended in the sect's defeat" — DP-1 violation. Every exchange is narrated.
- **Announcement in place of experience:** Writing "She broke through to the next realm" — DP-5 violation. The interior and exterior experience of every breakthrough is fully rendered.
- **Assertion in place of evidence:** Writing "He was furious" — DP-5 violation. The specific cognitive, emotional, somatic, and behavioral markers of this character's fury in this moment are rendered.
- **Expository dialogue:** Characters explaining world information to each other that they would both already know — DP-3 violation. Lore is lived, not lectured.
- **Flat combat:** "They exchanged dozens of blows and he landed the decisive strike" — DP-1 and DP-9 violation. Every exchange is narrated.
- **Dropped subtext:** Dialogue rendered as pure information exchange without internal responses, behavioral tells, or power dynamics — DP-2 violation.
- **Stilted dialogue (Canon Amendment 2026-10-08):** Clipped, formulaic, tally-like or report-form speech, one sentence length throughout a scene, or a run of lines that each open on a vocative — DP-2 violation (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.7), reported at the named dimension 16-DP for the author's ruling and never a FAIL.
- **Environmental vacuum:** An Area the Chapter Spec tags `[SENSORY ANCHOR REQUIRED]` that leaves the reader no concrete sensory anchor — DP-4 violation (Part 38 Rule 2.3.5a; Part 36 Area 9.3). An Area the spec does not tag is judged under Part 36 Area 9.1, and carrying few or no sensory channels is not a DP-4 violation.
- **Consequence-free events:** Plot events that change nothing about story state — DP-7 violation.

### 4.4 — Scene Density Law

A scene usually carries a set of narrative functions by the time it ends. Read on the Area (for a continued scene, on its Areas in order), it usually carries a character revelation (a new dimension of an established character becomes visible), a relationship development (the relationship state between two or more characters changes measurably, or the relationship between a character and someone absent or remembered does), a world-building contribution (something new about the world's history, power system, culture, or geography is woven into the scene), and a consequence (the scene changes something about the story state that cannot be unchanged). The four functions belong to the scene and not to each Area of a continued scene: an Area that continues a scene may carry one function, several, or none of its own. The scene is read at its last Area; before it, a function the scene has not yet carried is reported open and is not a failure (Part 36 Area 2.4). The four functions are guidance and not a gate. They are what a scene usually carries, read on the Area (for a continued scene, on its Areas in order) and judged at the scene's last Area. When the scene has not carried one of them by its last Area, Gate 1 reports it as information and never as a FAIL. The Chapter Spec and the story decide what a scene needs. A solo Area, in which one character thinks or decides, is lawful: with one character on the page, the relationship development is that character's bond to an absent person, a duty or a belief shifting. A solitary scene, a journey or a pause after a fight needs no exemption. Whether a function the report names is supplied, in the scene's last Area or by the repair path of Part 36 Area 16.6(a), is the author's ruling (Part 38 Rule 4.2.3). The functions stand apart from the word count range of each Area.

### 4.5 — Immersion Continuity Law

The immersion standard of this Area, once engaged at the start of a scene, must be maintained at that level for the entire scene, read on the Area. The standard is held by the scene and not by each paragraph, so a **quiet paragraph** is lawful. A quiet paragraph is a spare one (a short beat, a breath between two exchanges, a passage of plain movement, a paragraph that lets the one before it land) that still serves the scene: it carries the scene's movement, its viewpoint or its current, and at least one concrete thing, an act, a sensation, an object or an utterance. Quiet is not thin: summary in place of scene, functional description, assertion in place of evidence and the other forms listed in Area 4.3 are prohibited wherever they fall, in a quiet paragraph as in any other. The immersion is not a resource that is earned once and then coasted on: across the Area the world exists continuously, and every Pillar that one paragraph rests is carried by another paragraph of the same Area. A stretch of the scene in which the world stops being there is a gap in the texture, and there are no gaps in that texture.

### 4.6 — The Pre-Area Read Mandate (Previous Area at All Pacing Levels)

The Pre-Area Read Mandate has one scope at every pacing level: **before drafting each new Area, the previous Area of the Chapter (the last Area of the previous Chapter at a Chapter's first Area), and for a scene continued across Areas every earlier Area of that scene, are read in full from the written `.md` files (Rule P2-Area-2).** Older history is not re-read file by file. It reaches the writer through the ledgers read with the governing Parts (the Living fields, the Canon Events Log and the chapter summaries), and when a fact from an older Area must be checked, that Area is opened by search and read at the passage that matters. The Continuity Integration Pillar (DP-10) is satisfied by this reading together with the ledgers: it cannot be met by sampling or summarizing the Areas read, and it cannot be met by leaving a ledger fact unread. The scope grows with the Area and not with the length of the novel, so the Mandate is as workable at Chapter 30 as at Chapter 1, and it admits no exception for session boundaries.

### 4.7 — Depth Is Not Repetition

The depth standard of this Area is achieved by widening the prose — adding perception, interiority, and world texture around each thing the scene contains: more of what the POV notices, more of what the body carries, more of the world's grain in the scene's objects, sounds, and procedures. It is never achieved by restating content the prose has already delivered in an additional layer. An Area that delivers one insight as image, then names it in narration, then restates it in dialogue has not gained depth; it has spent its word count on redundancy while the breadth the standard actually demands went unwritten. Depth is breadth of lived detail, not repetition. The countable rule is N28-16 (the Single-Statement Standard, Area 13); a revision that answers "not deep enough" by re-delivering existing content through another channel fails this Area and N28-16 simultaneously.

## Area 5 — Tactical Variation Law

### 5.1 — The Tactical Freedom Principle

Combat, negotiation, information warfare, and social strategy are all domains in which the author has complete freedom to invent specific tactical choices, reversals, surprises, and variations — provided those choices are consistent with the power levels, psychological states, and ideological positions of the characters making them, and do not violate Part 5's force legitimacy framework, Part 3's power ceiling, or any other governing Part.

Tactical surprise is not only permitted — it is required. A novel whose conflicts are always resolved by the expected application of established power is a novel with no tension. The Starward Saga's combats, negotiations, and strategies must feel genuinely unpredictable while remaining genuinely consistent.

### 5.2 — Permitted Tactical Variations

**Unexpected Tactics:**
A character may deploy a tactical approach that their opponent (and reader) did not anticipate, provided the tactic is: (a) consistent with the character's cultivation or technology level and specialization type; (b) consistent with the character's psychological state and ideology; (c) not dependent on power that the character has not established; and (d) consistent with the environment and context established in the prose.

Example: A character with a water affinity, in a desert environment, using the psychological anticipation of their own limitation to deliberately bait an opponent into a formation that concentrates ground moisture — is a governed tactical surprise. A character with a water affinity suddenly manifesting fire techniques to win a fight — is not.

**Emotional Reversals:**
A character may experience an emotional reversal — a sudden shift in emotional state produced by an event or realization — that changes their tactical approach mid-scene. This reversal must be: (a) traceable to established prior experience in the prose; (b) consistent with Part 26 psychological architecture; and (c) consequentially meaningful — the reversal must change something.

Example: A cultivator about to deal a killing blow who suddenly recognizes in their enemy the face of their dead mentor, and who is momentarily destabilized by grief — producing an opening that their enemy exploits — is a governed emotional reversal. A cultivator who inexplicably becomes compassionate mid-combat with no prior characterization of compassion — is not.

**Sudden Reinforcements:**
Reinforcements may arrive unexpectedly, provided their arrival is: (a) causally grounded — there is an established reason why these characters or forces would be in this location at this time; (b) proportionally appropriate to the Part 5 escalation framework; (c) not dependent on travel capabilities that contradict Part 15.

**Environmental Twists:**
The environment may intervene in conflict in unexpected ways — terrain collapses, weather shifts, energy formation zone disturbances, Sacred Ground amplification changes, previously undetected formation array activation. These must be: (a) consistent with Part 13's location governance; (b) causally traceable — the environmental element was present but undetected, not invented in the moment; (c) consequentially meaningful.

**Strategic Deceptions:**
Characters may deceive opponents through feints, false retreats, false surrenders, false reveals of power level, and strategic manipulation of information — governed by Part 24's information architecture. Deceptions must be: (a) consistent with the deceiver's cultivation or technology capability to execute; (b) consistent with the deceiver's ideology and psychological state; (c) susceptible to detection through appropriate counter-intelligence or soul sense use by the deceived party.

**Unexpected Alliances:**
Characters or factions may form alliances that surprise the reader, provided those alliances are: (a) causally motivated by shared interest or ideology that is consistent with Part 20 governance; (b) consistent with the parties' established Part 23 affiliation positions; (c) rendered with full consequence weight — unexpected alliances produce IE events and CSS events.

### 5.3 — The Tactical Consistency Requirement

All tactical choices must be consistent with the character's established profile. A character who has been characterized as direct, aggressive, and confrontational throughout fifty pages of prose may not suddenly deploy a strategy of subtle long-term manipulation without an established causal reason for the behavioral shift — and that causal reason must be rendered in the prose. The prohibition on causally unsupported surprise applies to tactics as forcefully as to any other creative act.

### 5.4 — Prohibited Tactical Violations

The following tactical choices are prohibited regardless of narrative convenience:

- Deploying power that the character has not established (surprise breakthroughs mid-combat without prior narrative preparation are prohibited per P28-10 and Part 3 breakthrough requirements)
- Tactical choices that contradict the character's ideology and psychological state without causal grounding
- Environmental interventions that are not traceable to established elements of the scene's location (inventing the environmental element at the moment it is needed)
- Reinforcements that require travel speed that violates Part 15 for the characters' cultivation or technology level
- Information-warfare tactics that require access to information the character cannot plausibly have per Part 24

## Area 6 — Behavioral Authenticity Law

### 6.1 — The Behavioral Freedom Principle

Characters, factions, civilizations, and institutions may behave in any way that is consistent with their governed identity. There is no behavioral script. The Canon governs what characters are (classification, ideology, psychology, consequence arc) — it does not specify what they do in any given moment. Behavioral freedom is the freedom to invent specific behavioral instances within governed categories.

### 6.2 — Character Behavioral Authenticity

Character behavior must be authentic to the character's full governed profile — not to a simplified version of that profile. A character is not merely "ambitious" or "compassionate" or "cold." A character has: a specific ideology category with specific variant (Part 20); a specific cultivation specialization type (Part 16); a specific psychological architecture with current PIS tier (Part 26); a specific Heart state (Part 4); a specific relationship web (Parts 17-19); and a specific consequence arc (Part 27). Authentic behavior is behavior that expresses the full complexity of this profile in the specific moment — not the simplified version.

**The Complexity Mandate:** Characters may not be reduced to their dominant trait in behavioral rendering. A character who is primarily SDI-Conquest in ideology may still experience moments of genuine tenderness toward a mentor — because their psychology (Part 26) has a relational architecture that predates and coexists with their ideological hardening. That tenderness is not inconsistent with their ideology — it is the complexity beneath the ideology. Behavioral authenticity renders both.

### 6.3 — Faction Behavioral Authenticity

Factions may take actions that surprise readers, provided those actions are consistent with the faction's: (a) Part 22 classification type and governance archetype; (b) Part 20 ideological orientation of its leadership; (c) Part 25 cultural paradigm type; (d) Part 23 current affiliation positions; and (e) Part 21 civilization tier context. Factions have internal politics, competing sub-factions, conflicting loyalties, and individual members who may act against faction policy. This internal complexity is behavioral authenticity — it makes factions feel like institutions populated by humans rather than monolithic entities with uniform will.

### 6.4 — Civilizational Behavioral Authenticity

Civilizations may respond to events in ways that reflect their CCT tier, civilization type, cultural paradigm, and current ideological state — and may behave in ways that readers find surprising within those parameters. A Stellar-tier civilization under ideological stress may behave with the paranoia and irrationality of a much lower-tier civilization. A Subsistence-tier civilization with extraordinary ideological coherence may achieve strategic outcomes that seem disproportionate to its resource base. These behavioral variations are governed — they must be traceable to the civilization's full canonical profile — but they are not pre-specified. The author determines which facets of the profile are dominant in any given moment.

### 6.5 — The Prohibition on Robotic Behavior

Behavioral authenticity forbids mechanical character rendering — the treatment of characters as governed entities executing their category definitions rather than as persons. A cultivator who always behaves exactly as their ideology category predicts, who never surprises themselves or the reader, who never experiences the gap between what they believe and what they feel — is a governance output, not a person. Part 28 requires that characters be rendered as persons: the gap between their public behavior and their private experience, between their ideology and their emotions, between what they say and what they mean, must be present in the prose. The Canon governs the architecture. The author peoples it with the irreducible specificity of personhood.

### 6.6 — Part 39 Interface

Area 6 states behavioral authenticity as a **permission and a prohibition**: characters may behave in any way consistent with their governed identity, and they may never be rendered as governance outputs executing their category definitions. Both halves are Part 28's.

What Area 6 does not itself supply is a **specification**. Rule 6.5 tells the author what the prose must not read like without giving the author, the drafting agent, or the ledger anywhere to record what this particular person actually does instead. **Part 39 (Character Behavioral Synthesis Law) is that specification**, and it is the executable form of this Area:

- Part 28 Area 6 says behavior must not be mechanical. **Part 39 says what this character does instead, in 36 registered Layers, and records it in Character Ledger Sections 11 and 12.**
- Part 28 Rule 6.2's Complexity Mandate forbids reducing a character to their dominant trait. **Part 39 Rule 39.40.2.1 asks for it in the registry**: registered contradiction pairs per character above Tier C (two is the guide).
- Part 28 Rule 6.3 and 6.4 permit factions and civilizations to behave with internal complexity. **Part 39 Layer L19 and the Part 37 Collective Behavior Code block are where that complexity is stored and audited.**
- Part 28 Rule 6.5's prohibition is enforced at **Gate 1 Check #20** (Part 39 Area 43) and its change-detection counterpart **Check #21** (Part 39 Area 44).

**Sovereignty.** Part 28 retains the freedom principle, the Complexity Mandate, and the Prohibition on Robotic Behavior in full. Part 39 owns the registry, the drift law and the audit. Neither Part may excuse a technical requirement of another Part (Part 39 P39-14). Where this Area and Part 39 appear to conflict on what behavior is *permitted*, Part 28 governs; where they appear to conflict on how behavior is *specified, recorded or audited*, Part 39 governs.

## Area 7 — Emotion-From-Experience Law

### 7.1 — The Emotion-From-Experience Principle

All emotional states experienced by characters must be traceable to specific experiences in the prose. No emotion may be asserted without being grounded. A character does not simply feel grief — they feel grief because the specific person they loved in the specific ways established in the prior prose is now gone, and because their Part 26 psychological architecture, their Part 4 Heart state, and their Part 20 ideology all shape the specific texture of what grief means for this particular person.

### 7.2 — Emotional Authenticity Standards

**Grounding Requirement:** Every emotional state must have a causal chain traceable to prior prose events. The cause may be immediate (the death just witnessed) or deeply historical (the grief is for a person the reader never met, but whose absence has shaped every scene in which this character has appeared). The emotional response must be proportional to the established relationship between the character and the cause.

**Texture Requirement:** Emotions must be rendered with their full sensory, cognitive, and behavioral texture. The body carries emotion — the specific physical manifestations of grief in this cultivator's trained physique, in this soldier's conditioned posture, in this elderly merchant's weathered face — are specific and must be rendered as such. Generic emotional description ("she was sad," "he was angry") fails the texture requirement.

**Duration Requirement:** Significant emotional events — CSS-5+ consequences, IE-4+ ideology events, PIS-4+ psychological impacts — do not resolve immediately. They persist. They alter the character's behavior in scenes that follow. They resurface in unexpected moments. The Part 26 psychological governance framework requires this temporal spread. Part 28 reinforces it: emotions established in the prose must have a duration consistent with their magnitude.

**Interaction Requirement:** Emotional states interact with the character's cultivation, ideology, and tactical choices. A character in acute grief (PIS-7) does not fight at full effectiveness (per Part 26). A character in ideological ecstasy (IE-3 Expansion Event) takes risks that their baseline pragmatism would forbid. A character in the first shock of betrayal (IE-5 Core Shift Event) may speak with an honesty that their normal social register would filter. These interactions are not optional — they are Part 26 and Part 20 governance requirements that the emotional rendering must satisfy.

### 7.3 — Prohibited Emotional Rendering

The following are prohibited under the emotion-from-experience law:

- **Asserted emotion:** Telling the reader a character feels something without showing the specific physical, behavioral, and cognitive evidence of that feeling
- **Disproportionate emotion:** A character exhibiting emotional intensity that exceeds what their established relationship with the cause would produce
- **Causally disconnected emotion:** An emotional state with no traceable cause in prior prose
- **Instantaneous resolution:** A significant emotional wound (PIS-4+) resolving within the same scene without a complete Part 26 resolution pathway having been traversed
- **Emotional stasis:** A character who, after a CSS-7+ consequence event, shows no sustained emotional impact in subsequent scenes
- **Emotional uniformity:** A character who responds to all events with the same emotional register, regardless of what the events are and what the character's prior experience has established

## Area 8 — Cultural and Event Expansion Law

### 8.1 — The Cultural Expansion Principle

The cultural framework established by Part 25 defines the categories, axes, types, and paradigms of culture in the Starward Saga. Part 28 governs the free creative expansion of specific cultural content within that framework — the names, descriptions, histories, rituals, and meanings of actual cultural instances.

### 8.2 — Nameable and Expandable Cultural Content

**Tournaments and Competitions:**
Any competition within the cultivation and technology worlds may be freely invented: sect ranking examinations, inter-faction dueling tournaments, cross-civilizational cultivation grand competitions, technology innovation fairs, formation inscription championships, pill alchemy competitions, beast taming contests, military exercises, academic scholastic competitions, and all other competitive forms consistent with the cultural paradigm of the civilization hosting them. These events may be given full names, histories, prestige hierarchies, governance structures, prize systems, and social significance.

**Festivals and Ceremonies:**
All seasonal, cultivation-milestone, political, religious, harvest, astronomical, and martial festivals within any CPT framework may be freely invented and named. Their specific rituals — the sequence of events, the participants, the symbolic vocabulary, the exceptions and taboos — are free creative territory within Part 25's cultural governance.

**Customs and Traditions:**
The specific behavioral customs of any culture — greetings, farewells, forms of address, deference signals, hospitality protocols, gift exchange conventions, conflict resolution rituals, dispute adjudication customs, trading protocols, marriage customs, death customs, mourning periods, ancestral veneration practices — may be freely invented and described, governed by consistency with Part 25's CPT architecture for that culture. One carve-out applies: a form of address, honorific, or mode of address directed *at* a Hero-Ledger or Villain-Ledger character is a major-entity consultation under Area 20.4(f) and is never freely invented under this clause.

**Taboos:**
Every culture has things that are forbidden — not merely governed by law, but socially structured as deeply transgressive, spiritually dangerous, or cosmologically threatening. Specific taboos may be freely invented: topics that cannot be spoken of in certain contexts, actions that trigger social ostracism, spiritual prohibitions associated with particular cultivation affinities, technology-specific ethics boundaries, and sacred-space behavioral restrictions. Taboos must be consistent with the culture's Part 25 CPT.

**Governance Styles and Political Structures:**
Within the Part 22 governance archetype framework (8 types), specific institutional names, procedures, governing bodies, positions, titles, conventions, political traditions, constitutional customs, and political conflicts may be freely invented. A sect with an Elder Council governance archetype may have a specific Council of Seven Elders with specific names, specific areas of authority, specific historical traditions of authority-transfer, and specific ongoing political tensions — all of this is free creative territory.

**Political Conflicts:**
Specific political conflicts within or between factions and civilizations — succession disputes, ideological schisms, resource conflicts, border disputes, trade war dynamics, espionage revelations, treaty disputes, factional coup attempts — may be freely invented, provided they are consistent with the Part 22 faction governance of the institutions involved, the Part 20 ideology of the relevant actors, and the Part 21 civilization tier context. Political conflicts make the world feel lived-in and are required for narrative richness.

**Names of Culture:**
All culture-groups, ethnic designations, subculture names, regional identity labels, cultivation school lineage names, technology tradition school names, and all other cultural identity categories that Part 25 does not pre-specify may be freely named.

### 8.3 — The Cultural Consistency Requirement

All cultural inventions must be internally consistent with the CPT framework governing the culture producing them. A CPT-1 Hierarchical-Sacral civilization's festival must have hierarchical structure — the Ancestor-level cultivators do not perform the same ceremony as the Outer Disciples. A CPT-3 Mercantile-Exchange civilization's tournament will have entry fees, prize pools, and commercial sponsorships — because that is how prestige is constructed in mercantile cultures. Cultural inventions that contradict the fundamental logic of their CPT are Part 25 violations.

### 8.4 — Historical Lore Expansion

The Starward Saga takes place in a universe with deep history. That history — the specific civilizations that rose and fell before the story begins, the specific battles that shaped the current world order, the specific legendary cultivators and inventors whose names are still spoken, the specific cosmological events that altered the geography of the world — is almost entirely free creative territory. The author may invent as much historical lore as the novel requires, provided it is consistent with the physical and cultivation laws that were in effect during the historical period being described.

## Area 9 — Authorization Protocol

### 9.1 — The Two-Tier Authorization Structure

Creative acts in the Starward Saga fall into two authorization categories:

**Tier 1 — Free Creative Acts:** All creative acts that involve inventing instances within governed categories. These require no special authorization — they require only simultaneous compliance with all governing Parts. The vast majority of all creative choices fall in this category: naming, behavior, emotion, specific tactical choices, cultural invention, dialogue content, and prose expansion.

**Tier 2 — Part 28 Authorization Required:** Creative acts that involve elements of such magnitude or Canon-disruption potential that they require explicit narrative preparation, foreshadowing, and structural grounding before execution. See Area 9.2.

### 9.2 — Mandatory Part 28 Authorization Items

The following creative acts require Part 28 authorization — meaning they must be narratively prepared before they appear:

**From Part 3:**
- Narrative participation of a character at the Divine Sea Realm or above
- Deployment of an entity beyond the Divine Sea Realm (not defined)

**From Part 5:**
- Narrative deployment of an E7 Annihilation Conflict event

**From Part 7:**
- Narrative deployment of a Grand Convergence fusion practitioner
- Narrative deployment of a Fusion Origin Convergence practitioner
- Level 4 Cultivation-Technology Integration (all four paths simultaneously)
- Transcendent-ceiling fusion of any type

**From Part 8:**
- Narrative deployment of technology two or more tiers above the deploying civilization's technology ceiling (Part 8)

**From Part 10:**
- Narrative deployment of DC-2 digital consciousness systems
- Narrative deployment of S3+ AI sentience at exceptional ontological boundary cases

**From Part 12:**
- Narrative deployment of any Tier 5 Primordial Grade relic, artifact, or formation array
- Any formation array with effect radius at light-year+ scale (Part 12 Area 6.1 designates this as Part 28 territory)

**From Part 21:**
- Direct narrative participation of CCT-15+ Apex civilizations in the main story (as opposed to historical reference)

**From Part 33:**
- Narrative deployment of WS-VII Existential Class weapons
- Narrative deployment of any Absolute Prohibition weapon category

**From Part 32:**
- CT-Band Tier 15–20 vehicle narrative deployment in active story events (not merely as backdrop)

**The Authorization Standard:** Part 28 authorization means the creative act has been: (a) foreshadowed across at least two prior scenes; (b) structurally grounded in established world-state (the power, object, or civilization is known to exist before it acts); (c) consequentially integrated — its appearance produces CSS and IE events appropriate to its magnitude; and (d) consistent with all governing Parts for the element in question.

### 9.3 — The Authorization Failure Consequence

A Tier 2 creative act executed without fulfilling the Authorization Standard is a Canon violation of the highest severity — because these acts involve elements of world-altering magnitude that cannot be retroactively supported once executed. Their appearance without preparation produces a structural inconsistency that undermines reader trust and narrative coherence. The author must prevent this by always completing the Authorization Standard before executing any Tier 2 act.

## Area 10 — Simultaneous Multi-Part Compliance Law

### 10.1 — The No-Local-Decisioning Mandate

Local decisioning is the error of checking a creative act against only the most immediately relevant Part without checking it against the full Canon simultaneously. It is the most common source of Canon violations in complex world-building. Part 28 prohibits it absolutely.

**Why Local Decisioning Is Dangerous:**
A creative act that satisfies its most immediate governing Part may still violate five other Parts that bear on the same moment. A character who deploys a Named Move (Part 6 compliant) that draws on a cultivation affinity combination (Part 4 compliant) but whose power output contradicts their established realm ceiling (Part 3 violation) and whose deployment escalates the conflict to a tier that has not been narratively legitimized (Part 5 violation) and whose psychological state at PIS-7 makes such precise technique deployment implausible (Part 26 violation) — is a three-Part violation that only appears as a zero-Part violation if the check was local to Part 6.

### 10.2 — The Full-Canon Simultaneous Check

Every creative act must be checked against the following before execution:

| Check Category | Governing Parts | What to Verify |
|---|---|---|
| Power ceiling compliance | Part 3, Part 8 | Character's realm/tier authorizes this output |
| Classification compliance | Part 16 | Character's type classification is consistent with the act |
| Ideology compliance | Part 20 | Act is consistent with character's current ideology |
| Psychological compliance | Part 26 | PIS state makes this act plausible |
| Consequence trigger check | Part 27 | Does this act require CSS consequence generation |
| Ideology event trigger check | Part 20 | Does this act trigger IE events |
| Hero/Villain/Side Char Ledger check | Parts 17/18/19 | Does this require a Ledger update |
| Faction compliance | Part 22 | Act is consistent with faction governance |
| Affiliation compliance | Part 23 | Act is consistent with affiliation positions |
| Information compliance | Part 24 | Character has the information this act requires |
| Cultural compliance | Part 25 | Act is consistent with cultural norms |
| Location compliance | Part 13 | Location supports the act's requirements |
| Travel compliance | Part 15 | Arrivals/departures are travel-time consistent |
| Dialogue compliance | Part 29 | Speech is consistent with voice architecture |
| Tactical compliance | Part 5 | Escalation is consistent with force legitimacy |
| Synergy compliance | Part 31 | Any synergy outputs are consistent with integration model |

### 10.3 — The Cascade Check

When a creative act passes the simultaneous check, it may trigger further checks through cascade: the CSS consequence produced by the act requires its own ideological trigger check; the IE event triggered by the act requires a psychological impact check; the PIS change requires a subsequent combat performance check; the combat performance check requires a power ceiling re-verification. Cascade checking is not separate from the simultaneous check — it is the extension of it through the consequence chain.

### 10.4 — The Check Before the Act Rule

The simultaneous multi-Part check occurs before the creative act is executed in the prose, not after. Checking after execution creates the pressure to rationalize a completed act rather than to evaluate it fairly. The discipline of Part 28 is pre-execution discipline: the author conducts the simultaneous check, makes any necessary adjustments to the planned creative act, and only then renders it in prose.

## Area 11 — Lore Depth and Retroactive Enrichment Law

### 11.1 — Lore Depth as Creative Mandate

The world of the Starward Saga has a depth that no single novel volume will exhaust. Every location has a history. Every faction has a founding story, a defining crisis, a lineage of leaders. Every Named Move has an origin — who developed it, under what circumstances, what its failure cost, who inherited it and how they changed it. Every cultural custom has a reason — the historical event it commemorates, the fear it channels, the aspiration it expresses. Part 28 mandates that the author engage this depth wherever the prose benefits from it, and resists the temptation of shallow world-building in which the world only exists as far as the plot currently requires.

### 11.2 — Retroactive Enrichment Standards

When later prose enriches earlier established content, the enrichment must satisfy:

**Consistency:** The enrichment must not contradict anything established earlier. The history revealed in Volume 3 must be consistent with all implications of the content established in Volume 1.

**Organic Integration:** The enrichment must feel discovered rather than retrofitted. The reader must feel that this depth was always present in the world — the author was simply revealing what was already there. Retroactive enrichment that feels like a retcon is a narrative failure even when it does not constitute a Canon violation.

**Proportionality:** The depth of enrichment must be proportional to the narrative weight of the element being enriched. A minor location that appears in one scene does not require the same depth of historical enrichment as the hero's home sect. The author allocates enrichment resources proportionally.

**Consequence Preservation:** If earlier prose established consequences from an event, the retroactive enrichment of that event must be consistent with those consequences. If Volume 1 established that the founding of a sect involved a betrayal that still shapes the sect's internal culture, the Volume 3 history of that founding must be consistent with a betrayal having occurred.

### 11.3 — Ambiguity Clarification

Canon ambiguity — moments where the governing Parts do not fully specify behavior in a particular edge case — may be clarified by creative choice, and that clarification becomes Negotiable Canon once established. When a situation arises that no Part explicitly governs, the author applies the spirit of the most directly relevant Parts, makes a determination consistent with the full Canon, documents the determination as Negotiable Canon, and is thereafter bound by that determination in all similar situations.

## Area 12 — Prohibited Creative Acts

### 12.1 — Absolute Creative Prohibitions

The following creative acts are prohibited under Part 28 regardless of narrative convenience, dramatic appeal, or creative preference:

**Causally Unsupported Cultivation Jumps:**
A character may not advance in cultivation in a way that bypasses Part 3's breakthrough requirements without an established, Canon-compliant mechanism for the bypass. Surprise cultivation breakthroughs mid-combat — a character manifesting power they have not established, as a consequence of extreme circumstances — are prohibited. The only permitted exception is a breakthrough that has been narratively prepared: the character has been shown approaching the breakthrough threshold through established cultivation accumulation, and the breakthrough is triggered by an extreme circumstance that catalyzes readiness that was already present.

**Causally Unsupported Technology Jumps:**
A character or faction may not suddenly possess technology that exceeds their established TA axis tier without a Canon-compliant acquisition mechanism (cross-civilizational transfer, relic inheritance, faction merger, leading-edge breakthrough within the Part 9 one-tier individual leading edge rule). Technology that appears when the narrative needs it, without prior establishment, is prohibited.

**Causally Unsupported Location Changes:**
Characters may not appear in a location without a travel-time-consistent explanation for how they got there. Part 15's travel mechanics apply to all character movement. A character who is established in Location A cannot appear in Location B without the prose accounting for the travel time required at that character's established travel capability.

**Causally Unsupported Faction or Affiliation Changes:**
A faction may not change its Part 23 affiliation position with another faction (from rivalry to alliance, from neutrality to open conflict) without the establishing events having been rendered in the prose. The affiliation change is the consequence of events — the events must be shown. An affiliation change that occurs between chapters, without being rendered, is prohibited. A change may be revealed after the fact, in a planned flashback Area or through a character's first-hand report, when the Area then shows its causes and the Chapter Spec plans the reveal.

**Causally Unsupported Character Behavioral Reversals:**
A character may not reverse a deeply established behavioral pattern — an ideology, a loyalty, a tactical philosophy, a relational stance — without a causally grounded arc of change having been rendered in the prose. The change must be earned. A villain who is suddenly compassionate after twenty scenes of brutality without an established arc of psychological shift is a Canon violation.

**Information Miracles:**
Characters may not possess information they cannot plausibly have acquired through mechanisms consistent with Part 24's information architecture. A character who "just knows" something they have no established means of knowing — without a soul-sense revelation, a contact network use, an intelligence operation, or a lucky discovery rendered in the prose — is an information miracle and is prohibited.

**Consequence Evasion:**
A character may not escape consequences that Part 27 requires without a Canon-compliant resolution pathway being shown. Injuries do not heal instantly. Social consequences of betrayal do not dissolve in a single conversation. Ideological rupture events do not resolve before their Part 20 reconstruction pathway has been traversed. The protagonist is not exempt from consequence — Part 27 Standard N27-6 is absolute.

**Civilization Characteristic Violations:**
A civilization may not behave in ways that are inconsistent with its CCT tier and civilization type. A Subsistence-tier civilization cannot deploy Galactic-scale military force. A CS-Type civilization cannot de-prioritize cultivation in favor of technology investment without a complete ideological restructuring being rendered in the prose.

### 12.3 — Content Limits

This Area says what may be on the page. It sets limits and not craft: how a scene is written stays with Part 36 and the Chapter Spec. These limits stay hard when the craft rules of Parts 29 and 36 are read as guidance posts. Part 36 Dimension 16-D tests them.

**Rule 12.3.1 — What may exist on the page.**
Graphic violence, gore, harm, self-harm, abuse, slurs and profanity may exist on the page. The story does not look away from a wound or a death (Part 2 Rule 3.3.5), and how graphic a scene is, inside Rules 12.3.2 and 12.3.3, is for the Chapter Spec and the story to decide. Profanity may be spoken; an oath or a curse still draws its force from what it invokes and from who says it, and not from the vulgarity of the word (Part 29 Rule 29.19.3.5).

**Rule 12.3.2 — Harm to children.**
Harm to a child (Part 16 band YC-1, under 12) is not allowed in an individual case. Harm here means a wound, injury or death inflicted on a child and shown on the page; fear, grief, distress and the psychological events of Part 16 Rule 16.16.2.2 are outside this rule. Where a whole city or a whole world is destroyed, the story does not avoid the deaths of the children among the dead.

**Rule 12.3.3 — Sexual content.**
Only minor indications of sexual content may appear. The sexual act is not shown. The aftermath of a romantic encounter may appear, and the prose does not state it explicitly. The age gate stands: no romantic relationship, courtship or romantic arc is written for any character under age 17 (Part 17 Rule 17.8.6.8; Part 39 Rule 39.21.1.2).

**Rule 12.3.4 — The content tag.**
The Chapter Spec may carry an optional content tag `[CONTENT: …]` on an Area row (Part 38 Rule 2.3.5d). The words inside the tag are the author's own. A tag sets a scene's level inside Rules 12.3.1 to 12.3.3 and never moves a limit: no tag permits what Rules 12.3.2 and 12.3.3 withhold. An unset tag leaves the level to the drafter.

## Area 13 — Narration Standards

**N28-1 — The Full-Canon Pre-Execution Standard.**
Before any significant creative act is committed to prose, conduct the full simultaneous multi-Part check as specified in Area 10. No creative act of narrative consequence may bypass this check.

**N28-2 — The Prose Depth Standard.**
Every scene must simultaneously satisfy all ten Named Depth Pillars (DP-1 through DP-10) of the Prose Depth and Immersion Law (Area 4), weighed on the Area (Area 4.2). The size of each Area is read against Part 2: a count outside 1,280 to 2,400 words is a blocking finding, and the write waits for the author to confirm the Area or send it back (Area 4.1). Thin prose in any dimension — compressed combat (DP-1), dropped subtext (DP-2), expository lore delivery (DP-3), an Area tagged `[SENSORY ANCHOR REQUIRED]` that carries no sensory anchor (DP-4), flat emotional assertion (DP-5), imprecise power rendering (DP-6), consequence-free events (DP-7), cultural register slip (DP-8), bare single-sentence Named Move (DP-9), or orphaned facts (DP-10) — is reported at Gate 1 under the Depth Pillars dimension of Part 36 Check #16 (Dimension 16-DP) for the author's ruling. Depth is a craft matter and does not block; a power-ceiling or other canon breach does.

**N28-3 — The Name-Consistency Standard.**
All invented proper nouns — locations, factions, characters, techniques, events, customs — must be consistent with the linguistic and cultural register of the producing culture per Part 25. Names that impose anachronistic contemporary Earth linguistic patterns are prohibited.

**N28-4 — The Establishment-Immutability Standard.**
Once Negotiable Canon content is established in prose, it is immutable. The author must track established Negotiable Canon content and prevent contradiction. The Hero Ledger, Villain Ledger, and Side Character Profile are the primary tracking mechanisms for character-level Negotiable Canon.

**N28-5 — The Emotion-Grounding Standard.**
Every emotional state must be rendered with its causal roots visible in prior prose, its full physical and behavioral texture, and its appropriate duration. Asserted emotion is prohibited. Causally disconnected emotion is prohibited. Instantaneous resolution of significant emotional wounds is prohibited.

**N28-6 — The Tactical-Consistency Standard.**
Every tactical choice must be consistent with the character's established power level, psychological state, ideology, and the environmental and situational context of the scene. Tactical surprise is required; causally unsupported surprise is prohibited.

**N28-7 — The Cultural-Consistency Standard.**
Every invented cultural element — event, custom, tradition, taboo, governance form, political conflict — must be consistent with the Part 25 CPT of the culture producing it. Cultural content that contradicts the fundamental logic of its producing CPT is prohibited.

**N28-8 — The Retroactive-Enrichment Standard.**
Retroactive enrichment of earlier established content must be consistent with all prior implications of that content. Enrichment that feels retroactively contradictory to what was established is prohibited even when it does not formally violate a specific Canon law.

**N28-9 — The Authorization-First Standard.**
Any creative act designated as Tier 2 under Part 28's Authorization Protocol (Area 9) must complete the Authorization Standard — foreshadowing, structural grounding, consequence preparation — before execution. Tier 2 acts executed without authorization preparation are the most severe class of Canon violation.

**N28-10 — The Behavioral-Complexity Standard.**
No character may be rendered as a simplified execution of their dominant trait. Every character must be rendered with the full complexity of their governed profile — the gap between ideology and emotion, between public behavior and private experience, between what they say and what they mean. Robotic behavioral rendering is prohibited.

**N28-11 — The Scene-Completeness Standard.**
The scene-completeness standard is Area 4.4 (Scene Density Law), which holds the full statement. Extending or merging an Area changes its plan, so it is done only on the author's ruling (Part 38 Rule 3.5.2, substantial amendment).

**N28-12 — The Cascade-Consequence Standard.**
When a creative act produces a consequence cascade — CSS events triggering IE events triggering PIS events triggering behavioral changes — the full cascade must be rendered in the prose. Consequence chains may not be truncated at the first link for the sake of narrative pace. Pace is built through prose craft, not through consequence omission.

**N28-13 — The Novel-Register Standard (Em-Dash Prohibition; Contractions Barred in Narration and Dialogue).**
The Starward Saga is a novel, not a code comment, a journalism article, or a governance document. Its prose must read with the weight, formality, and cadence of a novel-grade literary series. Two typographical and lexical habits belong to non-novel registers: the em-dash is categorically prohibited in the Area body, and contractions are categorically prohibited in narration and in dialogue (Canon Amendment 2026-10-16, reversing the dialogue allowance of 2026-10-08):

- **No Em-Dash (—).** The em-dash character is prohibited across all Area body text. When the authorial impulse reaches for an em-dash, the prose must instead construct a full clause, open a colon where the second clause specifies the first, use a semicolon where two clauses are closely related, use a comma where the interruption is light, use parentheses where the interruption is a genuine aside, or restructure the sentence to remove the need for any interruption. Em-dashes compress what novel-grade prose must render through completed clause architecture; their pervasive use is a register collapse into the cadence of commentary.
- **No Contractions in Narration or Dialogue.** Apostrophe-elided forms (*haven't, isn't, don't, I'm, you're, it's, that's,* and all equivalents) are prohibited in the whole Area body, narration and direct speech alike. Every contracted form must be rendered in its full expanded form. The expanded form carries the weight appropriate to a cultivation epic in the narrator's voice. **Contractions are equally barred inside direct speech** (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1c as amended 2026-10-16); natural speech comes from full connected sentences (Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.7a).

N28-13 is authored in Part 29 Rules 29.2.1.1b and 29.2.1.1c and cross-referenced in Part 2 Rule 2.3.7. Audit Pass 1 and Audit Pass 2 both FAIL if the em-dash character is found in the Area body or a contraction is found anywhere in the Area body. Scope of "Area body" is the text of the Area file after its Area Title Block, to the end of the Area file; governance artefacts (Position Header Blocks, Chapter Header Blocks, Area Title Blocks, Audit Reports, ledger tables, and all Part-file structural content) are outside scope and may continue to use the em-dash and contractions where structural clarity benefits from them.

**N28-14 — The Dialogue Format Standard (Double Quotation Marks and Italics).**
All direct speech in the Area body is rendered in **double straight quotation marks AND italics simultaneously** per Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1 and Part 2 Rule 2.2.2. The canonical rendering is *"the exact spoken words"* with the quotation marks inside the italic span. Italics-only dialogue is prohibited (one lawful break: Part 28 Rule 20.8.2(c)). Double-quoted dialogue without italics is prohibited. Single quotation marks for primary speech are prohibited (they are reserved for nested reported speech per Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1a). N28-14 is a character-voice standard and applies to every Area under every Area-producing command; violation is an Audit Pass 1 and Pass 2 FAIL.

**N28-15 — The Closer-Variety Standard (countable).**
A narration paragraph may end on an aphoristic, summarizing, or antithesis closer only as a marked event, never as the default paragraph rhythm. Two counts are shown for every scene, counted over the Area under audit (Part 2 Area 1; Part 36 Area 17.5(b)), as information:

- **Consecutive reference:** in any three consecutive narration paragraphs of the Area, about one ends on an aphoristic closer. A run with more is shown as information.
- **Area ratio:** across the Area, about 25% or fewer of narration paragraphs end on aphoristic closers; a higher share is shown as information.

The scene-final paragraph is exempt from both readings — scene-close construction is governed by Part 36 §4.4, not by this standard — and the exempt scene-final paragraph is excluded from both counts. The scene-final paragraph is the last paragraph of the Area that ends the scene; the last paragraph of an Area that continues into the next is not scene-final and is counted. The figures of this standard are those stated above.

*Counting definitions (this standard is reported by count, so its terms are operational):* a **narration paragraph** is any paragraph that is not purely dialogue (not composed solely of spoken lines plus minimal attribution). An **aphoristic closer** is a paragraph-final sentence whose work is to abstract, summarize, generalize, or moralize what the paragraph just rendered — a maxim, a balanced antithesis, or a summary judgment on the paragraph's content — rather than to advance action, register a sensory perception, extend speech, or leave a concrete object or question standing unresolved. The classification test: a final sentence that tells the reader what the paragraph *meant* is an aphoristic closer; a final sentence that gives the reader one more concrete thing — an act, a sensation, an object, an utterance — is not.

*Negative exemplars (both individually well-made, which is the point: the violation is the accumulated rhythm, not any single line):* "It was not a heroic thing to be carrying. He carried it the way a man carries a debt." — a summary-judgment maxim sealing its paragraph. "The sergeant did not answer immediately, which was itself an answer." — a balanced antithesis standing as an entire single-sentence paragraph. This accumulated, metronomic rhythm is what this standard exists to break.

*Positive exemplars (the varied-closer palette — paragraphs may end on action, sensory perception, an unresolved object, or a line of speech):* "The boy behind the woman was watching a bird." — world texture through perception, no summary attached. "This morning there were four chairs. He did not like four." — dread left standing in a concrete object, no maxim drawn from it.

**N28-16 — The Single-Statement Standard (countable).**
An insight, thematic point, or piece of reasoning is usually rendered once in a scene, counted over the Area: a second delivery in a later Area of the same scene is a delivery in the same scene and is shown as information (Part 36 Area 17.5(b), element 19-2). Delivery channels do not stack: if an image conveys it, narration does not then name it; if narration renders it, dialogue does not restate it; if a character performs reasoning on-page, narration does not re-run the same arithmetic or logic, and no second delivery re-performs it unchanged. A restatement is legal only when it performs new work — misreading, irony, escalation, refusal, cost — and the new work must be legible: the audit must be able to name what the second delivery does that the first did not. Emphasis is not new work; making sure the reader understood is not new work.

*Canonical violation (the flagship stack):* a capacity gap between two forces is delivered once and then restated six more times within a single scene — computed in dialogue; re-computed in narration, which names its own redundancy; rendered again in detail; restated as aphorism; restated epistemically; restated in dialogue; and folded once more into the closing order. One delivery plus its consequence carries the entire sequence. That stack is the failure class this standard prohibits.

*Scope:* this standard governs cross-channel restatement — image to narration, narration to dialogue, dialogue back to narration — which Part 29's P29-9 (repetitive dialogue) does not reach; P29-9 continues to govern dialogue-to-dialogue repetition on its own terms. This standard is also the redundancy-side reconciliation of the Area 4 depth mandate: depth is widened, never restacked (see §4.7, Depth Is Not Repetition).

**N28-17 — The Demonstrated-Not-Named Standard (qualitative).**
Meaning the prose has demonstrated is not then explained. When the scene has shown a thing — a power differential through how bodies orient, a doctrine through two instruments disagreeing, a grief through what a hand does — narration does not step in afterward to name what was shown. The reader's act of understanding is part of the scene's work, and explanation confiscates it. This standard is the narration-side anchor of Part 36's qualitative dimension 16-H (Demonstrated-Then-Named Test): Part 36 defines the dimension and its judgment framework; this standard binds narration to it. Cross-reference Part 36 — the qualitative framework is not duplicated here.

**Enforcement.** The two counted standards above, N28-15 and N28-16, are reported at Gate 1 Check #19 as information, defined in Part 36's Area 17, on the Area under audit (Part 2 Area 1; Part 36 Area 17.5(b)). N28-17 is enforced through Part 36's qualitative dimension 16-H, of which it is the narration-side anchor.

**Cross-Reference — Plain Language and Narrative Impact: a coequal Tier-0 authority governing VOCABULARY.**
A craft-layer Authority Tier 0 rule lives at `.claude/guardrails/quality/guardrail-plain-language.md`, enforced at **blocking Gate 1 Check #22**. It governs **which words the sentence is built from** — plain everyday words in narration and in dialogue alike (`arithmetic` becomes `math`), grounded in this world of kingdoms, forges, musters and crowns — and whether narration **reached for impact and wonder where the scene earned it, and only there.** **Part 28 does not own that rule, does not restate it, and no N28 standard number exists for it**: the rule lives in the guardrail with its own Gate 1 check. The relationship is a division of labour, not a hierarchy. The standards above — N28-15, N28-16 and N28-17 in particular — govern the **structural presence** of narration craft: what a paragraph closes on, how many times an insight is delivered, whether a demonstrated meaning is then named. The guardrail governs the **words those structures are made of**. Both bind simultaneously on every Area; neither substitutes for the other, and neither reads the other down.

Three consequences bear directly on the standards above:

- **Sentence length is NOT restricted by the new guardrail.** Long, accumulating, multi-clause sentences are permitted and are part of this novel's voice. **The absence of any sentence-length limit is deliberate.** No standard in this Area may be read as importing one, and no agent may shorten or chop a sentence in the guardrail's name.

- **Plainness is not thinning.** A simpler word never licenses dropping an image, a specific detail, a sensory anchor, or a Depth Pillar (Area 4). A unit that loses density while gaining plainness has failed both authorities.

## Area 14 — The Flexibility Audit Protocol

### 14.1 — Purpose of the Flexibility Audit

The Flexibility Audit is the structured process by which the author evaluates a planned creative act against the full SSC Canon before committing it to prose. It is not a bureaucratic formality — it is the discipline that makes creative freedom safe. A creative act that clears the Flexibility Audit with full simultaneous-compliance verification is a creative act that the Canon supports. An act that fails the Audit is an act that must be modified before execution.

### 14.2 — The Five Audit Questions

Before executing any significant creative act, the author applies five questions:

**Audit Question 1 — Category or Instance?**
Is this creative act inventing a new instance within an existing governed category (permitted), or is it inventing a new category not authorized by the Canon (prohibited)? If the answer is "new category," the act must be referred back to the Canon for authorization.

**Audit Question 2 — What Parts govern this act?**
List every Part that has jurisdiction over this creative act. Be exhaustive. Then verify that the act complies with each governing Part simultaneously.

**Audit Question 3 — What does this act establish?**
Identify every piece of Negotiable Canon content that this act would fix in place. Verify that none of this content contradicts previously established Negotiable Canon. Verify that all of it is consistent with the Locked Canon of all governing Parts.

**Audit Question 4 — What consequences does this act trigger?**
Identify all CSS events, IE events, and PIS events that this act produces. Verify that all triggered consequences will be rendered in the prose with appropriate specificity and duration. Identify any cascade effects that must also be rendered.

**Audit Question 5 — Is Part 28 Authorization required?**
Is this act on the Tier 2 Authorization list (Area 9.2)? If yes, verify that the Authorization Standard has been satisfied before execution.

### 14.3 — Audit Failure Responses

When the Flexibility Audit reveals a compliance failure:

**Minor Failure:** The creative act violates one governing Part in a way that can be corrected by adjusting the act without changing its essential narrative purpose. Correct the adjustment and re-audit.

**Major Failure:** The creative act violates multiple governing Parts or violates a locked governance principle in a way that cannot be corrected without fundamentally changing the act. Return to narrative design and approach the same story goal through a different, compliant creative path.

**Authorization Failure:** The creative act is Tier 2 and the Authorization Standard has not been satisfied. Halt execution, return to prior scenes to build the required narrative preparation, and re-audit when the Authorization Standard is satisfied.

## Area 15 — The Contextual Earning Law

### 15.1 — Principle

Every unit of canon information delivered in prose — classification names, location names, faction references, character history, cultural customs, technological capabilities, cultivation mechanics, relationship state, timeline detail, and any other fact governed by the SSC Canon — must be **load-bearing in its scene**. Information is load-bearing when at least one of the following is true:

- It is the **consequence** of what the scene has just rendered (the scene earned the telling).
- It is the **cause** of what the scene is about to render (the telling earns the next beat).
- It **changes** what a character understands or decides within the scene (the telling is itself the event).

Information that satisfies none of these three conditions is **standalone information** and is best kept out of the prose (guidance: a case is reported as information, and the Chapter Spec and the scene decide).

### 15.2 — The Character-Sheet Failure Mode

Standalone information reads as a character sheet: a label placed next to a character or a world-element for the reader's reference, not for the story's motion. The diagnostic test is deletion. If the sentence containing the information can be deleted without any subsequent sentence losing its cause, its sense, or its emotional charge, then the information was standalone and should not have been present.

A scene suffering the character-sheet failure mode is technically canon-compliant in every other respect — every fact may be accurate, every ledger state may be honored, every rule may be obeyed — and still read as stale, rigid, and lifeless. Accuracy without motion is not prose. It is a manifest.

### 15.3 — Who May Deliver What

Any character may voice any canon information, including technical classifications, deep-canon terminology, and specialized vocabulary, **provided Section 15.1 is satisfied**. The Law is not about speaker qualification. It is about scene-tissue.

- A child may name a cultivation realm if the scene has built the child's reason to know it and the naming drives the next beat.
- A villager may reference a distant faction if the villager's relationship to that faction is what the scene is about.
- A king may speak the name of a Heart state to his queen if the saying of it shifts what she does next, or if it resolves what just passed between them.
- A commoner may observe a technology tier if the observation is itself the moment of the scene — fear, recognition, refusal, decision.

The Law removes no vocabulary from any speaker. It removes only vocabulary that is not earned by its surrounding scene.

### 15.4 — The Three-Beat Test

For any piece of canon information in an Area, three beats must be locatable in the surrounding text, which reads across the neighbouring Areas of the same scene where the scene continues:

- **Beat Before.** Within the preceding several sentences, something in the scene made this information possible, necessary, or loaded. A reason to know, a reason to ask, a reason to notice, a stake that makes the fact matter, a pressure that makes the naming land.
- **The Beat.** The information is delivered through one of the preferred mechanisms in Section 15.5 — sensed impression, rendered use, consequential dialogue, or earned exposition — not declared.
- **Beat After.** Within the following several sentences, something is caused, unlocked, changed, decided, or refused because the information was delivered. The telling produces motion.

If any of the three beats is absent or thin, the information is standalone and the prose falls short of the Law (reported as information; the Chapter Spec and the scene decide). A Beat After that falls in an Area not yet written is reported open at the Gate 1 of the Area that delivers the information and is judged when the Area that follows is audited.

### 15.5 — Preferred Delivery Mechanisms

Even when contextually earned, information should prefer the mechanism that carries the most scene-work:

- **Sensed impression** (governed by Part 3 Area 3.3 Heavenly Scale for cultivation power; analogous sensory registers for other information categories) — the physical, environmental, and emotional experience of encountering the thing.
- **Rendered use** — a capability, relationship, or faction exercised in the scene such that its shape, cost, and consequence imply the classification without naming it.
- **Comparative behavior** — other characters responding, deferring, recoiling, posturing, calibrating; their reactions carry the naming.
- **Consequential naming** — a character names the thing because the naming itself does work: resolves a question, shifts a relationship, opens or closes a decision.
- **Earned exposition** — direct explanation, permitted when and only when a character in the scene needs the explanation as much as the reader does, and that need arose within the scene.

### 15.6 — Immersion Primacy

The immersion of the reader is the terminal value. An Area that is technically compliant with every other canon provision but reads as a recitation of facts falls short of this Law regardless of those compliances (reported as information). The test is not "was the information accurate" but "was the information *alive* in the scene."

This principle governs every tie-break between Contextual Earning and any other rule that would push the prose toward literal declaration. Where another rule appears to require a declaration that would violate Section 15.1, the declaration must be converted into an earned delivery, or the rule's underlying intent must be satisfied through an alternative mechanism that does not break immersion.

### 15.7 — Applicability Scope

This Law applies to all information categories in the canon system, including but not limited to:

- All classification vocabulary (cultivation realms and levels, Meridian Roots, affinities, soul talents, Heart Levels, technology tiers, CS%/TS% synergy states)
- All location references (star systems, worlds, continents, kingdoms, villages, sacred grounds, cosmological tiers)
- All faction, sect, civilization, and affiliation references
- All character relationships, histories, and backstory
- All cultural, ritual, and custom references
- All timeline and chronology references
- All power-system mechanics (cultivation steps, technology operation, combat techniques, Named Moves, fusion stages)
- All relic, weapon, vehicle, and artifact identification

No category of canon information is exempt.

### 15.8 — Failure Grades

Shortfalls against the Contextual Earning Law are described by grade, as information only (nothing is blocked; the Chapter Spec and the author decide):

- **Minor** — one occurrence of standalone information, correctable by local revision without restructuring the scene.
- **Major** — multiple standalone units, or one standalone unit that labels a character, location, or faction without any surrounding scene-work.
- **Critical** — the scene's core structure consists of standalone information delivery. The character-sheet failure mode in full form. Such scenes are best redesigned, not revised.

### 15.9 — Relationship to Other Canon Provisions

This Area relates to two other provisions:

- **Part 2 Area 1 section 1.6.3 "Canon Information Delivery"** sends delivery of canon information through the Three-Beat Test. Explicit identification is the consequential-naming mechanism of Section 15.5, subject to the Three-Beat Test.
- **Part 28 Area 7 Emotion-From-Experience Law** — Area 7 governs emotion specifically; Area 15 is its structural generalization to all canon information. Where the two overlap (emotional information deployed as fact), both apply; the Three-Beat Test is the operational check.

The Heavenly Scale system (Part 3 Area 3.3) remains the canonical delivery mechanism for cultivation-power information and is reinforced, not constrained, by this Law.

### 15.10 — Delivery Sequencing Under the Two-Audit Mandate

The Contextual Earning Declaration that follows every Area is delivered in Gate 1 Chat Delivery immediately after the Area body and immediately before the Gate 1 Post-Draft Audit Report (see `guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md` and `guardrails/commands/guardrail-command-law.md`). The Gate 1 Position Header Block precedes the Area body in chat; the Contextual Earning Declaration follows the Area body; the Post-Draft Audit Report (Audit Pass 1 of 2) follows the Contextual Earning Declaration. The Area body in chat is the full text of the Area, verbatim: at every Gate 1, after every revision and in `[REVISE AREA]` it is never replaced by a summary, an excerpt, a paraphrase or a file path, and when a question to the author is about the draft the full text is printed before the question. The Post-Write Audit (Audit Pass 2 of 2) executes later, after Gate 2 file writes, and re-checks Contextual Earning compliance against the written `.docx` and `.md` files. An Area that passes the Three-Beat Test at Post-Draft Audit but loses a Beat in the written files (e.g., truncated prose, dropped paragraphs) is a Post-Write Audit FAIL, repaired by the orchestrating command re-running the two writer scripts over the confirmed text and re-running the audit lane (`guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`, Gate 2); the ledger lane runs beside the audit lane, and its writes stand because the confirmed text is unchanged.

The Gate 1 Position Header Block itself does **not** count as information delivery under the Three-Beat Test — it is a chat-only governance artefact outside the prose body and therefore exempt from Contextual Earning scrutiny. Only the Area body is evaluated for Contextual Earning.

## Area 17 — Lore Creation Law

### 17.1 — The Lore Mandate

Every entity introduced in prose, or referenced by proper name in prose as a named entity, must have lore recorded in its governing ledger before or within the same Gate-2 sequence as the prose that introduces it. Lore is not optional decoration — it is the texture that turns governed categories into inhabited instances. A governed category (a faction type, a location tier, a cultural paradigm, a character classification) tells the reader *what kind of thing* an entity is. Lore tells the reader *who this particular thing is*: where it came from, what shaped it, whom it has harmed or helped, what it remembers, what it fears, what it still owes.

Without lore, Negotiable Canon (Area 2) collapses into naming alone — a faction has a name but no history, a festival has a name but no origin, a kingdom has a name but no weight. With lore, every named entity becomes a fixed point around which future prose may gather depth. The Lore Mandate closes the gap between the Naming Freedom Law (Area 3) and the Contextual Earning Law (Area 15): the author is free to invent names (Area 3), but every named invention must be anchored in recorded lore (Area 17) so the Three-Beat Test (Area 15.4) has substance to draw from when the prose earns its delivery.

**The Mandate:** An Area that introduces or references a named entity without triggering a corresponding ledger lore capture is a Part 28 Area 17 violation, audited under ALCA Mode A dimension AL-10 (Lore Completeness Alignment; `guardrails/audit/guardrail-alca.md`). The violation is a Post-Write Audit failure; the Area does not pass audit until the lore capture is complete.

**Scope of "Named Entity":** A named entity is any entity given a proper noun in prose — a city, a kingdom, a faction, a festival, a weapon, a relic, a vehicle, a character (named, not merely referenced by role), a cultural tradition, an information artifact, a governance body. Unnamed background references (a road, a merchant, a crowd) are not named entities and do not trigger the Mandate. Once a name is given — once "the merchant" becomes "Merchant Olara" — the Mandate activates for that entity.

### 17.3 — The Breadth-and-Depth Standard

Lore for all named entities must be created with extreme breadth and depth. This Sub-Area operationalizes that standard.

**Breadth:** Every axis listed in 17.2 for the entity's category must be present in the ledger entry — not just the axes that the current scene touches. A kingdom's lore entry must include its economy even if the current scene is about its court politics. A faction's lore entry must include its recruitment practices even if the current scene is about its military operations. Absence of an axis is a completeness failure, audited under AL-10; an axis that a stub record (see the Proportionality Exception) reads as `open` is present. The breadth standard is what prevents lore from being written only to the scene's immediate need and then becoming contradictory when a later scene draws from an unaddressed axis.

**Depth:** Each axis must carry enough content to seed at least one future prose scene without contradiction. A one-line placeholder ("founded long ago by a great warrior") fails depth. A depth-compliant entry gives: specific names, specific dates or era-anchors, specific founding events, specific named successors or known figures, specific signature cultural or political features, and at least one ongoing tension or open question that future prose can resolve. Depth is measured not by word count but by generativity — a depth-compliant entry is a scene-seed; a depth-insufficient entry is a label.

**Proportionality Exception:** The breadth-and-depth standard applies at full strength to entities of narrative significance — locations the heroes visit, factions the heroes interact with, characters who speak, cultural events that frame the story. For strictly background entities that are named but not narratively active (a neighboring kingdom referenced once, a historical faction mentioned in passing), a **stub record** is enough: the name, a one-line role and only the axes the mention touches. Every other axis reads `open`: it is not established, and filling it later is enrichment, not contradiction (Area 17.5). A stub record needs no scene-seed generativity. Background entities may be promoted to full breadth-and-depth at any later point if a future scene activates them.

**The Depth Test:** For any lore entry, apply the scene-seed question: "Can I write a pacing-appropriate immersion scene (all ten Depth Pillars satisfied) in this location / about this faction / featuring this character / rendering this festival using only what is in the ledger?" If the answer is yes, depth is satisfied. If the answer is no, the ledger entry must be expanded.

### 17.4 — Lore Recording Protocol

**Ledger Schema Integration:** Every ledger folder's `_index.md` shall include a dedicated **Lore Section** specification whose structure matches the entity category's axes per 17.2.

**First-Appearance Capture:** First-appearance prose of a named entity triggers lore capture in the same Gate-2 sequence as the prose's ledger state update. Gate 2 follows `guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`: the per-Area `.docx` and `.md` pair is written, then the audit lane, the ledger lane and the scorer lane run together beside one another. The ledger lane (`guardrails/ledger/guardrail-ledger-update-law.md`) updates the state of affected entities and records lore for named entities introduced or first-named in this Area, creating the entity's ledger file if it does not exist and populating it with the minimum lore fields of this Area. The audit lane cannot see the ledger lane's return, so it reports the lore obligation as pending merge, and the orchestrating command merges the two. Gate 2 is not complete until lore is recorded for every named entity in the Area that lacks prior lore.

**Narrative Summary Paragraph Mandate:**
Every ledger section that is touched by an Area must be enriched with a **narrative summary paragraph** — not just field values. This paragraph synthesizes what the section now knows about the entity in coherent, prose-quality language. The paragraph is updated cumulatively with each new prose contribution:
- The first time a section is populated, the paragraph captures everything the introducing prose established about that aspect of the entity.
- Each subsequent Area that adds to the entity's characterization extends or revises the paragraph to incorporate the new information.
- The paragraph is written in third person, present tense, as a description of the entity's current canonical state — not as a log of what happened.

**What "narrative summary paragraph" means in practice:**
- **Character Section 5 (Psychology):** "Varys presents as composed and strategically cautious, but the prose has established a buried guilt response when his kingdom's vulnerability is mentioned directly — his jaw tightens, his left hand closes. He does not acknowledge this response consciously. His PIS baseline is 2 (Mild Stress) with a latent spike to PIS-4 when sovereignty is threatened."
- **Location Section 2 (Character):** "Azure Sky Kingdom is a mid-range cultivator civilization built on the Lyraen River's eastern bank, with architecture that mixes grey river-stone with carved azure-veined marble. The smell of the river and of river-market commerce dominates the lower districts. The royal district sits elevated, visible from 30 km."
- These are not lists. They are short scene-seed paragraphs that any author could use to write the next scene involving this entity without inventing facts.

**Pre-Area Lore Read:** The Pre-Area Read Mandate (Area 4.6) includes reading the Lore Section of every ledger entity file referenced by name in the Areas read under Area 4.6. This ensures continuity of lore voice and prevents contradiction.

**Audit Hook:** No Area may pass Post-Write Audit if any of its named entities lacks the minimum lore fields specified by 17.2, checked on the merged result of the audit and ledger lanes; for a stub record (Area 17.3) the minimum is the name, the one-line role and the axes the mention touches, and an axis that reads `open` counts as present. The audit hook is ALCA Mode A dimension AL-10 (Lore Completeness Alignment), which accepts `open`.

### 17.5 — Lore Consistency and Retroactive Enrichment

**Establishment:** Once lore is recorded in a ledger, it is fixed under the same Establishment Threshold as Area 2.3 (Negotiable Canon Establishment Rule). The lore becomes a reference point that future prose and future ledger updates may neither contradict nor silently overwrite.

**Retroactive Enrichment:** Later prose may enrich established lore — deepen the founding story, add named subordinates, extend the cultural calendar, reveal previously unknown branches — per the Retroactive Enrichment Rule (Area 2.4 / Area 11.2). Enrichment must be consistent with all prior established lore on that entity.

**Contradiction Handling:** A contradiction between new prose and established ledger lore is a CCIA violation. The prose may not overwrite the ledger lore unilaterally. Resolution pathway: (a) identify the contradiction during the Post-Write Audit; (b) determine whether the new prose or the existing ledger lore is the correct canonical state; (c) if the existing lore is correct, the prose is revised; (d) if the new prose is correct and the existing lore is wrong, a formal Canon amendment is made to update the lore (the ledger lore does not change via prose fiat — it changes via formal amendment).

**Silent Overwrite Prohibition:** Editing ledger lore without a corresponding formal amendment or Post-Write Audit finding is prohibited. This preserves the audit trail of how lore has evolved and prevents the ledger from silently diverging from the prose that established its content.

### 17.6 — Lore Delivery in Prose (Alignment with Contextual Earning Law)

The Lore Creation Law (Area 17) governs lore's **existence** in the ledger. The Contextual Earning Law (Area 15) governs lore's **delivery** in the prose. The two Areas are complementary, not redundant.

**Storage vs. Rendering:** The ledger stores the full lore to the Breadth-and-Depth Standard (17.3). The prose earns whatever fragment of it the scene requires through the Three-Beat Test (Area 15.4). A festival's full ritual calendar lives in the ledger; the scene renders only the fragment that the Three-Beat Test makes load-bearing. A kingdom's full economic profile lives in the ledger; the scene renders only the aspect the current beat activates. A faction's full recruitment protocol lives in the ledger; the scene shows only the moment of recruitment that the current character is living through.

**The Author's Workflow:** (1) Before writing the scene, read the ledger lore for every named entity that will appear. (2) During writing, select the fragments of that lore that the scene's Three-Beat Test can earn. (3) Render those fragments through the preferred delivery mechanisms of Area 15.5 (sensed impression, rendered use, comparative behavior, consequential naming, earned exposition). (4) Leave the rest of the lore in the ledger, where it waits to be earned by a future scene.

**Immersion Preservation:** A scene that dumps ledger lore — that renders the full founding history of a kingdom when the scene's Three-Beat Test earns only the fact that the current king is the founder's grandson — is a Contextual Earning Law violation (Area 15), not a Lore Creation Law success. The two Areas together require: *lore exists in full, but is delivered in fragments that the scene earns*. Lore completeness is the precondition for earned delivery; earned delivery is the precondition for immersion; immersion is the terminal value.

**No Conflict With Breadth-and-Depth:** Writing full lore to the ledger does not pressure the prose to render it. The ledger is a reservoir. The prose is a draw. A deep reservoir makes for rich, non-repetitive draws over many scenes. A shallow reservoir forces the prose to invent, which returns the author to the contradiction-risk that Area 17 exists to prevent.

## Area 18 — Structural Naming Mandate (Novel-Part / Chapter / Area Names)

### 18.1 — Principle

Every Area in the Starward Saga exists at an exact position within a five-tier narrative hierarchy — **Phase → Section → Novel-Part → Chapter → Area** — and every level of that hierarchy carries **both a number and a name**. Numbers locate the unit in the structural index; names carry its identity, theme, and function. A numbered but unnamed narrative level is structurally indistinct from every other numbered-but-unnamed level at the same tier, which erodes the reader's (and the author's) ability to hold the story's shape. The Starward Saga therefore mandates that **Novel-Part, Chapter and Area each carry an explicit name** at every Gate 1 chat delivery, and at Gate 2 in the files and the ledger reference that Rules 18.3.3 and 18.3.4 name, without exception.

Phase names and Section names are already Locked Canon under Part 30 (e.g., Phase 1 = "The Roots of Becoming"; Section 1 = "Azure Sky and Gale"). This Area extends the naming discipline downward to the three sub-Section tiers, which are governed by Part 2 (novel structure) and Part 28 (Negotiable Canon instancing).

### 18.2 — The Three Named Tiers

**Novel-Part Name.** Every Novel-Part within a Section carries a name. Novel-Parts are the largest sub-Section narrative units (Part 2 Area 1 Tier 3). A Section's Novel-Parts are thematically coherent sub-arcs of the Section's overall narrative, and the Novel-Part name captures the sub-arc's defining movement. *Example form:* "The Morning of the Twin Oath," "The Bells Before the Envoys," "First Light on the Sapphire Basin."

**Chapter Name.** Every Chapter within a Novel-Part carries a name. Chapters are the primary narrative pacing units (Part 2 Area 1 Tier 4). Chapter names function as the reader's in-book navigational headings and must carry enough specificity to distinguish the Chapter from its neighbors while remaining evocative rather than functional. *Example form:* "The Brass Bell and the Alloy Bell," "The Gallery of Seven Portraits," "Three Days East of Threefold."

**Area Name.** Every Area within a Chapter carries a name (an "Area Description" in Part 2 terminology). The Area is the written unit of the novel (Part 2 Area 1 Tier 5): one author-session writing pass, one file pair (`area-NN.docx` + `area-NN.md`). An Area may be a complete scene, or one of several consecutive Areas of a scene that continues. The Area name captures the Area's specific narrative content in a brief nominal phrase. It is the handle the author uses to identify this exact Area at Gate 1 and in the ledger reference, and the reader meets it in the Area Title Block that stands at the head of the Area file (Rule 18.3.3). *Example form:* "Varya Wakes to the Paired Bells," "The King and Queen at the Balustrade," "The Alliance at the Threshold."

### 18.3 — The Naming Mandate (Categorical)

**Rule 18.3.1 — Names Are Mandatory, Not Optional.**
At every Gate 1 chat delivery for any Area produced by any Area-producing command (the commands enumerated in Rule 19.2), the Gate 1 Position Header Block must carry **all three** tier names (Novel-Part, Chapter, Area) in addition to the Phase and Section names. A Position Header Block that displays only numbers, or that displays an em-dash or "TBD" or "(if any)" placeholder in any of the three tier-name fields, is a Rule 18.3.1 violation and the Post-Draft Audit FAILS.

**Rule 18.3.2 — Names Must Be Established Before Gate 1.**
Before Gate 1 Chat Delivery, the author must establish the Area's Novel-Part, Chapter and Area names. The Chapter Spec carries them for every Area it plans, and the Area's own name is confirmed there (Part 38). For the first Area of a new narrative container (e.g., Area 1 of Chapter 1 of Novel-Part 1 of a Section), the names are authored as part of the spec build that precedes drafting. For every subsequent Area in the same container, the existing container's name is inherited unchanged; only the new Area's own name is freshly authored. Names, once established, are Base Information (B) in the ledger.

**Rule 18.3.3 — Names Must Appear In Gate 2 File Writes.**
At every Gate 2 file write, the file-level Chapter Header Block (Part 2 Rule 6.3.1 / 7.2.1) must carry the Chapter name and the Novel-Part name (in its Novel-Part sub-line), and every Area file (`area-NN.docx` and `area-NN.md`) must carry the Area name in the canonical Area Title Block at its head, immediately above the Area's date line and body. Between Areas of a Chapter file the Area Title Block is the only boundary (Part 2 Rules 6.3.2, 6.3.2a, 10.5.1). A Gate 2 file write that omits any name this Rule requires in that file is a Post-Write Audit FAIL, repaired by the orchestrating command re-running the two writer scripts with the correct flags over the same text and re-running the audit lane; the ledger lane runs beside the audit lane, and its writes stand because the text is unchanged (`guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`, Gate 2).

**Rule 18.3.4 — Names Must Appear In The Ledger Reference Code.**
The canonical Reference Code `Phase.Section.Part.Chapter.Area` (e.g., `1.1.1.001.01`) identifies the Area by number. When recording Area events in any ledger (character, location, faction, world, etc.), the event row must carry the Reference Code **and** the full name-chain (Phase Name / Section Name / Novel-Part Name / Chapter Name / Area Name) so the ledger is readable without cross-referencing the structural index. Ledger event rows that carry only the numeric Reference Code without name accompaniment are a Rule 18.3.4 violation.

### 18.4 — Name Authoring Standards

**Rule 18.4.1 — Register and Length.**
Names at all three tiers follow the Starward Saga's literary register (Part 2 Area 2, Part 29 dialogue voice) — evocative, image-bearing, avoiding functional or database-like formulations. A Chapter name of "Chapter 1" (bare number) is invalid. A Chapter name of "Introduction" (functional label) is invalid. A Chapter name of "The Brass Bell and the Alloy Bell" (image-bearing) is valid. Suggested length ranges: Novel-Part names 3–9 words; Chapter names 2–8 words; Area names 3–10 words.

**Rule 18.4.2 — Uniqueness Within Container.**
Names must be unique within their immediate container. Two Chapters within the same Novel-Part may not share a Chapter name. Two Areas within the same Chapter may not share an Area name. Across containers, identical names are permitted but discouraged for clarity.

**Rule 18.4.3 — Thematic Coherence With Parent Tier.**
A Chapter's name should be in evident thematic coherence with its Novel-Part's name, which should be in evident coherence with its Section's name, which should be in evident coherence with its Phase's name (all governed by Part 30). The name-chain read as a whole should read as a narrative ladder, not as a set of disconnected labels.

**Rule 18.4.4 — Stability After First Use.**
Once a name has been used in a Gate 1 chat delivery that reaches `[CONFIRM WRITE]`, or in a Gate 2 file write, it is stable; a name delivered in a draft that `[SKIP WRITE]` discarded is not established, and a name whose prose a rewind or a redraft archives returns to `proposed` (Part 38 Rule 7.2.1). Renaming a previously-delivered Novel-Part, Chapter or Area requires a formal Canon amendment and a coordinated rewrite of every downstream reference (ledgers, other Areas that reference the name, file-system folder names). Casual renaming is prohibited. **Rename route for a Chapter or an Area:** the coordinated rewrite is one `/update-ledger` pass over the container's Reference Codes, which rewrites the name in every ledger event row that carries one of them (the rows keep the full name-chain, Rule 18.3.4), and `[REVISE AREA]` for every written Area file whose Area Title Block or Chapter Header Block carries the old name (Rule 18.3.3): an Area's own name sits in its Area Title Block, and a Chapter name in the Chapter Header Block of each Area file. This rule governs the names of Novel-Parts, Chapters and Areas; in-world names (people, places, factions, objects) fix under Area 20.3, when the delivered prose is confirmed.

### 18.5 — Interaction With Other Rules

- **Gate 1 Position Header Block** (`guardrails/commands/guardrail-command-law.md`): Rule 18.3.1 makes the Position Header Block's Novel-Part / Chapter / Area fields mandatory, never optional "if any" placeholders.
- **Two-Audit Mandate** (`guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`): Post-Draft Audit (Pass 1) includes a check that all three tier names are present and conform to Rules 18.3.1 and 18.4.1–18.4.4. Post-Write Audit (Pass 2) includes a check that the names Rule 18.3.3 requires appear in the written files: the Chapter name and the Novel-Part name in the Chapter Header Block, and the Area name in each Area file.
- **Part 2 Area 6 (Docx File Structure):** The Chapter Header Block (Rule 6.3.1) and the Area Title Block (in every Area file) carry names per Rule 18.3.3.
- **Part 2 Area 7 (Markdown File Structure):** The mirroring rules (Rules 7.2.1 and 7.2.2, and the Area Title Block rule) apply to `.md` files identically.
- **Part 28 Area 3 (Naming Freedom Law):** Novel-Part, Chapter and Area names are Negotiable Canon under Area 3 — the author is free to invent them within the register and coherence standards of this Area 18. Once established, they become Base Information (B).

However, if a Chapter name or Area name uses an in-world proper noun (e.g., "The Twin Oath Shrine" as an Area name), that in-world noun's Area 17 lore entry must already exist per the Lore Mandate.

### 18.6 — Failure Grades

- **Minor.** One missing name at one tier in a single Area delivery, correctable by adding the name before Gate 2.
- **Major.** Multiple missing names in a single Area delivery, or persistent missing names across multiple Area deliveries in the same session.
- **Critical.** Delivery of any Area to file with one or more of the names Rule 18.3.3 requires in that file missing from the on-disk files. Post-Write Audit FAIL; the orchestrating command rewrites the file pair with the names over the same text and re-runs the audit lane, the ledger lane's writes standing because the text is unchanged; the Area is not accepted into the canonical body until the audit lane passes.

### 18.7 — Tentative-Count Range Discipline (Position Header Block)

The Position Header Block carries an ordinal-of-total count under each tier name. Only the **Chapter** tier carries a low–high range, and it is the Chapter's Areas-in-this-Chapter bracket drawn from the Chapter Spec (Part 2 Rule 9.3.1; Part 38 Rule 38.5): the total renders in the form `[M_low]–[M_high]` (a single en-dash `–`, not a hyphen `-` and not an em-dash `—`). The Phase and Section tiers render their total as a single integer per Part 30. The Novel-Part tier shows the length of its plan list as information, with no separate range declared or stored, and the Area tier has no total range, because the Area is the atomic written unit and has no child count to bracket (Part 2 Rule 9.3.1). A `~` "approximation" prefix is non-compliant: the range itself carries the tentativeness. If no Chapter range has yet been authored, the `of [M_low]–[M_high]` clause is omitted and the line reads simply `*Chapter [N]*`. The Tier Scope Bracket emitted after every Area (Part 2 Area 9 Rule 9.10.1) is the authoritative range source. A Chapter range in the Position Header Block that diverges from the active Chapter bracket is corrected in the report; it is a display mismatch, not a story finding and not a FAIL.

## Area 19 — Decision Consultation Gate (Author-as-Rudder Law)

### 19.1 — Principle (Assistant-Proposed Scene-Setting With Always-Available SKIP)

The Starward Saga is an authored novel series. The user is its author. The assistant is its writer — the instrument that renders the author's narrative direction into prose at the immersion depth of Area 4 (ten Named Depth Pillars DP-1 through DP-10). Major narrative-direction decisions belong to the author, not to the writer. The assistant's role is to execute the author's chosen direction with full Canon compliance and full prose craft, not to invent the direction unilaterally.

The Decision Consultation Gate is the **scene-setting collaboration mechanism** by which the author contributes to the design of each Area without writing the prose itself. The assistant — having read the Canon files and identified the open creative space the upcoming Area inhabits — composes a small set of concrete, mutually-exclusive **scene-setting options** that it has crafted from its own creative judgement, and presents them to the author as choices. The author either selects an option (and thereby contributes to the story design at the directional level) or selects an explicit **SKIP option** that returns control of that specific decision to the assistant. The author may SKIP any individual question, all questions, or use the per-Area `[NO CONSULT]` waiver to skip the entire Gate.

This reframes the Gate's relationship to authorship in two important ways:

1. **The assistant is the option author; the user is the option chooser.** The assistant is not asking the user to write the scene from scratch — it is asking the user to choose among scene-setting directions the assistant has already crafted. This places the design burden on the assistant (where it belongs as the writer) and the directional steering on the author (where it belongs as the rudder).

2. **SKIP is always available on every directional question, on every Area.** (A canon-compliance question is not a directional question: it is resolved, never skipped; `guardrails/commands/guardrail-command-law.md`.) The author may at any moment hand a specific decision back to the assistant by selecting "SKIP — let AI decide." This converts every consultation into an opt-in collaboration rather than a mandatory questionnaire — the author contributes where they wish to contribute, and the assistant proceeds on its own creative judgement everywhere else. SKIP is per-question and is in addition to (not a replacement for) the per-Area `[NO CONSULT]` waiver and the No-Open-Branches skip path.

The Gate runs once per Area. The Chapter Spec has already settled what it plans for each Area (Part 38), so the Gate asks only about what the spec and the Canon leave open.

The Gate keeps the author in an active steering position rather than a reactive one (accepting or rejecting an already-committed prose draft). It requires the assistant to **propose scene-setting options to the author for collaborative selection before any Area drafting begins**, with the author free to select any option or to SKIP and return control to the assistant.

The author is the rudder. The writer is the wind in the sails. The Decision Consultation Gate is the moment before each leg of the voyage when the writer offers the author a small set of crafted course options and the author chooses one — or hands the wheel back to the writer.

### 19.2 — The Decision Consultation Mandate

Every Area-producing command (`[START STORY]`, `[NEXT AREA]`, `[NEXT CHAPTER]`, `[NEXT PART]`, `[NEXT SECTION]`, `[NEXT PHASE]`, `[REVISE AREA]`, `[REDRAFT AREA]`, `[REDRAFT CHAPTER]`) must, before any Area drafting begins, present the user with a **multi-question AskUserQuestion sequence** covering the major narrative-direction decisions for the upcoming Area. `[REVISE AREA]` runs the Gate in a short form: it asks only about the open branches the requested change creates (a new name, a change of scope).

**Single-Area Scope.** The Decision Consultation Gate governs exactly **one** Area per invocation, never several. This is the same One-Area-Per-Invocation discipline that governs the rest of the Two-Gate pipeline (`commands/guardrail-command-law.md`), extended explicitly to planning: even when a structural moment plausibly needs more than one Area to execute (a wide establishing beat followed by a close character beat, for instance), the Decision Consultation Gate, the planning pass, and the draft are each run for the *first* of those Areas only. The second Area gets its own full cycle — its own Decision Consultation Gate, its own outline confirmation, its own draft, its own Gate 1/Gate 2 — only after the first Area has been confirmed, written, and (if the command sequence calls for it) explicitly re-invoked. No command plans, drafts, or presents two Areas in a single pass. Where a scene continues across consecutive Areas, each Area still runs its own full cycle, and each Gate 1 reads the Areas of that scene already written together with the new draft (Part 2 Rules P2-Area-2 and P2-Area-6; Part 36 Area 16.6).

The Decision Consultation Gate executes immediately after command receipt and Pre-Area Read (Area 4.6) and immediately before drafting. Drafting may not begin until the user has answered every question in the sequence (or explicitly waived consultation per Rule 19.4).

### 19.3 — Decision Categories (Scene-Setting Options on Directional Branch Points)

The Decision Consultation Gate consults the author **only on directional branch points where the prior Canon genuinely leaves multiple Canon-compatible paths open and the author's choice meaningfully changes the narrative trajectory of the upcoming Area or downstream Areas.** For each genuine branch, the assistant **composes a small set of scene-setting options it has crafted from its own creative judgement** and presents them to the author for selection (with always-available SKIP per Rule 19.4). The categories below describe the *kinds* of branch points that may qualify — not a checklist that must be invoked at every Area. The assistant determines, by reading the Canon files at the moment of command receipt (decisions are sourced from the files, never from memory), which categories actually contain open branch points for the upcoming Area; categories already settled by the Canon are NEVER asked about.

1. **Narrative-Direction Branch Points (the primary category).** Where the upcoming Area has two or more plausible narrative directions all consistent with the prior Canon and all consistent with downstream Phase/Section/Novel-Part architecture per Part 30, the choice between those directions is the author's. Examples of qualifying branch points: should the protagonist remain alone in the scene or be joined by another established character whose presence the Canon does not require either way? should the upcoming event resolve quickly or be drawn out across an additional Area, where no confirmed Chapter Spec has settled the scene's span? should an emergent cultivation trial be passed, failed, or deferred? Examples of disqualifying non-branches: anything Phase 1 architecture under Part 30 has already settled; anything the protagonist's established character arc requires; anything the prior Area has already committed to; anything a confirmed Chapter Spec has settled (Part 38 Rule 4.1.1), the Area's pacing and size target, its opening and closing types, and the cuts, cliffhangers, viewpoint changes and time skips planned in its block among them (Part 38 Rules 2.4.3 to 2.4.8); and the settled items of the confirmed Section Spec and Novel-Part Spec, which are carried into the Chapter Spec (Part 38 Rule 9.5.1).

2. **Character-Introduction Decisions for Genuinely New Characters.** (narrowed by Area 20.4: only a character entering the Hero or Villain Ledger, or another major entity, is put to the author; minor names are autonomous) Where the upcoming Area introduces a character who is not yet named in any prior Area, ledger, or Canon Part, and whose name is not derivable from the existing Canon, the author chooses the name (and any other establishment-level attributes left open by the Canon: gender, age, role-position, etc.). For characters already named anywhere in the existing Canon (Part files, ledger entity files, prior Areas), names are NEVER asked about — they are read from the canonical source.

3. **Major Cultivation/Technology/Combat Decision Points** (where the upcoming Area stages an active cultivation trial, technology integration moment, or combat encounter). Where Parts 3–10 leave multiple Canon-compatible outcomes open (e.g., which Affinity surfaces first; which technology integration path is taken; how a combat exchange resolves at the tactical level), the author chooses the outcome. Where Parts 3–10 settle the outcome (e.g., the protagonist's Realm at a fixed Phase milestone), the outcome is NEVER asked about.

4. **Major Emotional/Relational Direction Points** (where the upcoming Area hinges on a relational or emotional decision the character could plausibly make either way under Part 28 Area 6 Behavioral Authenticity Law). Example: "should Halise speak first or wait for Varya to break the silence?" — the answer changes the relational dynamic of the Area. Counter-example: "what is Varya's emotional register at her own dawn-vigil?" — the Canon and the Area's framing already settle this; not an open branch.

5. **Scope Decisions for the Upcoming Area** (where the upcoming Area could plausibly cover a wider or narrower span of in-world time/event without violating Part 2 Area 9 Tier Scope Brackets, and the Chapter Spec has not settled it). Example: "should this Area end at first light, at the morning meal, or at the protagonist's first formal lesson of the day?" The author's choice readjusts the active Tier Scope Bracket per Part 2 Rule 9.5.1. Pacing and Area size are not scope decisions and are not asked here: the Chapter Spec decides both for every Area (Rule 19.3a).

Categories whose contents are fully fixed by prior Canon — protagonist name when established, scene location when established by the Phase/Section frame, emotional register when established by the established character arc, the canon-information unit an Area must earn when fixed by the contextual-earning sequence — are **NEVER asked about**. Asking about already-Canon-settled facts is a Rule 19.4 violation (the **No-Asking-What-Canon-Already-Settles Rule**).

### 19.3a — Pacing Is Decided in the Chapter Spec

Pacing (Fast, Medium, Slow) and the Area size target (1,600, 1,800 or 2,000 words) are decided for every Area in the Chapter Spec (Part 38 Rule 2.3.3), where the author has already answered them. The Gate does **not** ask them. The Chapter Spec's pacing value for the Area is carried into the Gate 1 Position Header Block, and the Position Header Block shows the pacing value as carried from the Chapter Spec, so that a later reader can see the answer was given by the author in the spec and never assumed (Rule P2-Pacing-2).

An Area whose Chapter Spec block carries no pacing value is not drafted until the spec supplies one: the missing value is a spec-completeness finding and is sent back to the Chapter Spec (`/chapter-spec`), not asked at the Gate. If the author wants a different pacing for an Area, the change is made in the Chapter Spec, and the Gate then carries the new value.

### 19.4 — Question Authoring Standards

- **Always-Available SKIP Option (load-bearing).** Every question presented in any AskUserQuestion call under this Gate must include an explicit option labelled `SKIP — let AI decide` (or a near-equivalent phrasing that unambiguously hands the decision back to the assistant). The SKIP option is mandatory **on every directional question, on every Area**; a canon-compliance question carries no SKIP, because it is resolved by the author and not skipped (`guardrails/commands/guardrail-command-law.md`). It is in addition to (not a replacement for) the implicit "Other" escape hatch the AskUserQuestion tool provides automatically. Selection of SKIP by the author returns control of that specific decision to the assistant, which then proceeds on its own Canon-grounded creative judgement for that decision. SKIP is per-question (single decision returns to assistant), distinct from the per-Area `[NO CONSULT]` waiver (entire Gate is bypassed for one Area). An AskUserQuestion call that omits SKIP from any of its directional questions is a Rule 19.4 violation — Failure Mode C below.
- **Assistant-Proposed Scene-Setting Options.** The assistant — having read the Canon files and identified the open creative space — composes the option set for each question from its own creative judgement. Options should be concrete scene-setting choices (e.g., "Open in Varya's chamber at first light" / "Open at the practice yard with Aegon already arrived" / "Open in the Sun Hall during morning audience"), not abstract framings. The assistant is doing the design work; the author is doing the directional selection.
- **No-Asking-What-Canon-Already-Settles Rule (load-bearing).** Before composing any AskUserQuestion call, the assistant must read the relevant Canon Part files, ledger entity files, and prior Area files at the moment of command receipt (decisions are sourced from the files, never from memory) to determine which decisions are already fixed by the Canon and which are genuinely open. **Already-settled facts are NEVER asked about.** This includes, non-exhaustively: protagonist name when established by Part 30 Phase architecture, ledger entry, or prior Area; scene-opening location when fixed by the Section/Novel-Part/Chapter/Area frame; the settled items of the confirmed Section Spec and Novel-Part Spec, carried into the Chapter Spec (Part 38 Rule 9.5.1); the pacing and size target of the Area, fixed in the Chapter Spec; emotional register when fixed by the established character arc and the Area's place in the Contextual Earning sequence; the canon-information unit an Area must earn when fixed by the prior-Area continuity and the upcoming Area's position in the scene's earning trajectory; cultivation outcomes settled by Parts 3–10 milestones; cosmological/world details settled by Parts 13–15. A Decision Consultation that surfaces already-Canon-settled facts as if they were open choices is a Rule 19.4 violation and re-frames the author as a redundant decision-maker rather than as the rudder. **Empirical test:** if the answer to the candidate question is already discoverable by reading the existing Canon files or the Chapter Spec, the question must not be asked.
- **Directional decisions only.** The Decision Consultation Gate consults on **directional branch points** per Rule 19.3 — choices that meaningfully change the narrative trajectory of the upcoming Area or downstream Areas. Micro-prose decisions — word choice, sentence rhythm, paragraph structure, dialogue phrasing, internal-monologue voice — remain with the assistant as part of the writing craft. Equally, Canon-settled facts remain with the Canon and are not surfaced as questions.
- **Concrete options, not open-ended prompts.** Each question presents 2 to 3 mutually-exclusive concrete scene-setting options plus the mandatory SKIP option (three or four options in all, four being the tool maximum) plus the implicit "Other" escape hatch (which the AskUserQuestion tool provides automatically). The total option count per question is therefore 3 or 4 visible options (2 to 3 scene-setting + 1 SKIP), with "Other" implicit. Open-ended "what should happen?" prompts are non-compliant because they fail to give the author a starting frame for choice.
- **Persistent consultation until 100% clarity (Exhaustive Clarity Mandate).** A single AskUserQuestion call carries 1–4 questions (the tool maximum). The assistant issues as many follow-up AskUserQuestion calls as are needed — the count is bounded by *completeness*, not by convenience or proportionality. If an author's answer to one question reveals a new genuine open branch (per Rule 19.3's five categories, or a scope/naming/tone consequence the chosen direction now creates), that new branch is surfaced in a further round rather than resolved silently by the assistant. Consultation continues, round after round, until no genuine open branch point remains — either because the author answered it or because the author explicitly selected SKIP for it. Where every one of the five 19.3 categories is Canon-settled (zero open narrative branches), the assistant may skip the whole AskUserQuestion call, declare the Gate satisfied via "no open narrative branch points found," and proceed to sequence step 1 (Pre-Area Read). Skipping for genuine cause is not a Rule 19.2 violation; asking about non-branches, or terminating consultation while a genuine branch remains open, are both violations. The same definition of "100% clarity" governs the Section Spec, Novel-Part Spec and Chapter Spec builds, stated once in Part 38 Rule 38.13, Clarity Mandate for All Spec Builds.
- **Recommended option marked.** When the assistant has a Canon-grounded recommendation among the offered scene-setting options (e.g., for Canon-coherence reasons or for narrative-craft reasons), it may mark that option `(Recommended)` and place it first per AskUserQuestion convention. The recommendation does not bind the author; it surfaces the assistant's craft judgement for the author's information. The SKIP option is never marked `(Recommended)` — its purpose is to hand the decision back to the assistant unmarked. Where an option could name an entity or leave it unnamed, the named version is the recommended one, and naming is never presented as a cost, risk or downside of any option (Rule 20.9).
- **Per-Area Waiver.** The author may waive Decision Consultation for an entire Area by issuing the explicit token `[NO CONSULT]` after the Area-producing command but before the assistant emits the AskUserQuestion call. A waived Area proceeds directly to drafting; the assistant uses its best Canon-grounded judgement on all categories, and the Chapter Spec's plan stands. `[NO CONSULT]` is per-Area only and does not establish a session-level waiver. The per-question SKIP option (above) is the lighter-weight equivalent for handing back individual decisions.

### 19.5 — Sequencing Within the Two-Gate Output

The Gate 1 and Gate 2 sequence is owned by `guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md` and `guardrails/commands/guardrail-command-law.md`; this Area adds only the consultation that precedes it:

```
0. Decision Consultation Gate (this Area — multi-step AskUserQuestion)
1. Pre-Area Read (Area 4.6)
2. Area Drafting (Prose Depth and Immersion Law — Part 28 Area 4.1 + Part 2 Area 1 section 1.6.2.1 — all ten Depth Pillars (DP-1 through DP-10) weighed on the Area (Area 4.2); Area size per Part 2 Rule P2-Area-7, one of the three targets set in the Chapter Spec; a count outside 1,280 to 2,400 words is a blocking finding and the write waits for the author to confirm the Area or send it back)

Its outputs (the user's answers) become drafting inputs. If a Decision Consultation answer is itself Canon-incompatible (e.g., the user picks a name that violates Part 28 Area 18 register/length, or picks a location that violates Phase 1 Locked Canon under Part 30), Gate 1 catches it in the draft (the Post-Draft Audit, step 3) like any other Canon FAIL and the Correction Protocol applies; a remedy that would change what happens on the page is put to the author as a re-framed question that surfaces the Canon constraint, not absorbed.

### 19.6 — Failure Grades

Two distinct failure modes are graded, with a third for the SKIP option:

**Failure Mode A — Missed Consultation on a Genuine Branch.**
- **Minor.** Missing one open directional branch point that should have been consulted. Correctable by issuing the missed-branch AskUserQuestion call before drafting begins.
- **Major.** Missing multiple open branch points, or proceeding past sequence step 0 to step 1 without any consultation in an Area that contains open branches. Correctable only by halting drafting, issuing the consultation, and restarting the sequence at step 0.
- **Critical.** Drafting and writing an Area all the way through Gate 2 file writes without ever having presented a Decision Consultation, in an Area that contained open branches. ## Area 20 — Entity Naming Law

### 20.1 — Principle

Area 3 (Naming Freedom Law) establishes that the author MAY name any instance the Canon does not pre-specify. Area 20 establishes when the prose MUST. A world in which significant entities go unnamed reads as a stage set: "the guild," "the northern town," "the officer" are placeholders, and placeholders break the immersion mandate (Area 4) as surely as thin prose does. Every entity that matters to the story carries a name; every name is crafted thematically by the methodology of 20.3; and naming obligations are enforced at Gate 1 with the same blocking severity as the dialogue-format and ledger checks. Naming is not decoration — an entity's name is the anchor to which the Area 17 Lore Creation Law attaches its ledger record, and an unnamed significant entity is an entity the ledger system cannot track.

### 20.2 — The Narrative-Significance Test

An entity introduced in an Area — person, region, location, settlement, building, faction, guild, sect, organization, cultural event, institution, vessel, or named-object candidate — MUST be given a proper name within that same Area if it meets ANY of prongs 1, 2, 4 or 5 below, or if the story makes it matter as prong 3 describes:

1. **It speaks.** Any entity delivering dialogue, however brief, is named in that Area. There is no role-only speaker: a gate guard's one challenge and a herald's call each carry a name. A newcomer who only speaks, and whose words carry no consequence, is a Tier D person (Part 19 Area 1.2): the Area Maker names the person by the 20.3 methodology and picks the compact code alone, with no question to the author (Part 19 Rules 19.1.2.3 to 19.1.2.7; Part 39 Section 3.5), and the ledger record is the Tier D registry row. A speaker who acts or decides is not Tier D and is flagged to the author (Part 19 Rule 19.1.2.4).
2. **It acts with consequence.** Its action changes story state (DP-7): it injures, aids, decides, blocks, reveals, or transacts in a way the story must remember.
3. **It recurs.** It comes back in a later Area. Recurrence is one sign among others that the entity matters; the story decides, and a name given at that moment is never a retroactive revision of the earlier unit.
4. **It carries relational or consequence weight.** An IE, CSS, or PIS event, a relationship bond, or a ledger obligation attaches to it.
5. **The POV dwells on it.** The prose renders it with distinguishing detail that creates a reader expectation of identity.

An entity failing all five prongs is background texture and MAY remain unnamed (a passing crowd, an anonymous servant, gate guards who say nothing). Background texture that later gains significance is named at that later moment under prong 3. The test says where naming is required and says nothing more (Rule 20.9.4). "MAY remain unnamed" is a permission and never a recommendation; below the line the recommended default is to name. Check #17(a) fails an unnamed entity that passes the test.

### 20.3 — Thematic Naming Methodology

Every name is crafted through the following sequence, in order:

1. **Ledger scan first.** Before crafting any name, scan the relevant ledger `_index.md` registries for existing names. An entity that already has a canonical name is never renamed (Area 3.4); a new name must not collide with or closely echo an established name in the same narrative web. The scan of a monster name includes `ledger-character-monster/_index.md` (Part 40 Rule 40.13.2.1).
2. **Register second.** The producing culture's Part 25 CPT governs the linguistic and aesthetic register (Area 3.3). Azure Sky Kingdom names carry the dual-track paired-oath register; Gale Kingdom names carry the wind-and-oath single-affinity register; guild names carry charter-noun constructions; and so on per the established cultural canon.
3. **Entity-type convention third.** Persons follow the established naming patterns of their family, culture, and station (e.g., the Azure Sky royal family's established V-alliteration — Varys, Valia, Varya — is a family pattern, not a kingdom-wide rule). Locations compound feature and function (Sapphire Basin, Windward Pass, Ember Reach). Factions and institutions carry charter nouns (Azure Crafters' Concord, Skylight Analytic Hall). Events commemorate (Festival of the Twin Oath). Roads and infrastructure carry destination or feature (Sapphire Road, Southern March Trail).
4. **Story resonance fourth.** The name should carry a thematic charge proportionate to the entity's narrative function — evocative for the significant, plain for the humble — without telegraphing future twists.
5. **Distinctiveness check fifth.** The name must be phonetically and visually distinct from every other name active in the same scene-web, so no reader confuses two entities.
6. **Originality check last.** No new name may equal a proper name from a widely known published work (a character, place, faction, artifact or world from a novel, film, game or series). A candidate that does is replaced before delivery. No one can check every published work, so a name that cannot be cleared with confidence is flagged to the author for review, and an established name found to match is flagged to the author, never silently changed (Area 3.4).

Contemporary-Earth anachronistic patterns remain prohibited (Area 3.3). Once a crafted name is delivered at Gate 1 and the prose is confirmed, the name is established and immutable (Area 3.4) and its Area 17 ledger lore capture executes at Gate 2.

### 20.4 — The Consultation Split: Major vs. Minor Entities (narrows Area 19 Rule 19.3 category 2)

**MAJOR entities — author-consulted.** The name of a major entity is a Decision Consultation Gate question (Area 19) before drafting: the assistant crafts 2 to 3 candidate names by the 20.3 methodology and presents them with the always-available SKIP option, three or four in all (Rule 19.4). Major entities are:
- (a) any character entering the Hero Ledger or Villain Ledger (antagonistic first appearances always require AskUserQuestion);
- (b) kingdoms, states, empires, and civilization-scale polities;
- (c) factions of kingdom-scale power tier or above (Part 22);
- (d) Sacred Grounds, World Trees, and every Area 9 Tier 2 Authorization entity;
- (e) any entity whose Debuts cell in the Chapter Spec Area row carries a MAJOR-name consultation state of consulted or pending (Part 38 Rule 2.3.4);
- (f) any form of address, honorific, or mode of address directed *at* a Hero-Ledger or Villain-Ledger character that is not already recorded as author-confirmed in that character's ledger file — such a convention recurs across the character's entire ongoing dialogue presence and is therefore never covered by the minor-entity autonomous allowance below, even where the ledger field is marked "Negotiable Canon, to be established in prose" (`guardrails/quality/guardrail-entity-naming-law.md`, Address/Title Conventions Are Not Free Invention);
- (g) every sentient monster, every Tier A or Tier B monster Individual, every Tier A or Tier B monster Kind, and any monster Kind that recurs at Section scale (Part 40 Rule 40.13.3.1). Every other monster name is MINOR, named by the assistant, with `[REVISE AREA]` as the author's veto.

**MINOR entities — AI-named autonomously.** Everything below the major threshold — Tier B, C and D side characters (the placement of a Tier A side character is open for the author's ruling), local locations (streets, shops, taverns, villages of passing significance), minor organizations and local branches, local cultural events, named objects below the Part 12 relic tier, and infrastructure — is named autonomously by the assistant through the 20.3 methodology, with no consultation question. The author's control is preserved through [REVISE AREA] at the Inter-Gate Confirmation: any autonomous name may be rejected and recrafted before the prose is written to file.

**Scope of consultation.** This split narrows Area 19 Rule 19.3 category 2. Under Area 20, only major entities (as defined above) are consulted; minor-character and minor-entity names are autonomous.

### 20.8 — Named Items: Form and Register


**Rule 20.8.1 — What a named item is, and what it is not.**
A named item is a made or used thing that the story gives a proper name of its own. The plain test: a character could hold it, carry it, wear it, eat or drink it, apply it, read it, ride or sail it, or run it, and the story calls it by that name. Its Type is one of: apparatus or device; remedy; gear or tool; weapon; system or service (the thing that is run, such as a signal line, and never the body that runs it); material; food; drink; dress or adornment; vessel or vehicle; relic or artifact; formation array; energy cache; or other item (for example a manual title, Part 42). Anything that is an item gets registered, and `other item` is the catch-all, so that no named made or used thing lacks a Type. A named document (a letter, charter, map, codex, order, account book) is set in italic like an item but is registered in `ledger-society-information`, not here (Rule 20.8.7). A manual title is a named item of Type `other item` registered by ID in the Manual Ledger (`ledger-manuals/`), with a pointer row in `ledger-named-items`; it is italic.

A named item is not any of the following, and each of these keeps its own form and its own ledger:
- (a) a person, place, kingdom or state, faction, guild or institution, or a title, honorific or form of address (Rule 20.4(f)); a monster, a Kind of monster, or a monster's name, which is a living being and is set in roman, as a person or a species is (Part 40 Rule 40.13.1.1);
- (b) a Named Move, Named Technique or Named Cultivation Practice, or a cultivation level designation (bold-italic under Part 2 Rules 3.2.1 and 3.2.1a; every Named Move is recorded in its own generic register, `ledger-named-moves`, and in the entries of those who use it, Part 6 Rule 6.5);
- (c) a named document: a letter or message, charter, decree or order, map, codex, record or account book, or any other named written thing. A named document is set in italic by Rule 20.8.2, and it is registered in `ledger-society-information` and not in the named-item register (Rule 20.8.7). A Named Relic, Named Artifact, formation array or energy resource cache is a named item, with the Types relic or artifact, formation array and energy cache; each is set in italic and registered in the named-item register, with its descriptive entry staying in `ledger-energy-relics-artifacts` (Part 12 Rule 2.2.3).
- (d) an agreement, treaty or accord (Part 23), which is set in roman and is recorded in `ledger-society-information` with its home file in `ledger-people-society-affiliation` (Rule 20.8.7); an event or festival, a research program (Part 9), a scientific framework or paradigm event (Part 10), an intelligence operation (Part 24), or a named route, gate or distance (Parts 14 and 15): each has its own ledger under Part 37 Area 3; a month or weekday name of the calendar (Part 41) is likewise not a named item, is set in roman like a place name, and is recorded in `ledger-worlds-calendar`;
- (e) a building, site or facility that people go to: it is a place. The apparatus used there is the item;
- (f) a species-language term (Part 16 Rule N16-14, already italic): it is written as that rule says, and where such a word also names an item the item is still entered in the register;
- (g) a category term or tier label from the numbered Parts (Power Crystal, Core, Mortal-grade, a weapon grade), or a plain description with no proper name ("the salve", "a practice sword").

Where a named item already has a home ledger entry (`ledger-technology-scale` for systems and devices, `ledger-technology-weapon`, `ledger-technology-vehicle`, `ledger-energy-relics-artifacts` for a relic, artifact, formation array or energy cache; and `ledger-technology-science` for a named knowledge asset, which is a document and is pointed at from `ledger-society-information`), that entry stays the only owner of every descriptive fact, and the register holds the name and a pointer to it (Rule 20.8.4).

**Rule 20.8.2 — Form: italic, name only, roman inside italic.**
- (a) **Italic at every mention.** The name of a named item is set in italic in the Area body (the scope of Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1b) and in every spec file (Section Spec, Novel-Part Spec and Chapter Spec), at its first use and at every use after. In Markdown the form is a single-asterisk span, `*First Grip*`. In the Word file it is a character-level italic run. Both writer scripts read a single-asterisk span as an italic run (`_parse_inline_formatting` in `area_docx_creator.py`; `area_md_creator.py` passes the text through). A name is never set in bold or bold-italic. A Named Relic, Named Artifact, formation array and energy cache are written this way too: `the *Heaven-Shattering Edge*`.
- (b) **The name only.** The italic covers the registered name exactly. A possessive ending (`'s`) and any other ending added to the name stay outside the italic: `the *First Grip*'s strap`. The leading article is never part of the name. The register holds the name without it, and the prose writes the article in plain type before the italic name: `the *Hourline*`, and `The *Hourline*` at the start of a sentence.
- (c) **Roman inside a passage that is already italic.** Inside speech (which Part 2 Rule 2.2.2 and Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1 set wholly in italic), inside an italic thought, or inside any other italic span, the name is set in roman, so that it still stands out. The italic span closes at the word before the name and opens again at the word after it, the same break Part 2 Rule 7.2.5 already uses for a beat or a tag inside speech; a space or a mark of punctuation that touches the name stays roman with it, so that every asterisk sits beside a space. The speech stays double-quoted and every other word of it stays italic; the closing double quotation mark stays inside the italic. Example in the Markdown form: `*"Bring the* First Grip *to me, and be quick."*`. Inside a passage that is set in plain type by rule (the angle-quote forms of Part 29 Rule 29.14.3.3) the name is italic, as everywhere else.
- (d) **One name, one form.** The same item is always written the same way: the same spelling and capitals, no leading article inside the name (it is plain type outside the italic), and the same italic. A short form or nickname that the story uses is recorded as an alias in the register and is set in italic too. A name changes only by Canon Amendment at the author's word (Area 3.4), and the old name stays in the Aliases cell.
- (e) **Where the form stops.** Where the name sits inside a longer name that has a form of its own (a Named Move, or a relic's name), the longer name's form governs and nothing nests. A structural name in a written Area file (a Phase, Section, Novel-Part, Chapter or Area title) is a heading, which the writer scripts set from plain text; a named item inside one is not italicised there. The form is not required in ledger entity files, audit reports, the session log or chat; the one place it is required outside the Area text and specs is the Entity Name cell of the register (and of the information register for a named document, Rule 20.8.7).
- (f) **Emphasis guard.** Italic emphasis on an ordinary word is free, and this rule adds no ban on it. The one guard: a word that is a registered item name is never italicised for emphasis or for any reason other than naming that item, because the italic would read as the item.

**Rule 20.8.3 — Naming a new item.**
A new item's name is crafted by the 20.3 sequence, and the ledger scan of its first step includes `ledger-named-items/_index.md` (and `ledger-society-information/_index.md` for a named document or agreement). Under 20.4 a named item is a MINOR entity: it is named autonomously and the author's veto path is `[REVISE AREA]`; an item that recurs at Section scale is MAJOR and is author-consulted. A name proposed in a spec is a proposal and nothing more (Part 38 Rule 1.3.2): it is not entered in the register until the Area that writes it is confirmed and written.

**Rule 20.8.4 — The register: a ledger obligation at Gate 2.**
- (a) **Every named item is entered.** Every named item written into a confirmed and written Area is entered as a row of `ledger-named-items/_index.md` by the Post-Write Ledger Update of that Area (Part 37 Threshold O-4, schema Area 3.8). Its Type is one of the Types of Rule 20.8.1; a relic, artifact, formation array or energy cache is entered here too, with its home entry in `ledger-energy-relics-artifacts`. The register has no significance test: a name that appears once counts, and so does a name that appears only in speech. `[SKIP WRITE]` discards the name and nothing is entered. A manual title is entered at Gate 2 as a pointer row whose Entity File is the Manual Ledger CSV file that holds the manual and whose cell names the `manual_id` (Part 42 Rule 42.12.4.4).
- (b) **A pointer, never a copy.** The row holds the name in its italic form (without a leading article), the Type, one plain line on what the item is, who made or named it, the first Area, the home ledger entry or `none`, the status, the date and the naming checks. It never copies a value from a home ledger entry. Where a home entry exists it owns every descriptive fact and the row mirrors only the name, the kind and the first Area (Part 37 Area 5, one owner for a fact held in two places).
- (c) **The home entry is not waived.** Whether the item also earns a home ledger entry is decided by its own Part 37 threshold (O-1 for a relic, artifact, formation array or energy cache, O-2 for a weapon, O-3 for a vehicle, and the Area 3 thresholds). The register adds a row and never replaces a home entry.
- (d) **Read before drafting.** The Pre-Write Ledger Check reads the register for every item the unit will use, resolving names through the Entity Name and Aliases cells, so that the item is written in its registered form.
- (e) **Hand-off route.** The Area Maker's hand-off lists each item that is new in the unit, one line each. The Gate 1 checker reconciles that list against the italic names in the draft and returns the checked list; the orchestrating command passes it unchanged to the Gate 2 ledger instance, the same route as the Tier D block. The same block lists each named document and each named agreement, treaty or accord that is new in the unit, marked for `ledger-society-information` (Rule 20.8.7).
- (f) **No backfill is required.** Names the ledgers already record enter the register when the Area that writes them is confirmed. The author may direct a backfill at any time.

**Rule 20.8.5 — Enforcement.**
- Gate 1 **Check #12** reads the form of Rule 20.8.2 beside the bold-italic reading it already makes: an item name not set in italic, set in italic inside an italic span, set in bold or bold-italic, set with its leading article inside the italic or the name, or written in a form other than the registered one, is a FAIL, as a missing bold-italic on a Named Move is. The check reads the emphasis guard of 20.8.2(f) in the same pass: a registered item name italicised for emphasis is reported there, and the form of a named document and of an agreement (Rule 20.8.7) is read the same way. **Check #5** reads the roman name inside speech as lawful.
- **Check #17**: the name itself is read under (b), the register row is the obligation that (c) flags, and an item the 20.2 test says must be named and is not is (a).
- **ALCA AL-10** reads the register in both directions: a named item in a written Area with no row, and a row whose name is not written as registered.
- The **light audit** of each spec in the chain reports an item name that is not in italic as a finding. The planner corrects it in the draft before `[CONFIRM SPEC]`; it needs no ruling from the author.

**Rule 20.8.6 — The purpose that survives.**
Against the dialogue-format mandate (Part 2 Rule 2.2.2; Part 29 Rule 29.2.1.1) this rule makes one allowance: the name of a named item may stand in roman inside an italic span. The purpose of that mandate, that spoken words are never mistaken for narration, holds, because every other word of the speech stays double-quoted and italic. The rule leaves the form of Named Moves and cultivation levels, and the ledger of any person, place, faction or move, as they are. Its one reach into another Part is that Part 12 Rule 2.2.3 sets Named Relics, Named Artifacts, formation arrays and energy caches in italic and not in bold, so that a named object of narrative significance stands out from a plain description.

**Rule 20.8.7 — Named documents and agreements: form and the information register.**
Roman for agreements, treaties and accords; italic for named maps and documents. The folder that stores all information (`ledger-society-information`, Part 37 Area 3.4) is the register for both.
- (a) **A named document** (a map, a letter or message, a charter, a decree or order, a codex, a record or account book, or any other named written thing) is set in italic by Rule 20.8.2(a) to (f): italic at every mention, the name only, no leading article inside the name, roman inside an italic passage. It is registered as a row of `ledger-society-information/_index.md`, with its Kind written in the Kind column of that register, and not in the named-item register (Rule 20.8.1(c)). A document that holds a classified finding keeps its home entry in `ledger-technology-science` and is pointed at from the information register.
- (b) **An agreement, treaty or accord** is set in roman, with no italic and no other mark. It is registered as a row of `ledger-society-information/_index.md` that points at its home file in `ledger-people-society-affiliation` (Part 23; Part 37 Area 3.3), which stays the only owner of its parties, terms and state (Part 37 Area 5, one owner).
- (c) **The duty is the same as for an item.** Every named document and every named agreement, treaty or accord written into a confirmed and written Area is entered by the Post-Write Ledger Update of that Area, with no significance test; a name that exists only in a spec, or in a draft discarded by `[SKIP WRITE]`, enters nothing. No backfill is required: the agreements the affiliation ledger already holds are entered when an Area that writes them is confirmed, or on the author's direction.
- (d) **Enforcement.** Check #12 reads the form of a named document (italic) and of an agreement (roman) as it reads an item (Rule 20.8.5). Check #17(c) reads the register row as the Gate 2 obligation.
- (e) **A manual is not a named document.** A manual of the Manual Ledger (Part 42) is not a named document of this Rule. It is registered in the Manual Ledger and its title is a named item (Rule 20.8.1).

### 20.9 — Naming Is Not a Cost


**Rule 20.9.1 — Naming is never weighed as a cost.**
In every consultation, plan, option set, recommendation, finding and report (the Decision Consultation Gate of Area 19; the build rounds of `/section-spec`, `/part-spec` and `/chapter-spec`; the Outline Planner's plans; the Continuity Checker's reports; every prompt a command puts to the author), giving a name to a person, thing, place, move or any other entity is never presented as a cost, a risk, a burden or a downside of an option. The work that a name creates is routine: a ledger entry, a register row, a Gate 2 obligation, a collision scan, a consultation round for a MAJOR name. That work is not weighed for or against an option. It does not make an option shorter, lighter or safer, and it is never a reason to leave an entity unnamed.

**Rule 20.9.2 — The named version is the recommended default.**
Where an option could name an entity or leave it unnamed, the named version is the one marked `(Recommended)` (Rule 19.4), and a plan or a spec writes the entity with its name. An unnamed variant is offered only when the author asks for it, or when the story has a reason of its own that has nothing to do with ledger work (a figure whose anonymity is the point of the scene). Anonymous background texture stays lawful (Rule 20.2) and is not turned into a question.

**Rule 20.9.3 — What is not touched.**
The author consultation for MAJOR entity names (2 to 3 crafted candidates plus SKIP, three or four in all, Rule 20.4); the Part 25 cultural-register check, the collision scan, the distinctiveness and originality checks of Rule 20.3; the no-anachronism rule (Area 3.3); name immutability (Area 3.4); the Gate 2 ledger obligation (Check #17(c), Rule 20.8.4) and the Part 37 creation thresholds all stand. They are how a name is made well and recorded. This rule only forbids counting them against naming.

**Rule 20.9.4 — The significance test, reconciled.**
The Narrative-Significance Test (Rule 20.2) is the line at which naming becomes required, and an unnamed entity that passes it is a Check #17(a) FAIL. Its sentence that background texture "MAY remain unnamed" is a permission and is never a recommendation: below the line the recommended default is to name, and a name given below the line is ordinary and carries the same Gate 2 consequences as any other (the Part 37 thresholds decide which entry it earns; a named item enters the register, Rule 20.8.4). The only reasons to leave something unnamed are the story's own.
