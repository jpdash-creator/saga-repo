## Overview

Part 14 is the Starward Saga Canon's World Systems Distance Law — the governing domain for distance as an active governance variable across every scale of the universe. Part 13 defines what locations *are*: their classification, their environmental properties, their place in the cosmological hierarchy. Part 14 defines *how far apart everything is* and *how far every system can perceive, reach, and act* — the measurement framework, the canonical distance thresholds, and the range tables that bound cultivation perception, technology sensors, technique projection, weapon reach, and formation array coverage.

Ten foundational principles (Area 1) establish that distance is a governance variable, not mere description; that distance is scale-relative; that distance perception scales with cultivation realm and technology tier; that high-energy environments modify effective operational range without changing raw distance; that dimensional distance is orthogonal to physical distance; that distance narration must be immersively consistent; that the Cosmic Measurement Scale is canon-primary and uses real-world units; that every distance claim requires a scale context; that FTL compresses transit time but never erases distance; and that the Primordium's distance is undefined and outside standard measurement.

From these foundations, Part 14 builds outward across 17 Areas: the Cosmic Measurement Scale with its full tier reference, summary table, abbreviations, and directional notation (Area 2); the canonical distance thresholds for every level of Part 13's cosmological hierarchy (Area 3); cultivation distance perception and effect range — soul sense by realm, the full modifier and stacking system, and technique propagation distance (Area 4); technology distance perception and effect range — sensor, communication, and weapon range by technology tier (Area 5); formation array distance governance (Area 6); the distance interface with the cosmological hierarchy, including minimum separations, the Overlap Rule, and Sacred Ground influence zones (Area 7); distance narration standards (Area 8); special distance cases — FTL, dimensional shortcuts, Sacred Ground interaction, biological non-cultivator perception, and full dimensional distance governance (Area 9); the joint-governance interfaces with Part 3 (Area 10), Part 5 (Area 11), Part 8 (Area 12), Part 13 (Area 13), Part 15 (Area 14), and Parts 11, 12, and 31 (Area 15); the author quick reference with the Six Distance Questions and five worked governance scenarios (Area 16); and the full cross-Part dependency table (Area 17).

## Area 1 — Foundational Principles of Distance Law

### 1.1 — Core Principles

**P14-1 — Distance Is a Governance Variable**
Distance is not merely a descriptive quantity in the Starward Saga — it is an active governance variable that determines what interactions are possible, what perceptions are achievable, what techniques can reach, and what effects can propagate. A cultivator with soul sense range of 500 km is governed differently in a battle 600 km away than in one 400 km away. A civilization with sensor range of one light-year cannot detect a threat two light-years distant regardless of how powerful its other capabilities are. Distance governance ensures that the reach of any system — cultivation, technology, or biological — is bounded by defined, consistent rules.

**P14-2 — Distance Is Scale-Relative**
The same numerical distance means profoundly different things at different cosmological scales. One kilometer is the full diameter of a large Village or a small Town at planetary surface scale; it is the distance between neighboring streets in a galaxy-spanning empire's administrative hub. The Cosmic Measurement Scale (Area 2) is designed to express distance in units calibrated to the scale at which they are meaningful. Authors use scale-appropriate notation when narrating distance — meters for cultivation combat, AU for orbital operations, light-years for interstellar campaigns.

**P14-3 — Distance Perception Scales with Cultivation and Technology**
A Mortal Tier 1 human perceives and operates at distances measured in meters. An Absolute God-realm cultivator perceives and operates at distances measured in hundreds of kilometers. A Tier 10 civilization's sensor arrays perceive and operate at distances measured in hundreds of light-years. What constitutes "far" and "close" is a function of the observer's cultivation realm and/or technology tier, not an absolute property of the distance itself. This principle governs all narration involving distance — the author renders distance through the perception of the most capable observer present, not through a universal objective frame.

**P14-4 — Distance Contracts in High-Energy Environments**
In locations whose ambient energy density is not Standard (any class other than Class 2, per Part 13 Area 2.3), certain distance-dependent effects have their effective range modified. Soul sense range extends from Class 3 upward and shrinks in Class 0 and 1. Formation array effects propagate farther in high-ambient-energy zones. FTL transit efficiency varies by ambient energy density in certain physics configurations. Distance governance must account for these environmental modifiers — the raw distance is fixed, but the effective operational range of systems within that distance is modified.

**P14-5 — Dimensional Distance Is Orthogonal to Physical Distance**
Two locations separated by vast physical distance may be adjacent in a dimensional rift or portal network — effectively zero travel distance. Two locations physically adjacent may be separated by a dimensional boundary that makes them operationally inaccessible to one another. Dimensional proximity is governed by Part 13 (Area 12 — Dimensional Structure Governance); Part 14 governs the physical-distance layer and the rules for how dimensional shortcuts interact with canonical distance governance.

**P14-6 — Distance Narration Must Be Immersively Consistent**
Distance in prose must be rendered consistently — a location described as "three days travel" at one technology tier must be a different duration at a higher tier without contradiction. A soul sense range described as "hundreds of kilometers" in one chapter must be consistent with the cultivator's realm in the Part 14 range table. Authors use Part 14's Cosmic Measurement Scale and its narration rules (Area 8) to maintain distance consistency across books.

**P14-7 — The Cosmic Measurement Scale Is Canon-Primary**
The Cosmic Measurement Scale (CMS) (Area 2) is the canonical measurement framework. It uses real-world metric and astronomical units (kilometers, AU, light-years, etc.) as both prose units and calibration references — these are the standard and familiar language of science, which lends the series scientific grounding while maintaining cosmological scope. Prose uses CMS units naturally. Authors do not invent proprietary fictional units.

**P14-8 — Every Distance Claim Requires a Scale Context**
Distances stated without scale context are ambiguous and create governance gaps. "The army was two kilometers away" requires scale context: planetary surface? Inside a star system? Relative to what? Part 14 requires that all canonical distance statements establish their scale context — which level of the cosmological hierarchy the distance operates within — before any distance-dependent governance rule can apply.

**P14-9 — FTL Does Not Erase Distance**
Faster-than-light travel and communication (Part 10, Area 5; Part 8, Area 15.9) compresses effective travel time but does not eliminate distance as a governance variable. The distance still exists; FTL changes how much time is required to traverse it. FTL-capable civilizations still have strategic frontiers, still operate within range constraints, and still experience distance as a meaningful military, political, and narrative variable. FTL makes the universe accessible but not small.

**P14-10 — The Primordium Distance Is Undefined**
The Primordium (Part 13, P13-10) does not conform to standard distance measurement. Within the Primordium, distance, direction, and scale depend on the specific sub-region, the power of the entity navigating it, and the relationship between planes. Distance in the Primordium cannot be mapped from outside it. Authors depicting events in the Primordium do not apply Part 14's CMS or canonical distance ranges — they require Part 28 (Creative Flexibility Law) authorization.

## Area 2 — The Cosmic Measurement Scale (CMS)

### 2.1 — Design Philosophy

The Cosmic Measurement Scale (CMS) is the canonical distance measurement framework of the Starward Saga universe. It supersedes and fully replaces the older Void Notation Scale (VNS) and the even older Scale Notation System (SNS). The CMS is designed for three purposes:

1. **Scientific grounding** — using real-world metric and astronomical units gives the series a hard-science foundation that readers can intuitively understand and that creates natural wonder when the scale becomes clear
2. **Scale clarity** — each unit belongs to a recognizable tier; readers know exactly what "200 light-years" means versus "200 meters"
3. **Author governance** — precise, universally understood values ensure distances stated in different books are consistent without requiring a proprietary conversion table

The CMS uses real-world units throughout:
- **Metric units** for sub-planetary scale: millimeter (mm), centimeter (cm), meter (m), kilometer (km), megameter (Mm = 1,000 km), gigameter (Gm = 1,000,000 km)
- **Astronomical units** for system-to-interstellar scale: AU (1 AU ≈ 150 million km), light-second (~300,000 km), light-minute (~18 million km), light-hour (~1.08 billion km), light-day (~26 billion km), light-week (~182 billion km), light-year (~9.46 trillion km)
- **Cosmological units** for large-scale structure: kilolight-year (kly = 1,000 light-years), megalight-year (Mly = 1,000,000 light-years), gigalight-year (Gly = 1,000,000,000 light-years)

All CMS units are directly usable in prose. Characters in the Starward Saga universe use these terms naturally — they evolved alongside scientific development, just as humans have used "meters" and "light-years" as their civilization developed.

### 2.2 — The Sixteen Scale Tiers

---

#### CMS Tier 0 — Precision Scale
**Unit:** Millimeter (mm) / Centimeter (cm)
**Reference:** 1 mm = 0.001 m; 1 cm = 0.01 m
**Context:** Cultivation meridian precision work, inscription-level technique placement, acupuncture-equivalent node targeting, formation array inscription spacing at finest grade, weapon technique edge-alignment.
**Scale Description:** The tier of surgical precision — the gap between a meridian node and the nearest channel. A cultivator who cannot maintain sub-centimeter precision in their inscription work will not produce functioning arrays.
**Example prose:** "The inscription deviated by less than two millimeters from the meridian node — within tolerance for a third-ring formation, but an elder would still have revised it."

---

#### CMS Tier 1 — Human Scale
**Unit:** Meter (m)
**Reference:** 1 m = standard metric unit
**Sub-scale:** 10 m (room-crossing), 100 m (building scale)
**Context:** Individual combat range, room and building dimensions, direct weapon range, formation array inscription radius at small scale, aura radius for low-realm cultivators.
**Scale Description:** The tier of a person's immediate world — arm's reach, sword range, the width of a hall. Combat at this scale is personal: eye contact, breath, the sound of footsteps.
**Example prose:** "He stopped forty meters from the gate, sword already drawn, close enough to see the guards' pupils dilate before they registered what was standing in front of them."

---

#### CMS Tier 2 — Battlefield Scale
**Unit:** Kilometer (km)
**Reference:** 1 km = 1,000 m
**Sub-scale:** 500 m (half-kilometer), 100 m (stadium scale)
**Context:** Battlefield distances, settlement-to-settlement separation at local scale, soul sense range for senior Mortal Tier 2 cultivators, small formation array effect radius.
**Scale Description:** The tier of a battlefield — where armies line up, where cultivators watch each other across a valley floor, where the sound of war arrives seconds after its light.
**Example prose:** "The battle lines stretched across three kilometers of valley floor — more ground than any cultivator below the Void God level could hold alone, and the enemy had six."

---

#### CMS Tier 3 — Regional Scale
**Unit:** Megameter (Mm) — 1 Mm = 1,000 km; or expressed as hundreds of kilometers
**Reference:** 1 Mm = 1,000 km
**Sub-scale:** 100 km (regional coverage), 500 km (province-scale)
**Context:** Province-scale geography, mountain range width, regional cultivation footprint (Core Formation+), aura radius for Divine Transformation and above, large formation array networks, the governed reach of Towns, Cities and Capitals.
**Scale Description:** The tier of provinces and natural features — the width of a mountain range, the distance a regional lord's authority radiates, the reach of an elder's awareness across their domain.
**Example prose:** "The Divine Ascension elder's fury unfolded across eight hundred kilometers, and three provincial capitals sent messengers before the day was done."

---

#### CMS Tier 4 — Planetary Scale
**Unit:** Gigameter (Gm) — 1 Gm = 1,000,000 km; or expressed as thousands of kilometers
**Reference:** 1 Gm = 1,000,000 km; Earth diameter ≈ 12,742 km; Earth circumference ≈ 40,075 km
**Sub-scale:** 10,000 km (planetary diameter), 40,000 km (planetary circumference), 6,000 km (deep mantle depth)
**Context:** Continent-scale geography, planetary-spanning cultivation effects (Divine Sea Realm), global weather system scale, empire-spanning territorial governance, planetary formation array networks.
**Scale Description:** The tier of a world — the diameter of a habitable sphere, the span of an ocean, the reach of a cultivator whose soul sense covers an entire planet's surface.
**Example prose:** "The Divine Sea Realm patriarch's awareness stretched outward — a sphere of impossible perception twelve thousand kilometers across, centered on a man sitting in perfect stillness amid the ruins of what had been a city."

---

#### CMS Tier 5 — Orbital Scale
**Unit:** Astronomical Unit (AU) / light-minute / light-hour
**Reference:** 1 AU ≈ 149.6 million km ≈ 8.317 light-minutes; 1 light-minute ≈ 18 million km; 1 light-hour ≈ 1.08 billion km
**Sub-scale:** 1 light-second (~300,000 km; lunar distance), 1 light-minute (~18 million km; inner planetary scale), 1 AU (~150 million km; habitable zone orbital scale), 5 AU (inner giant planet orbits), 30 AU (outer planetary system edge)
**Context:** Planetary orbit operations, orbital station placement, moon-to-planet distances, intra-star-system transit, soul sense boundary for the most powerful cultivators, planetary defense systems.
**Scale Description:** The tier of a star's immediate family — from low orbit to the edge of the habitable zone. At this scale, light itself becomes a meaningful delay: a message sent to a station 1 AU away takes over eight minutes to arrive.
**Example prose:** "The orbital station sat at 2 AU from the primary star — just beyond the habitable zone — its sensor arrays sweeping the full inner system with resolution sharp enough to track individual ships at half an AU."

---

#### CMS Tier 6 — Deep System Scale
**Unit:** Light-day / light-week / light-month
**Reference:** 1 light-day ≈ 26 billion km; 1 light-week ≈ 182 billion km; 1 light-month ≈ 790 billion km (~5,300 AU); 1 light-year ≈ 63,241 AU
**Sub-scale:** 1 light-day (about 170 AU, the system edge), 1 light-week (outer system), 1 light-month (far outer system, Oort cloud analog region)
**Context:** Outer star system navigation, deep system mining and surveillance, Oort-equivalent cloud distances, maximum subluminal patrol range at Tier 7 technology, the outer fringe of a star's gravitational dominance zone.
**Scale Description:** The tier of the deep outer system — where worlds are ice giants and frozen dwarfs, where the star is a bright point rather than a disc, and where subluminal transit takes months rather than hours.
**Example prose:** "The patrol fleet had been out in the deep system — four light-days from the primary, in the scattered disc beyond the ice giants — when the anomaly hit their scanners."

---

#### CMS Tier 7 — Interstellar Scale
**Unit:** Light-year (ly)
**Reference:** 1 light-year ≈ 9.461 × 10¹² km ≈ 63,241 AU
**Sub-scale:** 0.5 ly (near-interstellar), 1–5 ly (neighboring star systems), 10–50 ly (near-neighborhood), 100 ly (local cluster boundary)
**Context:** Interstellar transit (FTL required for practical use), interstellar communication lag (pre-FTL), star-to-star separation, the distances between civilizations before they develop cluster-scale reach.
**Scale Description:** The tier that defines the fundamental isolation of civilization — the gap between stars. Four light-years to the nearest star. Even at FTL, these distances carry weight: supply lines stretch across light-years, battle plans account for light-year-scale travel times, and communication lags accumulate into years for pre-FTL empires.
**Example prose:** "The fleet had been in void-passage for three days crossing the 4.2 light-years between their home system and the neighboring star — still within the same sparse local cluster arm, and already the homesickness felt like a physical thing."

#### CMS Tier 8 — Near-Cluster Scale
**Unit:** Kilolight-year (kly) — low range
**Reference:** 1 kly = 1,000 light-years
**Sub-scale:** 10 ly – 100 ly (near-cluster interior), 100 ly – 500 ly (cluster diameter), 500 ly – 1 kly (cluster fringe)
**Context:** Star cluster navigation, civilization expansion within a local cluster (what Part 13 calls a Star Cluster location tier), strategic depth within a cluster-controlling civilization, long-range sensor coverage at Tier 10+ technology.
**Scale Description:** The tier of a star cluster — hundreds of star systems, hundreds of years of light travel between one end and the other. A civilization that can navigate freely at this scale has truly transcended planetary origin and become a cluster power.
**Example prose:** "The war had been fought across four hundred light-years of the cluster's core — the kind of campaign where each engagement was a separate strategic theater, governed by relay stations whose messages arrived months after the battle was decided."

#### CMS Tier 9 — Deep Cluster to Quadrant Scale
**Unit:** Kilolight-year (kly) — full range
**Reference:** 1 kly = 1,000 light-years; 1–10 kly covers deep cluster to galactic spiral arm section
**Sub-scale:** 1 kly (spiral arm sub-section), 5 kly (spiral arm segment), 10 kly (wide quadrant scale)
**Context:** Star Quadrant-scale political geography (Part 13), multi-cluster civilization territory, galactic arm navigation, strategic distances for civilizations beginning to operate at the galactic spiral arm level.
**Scale Description:** The tier of a galactic quadrant — the domain of civilizations that have expanded from a single cluster to control thousands of star systems across thousands of light-years. At this scale, governance requires FTL communication infrastructure; even FTL fleets take days to cross from one end to the other.
**Example prose:** "The Arc administration governed eight thousand light-years of the spiral arm's inner edge — a territory so vast that its frontier commanders made strategic decisions unilaterally because waiting for orders from the center took too long even at FTL-comm rates."

#### CMS Tier 10 — Galactic Scale
**Unit:** Kilolight-year (kly) — galactic range / Megalight-year (Mly) — sub-unit approaching
**Reference:** 1 kly to 100 kly covers full galactic scale; Milky Way diameter ≈ 105,700 ly ≈ 105.7 kly; galactic core ≈ 26,000 ly from Earth
**Sub-scale:** 25 kly (spiral arm length), 50 kly (half-galaxy), 100 kly (full galaxy diameter)
**Context:** Full Galaxy-tier civilization territory (Part 13 Galaxy level), galactic navigation, civilizations operating at the scale of an entire galaxy, the scale at which individual star systems become statistical background.
**Scale Description:** The tier of a full galaxy — a hundred thousand light-years from one edge to the other, hundreds of billions of star systems. At this scale, even a "local region" contains millions of inhabited worlds. Civilizations that operate here think in timescales measured in thousands of years and distances that dwarf anything previously encountered.
**Example prose:** "They controlled the galaxy's western spiral arm entirely — forty thousand light-years of territory, hundreds of millions of inhabited systems — and yet the rival power across the core was still out of reach without a journey that consumed more than a year even at their best FTL rating."

#### CMS Tier 11 — Galaxy Cluster Scale
**Unit:** Megalight-year (Mly)
**Reference:** 1 Mly = 1,000,000 light-years = 1,000 kly; Local Group diameter ≈ 10 Mly; Virgo Cluster ≈ 15 Mly across
**Sub-scale:** 0.5 Mly (galaxy-to-galaxy separation in a cluster), 1–5 Mly (compact cluster diameter), 5–20 Mly (extended cluster)
**Context:** Galaxy Cluster-tier civilization (Part 13 Galaxy Cluster level), navigation between galaxies, inter-galactic civilization operations, the separation between individual galaxies within a cluster.
**Scale Description:** The tier of the intergalactic void — the emptiness between galaxies. The distances here are so vast that even light takes millions of years to cross them. Civilizations operating at this scale have transcended individual galaxies entirely; they think of galaxies the way planetary civilizations think of continents.
**Example prose:** "The two galaxies were separated by barely three million light-years — close neighbors by cluster standards — yet the void between them had swallowed entire expedition fleets without a trace, and no civilization below the Galaxy Cluster tier had successfully maintained a supply line across it."

#### CMS Tier 12 — Galaxy Supercluster Scale
**Unit:** Megalight-year (Mly) — large range
**Reference:** 10 Mly – 1,000 Mly; Laniakea Supercluster ≈ 520 Mly across; Hercules-Corona Borealis Great Wall ≈ 10 Gly
**Sub-scale:** 10 Mly (cluster-to-cluster within supercluster), 100 Mly (supercluster diameter), 500 Mly (large supercluster complex)

At this scale, individual galaxies are specks; clusters are neighborhoods. Civilizations here exist in a state that mortals can barely comprehend as "civilization" — they are more like forces of nature with intentionality.
**Example prose:** "The supercluster they called home spanned over four hundred million light-years — a filament of matter and energy so vast that even their fastest emissaries took centuries to travel from one administrative cluster to another."

#### CMS Tier 13 — Universal Scale
**Unit:** Gigalight-year (Gly)
**Reference:** 1 Gly = 1,000,000,000 light-years = 1,000 Mly; Observable universe diameter ≈ 93 Gly; Hubble distance ≈ 13.8 Gly (age × c); Cosmic microwave background horizon ≈ 46.5 Gly

**Example prose:** "Their communication array spanned thirty gigalight-years — a network that, by any lesser civilization's standard, would constitute an empire unto itself. To them, it was the local subnet."

#### CMS Tier 14 — Trans-Universal / Multiverse Scale
**Unit:** Trans-universal (no fixed metric — expressed as "universe-lengths" or dimensional substrate separation)
**Reference:** Beyond standard physical measurement; expressed as N × [observable universe diameters] or as dimensional separation coefficients
**Sub-scale:** Not applicable — trans-universal distance is topological, not spatial in the conventional sense
**Context:** Multiverse navigation, separation between distinct universes within the Multiverse (Part 13 Multiverse level), dimensional substrate distance, the scale of entities and civilizations operating between universes.
**Scale Description:** The tier that defies description in metric terms. The distance between universes is not a number of light-years — it is a topological relationship between distinct spacetime manifolds. Entities that navigate here do not travel through space; they navigate the structure of existence itself.
**Example prose:** "Crossing between universes was not a journey measured in light-years. It was a transition measured in the depth of one's understanding of what existence meant — and most who attempted it without that understanding ceased to exist in a way that could not be undone."

#### CMS Tier 15 — Primordial / Absolute Scale
**Unit:** Absolute — undefined in metric terms
**Reference:** The scale of The Primordium and Absolute Chaos; beyond measurement by any physical framework
**Sub-scale:** None — Primordial distance is the terminal tier of the CMS
**Context:** The Primordium and Absolute Chaos (Part 13 terminal tier), substrate-level existence, the scale of entities whose presence is not located in space but constitutes space. All Primordial-scale events require Part 28 (Creative Flexibility Law) authorization.
**Scale Description:** There is no measurement here. The Primordium does not have distances — it has gradients of becoming. Absolute Chaos does not have a diameter — it is the boundary condition within which all measurement becomes possible. To ask "how far" at this scale is to ask a category error.
**Example prose:** "The entity did not move toward them across a distance. It simply became present, in the way that gravity becomes present — not by traveling, but by being the kind of thing that alters the nature of the space around it."

### 2.4 — Unit Abbreviations and Combined Notation

**Standard abbreviations in prose and governance text:**
- mm = millimeter, cm = centimeter, m = meter, km = kilometer
- Mm = megameter (1,000 km), Gm = gigameter (1,000,000 km)
- AU = astronomical unit (~150 million km)
- ls = light-second, lm = light-minute, lh = light-hour, ld = light-day, lw = light-week
- ly = light-year, kly = kilolight-year, Mly = megalight-year, Gly = gigalight-year

**Combined notation for precision:**
- "2.3 AU from the primary star"
- "140 light-years deeper into the cluster"
- "the fleet advanced 500 km in under a second at the cultivator's pace"
- "the Sacred Ground's influence extended 80 km from the core, with detectable fringe effects at 200 km"

When rounding serves prose better, natural language approximations are valid: "a few light-years," "dozens of kilometers," "hundreds of AU." The CMS does not require mathematical precision in fiction — it requires scale clarity and internal consistency.

**Time units:** A standard hour is the ordinary hour of 3,600 seconds. Planetary time is not fixed: on a habitable planet a year runs 300 to 400 days and a day runs 20 to 30 standard hours, and Saha's day is 26 standard hours. Weeks, months and their names differ from planet to planet and are recorded in that planet's location ledger when the story goes there. *(Pointer: for Saha the year, the months, the week and their names are set in Part 41, Calendar Law, Area 2, Epoch, year and months, and Area 3, Weeks and weekdays, and are recorded in `ledger-worlds-calendar`; a day on Saha is a Saha day of 26 standard hours. The rule for every other planet is unchanged.)*

### 2.5 — Directional and Relative Notation

In-universe directional reference for navigational prose:
- **Coreward / Rimward:** toward or away from the galactic core
- **Spinward / Anti-spinward:** with or against the galaxy's rotation
- **Deepward / Shoreward:** toward the void between galaxies (deep) or toward a settled cluster (shore)
- **Above / Below the galactic plane:** relative to the galaxy's disc
- **Inward / Outward:** within a star system, toward or away from the star

These directional terms are used with CMS units: "140 light-years coreward," "500 km south and dropping toward the valley floor," "3 AU outward from the habitable zone."

## Area 3 — Canonical Distance Thresholds by Cosmological Scale

### 3.1 — Purpose

This Area establishes the canonical distance ranges within and between each level of Part 13's cosmological hierarchy. These ranges are the governance-level definitions — all narrative distance claims must be consistent with these ranges or require a documented explanation.

---

### 3.2 — Settlement Scale Distances (Village, Town, City, Capital)

A settlement's size is set by the Kingdom it stands in, not by the settlement's own class. The class band of the Kingdom (the three bands of Area 3.3: Km-1 to Km-5, Km-6 to Km-8, Km-9 to Km-10) gives the range, and the Kingdom's own territory diameter (Area 3.3) is the cap: no settlement is wider than the Kingdom or Empire that holds it. The figures below are the **built-up diameter**, the widest continuous extent of the inhabited and defended ground, from outermost wall or building to outermost wall or building, including the part of the settlement's formation array that covers it. A settlement outside the Kingdom column it belongs to is recorded with its reason in its ledger entry. A settlement that belongs to no Kingdom takes the column of the Kingdom or Empire whose territory holds it, or the column its ledger entry names.

### 4.2 — Soul Sense Range Modifiers

**Environmental Modifiers:**
| Ambient Energy Class | Range Modifier (soul sense range and formation array radius) |
|---|---|
| Class 0 (Void) | −80% (devastating suppression; survival itself is the concern) |
| Class 1 (Minimal) | −20% |
| Class 2 (Standard) | Standard (0% modifier) |
| Class 3 (Enriched) | +10% |
| Class 4 (Dense) | +20% |
| Class 5 (Saturated) | +35% |
| Class 6 (Vortex) | +50% |
| Class 7 (Sacred Ground) | +70%; qualitative improvement in resolution at all ranges |

**Active Suppression and Interference:**
| Condition | Soul Sense Range Modifier |
|---|---|
| Suppression formation active (Part 12 Tier 2+) | −50% within the formation |
| Void Zone proximity (within 1 km) | −60% |
| Active combat energy saturation (battlefield) | −20% (noise masking) |
| Competing high-realm soul sense (within range) | Variable; contested by Part 5 rules if in combat |
| Dimensional boundary present | −40% beyond the boundary |
| FTL space / hyperspace equivalent | Soul sense non-functional — dimensional substrate inaccessible |

**Soul Sense Modifier Stacking Rules (Rule 4.2.1):**
Multiple soul sense modifiers from different categories stack according to the following priority and floor rules:

1. **Hard suppression modifiers first:** The "Soul sense non-functional" condition (FTL space) is absolute — no other modifier interacts with it. If FTL applies, the result is always non-functional regardless of enhancements.

2. **Category stacking:** Modifiers from different categories (Environmental, Active Suppression/Interference, and enhancements) stack additively. A cultivator in a Class 6 Vortex environment (+50%) inside an active suppression formation (−50%) has 0% net modification from standard (the two effects cancel).

3. **Maximum suppression floor:** Stacked suppression modifiers cannot reduce soul sense range below **5% of the cultivator's standard range at their realm** — soul sense retains minimal residual function at this floor. This floor is the "bare existence sense" that all but completely suppressed soul cultivators retain. The only exceptions are hard suppression (FTL space) and artificially constructed absolute suppression formations at Tier 4+ specifically designed to eliminate all soul sense — those are below-floor exceptions that must be explicitly designated.

4. **Enhancement cap:** Stacked enhancement modifiers from multiple simultaneous positive sources (e.g., Sacred Ground + high ambient energy zone + exceptional personal soul rung) are capped at **+100% above standard** — effective doubling of baseline range. Higher soul rungs already incorporate advanced range; stacked environmental bonuses cannot multiply a cultivator's range indefinitely.

5. **Competing high-realm soul sense resolution:** When two soul sense fields overlap in contested space (Part 5 combat), the higher soul rung cultivator projects normally; the lower-rung cultivator experiences a −15% to −40% interference penalty scaled to the soul rung gap (≤2 rung gap: −15%; 3–5 rung gap: −25%; >5 rung gap: −40%). This is not a mutual modifier — only the lower-rung cultivator's effective range is reduced.

6. **Example stacking:** A Xiantian cultivator (standard range ~80 km at True God) in a battlefield with active combat saturation (−20%), near a Void Zone (−60%), and inside a Tier 2 suppression formation (−50%) would calculate: 80 km × (1 − 0.20 − 0.60 − 0.50) = 80 km × (−0.30) → floor applies → 80 km × 0.05 = 4 km residual soul sense. The cultivator is severely limited but not blind.

## Area 8 — Distance Narration Standards

### 8.1 — The Five Narration Principles

**Narration Principle N14-1 — Use Scale-Appropriate Units:**
Every distance statement in prose uses the CMS unit appropriate to the scale being described. Authors do not mix scales without narrative purpose. Combat scenes use meters and kilometers. Regional descriptions use hundreds of kilometers to megameters. Interstellar descriptions use light-years and kilolight-years. Cosmological descriptions use megalight-years and gigalight-years.

**Narration Principle N14-2 — Render Distance Through Character Perception:**
Distance is rendered through the most capable observer's perception in any scene. A Grade 10 cultivator does not describe a threat as "far away" if it is within 300 km — that is their active soul sense range. A Mortal Tier 1 character may describe the same distance as "impossibly distant." The narrative voice matches the character's perceptual capability.

**Narration Principle N14-3 — Distance Implies Travel Time:**
Every distance description that implies action — "he crossed the valley," "the fleet advanced," "the technique reached" — must be consistent with the travel law of Part 15. Authors use distance and time together: "three kilometers in a breath at Void God pace" or "a fleet that took weeks to cross the four light-years between systems." Distance without implied time is static; action requires both.

**Narration Principle N14-4 — Technique Range Must Match Realm:**
A technique that strikes a target must be achievable at the described distance per Area 4.3's technique range table. A Warrior-level cultivator cannot strike a target two kilometers away with a direct technique. Authors check Area 4.3 before staging technique range in combat.

**Narration Principle N14-5 — Environmental Distance Modifiers Are Active:**
If a scene is set in any ambient energy environment other than Class 2, the soul sense range modifiers from Area 4.2 are active. If a scene involves a Tier 11 civilization, the sensor ranges from Area 5.1 are active. These modifiers cannot be selectively ignored — they are world-law, not optional flavor.

---

### 8.2 — Distance Consistency Between Books

When writing a sequel or parallel book set at the same location or scale:
1. Use the same CMS units for the same locations
2. Check Part 14 Area 7.3 for Sacred Ground influence zones if the scene is near a previously established Sacred Ground
3. Check Part 5 battlefield spatial constraint tables for any combat scene
4. Check Part 3 Area 20 for soul sense footprint consistency with the cultivator's established realm
5. Check Part 8 technology tier for sensor and weapon range consistency

### 8.4 — Named-Place Distances Come From Registry B

**Rule 8.4.1 — Cite the Row.**
When a Chapter Spec Place field, a ledger entry or an Area states a distance or a journey time between two named places, it takes the figure from the Registry B row in `ledger-worlds-distance/_index.md` for that pair. Where no row exists, the writer adds the legs of the nearest rows together and records the new pair as a row at Gate 2.

**Rule 8.4.2 — Sketch Rows Give Way to the Written Area.**
A row marked SKETCH is a seed, not a fact. The first Area that states the distance or time may confirm it or change it; the row is then updated in the same Gate 2, the SKETCH flag is removed on confirmation, and every file that quoted the old figure is corrected. A row without the SKETCH flag is canon and is changed only by a formal amendment.

**Rule 8.4.3 — One Speed per Figure.**
A journey time names the speed it was worked at (the standard on-foot, road or airship speed of rows DIST-S1 to DIST-S3, or a stated other). Two scenes that give different times for the same road must differ in speed, not in distance.

## Area 9 — Special Distance Cases

### 9.1 — FTL Distance Governance

Faster-than-light travel (Part 10, Area 5; Part 15 governing the mechanics) requires the following distance-governance rules:

**Rule P14-FTL-1 — FTL Compresses Transit Time, Not Distance:**
A light-year is still a light-year in FTL space. The canonical distances of Area 3 remain fixed regardless of transit method. FTL changes the time-to-distance ratio; it does not change the distance itself.

**Rule P14-FTL-2 — FTL Navigation Requires Distance Knowledge:**
FTL transit requires knowing the destination distance and direction. Navigation errors proportional to distance are a valid narrative mechanic — a misjudged FTL jump by 1 AU per light-year (Tier 8, Part 15 Area 14.2) over 500 light-years deposits the navigator 500 AU off target at destination. Part 15 governs the jump mechanics; Part 14 governs the distance that must be accurately measured for successful transit.

**Rule P14-FTL-3 — FTL Space Has Null Cultivation Distance:**
While in FTL transit (hyperspace equivalent), soul sense does not function across normal spatial dimensions (see Area 4.2 modifier table). A cultivator in FTL transit cannot extend soul sense to external space. This is a hard rule, not a modifier.

**Rule P14-FTL-4 — Emergence Distance:**
FTL systems exit at a defined emergence point. The accuracy of the emergence point is a function of navigation technology tier. Emergence accuracy is the Part 15 Area 14.2 table (Tier 9 ±0.1 AU at 1 ly; Tier 11 ±0.001 AU at 1 ly). Part 15 governs the specific emergence mechanics.

### 9.2 — Dimensional Shortcuts and Physical Distance

When a dimensional rift, portal, or spatial fold bridges two physical locations:
- The physical distances of both origin and destination remain unchanged
- The traversal time through the dimensional pathway is governed by Part 15
- The dimensional pathway itself has no standard CMS distance — it is a dimensional bridge
- Destroying a dimensional pathway restores the full physical distance between the connected locations

**Rule P14-DIM-1 — Dimensional Bridges Do Not Reduce Canonical Distance:**
A portal network connecting a Village to a galactic core capital does not make them adjacent in canonical distance — it makes them adjacent in travel access. The world-building of who controls the portal, what it costs to use, and what blocking it means in strategic terms must account for the canonical distance being bridged.

### 9.3 — Distance and Sacred Ground Interaction

Sacred Grounds do not modify physical distances. A Sacred Ground's elevated ambient energy extends the effective soul sense range within and around it (Area 4.2 Class 7 modifier), but it does not compress or expand physical distances. An object 50 km from a Sacred Ground is still 50 km away regardless of the Sacred Ground's grade.

**Rule P14-SG-1 — Elevated Ambient Energy in Sacred Grounds:**
The Class 7 ambient energy modifier (+70% soul sense range, +70% formation array radius) applies within the core effect zone of a Sacred Ground (Area 7.3). In the fringe influence zone the modifier tapers from the Class 6 value (+50%) at the inner edge to the Class 3 value (+10%) at the outer edge. This modifier is centered on the Sacred Ground's core zone boundary, not on individual practitioners within it. Practitioners who step outside the fringe influence zone (Area 7.3) lose the modifier.

### 9.5 — Dimensional Distance Governance

> **Governing principle:** Dimensional distance — the spatial relationship between different dimensional layers, planes, or cross-dimensional positions — operates by a different framework than physical CMS distance. This Area establishes the canonical rules for how distance functions across dimensional boundaries.

**Rule P14-DIM-2 — Dimensional Layers Are Not at CMS Distance from Each Other:**
The dimensional layers of the Starward Saga universe (physical dimension, soul-cultivation metaphysical layer, dimensional pockets/storage spaces, the spatial layer accessible to Heaven Treading cultivators) are not "above," "below," or "x kilometers away from" the physical dimension. They are co-present but orthogonal — occupying the same physical coordinates as the physical world but inaccessible through standard spatial movement. There is no CMS distance between a physical location and its corresponding position in the soul-cultivation metaphysical layer. A cultivator "reaching into" the metaphysical layer does not travel a distance; they shift their interaction mode.

**Rule P14-DIM-3 — Soul Sense Across Dimensional Layers:**
When a cultivator's soul sense crosses a dimensional boundary (the −40% modifier in Area 4.2 applies), the effective range is calculated after applying the modifier to the cultivator's standard range. Beyond the −40% modifier, cross-dimensional soul sense operates by the same range-and-resolution rules as standard soul sense — the boundary is a filter, not a wall. Soul sense can detect entities and formations in adjacent dimensional layers; the modifier reflects the attenuating effect of the dimensional boundary on perception clarity and range.

**Rule P14-DIM-4 — Cross-Dimensional Technique Projection:**
Named Moves (Part 6) that project effects across a dimensional boundary are subject to the following additional range constraint: maximum cross-dimensional technique range is **50% of the cultivator's standard maximum technique range** at their realm (Area 4.3), regardless of the technique's normal range at that cultivation level. This 50% cap stacks with all other range reductions (soul sense dependency, combat energy attenuation). Only techniques specifically designed for cross-dimensional action (Soul attacks traveling through the metaphysical layer — governed as an exception in Area 4.3) are exempt from this cap; standard Named Moves attempting cross-dimensional projection are not exempt.

**Rule P14-DIM-5 — Dimensional Pocket Governance:**
Dimensional storage spaces (spatial artifacts — Part 12 Area 9.2) exist at a dimensional "depth" that is a function of the artifact's tier: Tier 1 storage artifacts access a shallow dimensional pocket with limited internal volume; Tier 4–5 access deep dimensional pockets with vast internal volume. The internal volume of a dimensional storage pocket is not CMS-measurable from the physical world — it has its own internal spatial extent, which the artifact's tier governs. However, the physical size of the artifact (its external container in the physical world) is a CMS-measurable object like any other.

**Rule P14-DIM-6 — Heaven Treading Spatial Dimension:**
Heaven Treading (Part 15, Area 6.3 — travel mechanics) involves brief traversal of a spatial layer adjacent to the physical dimension. The distance bridged by a single Heaven Treading step is the canonical CMS distance between origin and destination — the cultivator traverses that distance through the spatial layer rather than through physical space. The spatial layer traversal itself takes seconds to minutes (Part 15 Rule 6.3.1) but the distance bridged is real CMS distance (P14-DIM-1 still applies). Dimensional pathway mechanics (Part 15) govern the traversal cost and limits; Part 14 governs the canonical distance being bridged.

**Dimensional Distance Summary Table:**

| Dimensional Scenario | Governing Distance Rule |
|---|---|
| Physical location to adjacent dimensional layer | No CMS distance — they are co-present (P14-DIM-2) |
| Soul sense crossing dimensional boundary | Standard range × 60% (−40% modifier); modifier from Area 4.2 |
| Named Move projected cross-dimensionally | Standard range × 50% cap (P14-DIM-4) |
| Internal volume of dimensional storage pocket | Governed by artifact Tier; not CMS-measurable from outside (P14-DIM-5) |
| Distance bridged by dimensional portal/rift | Equal to canonical CMS distance between connected points (P14-DIM-1) |
| Distance bridged by Heaven Treading step | Equal to canonical CMS distance between origin and destination (P14-DIM-6) |

## Area 16 — Author Quick Reference

### 16.1 — The Six Distance Questions Before Writing a Scene

Before staging any scene involving distance-dependent interactions:

**Q1: What scale tier is this scene at?**
→ Choose the appropriate CMS unit (Area 2.2). Don't mix tiers without narrative purpose.

**Q2: What is the most capable perceiver's soul sense or sensor range?**
→ Check Area 4 (cultivation) or Area 5 (technology). Apply ambient modifiers from Area 4.2.

**Q3: Can the technique / weapon / effect reach the stated distance?**
→ Check Area 4.3 (cultivation technique range) or Area 5.3 (technology weapon range). If not, the interaction is canon-invalid.

**Q4: If crossing a large distance, what does FTL or dimensional path change?**
→ Apply P14-FTL-1 (FTL changes time, not distance) and P14-DIM-1 (dimensional bridges don't change canonical distance).

**Q5: Are any distance modifiers active?**
→ Check ambient energy class (Area 4.2 modifier table); check if in FTL space (soul sense null); check if dimensional boundary is present.

**Q6: Is the narrated distance internally consistent with the scene's scale?**
→ Check Area 8.3 common errors. A cultivator's soul sense range must match their realm per Area 4.1.

### 16.2 — Five Governance Scenarios

**Scenario A: Cultivator claims to sense an enemy at an extreme distance**
1. Check the cultivator's realm → Area 4.1 maximum soul sense range
2. Apply ambient energy class modifier → Area 4.2
3. Check for suppression formations, void zones, or dimensional boundaries → Area 4.2
4. If the stated distance exceeds modified max range: the cultivator cannot sense the target; this must be noted in prose or explained by a special technique (Part 6)

**Scenario B: Formation array used to protect an entire planet**
1. Tier 4 Formation Array max radius = planetary scale → Area 6.1 (confirmed valid)
2. Optimal CPM and Sacred Ground placement can achieve this upper tier → also Area 6.1
3. Effect radius extends to planetary scale with maximum ambient energy and investment
4. Part 12 governs the array construction; Part 14 confirms the spatial coverage is valid

**Scenario C: FTL fleet travels across a Star Cluster**
1. A typical Star Cluster spans ~100–500 light-years → Area 3.5
2. In CMS terms: expressed directly as "three hundred light-years"
3. FTL at Tier 10 standard → transit time governed by Part 15 Area 11.3
4. P14-FTL-1 applies: the light-year distance is unchanged; only transit time is affected
5. Soul sense is null for the cultivators aboard during FTL → P14-FTL-3

**Scenario D: Sacred Ground's influence felt from a settlement nearby**
1. Identify the Sacred Ground's S-Grade → Part 13 Area 9
2. Check Part 14 Area 7.3: core effect zone and fringe influence zone for that grade
3. If the settlement is within the fringe zone: Core Formation+ cultivators passively detect the enhanced ambient energy; lower realm cultivators require active soul sense
4. If beyond the fringe zone: no ambient effect reaches the settlement

**Scenario E: Galactic-tier entity observes events across a full galaxy**
1. Tier 11+ technology sensor range: full galaxy ~100 kly (active), several hundred kly (passive) → Area 5.1
2. Divine Sea Realm cultivation soul sense: planetary scale only → Area 4.1
3. A galaxy-spanning entity without Tier 11+ technology cannot observe galaxy-scale distances through cultivation alone — soul sense has hard range limits
4. The gap between what cultivation perceives (planetary max) and what high-tier technology perceives (galactic scale) is one of the central asymmetries of the Starward Saga world
