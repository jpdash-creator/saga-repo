## Overview

Part 5 is the Starward Saga Canon's Cultivation Combat and Fight Law — the governing domain for every combat interaction at every scale, from a street fight between two cultivators to an annihilation-level conflict between divine-tier civilizations. Part 5 exists so that escalation is never arbitrary, never emotionally driven by narration alone, and never destabilizing to canon structure: combat must feel intense and violent, but must remain mechanically lawful. Part 5 governs initiation, force legitimacy, mutual hostility, escalation tiers, engagement mutuality, intensity containment, third-party interference, proxy and alliance rules, battlefield constraints, retreat, victory/defeat/draw/surrender, post-combat obligations, leader death, civil war, and combat resolution. It does not grant power, alter cultivation advancement (Part 3), define technique taxonomy (Part 6), govern soul combat mechanics (Part 4), or format combat prose (Part 2).

From its foundational mandate (Area 1), Part 5 builds outward across 16 Areas: the ten governance areas index (Area 2); the nine Immutable Principles that no mechanism below a formal [CANON AMENDMENT] can override (Area 3); the Force Legitimacy framework that decides whether a violent act is combat, assault, assassination, collateral force, or power accident, and who holds protected status (Area 4); the seven-tier escalation classification system E1–E7 with its per-tier parameters, escalation triggers, and the parallel Formal Duel Protocol (Area 5); the engagement entry conditions and mutuality law that mark when combat formally begins (Area 6); the per-tier intensity ceilings, the Proportional Force Rule, escalation violations, and the soul technique escalation table (Area 7); the classification and governance of everyone beyond the primary combatants — observers, rescuers, opportunists, proxies, ambush elements, and alliance activation (Area 8); the spatial footprint of combat by Heavenly Scale Grade, protected zones, battlefield declaration, and cross-dimensional combat (Area 9); the stabilization of multi-faction chaos (Area 10); command succession and the Leaderless State when a leader dies in war (Area 11); termination triggers, victory and defeat classifications, draws, the Surrender Protocol, and post-combat obligations (Area 12); the Civil War doctrine for internal conflicts at E5 and above (Area 13); the three legitimate forms of leaving combat and the four pursuit limits (Area 14); the narrative-calibration guidance for keeping escalation coherent with the story's current in-world scale (Area 15); and the craft guidance for narrating a battle through focal combats and one wide shot (Area 16).

This file is the single authority for the whole of Part 5; the Cross-References table and Governing Authority statement stand in Appendix A and Appendix B.

## Area 1 — Foundational Principles and Governing Mandate

### 1.1 — The Purpose of Part 5

Part 5 exists to:

1. Provide a deterministic structure for combat interaction at every scale from personal to civilizational.
2. Prevent uncontrolled escalation across conflict tiers by classifying all conflict into defined, governed categories.
3. Define what constitutes a fight, duel, skirmish, engagement, battle, campaign, war, or annihilation-level conflict.
4. Ensure combat respects all other Parts in the SSC Canon system simultaneously.
5. Ensure that combat mechanics remain consistent across: individual fights, faction wars, civil conflicts, proxy engagements, and chaotic multi-party battlefields.

Part 5 ensures that escalation is not arbitrary, not emotionally driven by narration alone, and not destabilizing to canon structure. Combat must feel intense and violent, but must remain mechanically lawful.

### 1.2 — What Part 5 Governs

Part 5 governs: Initiation of combat, Force legitimacy classification, Mutual hostility conditions, Escalation tier classification, Engagement recognition and mutuality, Intensity containment by tier, Third-party interference rules, Proxy and alliance combat rules, Battlefield spatial and cosmological constraints, Retreat mechanics, Tactical reset, Victory conditions, Defeat classification, Draw conditions, Surrender protocols, Post-combat obligations, Leader death and command continuity, Multi-faction battlefield stabilization, Civil war conditions, Proxy warfare escalation, Combat interruption, and Combat resolution.

### 1.3 — What Part 5 Does NOT Govern

Part 5 does NOT: Grant power to any character, Accelerate or alter cultivation advancement, Change the cosmological structure of the world, Modify narrative importance of any event or character, Alter identity ownership or faction affiliation, Define technique classifications or move taxonomy (that is Part 6), Define power comparison outcomes (that is Part 3), Define technology tiers or technology combat output (that is Part 8; technology-involved combat is adjudicated under this Part using the cultivation-equivalent output in Part 8 Area 8.2, and a conflict that holds more than one kind of force follows Area 7.7), Define soul combat mechanics (that is Part 4), Define prose formatting of combat scenes (that is Part 2), Override any rule that falls within another Part's domain.

Part 5 operates strictly inside its domain boundaries.

## Area 2 — Governance Areas Overview

The ten governance areas of Part 5 are:

**Governance Area 1 — Force Legitimacy (Area 4):**
Determines when violence is recognized as combat versus assault, assassination, or accident. Defines protected status, initiation conditions, and declaration doctrine.

**Governance Area 2 — Escalation Categorization (Area 5):**
Classifies all conflicts into seven deterministic escalation tiers (E1 through E7) plus the Formal Duel special classification. Defines the conditions under which each tier exists and how conflicts escalate between tiers.

**Governance Area 3 — Intensity Control (Area 7):**
Defines the upper and lower bounds of force expression permitted within each escalation tier. Governs proportional force, involuntary escalation, and escalation violations.

**Governance Area 4 — Engagement Recognition (Area 6):**
Determines when two or more parties are officially in combat — defining the moment of engagement entry, mutual hostility threshold, one-sided engagement conditions, and disengagement versus termination.

**Governance Area 5 — Third-Party Interference (Area 8):**
Defines the legitimacy of interruption, assistance, betrayal, proxy involvement, observer rights, and alliance activation in active combat.

**Governance Area 6 — Battlefield Containment (Area 9):**
Defines spatial and cosmological containment of combat events. Establishes protected zones, battlefield footprint by cultivation grade, cross-dimensional combat rules, and sacred ground interaction.

**Governance Area 7 — Termination Conditions (Area 12):**
Defines when combat ends mechanically. Classifies victory, defeat, draw, and surrender. Establishes post-combat obligations.

**Governance Area 8 — Authority Interaction During Combat (Area 11):**
Ensures faction structure, leadership succession, and command continuity function during combat. Governs leader death consequences, command transfer, and the leaderless faction state.

**Governance Area 9 — Multi-Actor Stabilization (Area 10):**
Prevents chaotic multi-faction escalation from breaking the deterministic structure. Defines chaos threshold, faction alignment tracking, opportunistic entry rules, temporary ceasefire protocols, and coalition formation.

**Governance Area 10 — Escalation Coherence Guidance (Area 15):**
Provides guidance for ensuring escalation tier classification feels coherent with the current in-world power and political scale. The arbitration framework in Area 15 helps authors assess whether a proposed conflict scope fits the story's current scale, and what narrative development may be needed before a larger-scale conflict can be earned.

Area 13 (Civil War and Internal Conflict) and Area 14 (Retreat, Withdrawal and Tactical Reset) sit outside these ten headings and carry their own rules. Area 16 (Writing a Battle) is craft guidance in the manner of Area 15; it is not a governance area and adds no mechanic.

## Area 3 — Immutable Principles

The following principles are immutable. They cannot be overridden by narrative intent, creative variance, faction authority, or any mechanism below a formal [CANON AMENDMENT] (`guardrails/commands/guardrail-command-law.md`).

**IP-1:** Combat is deterministic in mechanics. Every combat event has a mechanically defined structure. No combat event may exist without classification under this Part.

**IP-2:** Escalation must be categorized. No conflict may exist in an undefined or unclassified state. If a conflict cannot be immediately classified, it defaults to the lowest applicable tier until reclassification conditions are met.

**IP-3:** Combat cannot bypass cultivation advancement conditions. No combat outcome — victory, defeat, near-death, or breakthrough stress — may produce advancement that bypasses the methods and stability requirements defined in Part 3, Area 4. Combat may create breakthrough conditions; it does not override breakthrough requirements.

**IP-4:** Combat cannot override restrictions in Location Law (Part 13). Protected zones, sacred ground rules, and dimensional boundaries defined by Part 13 remain in full force during combat. A battle cannot "happen" in a protected zone by narrative fiat.

**IP-5:** Combat does not change narrative weight. The significance of a character or event is determined by their role and impact in the story. Combat outcomes do not grant or remove narrative weight — they produce consequences (Part 27) that may affect narrative weight through their downstream effects.

**IP-6:** Combat must remain mechanically interpretable even in chaotic battlefield conditions. When multiple factions, cultivation levels, and spatial scales converge, the deterministic structure of this Part must still be applicable. If it cannot be applied, the conflict must be narratively resolved through the chaos dissolution rules in Area 10.

**IP-7:** All combat actions are validated under the audit gate (`guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`) and Part 2. The audit gate governs the validity of all narrative output. Part 2 governs how combat is rendered in prose. Both remain active during all combat events.

**IP-8:** Escalation tier classification should be coherent with the current in-world power and political scale. Area 15 provides guidance for assessing escalation coherence and what narrative development may be needed before larger-scale conflicts can be earned within the story. Advisory: an out-of-scale tier is reported to the author with the Area 15.4 steps and does not block by itself.

**IP-9:** Combat intensity operates inside escalation tier boundaries. A higher-level cultivator engaging in a lower-tier conflict must operate at the intensity ceiling of that tier. Exceeding the tier's intensity ceiling constitutes an escalation violation with defined consequences.

## Area 4 — Combat Initiation Legitimacy

### 4.1 — The Legitimacy Principle

Not all violence is combat under this Part's governance. The SSC Canon recognizes distinct categories of violent interaction, each with different governance rules and consequence implications. The category of violence determines which laws apply — combat rules, assassination rules (Part 24), accident rules (Part 27), or combinations thereof.

A violent act is classified as **combat** only when it meets the recognition conditions in Section 4.3. Violence that does not meet these conditions is classified under one of the non-combat categories and governed accordingly. This classification has narrative consequences — a character who commits assault when they claim to be engaging in combat is operating under a false legitimacy frame, which has social, legal, and consequence implications per Parts 25, 22, and 27.

### 4.2 — Force Legitimacy Categories

**Category FC-1 — Recognized Combat:**
Both parties are consciously engaged, mutually aware of hostility, and physically capable of responding. Combat rules apply in full. Both parties bear equal standing under the rules of engagement. This is the standard and default combat category.

**Category FC-2 — Declared Strike:**
The initiating party announces their intent to engage before striking. The target has a window — however brief — to prepare, retreat, or respond. The announcement need not be formal: readying a weapon visibly, releasing aura as a challenge signal, or a shouted warning all qualify. A Declared Strike becomes Recognized Combat the moment the target responds or the strike lands.

**Category FC-3 — Defensive Response:**
A response to a hostile action directed at oneself or one's recognized charges. Defensive Response is always categorized as Recognized Combat for the defending party, regardless of the initiating party's legitimacy status. A target of an assassination attempt may respond with full combat force even if the initiator's action was not recognized combat.

**Category FC-4 — Ambush / Surprise Attack:**
The initiating party achieves operational surprise — the target has no advance awareness of hostile intent. An ambush is tactically legitimate in combat culture but carries cultural and moral weight (Part 25). The moment the ambushed party becomes aware and responds, the engagement becomes Recognized Combat. Note: ambush on a protected person or in a protected zone is not combat — it is assault or assassination regardless of the power level of the parties.

**Category FC-5 — Assassination:**
Targeted killing with no combat engagement — the attacker's intent is to eliminate the target without allowing engagement. Assassination is classified separately from combat and is governed by Part 24 (Information and Secrecy Law) for information mechanics and by Part 27 (Consequence Law) for outcome management. Part 5 governs assassination only in the following respects: a target who survives an assassination attempt may respond under FC-3 (Defensive Response); an assassination that fails and becomes an engagement is reclassified as FC-4 (Ambush) at the moment of mutual awareness.

**Category FC-6 — Collateral Force:**
Power discharge that damages non-combatants or non-combat zones as a consequence of combat. Collateral Force is not combat directed at the non-combatants — it is the spatial consequence of combat actions. Collateral Force is governed by Area 9 (Battlefield Containment) and creates consequence obligations under Part 27. Cultivators are responsible for their Collateral Force regardless of intent.

**Category FC-7 — Power Accident:**
Unintended energy discharge — breakthrough states, Qi Deviation, Core instability, or power loss control that produces destructive force without combative intent. Power Accidents are not classified as combat. They may produce consequences identical to combat damage, but the absence of combat intent removes the initiating party from combat governance and places them under Part 27 alone. A Power Accident does not create an engagement — but it may trigger a Defensive Response (FC-3) from those who perceive it as an attack.

**Non-person adversary (beast, construct, autonomous system):** FC-1 applies to the person; CR-2 and the protected-status rules do not apply to the adversary; classify at E1 unless the counts in Area 5 say otherwise.

### 4.3 — Combat Recognition Conditions

For a violent interaction to be recognized as combat under this Part, **all three** of the following conditions must be met:

**Condition CR-1 — Capability of Engagement:**
Both parties (or at minimum the initiating party) must be conscious, physically capable of combat action, and not in a recognized protected status at the time of initiation. A sleeping cultivator cannot initiate combat. A cultivator cannot be engaged in combat once a valid surrender has taken effect under Area 12.5 (Post-Surrender Protections; violation of Area 12 surrender rules). A cultivator in mid-breakthrough cannot meaningfully engage in combat (they may emit power — that is Power Accident, not combat).

**Condition CR-2 — Mutual Awareness Threshold:**
At least one party must be aware of the hostile intent of the other. This condition is met by: overt hostile action by either party, aura release with aggressive signature, a formal or informal declaration of intent, or the ambush moment of detection (even one-sided awareness satisfies the threshold because the aware party is now engaged). Purely unconscious collateral damage from an unaware cultivator does not meet this condition.

**Condition CR-3 — Overt Hostile Action:**
At least one party must have taken a hostile action — defined as any physical, energetic, or soul-based action directed toward causing harm to the other party. Positioning, preparation, and aura assessment are not overt hostile actions unless the aura release constitutes a challenge declaration (see Section 4.4). The first technique activation, weapon draw with hostile intent, or physical strike constitutes the overt hostile action threshold.

### 4.4 — Pre-Combat Declaration Doctrine

A cultivator may choose to declare combat intent before taking overt action. This is the norm in cultivation society for fights between recognized cultivators of comparable standing. Declaration forms include:

- **Aura Release Declaration:** Releasing one's full aura signature directed at a specific target is universally recognized in cultivation society as a combat declaration.
- **Formal Verbal Challenge:** An explicit statement of intent to fight ("I challenge you," "Draw your weapon," "This ends now").
- **Weapon Presentation:** Openly drawing a weapon and directing it toward a specific target.
- **Cultivation Technique Activation with Visible Charge:** Activating a technique in preparation (charging energy visibly) before releasing it.

Declaration is not required for combat to be valid — the Ambush classification (FC-4) demonstrates that surprise engagement is legitimate. However, declaration affects the cultural and social standing of the initiating party (Part 25) and may affect the severity of consequences under Part 27 depending on whether the situation warranted silence or whether declaration could have been reasonably expected.

### 4.5 — Protected Status

The following categories of individuals cannot be engaged in combat without violating Force Legitimacy — attacks on protected individuals are classified as assault or assassination regardless of the attacker's intent to call it combat:

**PS-1 — Non-Cultivator Civilians in Non-Declared Combat Zones:**
An ordinary person who has taken no hostile action and is not in a zone declared as a battlefield under Area 9 protocols is protected. A cultivator who attacks such a person is committing assault, not engaging in combat.

**PS-2 — Formally Surrendered Combatants:**
A combatant is under protected status once a valid surrender has taken effect under Area 12.5 (Post-Surrender Protections). Further violence against a surrendered combatant is assault, not combat.

**PS-3 — Individuals Under Recognized Truce:**
A truce is a formal or mutually recognized temporary cessation of hostilities. Attacking during a truce is a Truce Violation — classified as assault and carrying maximum consequence weight under Part 27. Truce violations are among the most severe breaches in cultivation society (Part 25).

**PS-4 — Diplomatic Figures Under Recognized Diplomatic Status:**
Envoys, ambassadors, and negotiators operating under recognized diplomatic function are protected from combat engagement while their diplomatic status is active. Attacking a diplomat is not combat — it is a declaration of absolute hostility against the represented party and is treated as an act of war (the represented party may at once declare E6; until it does, the incident stays at its own tier) regardless of the power level of the attacker.

**PS-5 — Children Below Cultivation Age:**
Those who have not yet entered cultivation and who are not directly participating in combat as combatants retain protected status regardless of the conflict tier. Child soldiers forced into combat do not lose protection retroactively — the culpability transfers to the party that conscripted them.

**PS-6 — Cultivators in Medical Status:**
A cultivator who is receiving active treatment for wounds in a recognized treatment zone (Area 9.3) retains protected status while in that zone. Attacking a treatment zone is not combat — it is an atrocity classified under Part 27 at maximum severity.

### 4.6 — Initiation During Protected Periods

Violence initiated during protected periods — truces, surrender windows, negotiations, diplomatic visits — is never classified as combat under this Part. It is always assault or assassination and carries the full weight of consequence law (Part 27) without the mechanical protections that combat classification provides.

A cultivator who is attacked during a protected period may respond with full combat force under FC-3 (Defensive Response). Their response does not constitute a truce violation — the violation belongs entirely to the initiating party.

## Area 5 — Escalation Tier Classification System

### 5.1 — Overview

All conflicts in the Starward Saga are classified into one of seven escalation tiers (E1 through E7). This classification is not cosmetic — it determines the intensity ceiling, third-party rules, termination conditions, and battlefield containment requirements that apply to the conflict. The tier reflects the actual scale and nature of the conflict, not the power of the participants.

**Critical Principle:** Escalation tier and cultivation level are independent variables. A fight between two Divine Realm cultivators can be classified as E1 (Personal Combat) if it meets the E1 parameters. A war between Mortal Tier 2 factions can be E6 if it meets the E6 parameters. Tier classification is based on participant count, scope, duration, faction commitment, and stated objectives — not on the power level of those involved.

Whether the levels of the two sides make a whole engagement plausible, and how a lower side may lawfully hold, delay or beat a higher one, is checked by Part 44 (`cultivation-battle-plausibility-law`, Canon Amendment 2026-10-17; Gate 1 Check #23). Part 44 changes nothing in this Part.

The **Formal Duel** is a special combat classification that exists alongside the escalation tier system. It can occur within any tier (most commonly E1) but carries its own governance rules independent of tier.

### 5.2 — The Seven Escalation Tiers

---

#### E1 — Personal Combat

**Participant Count:** 2 to 10 combatants total
**Spatial Scope:** Immediate personal space; single location
**Duration:** Seconds to hours
**Nature:** Personal grievance, survival response, honor conflict, territorial dispute at personal scale, criminal encounter
**Faction Involvement:** Participants may represent factions but no formal faction-level commitment is required or implied
**Objectives:** Personal in nature — defeating, capturing, driving away, or killing specific individuals
**Power Expression Ceiling:** All cultivation levels may participate; intensity must remain proportional to the immediate combat space (see Area 7)
**Collateral Obligation:** Minimize collateral force to immediate combat zone; no acceptable civilian casualties beyond incidental
**Initiation Legitimacy Requirements:** Standard conditions under Area 4.3
**Examples:** A street fight between two cultivators, a survival response to an ambush, a disciple conflict over a resource, a confrontation in a marketplace

**E1 Escalation Trigger to E2:** Participant count grows beyond 10 in total through entry of additional parties, OR the conflict relocates while maintaining active engagement, OR duration extends with sustained hostile intent across multiple locations.

---

#### E2 — Skirmish

**Participant Count:** 2 to 50 combatants total
**Spatial Scope:** Localized area; single or closely adjacent locations
**Duration:** Hours to days
**Nature:** Contested local resource, border tension, patrol engagement, ambush response with reinforcement, local territorial claim
**Faction Involvement:** Possible but unofficial; participants may represent factions without their factions having formally committed forces
**Objectives:** Tactically defined — control of a specific position, elimination of a specific group, assertion of territorial presence
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Participants fight to their individual capability within the spatial footprint constraint; no deliberate strategic-level destruction intended
**Collateral Obligation:** Active measures to limit collateral force to the immediate engagement zone; civilian evacuation of the immediate area is expected if time permits
**Examples:** Border patrol clash, rival sect disciples fighting over a sacred spring, a caravan defense against a bandit group, a hunting party ambush

**E2 Escalation Trigger to E3:** Participant count grows beyond 50 in total, OR faction leadership formally commits units to the engagement, OR the conflict persists beyond 48 hours with continuous hostile intent.

---

#### E3 — Engagement

**Participant Count:** 50 to 500 combatants
**Spatial Scope:** Single location or tightly connected multi-site; recognizable front lines possible
**Duration:** Days to weeks
**Nature:** Contested territory, punitive strike, resource seizure, systematic elimination of opposition presence in an area
**Faction Involvement:** Recognized faction participation; mid-tier leaders directly commit units; the faction is aware of and directing the engagement
**Objectives:** Tactical objectives with strategic implications — control of a location, destruction of an opposing formation, seizure of resources
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Strategic-scale destruction permitted within the declared engagement zone; civilian displacement is an expected consequence and must be factored into pre-engagement planning
**Battlefield Declaration Requirement:** Formal or informal designation of the engagement zone is required under Area 9 before major actions begin
**Collateral Obligation:** Civilian areas outside the declared engagement zone must be protected
**Examples:** Sect occupation of contested territory, a city guard response to a criminal organization, military enforcement operation in a rebellious district

**E3 Escalation Trigger to E4:** Participant count grows beyond 500, OR the conflict develops strategic objectives that require sustained multi-week prosecution, OR top-tier faction leadership directly assumes command of forces.

---

#### E4 — Battle

**Participant Count:** 500 to 50,000 combatants
**Spatial Scope:** Regional; multiple positions, established front lines, supply considerations
**Duration:** Days to months for a single battle; a battle may be one event within an E5 Campaign
**Nature:** Major strategic confrontation, decisive territorial contest, large-scale elimination of opposing military capacity
**Faction Involvement:** Major faction commitment; leadership-level decision to prosecute the battle; the battle represents a significant resource and personnel commitment
**Objectives:** Strategic — seizing or defending territory, destroying a significant portion of an opposing faction's military capacity, achieving a decisive blow that alters the strategic situation
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Theater-scale destruction capabilities permitted within designated battlefield; no attacks on non-combat population centers outside the military objective
**Battlefield Declaration Requirement:** Full battlefield declaration protocol under Area 9 required; protected zones must be formally identified before engagement
**Command Structure Requirement:** At least one side normally has an identifiable command hierarchy; a larger force with no command hierarchy is still E4 and is handled under the Chaos Threshold of Area 10
**Examples:** Major sect war battles, kingdom-versus-kingdom confrontations, large-scale military expeditions

**E4 Escalation Trigger to E5:** Multiple battles under unified strategic intent occur, OR the conflict develops into a sustained multi-month prosecution with multiple fronts, OR the conflict encompasses a regional strategic theater.

---

#### E5 — Campaign

**Participant Count:** Defined by constituent battles (no fixed cap; effectively unlimited within campaign scope)
**Spatial Scope:** Continental or multi-regional; multiple theaters of operation
**Duration:** Months to years
**Nature:** Systematic strategic conquest, sustained defensive campaign, prosecuted cause over multiple battles and operational theaters
**Faction Involvement:** Complete faction mobilization; economy of scale considerations; entire institutional apparatus engaged
**Objectives:** Campaign-level — territorial conquest, regime change, systematic destruction of opposing faction's capacity to resist, protection of homeland against systematic invasion
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Any within the Phase ceiling
**Formal Recognition:** By E5, all parties involved recognize the state of active campaign; it is no longer ambiguous
**Examples:** Sect alliance wars, continental unification campaigns, divine realm cultivator-led conquest operations

**E5 Escalation Trigger to E6:** Formal war declaration between political entities, OR the conflict develops into existential competition between major political powers with no exit condition below total victory or total defeat.

---

#### E6 — War

**Participant Count:** Effectively unlimited; entire political entities engaged
**Spatial Scope:** Continental or multi-continental; affects entire populations
**Duration:** Years to generations
**Nature:** Existential competition for dominance, resources, ideology, or survival; involves every level of society, not just military forces
**Faction Involvement:** All available resources committed; war is the dominant fact of the political entity's existence during the conflict
**Objectives:** War-level — regime change, territorial conquest at civilizational scale, ideological victory, or destruction of the opposing entity's capacity to ever threaten again
**Power Expression Ceiling:** Any within Phase ceiling; divine realm combat may occur; no artificial intensity ceiling other than Phase constraints
**Formal Declaration Requirement:** E6 requires either an explicit formal declaration of war between recognized political entities OR an escalation of hostilities so total that formal declaration is simply recognition of existing reality
**Peace Requirement:** E6 can only be terminated by formal peace — a recognized agreement that ends the state of war. See Area 12 for termination conditions.
**Examples:** Civilization-spanning wars, divine realm power bloc conflicts, wars of extinction between major political entities

**E6 Escalation Trigger to E7:** One or more parties declare or demonstrate the intent to entirely eliminate the opposing entity's existence — not defeat but obliteration.

---

#### E7 — Annihilation Conflict

**Participant Count:** Unlimited
**Spatial Scope:** Unlimited; may affect cosmological structures
**Duration:** Unlimited
**Nature:** One or more parties seek the total elimination of the opposing entity — not military defeat, not territorial conquest, but the absolute end of the opposing force's existence
**Faction Involvement:** Existential commitment — every resource, every life, every asset is relevant
**Objectives:** Annihilation — the opposing entity ceases to exist in any meaningful form
**Power Expression Ceiling:** None under this Part; Phase ceiling (Part 30, P30-4) remains applicable
**Formal Classification Trigger:** A conflict is reclassified as E7 when: a party publicly declares intent to annihilate the opposing entity, OR actions taken make annihilation the only logical conclusion even without declaration, OR the conflict's scale has exceeded all possibility of conventional termination
**Termination:** An E7 conflict can only terminate through: total annihilation of one party, intervention by a power so overwhelming that further conflict is impossible, or a paradigm-shifting event that fundamentally eliminates the cause of the conflict
**De-escalation:** E7 cannot be voluntarily de-escalated by either party once classified. The conditions that created E7 cannot be un-created by declaration alone.
**Examples:** Genocidal campaigns backed by divine realm power, cosmological-scale conflicts between existential forces, wars of extinction between divine-tier civilizations

### 5.3 — Formal Duel Protocol (Special Classification)

A **Formal Duel** is a distinct combat classification that operates parallel to, not within, the escalation tier system. A Formal Duel is always personal in scale (two combatants or two small representative groups) and is governed by pre-agreed rules. It is most commonly, but not exclusively, classified at E1.

**Conditions for Formal Duel Recognition:**
1. A challenge is issued — explicitly, publicly, or through recognized cultural ritual (Part 25 governs the cultural forms by faction)
2. The challenge is accepted — the challenged party acknowledges the duel
3. Terms are established — victory conditions, power ceiling agreement if any, life/honor/wager stakes, and time/place
4. Witnesses are designated — at least one recognized witness per side, or a mutually recognized neutral witness
5. The duel proceeds under the established terms

**The Five Formal Duel Types:**

**FD-Type 1 — Life Duel:**
Fought to the death. Surrender is not an accepted termination. The duel ends only when one combatant is killed or so thoroughly defeated that death is imminent and inevitable. Life Duels are governed under the strictest honor codes — interruption by any party (including allies of the combatants) except a legitimate rescue (II-4) is a severe violation. Life Duels have the highest cultural significance and consequence weight.

**FD-Type 2 — Submission Duel:**
Fought until one party cannot continue fighting or formally submits. Submission may be physical (incapacitation, inability to rise) or explicit (spoken surrender, weapon cast down, aura fully withdrawn). This is the most common formal duel type. Victory requires clear, unambiguous incapacitation or submission — a cultivator who is injured but still capable of fighting has not yet submitted.

**FD-Type 3 — Honor Duel:**
Fought to demonstrate the superior skill, cultivation, or technique of one party over another. The goal is not to kill or incapacitate but to make the comparative quality unmistakable. An Honor Duel may be terminated when: one combatant acknowledges the other's demonstrated superiority, a recognized audience consensus declares a winner, or a designated judge calls the outcome. Honor Duels are the most culturally stylized form of duel.

**FD-Type 4 — Wager Duel:**
Stakes are agreed before combat — territory, resources, disciples, information, oaths, or any other agreed currency. Victory transfers the agreed stakes to the winner. The duel type (Submission, Honor, etc.) is agreed alongside the stakes. The Wager Duel is binding — the losing party's faction, sect, or recognized authority is obligated by the outcome.

**FD-Type 5 — Representative Duel:**
Two parties with a larger conflict resolve it through single combat between designated champions. The outcome of the representative duel binds both sides as if it were the outcome of the larger conflict. A Representative Duel requires: mutual agreement on the binding nature of the outcome, designation of champions by each side, and recognized witnesses to the agreement. A party that refuses to honor a Representative Duel's outcome retroactively escalates the conflict by one tier.

**Duel Invalidation Conditions:**
A formal duel is invalidated if: either party violates the agreed terms (fighting style, power ceiling, technique restrictions), the witness structure is compromised (witnesses removed or threatened), third-party interference occurs with the consent or direction of one party, or either party engages outside the declared duel space. Invalidation of a duel caused by one party is equivalent to that party's defeat under the original terms.

**The Right to Challenge:**
Any cultivator may formally challenge any other cultivator. Cultural norms (Part 25) govern whether a particular challenge is honorable or dishonorable by power differential. Challenging someone significantly weaker than oneself without proportional stakes is culturally dishonorable in most cultivation societies. The challenged party has the right to: accept under the proposed terms, propose modified terms, or refuse. Refusal carries cultural weight — in many cultivation societies, refusing a challenge is a form of concession. The specific cultural interpretation of refusal is governed by Part 25.

### 5.4 — Escalation Tier Determination Rules

**Rule 5.4.1 — Classification at Initiation:**
Every conflict is classified at its tier at the moment of initiation based on the immediately visible parameters: participant count, faction commitment level, stated objectives, and spatial scope. The classification may be revised as the conflict develops. When parameters point to different tiers, participant count and spatial scope set the floor; duration, faction commitment and objectives can only raise the tier.

**Rule 5.4.2 — Upward Revision:**
A conflict may be reclassified to a higher tier when the escalation trigger conditions for the current tier are met. Upward revision is automatic — once the trigger conditions are objectively met, the new tier applies regardless of whether either party formally acknowledges the escalation.

**Rule 5.4.3 — Downward Revision Prohibition:**
A conflict may not be reclassified to a lower tier without meeting the full termination conditions of the current tier. A war cannot become a skirmish by declaration alone. De-escalation requires meeting the termination conditions of the current tier and formally re-establishing a lower engagement level through agreement.

**Rule 5.4.4 — Retroactive Classification:**
Events that were not initially classified may be retroactively assigned a tier based on their actual parameters. Retroactive classification does not change the events themselves but establishes their canonical tier for consequence and cross-event consistency purposes.

**Rule 5.4.5 — Disputed Classification:**
If the tier of a conflict is narratively disputed between parties (e.g., one faction calls it a "border skirmish" while the other calls it an act of war), the domain routing of the audit gate (`guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`) determines the canonical classification based on the objective parameters defined in this Part, not on either party's political framing.

### 5.5 — Tier Interaction with Cultivation Level

**Rule 5.5.1 — Proportional Force Obligation:**
A cultivator engaging in a conflict classified below their combat capability must limit their force expression to what is consistent with that tier's intensity ceiling (Area 7). A Divine Realm cultivator in an E1 personal combat cannot casually discharge continental-scale destruction. The tier governs the rules; the cultivator's power governs their capability. The two are independent.

**Rule 5.5.2 — Involuntary Escalation:**
If a cultivator cannot control their power output — due to breakthrough state, Deviant Breakthrough, emotional breakdown, or Core destabilization — the conflict's effective tier automatically elevates to match the intensity being expressed, regardless of the initiating tier. Involuntary Escalation is a canon event with consequences under Part 27; it does not excuse the escalation.

**Rule 5.5.3 — Escalation Consent:**
Deliberately escalating a conflict's tier beyond the current classification requires at minimum the implicit consent of at least one other party through their actions. A unilateral escalation that draws unwilling third parties into a higher-tier conflict is a Force Legitimacy violation and carries maximum consequence weight under Part 27.

## Area 6 — Engagement Recognition & Mutuality Law

### 6.1 — Definition of Engagement

An **engagement** is the canonical state in which two or more parties are actively committed to hostile action against each other. Engagement is distinct from:

- **Encounter:** Two parties are in proximity but have not taken hostile action
- **Surveillance / Pursuit:** One party is tracking another with no overt hostile action
- **Positioning / Preparation:** One or both parties are preparing for combat without initiating it
- **Standoff:** Both parties have the capability and intent to fight but neither has acted — this is pre-engagement, not engagement

Engagement begins at the moment the **engagement entry conditions** (Section 6.2) are met. Engagement ends at the moment the **termination conditions** (Area 12) are met. The period between these moments is the canonical combat state.

### 6.2 — Engagement Entry Conditions

A party enters engagement when **any one** of the following occurs:

**EC-1 — Overt Hostile Action:**
The party takes a physical, energetic, or soul-based action directed at causing harm to another party. This is the clearest entry condition and the most common.

**EC-2 — Formal Challenge Accepted:**
A formal challenge is issued and accepted — both parties are in engagement from the moment of acceptance, even before physical action begins.

**EC-3 — Aura Release as Declaration:**
A cultivator releases their full combat aura specifically directed at another cultivator in a manner recognizable as a combat declaration (not accidental aura expression). At Grade 6 and above, aura release of this type is universally recognized as engagement declaration. Below Grade 6, context determines whether aura release constitutes a declaration or a display.

**EC-4 — Response to Hostile Action:**
A party that is attacked under any Force Legitimacy category (including ambush) enters engagement the moment they consciously respond with hostile intent. A defensive action that produces counter-force constitutes engagement entry for the defender — their response is not passive resistance but active combat participation.

**EC-5 — Third-Party Activation:**
A party designated as an active combatant by alliance activation (Area 8.6) enters engagement at the moment of activation, even before physical contact with the opposing force.

### 6.3 — One-Sided Engagement

In an ambush or assassination attempt, the attacking party enters engagement from the moment of initiation (they meet EC-1). The target may not be in engagement until they become aware and respond. This creates a window — however brief — in which the engagement is technically one-sided.

**Consequences of One-Sided Engagement:**
- The ambushing party is fully subject to all combat rules (escalation tier, intensity, etc.) from the moment of initiation
- The target is not bound by combat rules until they enter engagement through EC-4
- A target who remains unaware and takes no action is not in engagement; damage to them is processed under assault/consequence rules rather than combat rules
- The moment the target responds, mutual engagement is established

### 6.4 — Mutuality Threshold

Engagement becomes **mutual** when both parties have met at least one engagement entry condition. Mutual engagement is the standard combat state governed by this Part in full.

**One-Sided Engagement Rules:**
When engagement is one-sided (ambush condition), the combat rules still apply to the attacking party. The defending party's rights — including the right to respond with full Defensive Response (FC-3) force — are active from the moment they become aware. There is no advantage to the ambushing party from the engagement recognition perspective; they simply initiated first.

### 6.5 — Disengagement vs. Termination

**Disengagement** is the act of withdrawing from active hostile contact without meeting termination conditions. A disengaged combatant is no longer in immediate combat but remains in the engagement's scope — they can be pursued, re-engaged, and are still subject to the conflict's tier classification. Disengagement rules are governed by Area 14.

**Termination** is the canonical end of the engagement under the conditions defined in Area 12. A terminated engagement cannot be "re-opened" without a new initiation event meeting the conditions of Area 4.3.

### 6.6 — Engagement in Multi-Combatant Scenarios

In fights involving more than two parties:

**Rule 6.6.1 — Individual Engagement Tracking:**
Each party's engagement status is tracked independently. Party A may be in engagement with Party B and C, while Party D is an observer who has not entered engagement. Party D's transition from observer to combatant occurs when they meet one of the entry conditions (EC-1 through EC-5).

**Rule 6.6.2 — Entry Announcement in Formal Conflicts:**
In E3 and above conflicts, a party entering the engagement must be identifiable — their entry cannot be ambiguous. Unknown combatants without clear faction alignment are treated as hostile to all parties until identification is established (Area 10.3).

**Rule 6.6.3 — Exit Tracking:**
A party exits engagement through disengagement (Area 14) or termination conditions (Area 12). Exit is tracked per party — the overall engagement continues as long as any two parties remain in mutual engagement.

## Area 7 — Intensity Containment Boundaries

### 7.1 — The Tier-Relative Intensity Principle

Every escalation tier has an **intensity ceiling** — the maximum scale of destructive force that is structurally appropriate for that tier. This ceiling is not a physical limit on what cultivators can do; it is a canon governance limit on what they may do within a given tier's framework without triggering an escalation violation.

The intensity ceiling is defined by the spatial footprint, participant count, and scope of destruction that is consistent with the tier's classification. A conflict cannot maintain its classification as an E1 Personal Combat if the destructive footprint of its actions would affect thousands of people and alter terrain at continental scale. Either the tier reclassifies upward, or an escalation violation has occurred. The footprint that counts is the footprint of deliberate force (techniques and discharges); the unsuppressible passive aura and environmental response of a high-Grade cultivator (Part 3 Rule 3.4.4 and Rule 20.3.1) are staged as scenery and pressure, do not count toward the tier, and are not an escalation violation.

### 7.2 — Intensity Ceilings by Tier

**E1 — Personal Combat Intensity Ceiling:**
Destructive force is contained to the immediate combat space — typically no more than tens of meters in radius for standard combatants, up to hundreds of meters for very high-grade cultivators exercising significant restraint. No deliberate structural destruction of civilian infrastructure. No terrain alteration that affects third parties not present. The fight's effects end at the edges of the immediate engagement zone.

**E2 — Skirmish Intensity Ceiling:**
Destructive force may affect a distinct combat location — a building, a section of forest, a stretch of road. Limited terrain alteration is acceptable. No deliberate targeting of structures beyond the immediate tactical objective. Environmental damage is contained to the skirmish zone.

**E3 — Engagement Intensity Ceiling:**
Deliberate tactical destruction of the engagement zone is permitted. Buildings, formations, fortifications within the declared engagement zone may be destroyed. Civilian structures within the engagement zone that have been evacuated under Area 9 protocols may be destroyed for military necessity. Destruction outside the declared engagement zone is a containment violation.

**E4 — Battle Intensity Ceiling:**
Broad strategic destruction within the declared battlefield is permitted. Multiple locations may be destroyed. Terrain can be significantly altered within the battlefield. No deliberate attacks on civilian population centers outside the military objective. The battle's destructive effects are to remain within the theater of operations.

**E5 — Campaign Intensity Ceiling:**
Regional-scale destruction capabilities are deployable. Multiple theaters may sustain significant destruction. The campaign's destructive effects may alter regional geography. No deliberate planetary-scale attacks without E6 reclassification.

**E6 — War Intensity Ceiling:**
Continental-scale destruction capabilities are deployable. Divine realm combat may occur, bringing with it the inherent environmental consequences of that power level (Part 3, Area 3.3, Grades 13–17). No cross-dimensional attacks that could permanently damage cosmological structures unless the story's current scale and world-state support this level of conflict.

**E7 — Annihilation Conflict Intensity Ceiling:**
No intensity ceiling under this Part. Location Law (Part 13) remains applicable. Beyond these constraints, an E7 conflict authorizes unlimited destructive deployment consistent with the in-world power scale of participants.

**Footprint size by tier:**

| Tier | Size of the destructive footprint |
|---|---|
| E1 Personal Combat | Tens of meters in radius; up to hundreds of meters for very high-grade cultivators exercising significant restraint |
| E2 Skirmish | Up to about 1 km across |
| E3 Engagement | The declared engagement zone (Area 9.4) |
| E4 Battle | The theater: the declared battlefield |
| E5 Campaign | A region |
| E6 War | A continent |
| E7 Annihilation Conflict | No size limit under this Part (Part 13 still applies) |

**Move Types at full output, by tier (Part 6 Area 2):**

| Move Type | Used at full output from |
|---|---|
| Basic, Intermediate, Advanced, Elite, Signature | Any tier (E1 and up) |
| Fusion | E2 and up |
| Catastrophic | E3 and up |
| Mythic | E5 and up |

Below the listed tier a move is used restrained, as the Proportional Force Rule (7.3) requires; output past the tier's ceiling is an escalation violation (7.4).

### 7.3 — Proportional Force Rule

A cultivator whose cultivation capability exceeds the intensity ceiling of the current escalation tier must exercise deliberate restraint over their deliberate force to remain within the tier. A Divine Realm cultivator in an E1 duel is expected to calibrate their force expression to E1 parameters. This calibration is:

- **Culturally expected** in most cultivation societies for duels and personal combat (Part 25)
- **Canon-required** to maintain the engagement's tier classification
- **Not always achieved** — a cultivator who loses control of their output triggers the Involuntary Escalation rule

**Proportional Force Rule Compliance:**
A cultivator who maintains E1 force output despite having E6 capability is demonstrating exceptional control and cultural maturity. This is narratively valid and does not require mechanical justification. A cultivator cannot be "forced" by the rules to express their full power.

### 7.4 — Escalation Violations

An **Escalation Violation** occurs when a cultivator expresses force at a scale inconsistent with the current tier's intensity ceiling, unilaterally and without the implicit consent of at least one other party (which would constitute escalation by agreement). It is the same act as Rule 5.5.3, and its consequences are those listed below.

**Consequences of Escalation Violations:**
1. The conflict is automatically reclassified upward to the tier that matches the actual intensity expressed
2. The violating party bears sole responsibility for all consequences of the reclassification under Part 27
3. Third parties who become engaged due to the escalation violation did not consent to the conflict; their participation is a consequence of the violation, not an independent choice
4. In cultivation society, escalation violations carry severe social and political weight (Part 25)

Reclassification by intensity applies that tier's intensity, containment, third-party and termination rules only; participant, command and declaration tests are deemed met, and the conflict drops back when the excess force stops (Rule 5.4.3 is waived for this case).

**Involuntary Escalation (see also Section 5.5.2):**
An escalation violation caused by loss of power control (breakthrough state, emotional breakdown, Core destabilization) is classified as Involuntary Escalation. The consequences under Part 27 still apply — a cultivator who cannot control their power is responsible for its effects — but the social and cultural penalty is reduced relative to deliberate escalation.

### 7.5 — Intensity Floors in Formal Duels

A Formal Duel may include an agreed **intensity floor** — a minimum level of power expression required for the duel to be considered genuine. Intensity floors are most common in:
- Honor Duels where the point is to demonstrate capability, not merely survive
- Wager Duels where one party suspects the other of sandbagging to minimize risk
- Duels in which power ceiling agreements are combined with power floor agreements for a balanced engagement

Intensity floor violations (fighting significantly below the agreed floor without cause) are treated as duel invalidation by the violating party.

### 7.6 — Soul Technique Escalation Tier Constraints

Soul techniques (Weapon Soul, Defense Soul, Trap Soul, Phantom Soul, Convert Soul, Devour Soul, Bind Soul, Resonate Soul, Nullify Soul — governed by Canon-Part 4 Area 2.3; the table applies to every Part 4 soul type by its effect, and Healing Soul and Sense Soul are unrestricted) operate in the **metaphysical layer** rather than the physical layer. This creates a special governance relationship with the escalation tier framework:

**Soul technique intensity is calibrated against soul rung differential rather than spatial footprint:**
Because soul techniques do not produce terrain-altering physical destruction, the standard spatial footprint metric (Area 7.2) does not directly apply. Instead, soul technique intensity within a given escalation tier is governed by the soul rung differential between attacker and target:

| Escalation Tier | Maximum Soul Technique Intensity | Soul Rung Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| E1 Personal Combat | Disorientation, pain, brief consciousness disruption | Soul rung differential ≤ 3; no permanent soul damage |
| E2 Skirmish | Consciousness suppression (temporary), technique disruption | Soul rung differential ≤ 4; no soul structure damage |
| E3 Engagement | Short-term memory disruption, soul exhaustion, temporary power access block | Soul rung differential ≤ 5; minor soul structure damage permitted |
| E4 Battle | Soul fragmentation (recoverable), extended power access block, emotional suppression | Soul rung differential ≤ 6; moderate soul damage permitted |
| E5 Campaign | Severe soul fragmentation, partial erasure of recent memories, semi-permanent consciousness suppression | No rung differential limit; permanent soul damage possible |
| E6 War | Soul destruction, permanent memory erasure, identity dissolution | No rung differential limit; soul death possible |
| E7 Annihilation | No soul technique constraints | Governed by Part 28 |

**Devour Soul special constraint:**
Devour Soul (Part 4 Area 2.3) is governed by a stricter escalation constraint than other soul techniques — its use at E1 through E3 is limited to soul force absorption that does not permanently diminish the target's soul structure. Sustained Devour Soul that causes permanent soul damage constitutes automatic escalation to E4 regardless of the physical footprint of the conflict.

**Soul technique invisibility:**
Because soul techniques leave no visible physical trace in most cases, escalation violations involving soul techniques are harder to detect than physical escalation violations. A cultivator who soul-attacks a target beyond the current tier's constraint may not trigger immediate social recognition of the violation — the target's visible suffering may appear to have physical causes. This invisibility makes soul technique escalation violations a canonical form of plausibly deniable escalation.

**Governance boundary:** Part 4 governs what soul techniques do and their mechanics. Part 5 governs the escalation tier constraints on their use in combat. Both must be simultaneously satisfied.

### 7.7 — Combined Force

When a conflict holds more than one kind of force — cultivation, technology, cultivation-technology synergy, or fusion — the Chapter Spec (Part 38) decides who wins. Part 5 does not weigh the kinds of force against each other and does not compare their sums. This is the Part 5 combined-force rule that Part 8 Rule 8.2.2 and Part 31 Area 7.1 point to. Each kind of force stays under the Part that governs it, and this Area adds no new limit: the result is written inside the limits those Parts already set (Part 3 Area 17.3, Part 8 Rule 8.1.2, Part 31 Rule 7.1.1). What this Area gives the writer is a model for how the win and the loss are written for each kind of force.

| Kind of force | Governed and bounded by | How the victor is written | How the loser is written |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cultivation | Part 3 (Areas 8, 12, 17), Part 4 (Area 2), Part 6 | Through the victor's own body, qi and Named Moves, shown at the victor's realm and Grade with the energy the win cost (Part 3 Areas 12 and 17; Part 6 Areas 5 and 11). | Through the loser's body, qi or soul giving out: wounds, exhaustion, meridian or soul damage (Part 3 Areas 8, 12.4 and 13.4; Part 4 Area 2.7), each recorded as an injury in the ledger, then the ending set by Area 12 and the Part 27 consequences. |
| Technology | Part 8 (Areas 8.1 and 8.2) | Through the machinery: weapons, vehicles, sensors, supply and numbers, at the output of their Part 8 tier. Technology wins by scale and reach rather than individual skill (Part 8 Rule 8.1.3), and it does not raise anyone's cultivation tier (Part 8 Rule 8.2.1). | Through systems disabled, destroyed, out of power or cut off, and the people who ran them lost, wounded or captured, then the ending set by Area 12. |
| Synergy, Model C | Part 31 (Areas 5.3, 7.1, 7.2, 17.2) | Through a system built around the practitioner that answers the practitioner's qi in real time and cannot be split from it. Written as one result neither part produced alone, never as cultivation force plus technology force (Part 31 Area 17.1). | Through the joined build failing: take out the practitioner or the system and the build cannot function (Part 31 Areas 5.3 and 17.2). |
| Synergy, Model D | Part 31 (Areas 5.4, 7.1, 7.2, 17.2) | Through one unified system steered by the practitioner's intent, with no clean line between qi and machine. Written as power that is simply the practitioner's own (Part 31 Areas 5.4 and 17.2). | Through the whole system failing, because the practitioner's own architecture is a part of it (Part 31 Area 5.4); the damage is written on the practitioner and the system together. |
| Fusion | Part 7 (Areas 8 and 11), Part 6 (Area 10) | Through the fused architecture: two streams worked as one, in Fusion Combat Mode at higher output for a limited time, with the entropy it builds shown (Part 7 Areas 8.1 and 11.1; Fusion Moves under Part 6 Area 10). | Through the fusion giving way: entropy exploited, then destabilization or collapse and the loss of the fusion-dependent techniques (Part 7 Areas 8.3, 8.4, 11.4 and 11.5). |

Model A synergy (the two tracks acting side by side) is written with the Cultivation and Technology rows, and Model B (technology run on cultivation energy) with the Technology row and the cultivation feed shown (Part 31 Area 17.2). In every row the Chapter Spec chooses the winner; the table only says how the win and the loss are written. The end of the fight is classified under Area 12 as for any other combat.

### 7.8 — A Limit Break Inside an Engagement

**Rule 7.8.1 — Source.** A limit break (a striving, limit-breaking or chaotic burst) is defined in Part 3 Area 7.5: the lift by affinity, the cost tiers, the hard caps and its interaction with the 3-Level Rule, the lethal threshold, Qi Deviation and breakthrough surge. This Part restates none of it.

**Rule 7.8.2 — It Changes Nobody's Right to Fight.** A limit break never changes who may begin a fight, who may end it or how (Areas 4, 6 and 12). It is a priced burst a combatant uses inside an engagement already lawful.

**Rule 7.8.3 — Intensity and the Proportional Force Rule.** The burst does not lift the tier's intensity ceiling (Area 7.2) and does not excuse force past Proportional Force (Area 7.3). It is not a licence to exceed the tier: the combatant's output, lifted, is still read against the ceiling of the fight's E-tier.

**Rule 7.8.4 — E-Tiers.** A limit break does not move a fight up an E-tier by itself. The tier is classified by scale, scope and objectives (Area 5.1).

**Rule 7.8.5 — Retreat and Surrender.** A combatant who has used a Tier 2 or Tier 3 burst is injured, fainted or in Qi Deviation. They may retreat or surrender (Areas 12.5 and 14), and a combatant down from a burst is incapacitated for the purposes of protected status.

**Rule 7.8.6 — Termination and Aftermath.** A fight ended by a collapse after a burst is classified by the ordinary victory and defeat classes of Area 12; the burst earns no class of its own. The injury, faint or deviation is a post-combat obligation (PO-1, Area 12.6) and is recorded in the character ledger, and its consequences run under Part 27.

**Rule 7.8.7 — Checked by Part 44.** Whether a limit break is plausible in a whole engagement is checked by Part 44 (Gate 1 Check #23, element 23-4), not here.

## Area 8 — Third-Party & Proxy Combat Rules

### 8.1 — Third-Party Classification

Every individual or group present in the vicinity of an active conflict is classified as one of the following at any given moment:

**TC-1 — Active Combatant:**
Directly engaged in the conflict. Has met one or more engagement entry conditions (Area 6.2). Subject to all combat rules in full.

**TC-2 — Observer:**
Present in the vicinity of the conflict but explicitly non-participating. Recognized as neutral by at least one active party. Has committed no hostile action and has a recognized reason for presence (witness to a duel, news envoy, neutral faction representative, historian). Has protections under this classification (Section 8.2).

**TC-3 — Rescuer:**
Present specifically to extract a participant from the conflict without entering the combat. Has no hostile intent toward any combatant. Distinguished from Active Combatant by the absence of hostile action; if a Rescuer takes hostile action to achieve the rescue, they reclassify as Active Combatant.

**TC-4 — Opportunistic Combatant:**
Enters an ongoing conflict for independent benefit unrelated to either established party's objectives. Examples: a third party attacking the weakened victor of an existing fight to seize their resources, a faction using an ongoing war to advance their own territorial agenda. Opportunistic entry rules are in Area 10.3.

**TC-5 — Proxy Fighter:**
Represents a non-present party by formal agreement. Governed by Section 8.5.

**TC-6 — Ambush Element:**
A hidden party waiting to enter the conflict at a specific moment. Classified as an Active Combatant from the moment they enter engagement entry conditions (EC-1 or EC-3), even if physically hidden.

**TC-7 — Bystander:**
Present without intent or relevance to the conflict. Not a participant, not an observer by choice, simply in proximity. Has full civilian protection under Area 4 (PS-1). Harm to bystanders is classified as Collateral Force (FC-6).

### 8.2 — Observer Rights and Protections

A recognized Observer is protected from combat targeting while maintaining Observer status:
- Cannot be attacked by any Active Combatant without Force Legitimacy violation
- Cannot be used as cover, hostage, or leverage without the using party incurring a severe Force Legitimacy violation
- Must not interfere with the combat in any way — signaling, warning, supplying, or communicating advantageously to any combatant forfeits Observer status
- Observer status ends the moment the Observer takes any action that would qualify as engagement entry (EC-1 through EC-5)

**Mandatory Observer Roles:**
Formal Duels require witnesses — individuals who hold Observer status by design to ensure the duel's legitimacy is verifiable. Mandatory Observers cannot abandon their witness role without invalidating the duel.

### 8.3 — Legitimate Intervention

A third party may legitimately enter an ongoing conflict under the following conditions:

**LI-1 — Alliance Obligation (see 8.6):**
A faction or individual bound by recognized alliance obligation enters the conflict in fulfillment of that obligation. Alliance intervention is legitimate and recognized from the moment of entry.

**LI-2 — Master/Teacher Rescue Right:**
A recognized master, teacher, or senior authority figure may intervene to extract their student/junior from a combat situation that has reached existential danger. This is the Rescuer role (TC-3) — the master's intervention is not an entry into the combat's objectives but a protective action. If the master takes hostile action against the opposing combatant beyond what is necessary to extract the junior, they become an Active Combatant.

**LI-3 — Faction Member Defense:**
A faction has the standing right to defend its recognized members who are under attack. Faction defense intervention is classified as E3 or above if it involves formal faction forces, or E1/E2 if it involves nearby faction members acting independently.

**LI-4 — Humanitarian Intervention:**
Intervention to protect non-combatants from harm caused by combat exceeding its spatial containment. This is limited to protective action — placing barriers between the combat and the non-combatants, evacuating civilians, creating protective formations. Any hostile action against combatants transforms the intervener into an Active Combatant.

### 8.4 — Illegitimate Interference

The following actions constitute illegitimate interference in an active engagement:

**II-1 — Gang Intervention:**
A party enters an ongoing E1 or E2 combat with the intent of overwhelming one side through numbers without prior engagement justification. Gang intervention is illegitimate in most cultivation societies (Part 25) and carries consequence weight under Part 27.

**II-2 — Covert Material Support:**
Supplying weapons, healing resources, or technique scrolls to one party during an active engagement without formally entering the combat. This is illegitimate because it affects combat outcomes without accepting combat risk.

**II-3 — Environmental Manipulation:**
Altering the physical environment of an engagement to disadvantage one party (collapsing terrain, sealing space, redirecting environmental force) from outside the engagement without entering it. Environmental manipulation from outside the engagement is illegitimate interference regardless of the manipulator's intent.

**II-4 — Duel Interruption:**
Interrupting a Formal Duel without authorization is one of the gravest interference violations in cultivation society. The only conditions under which duel interruption is legitimate: the duel has been invalidated (Section 5.3), one party is in immediately fatal danger and a rescue (TC-3) is being attempted, or the duel's terms have been violated by one party.

**Consequences of Illegitimate Interference:**
A party that commits illegitimate interference becomes an Active Combatant in the conflict, regardless of whether they intended to participate. Their entry is treated as hostile to whichever party they harmed through their interference. Additional consequences are governed by Part 27.

### 8.5 — Proxy Combat

**Proxy Combat Definition:**
A Proxy Combat is a recognized combat in which one or more parties are represented by a designated fighter (the proxy) rather than fighting themselves. The proxy's outcome binds the party they represent. Where both sides field proxies to settle a larger conflict the fight is an FD-Type 5 Representative Duel: Section 5.3 sets the terms and binding effect; Section 8.5 adds only proxy willingness and the intervention penalty.

**Conditions for Valid Proxy Combat:**
1. The representing party explicitly designates the proxy with consent of the proxy
2. The opposing party recognizes and accepts the proxy arrangement before combat begins
3. The terms are declared: what the proxy's victory or defeat means for the representing party
4. Witnesses or recognized arbiters are present

**Proxy Fighter Requirements:**
A proxy must be a willing participant — forced proxies are not valid under this framework. Cultural norms (Part 25) govern the social expectations around proxy selection. In most cultivation societies, selecting a proxy vastly superior in power to the opposing fighter is dishonorable (the equivalent of using an overwhelming weapon in an honor duel).

**Proxy Outcome Binding:**
The outcome of a valid Proxy Combat is binding on the represented parties as if they had fought themselves. The represented party cannot contest the outcome on grounds of disagreeing with their proxy's choices in combat.

**Proxy Invalidation:**
If the representing party directly intervenes in their proxy's combat, the proxy arrangement is invalidated and the representing party becomes an Active Combatant. The conflict reverts to direct engagement terms.

### 8.6 — Alliance Activation in Combat

**Alliance Types:**
Alliances (governed in full by Part 22) exist on a spectrum from passive to active:

- **Passive Alliance:** Information sharing, non-aggression pact, general goodwill. Combat obligations: none.
- **Defensive Alliance:** Obligated to assist if a member is attacked first. Activates only on defensive engagement.
- **Mutual Defense Alliance:** Full mutual assistance obligation in any conflict where a member is engaged.
- **Combat Alliance:** Active coordination in specified conflicts; both parties are treated as a single combat unit for engagement purposes.

**Mapping to the Part 22 Area 24.2 and Part 23 Area 4.3 names:** Passive = Non-Aggression Pact or Resource Sharing Compact; Defensive = Limited Defense Compact; Mutual Defense = the other Part 23 Area 4.3 classes; Combat Alliance = Full or Grand Alliance in a named conflict.

**Activation Conditions:**
A Defensive or Mutual Defense Alliance activates when: the allied party is confirmed to be under attack (not pre-emptive strikes or Opportunistic Combat entry they initiated), the allied party has communicated the alliance activation request, and the activating party's leadership has the authority to commit forces. For Combat Alliances, the activation conditions are defined within the alliance terms.

**Alliance Activation Timing:**
An alliance-activated force is a legitimate addition to the existing conflict at the tier that matches the combined participant count and scope. Alliance activation does not itself trigger escalation — the resulting participant count may trigger escalation per Section 5.4.

## Area 9 — Battlefield Spatial & Cosmological Constraints

### 9.1 — Spatial Containment Principle

Every combat event has a **natural spatial footprint** determined by the power levels of the participants and the escalation tier. At low cultivation levels, this footprint is small and self-contained. At high cultivation levels — particularly at Grade 8 and above — the spatial footprint expands dramatically, and the environmental responses defined in Part 3, Area 3.3 are non-voluntary.

The Spatial Containment Principle states: combat should be contained to the minimum spatial footprint necessary for the engagement, with explicit recognition that containment becomes increasingly difficult and eventually impossible at high power levels. Deliberate expansion of the combat footprint beyond what the engagement requires is a force legitimacy violation.

### 9.2 — Battlefield Footprint by Cultivation Grade

The following are the natural combat footprints at each Heavenly Scale Grade. These footprints represent the area that is realistically affected by the combat's energy discharge, aura expression, and technique execution — not the area the combatants must occupy:

| Heavenly Scale Grade | Combat Realm | Natural Combat Footprint |
|---------------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Grade 0–5 | Mortal Tier 1–2 | Tens of meters; human-scale environments |
| Grade 6–9 | Xiantian | Hundreds of meters to several kilometers |
| Grade 10–12 | Core | Several kilometers to tens of kilometers |
| Grade 13–15 | Divine Transformation–Heaven Treading | Tens to hundreds of kilometers |
| Grade 16 | Divine Ascension | Continental scale (Part 3 Area 20.2) |
| Grade 17 | Divine Sea Realm | Planetary scale (Part 3 Area 20.2) |

Part 14 Area 4.3 gives the largest reach of one deliberate technique; the footprints here are the usual spread of a whole fight. A technique reaching past its row counts toward the 7.2 ceiling (Rule 7.4). Civilian safe distance = the Part 3 Area 20 harmful-zone radius of the Grade, plus the footprint in the row above.

**Containment Expectation vs Containment Requirement:**
At Grade 0–7, spatial containment to the immediate engagement zone is expected and achievable. From Grade 8 the passive aura and environmental responses occur non-voluntarily and cannot be suppressed (Part 3 Rule 3.4.4 and Rule 20.3.1); the deliberate force of a Grade 8–9 cultivator is still expected to be containable. At Grade 10–12, containing deliberate force requires deliberate effort and may not be fully achievable in extended combat. At Grade 13–15, full containment may be impossible — the environmental responses (Part 3, Area 3.3, Grades 13–15) occur non-voluntarily and cannot be suppressed. At Grade 16–17, combat is inherently a continental or planetary event; the concept of "containment" shifts from physical to tactical (ensuring non-combatants are not in the affected zone rather than limiting the zone itself).

### 9.3 — Protected Zones in Combat

The following zones retain protection during combat at all escalation tiers unless formally designated (PZ-1, PZ-2, PZ-5, PZ-6 only) as military objectives in E4 and above; PZ-3 and PZ-4 can never be so designated:

**PZ-1 — Civilian Settlements Not on Declared Battlefield:**
Any inhabited location that has not been declared part of a battlefield (in E3+ conflicts) or that is not directly within the engagement zone (in E1–E2) retains full civilian protection. Deliberate attacks on non-declared civilian settlements are Force Legitimacy violations regardless of escalation tier.

**PZ-2 — Sacred Grounds:**
As defined by Part 13 (Location Law). Sacred Grounds have inherent properties — power floors, power ceilings, dimensional anomalies — that make combat within them carry unique risks and restrictions. Combat in Sacred Grounds must comply with the specific restrictions of that Sacred Ground as defined in Part 13. Deliberate attack on a Sacred Ground that has not been declared a military objective may have in-world consequences beyond normal combat governance.

**PZ-3 — Recognized Medical Treatment Zones:**
Any location visibly identified as a medical treatment zone — marked by recognized healer symbols, faction medical corps insignia, or common cultivation society conventions — is protected. Attacking a treatment zone is a maximum-severity Force Legitimacy violation and one of the few combat actions that produces universal condemnation across cultivation society (Part 25).

**PZ-4 — Diplomatic Zones:**
Locations where recognized diplomatic functions are occurring (peace negotiations, envoy reception, formal ambassador meetings) are protected from combat action. Violation of a diplomatic zone is equivalent to attacking diplomatic personnel (Area 4.5, PS-4) and is treated as an act of war.

**PZ-5 — Declared Evacuation Corridors:**
In E3+ conflicts where battlefield declaration has designated civilian evacuation routes, those corridors are protected from combat action for the duration of their declared function.

**PZ-6 — Historical and Cultural Preservation Sites:**
Sites with recognized historical, cultural, or cultivation heritage significance. These sites have partial protection — they cannot be deliberately targeted without specific military necessity justification in E4+ conflicts. Deliberate cultural destruction without military justification carries severe consequence weight under Part 27.

### 9.4 — Battlefield Declaration Protocol

In E3 and above conflicts, before major combat operations begin, a **Battlefield Declaration** must be established:

**Declaration Components:**
1. Designation of the primary combat zone
2. Identification of protected zones within or adjacent to the combat zone
3. Establishment of evacuation corridors
4. Notification to non-combatant parties that the designated zone is now a combat area

At E3 components 1 and 2 may be spoken or signalled; at E4 and above all four are required in recorded form.

**Declaration Timing:**
Declaration must precede the opening of major operations by sufficient time to allow evacuation — in practice, hours to days depending on the scale of the protected zone and the speed of the combatants involved. Emergency combat conditions (defensive response to sudden invasion) may require declaration simultaneously with combat initiation, in which case the declaration requirement is met as soon as practically possible.

**Declaration Authority:**
Any party at E3 and above with command authority over their forces may declare a battlefield. Opposing parties need not agree to the declaration's scope, but the declaration binds the declaring party to respect the designated zones.

### 9.5 — Cross-Dimensional and Cross-Realm Combat

Combat that extends into different dimensional spaces (void spaces, spirit planes, the divine sea external layer, or dimensions defined by Part 13) is not fully governed by mortal spatial laws. The following rules apply:

**Rule 9.5.1 — Entry Legitimacy:**
Forcing a combat into a dimension where the opposing party cannot follow is a valid strategic action in E1–E4 but constitutes abandonment of the engagement by the party who entered the dimension alone. If only one party can access the dimensional space, the engagement cannot continue as mutual combat.

**Rule 9.5.2 — External Resolution:**
A combat that resolves entirely in another dimension produces its outcome in that dimension. Consequences (Part 27) apply in both the dimension where the combat occurred and in the mortal realm, as appropriate.

**Rule 9.5.3 — Return Obligation:**
A combat that enters a dimension does not terminate by virtue of dimensional crossing. If both parties survive the dimensional engagement, they may return to mortal space; the engagement status depends on the dimensional combat's termination conditions (Area 12).

### 9.6 — Sacred Ground Power Rules in Combat

Sacred Grounds (Part 13) may impose power expression restrictions that affect combat:

- **Power Ceilings on Sacred Grounds:** If a Sacred Ground limits power expression to a certain Grade, combatants of higher Grades cannot express their full power within that Ground. Combat within a power-ceiling Sacred Ground is automatically governed by the ceiling level for intensity purposes.
- **Power Floors on Sacred Grounds:** If a Sacred Ground has a minimum power floor for entry (only cultivators above a certain Grade can enter), then combat within is inherently at that minimum power level.
- **Sacred Ground Consequence Multipliers:** Some Sacred Grounds amplify the consequences of violence within them — death, injury, or collateral damage in such locations carries greater consequence weight under Part 27.

A Sacred Ground imposes a power ceiling, power floor or consequence multiplier only if its ledger entry records one; otherwise none applies and PZ-2 alone governs.

## Area 10 — Multi-Faction Chaos Stabilization

### 10.1 — The Chaos Threshold

A conflict reaches **Chaos Conditions** when three or more factions are simultaneously engaged in the same combat space at E3 or above, with conflicting or unclear alignment between them. At the Chaos Threshold, the normal bilateral engagement rules become insufficient to maintain deterministic structure, and the stabilization protocols of this Area apply.

Chaos Conditions may also arise in lower-tier conflicts when the participant count includes individuals whose alignment to any established position is unknown — a large E2 skirmish where multiple unknown parties enter without declaring their position constitutes a localized chaos condition.

### 10.2 — Faction Alignment Tracking

At all times during a multi-faction conflict, every participating party must be classifiable into one of four alignment states:

**AS-1 — Allied:** In coordinated combat cooperation with a designated coalition. Treat coalition members as friendly; treat coalition's opponents as hostile.

**AS-2 — Hostile:** In active combat opposition to a designated party or parties.

**AS-3 — Neutral:** Not participating; maintaining non-engagement by choice; cannot take hostile action without reclassifying.

**AS-4 — Uncommitted:** Present in the conflict zone but alignment not yet established; in the process of entering or observing without declared position.

**Chaos occurs** when the number of Uncommitted parties exceeds the number of declared Hostile parties, OR when active parties are attacking each other in ways that cannot be resolved by the current alignment map. This count applies only where 10.1 already holds (E3 or above, or the E2 case); TC-2 Observers and TC-7 Bystanders are not counted.

### 10.3 — Opportunistic Entry Rules

A faction or individual entering an ongoing conflict for independent benefit (TC-4 — Opportunistic Combatant) must:

1. **Declare alignment before striking:** An undeclared party that strikes without declaration is treated as Hostile to all parties until alignment is announced. This is not a social rule — it is a combat safety mechanism. In a chaotic multi-party fight, an unidentified attacker is treated as an enemy by everyone.
2. **Accept the current tier:** By entering the conflict, the opportunistic party accepts the current escalation tier's rules. They cannot enter at E6 behavior when the established conflict is at E3.
3. **Take responsibility for escalation:** If their entry causes the conflict to escalate (by expanding participant count past a tier threshold), the opportunistic party bears co-responsibility for the escalation consequences.

### 10.4 — Temporary Ceasefire Protocols in Multi-Faction Battle

Within a larger conflict (E5–E7), two or more active parties may establish a **Temporary Ceasefire** — a limited cessation of hostilities between specific parties that does not terminate the larger conflict.

**Validity Conditions:**
1. Both parties to the ceasefire explicitly agree (no unilateral ceasefire declarations that bind the other party)
2. The ceasefire has a defined scope (parties, duration, spatial boundaries)
3. The ceasefire does not obligate neutral or third parties to recognize it

**Ceasefire Integrity Rules:**
- A temporary ceasefire cannot be used as cover to maneuver for decisive advantage for longer than one "combat cycle" (defined as the approximate time required for the parties to recover to reasonable combat capacity). Extended repositioning under ceasefire protection is a ceasefire violation.
- A ceasefire violation by one party immediately terminates the ceasefire and treats the violating party as having initiated a surprise attack (FC-4).

### 10.5 — Coalition Combat Governance

When two or more factions form a **Combat Coalition** (AS-1 relationship), the coalition requires:

1. **Designated Commander:** One leader who speaks for the coalition in engagement and termination decisions. Other coalition members defer to this commander on questions governed by this Part.
2. **Shared Objective:** The coalition's objectives must be stated and agreed. Coalition members who pursue independent objectives during coalition combat are acting as Opportunistic Combatants (TC-4) for those independent objectives.
3. **Coalition Binding:** A coalition's commander may accept terms (ceasefire, surrender recognition, withdrawal) that bind all coalition members.

### 10.6 — Chaos Dissolution

When chaos conditions become so total that the deterministic structure of engagement cannot be maintained — when faction alignments cannot be tracked, when the combat has fragmented into dozens of independent personal combats without connection to any strategic objective, when the conflict has consumed its own coherence — the conflict must be narratively resolved through **Chaos Dissolution:**

**Chaos Dissolution Resolution Options:**
1. **Combat Termination:** All parties disengage simultaneously when a recognized authority (a dominant power, a neutral force, natural catastrophe, or the mutual exhaustion of all parties) imposes an end condition
2. **Emergence of Dominant Force:** One party achieves sufficient tactical dominance that other parties are forced into terminal flight or surrender, resolving the chaos through clarity
3. **External Imposition:** A party not in the conflict (often a higher-realm cultivator or institutional force from Part 22) enters specifically to terminate the chaos — this party's entry is a legitimate intervention under LI-4 if protective, or a force legitimacy action if coercive

## Area 11 — Leader Death & Command Continuity in War

### 11.1 — The Leader Death Rule

The death of a recognized leader in E4 and above conflicts is a **canonical governance event**, not merely a narrative beat. Its consequences — for the faction's combat capacity, for command authority, for the conflict's escalation state — are deterministic and must be tracked.

**Definition of Leader for this Area:**
A leader is the individual whose authority over the faction's combat actions is recognized by both the faction's members and by opposing parties as the final command authority for the current conflict. A faction may have a supreme leader in peacetime whose wartime command is held by a designated military commander — in combat governance, the **wartime command authority** is the relevant leader.

### 11.2 — Command Succession Protocol

When a leader dies in combat, succession follows this priority:

**Succession Step 1 — Pre-Declared Succession:**
If the leader named a successor before the conflict (as most factions do for E4+ engagements), the named successor immediately assumes command. There is no transition gap.

**Succession Step 2 — Organizational Succession:**
If no pre-declared successor exists, the next senior officer in the recognized faction hierarchy assumes command. Seniority is determined by the faction's internal governance rules (Part 22).

**Succession Step 3 — Emergency Council:**
If no clear next senior exists or if the senior officer is incapacitated, the surviving leadership council (or equivalent body per Part 22) convenes an emergency session to designate a commander. The faction remains under 11.2 succession during this window and enters the Leaderless State of 11.3 only if a combat cycle passes without a designated commander.

**Succession Step 4 — Field Acclamation:**
If organizational succession fails and council convening is impossible (the leadership is scattered, the battle is ongoing), field commanders may raise a leader by field acclamation — recognized by their forces through immediate consensus. Field-acclaimed leaders are recognized by this Part as having full command authority for combat governance purposes, even if their legitimacy is disputed by other institutional processes.

### 11.3 — Leaderless State

A faction enters **Leaderless State** when a combat cycle elapses after leader death without succession resolution. A **combat cycle** is defined as the time required for the parties to recover to reasonable combat capacity — roughly the duration of one significant exchange of forces or a period in which decisive action was possible. Default length of one combat cycle (Areas 10.4, 11.3 and 11.4): one hour at E4, one day at E5, one week at E6 and E7; the Chapter Spec may set another.

**Capabilities Suspended in Leaderless State:**
A faction in Leaderless State cannot: initiate new offensive operations, commit to new alliance obligations, escalate the conflict tier, accept unconditional surrender terms on behalf of the faction, or formally declare terms of engagement.

**Capabilities Retained in Leaderless State:**
A faction in Leaderless State can: continue existing defensive operations, maintain current positions, exercise local command decisions at the unit level, offer conditional surrender.

**Opposing Party Obligations in Leaderless State:**
The opposing party cannot use the Leaderless State window to force surrender terms that would not be accepted if the faction had functioning leadership — terms accepted under Leaderless State without reasonable succession resolution time are invalid under Part 27.

### 11.4 — Grief Response in Combat

When a recognized leader dies in active battle, the faction's forces may enter a **Grief Response** — a period of heightened emotional and combat volatility. This is governed primarily by Parts 25 and 26, but its combat implications are:

- A faction in Grief Response may engage in intensity violations without the normal deliberate-escalation consequence weight — the emotional disruption reduces but does not eliminate culpability
- Grief Response attacks are not to be treated as new declarations of war by the opposing party; they are a recognized consequence state of leader death
- The Grief Response period is not indefinite — it lasts one combat cycle, after which normal combat governance resumes

### 11.5 — Legitimate Targeting of Leadership

In E4 and above conflicts, targeting the opposing faction's military leadership is a legitimate strategic action. This includes: direct combat against leadership personnel, disruption of command structures, interception of leadership movements, and the deliberate prioritization of command-tier targets.

In E1–E3 conflicts, the targeting of faction leadership who are not directly present and engaged in the combat constitutes assassination (FC-5) rather than combat, and is governed accordingly. An E2 skirmish in which the opposing side's supreme leader happens to be personally present and fighting does not give the other party license to return later and assassinate that leader in their private quarters.

## Area 12 — Combat Termination & Victory Conditions

### 12.1 — Termination Triggers

Combat terminates — and the engagement state ends — when **any one** of the following conditions is met:

**TT-1 — All Opposing Parties Defeated:**
Every party on one side has been rendered unable to continue through death, incapacitation, or complete loss of combat capacity.

**TT-2 — Formal Surrender Accepted:**
One or more parties have offered formal surrender and the receiving side has accepted it, at which point it takes effect (Section 12.5).

**TT-3 — Mutual Exhaustion:**
All parties have reached a state where continued combat is physically impossible due to energy depletion, injury accumulation, or resource exhaustion. Mutual Exhaustion produces a Draw condition (Section 12.4).

**TT-4 — Objective Achievement:**
The initiating party's objectives have been achieved — the territory secured, the resource seized, the person captured or killed, the message delivered — and the initiating party ceases hostile action. If the opposing party also ceases, combat terminates. If the opposing party continues hostile pursuit, combat is not terminated from their side.

**TT-5 — External Termination:**
A force external to the combat imposes termination — overwhelming presence of a higher-power entity whose combat-ending authority cannot be refused, natural catastrophe that makes continuation impossible, or institutional intervention (a recognized authority with the power to enforce cessation).

**TT-6 — Mutual Disengagement:**
Both parties disengage from active hostile contact and neither pursues or re-initiates. This is the most ambiguous termination trigger — it requires that both parties have genuinely ceased hostile intent, not merely tactical pause. Mutual Disengagement terminates the engagement but not necessarily the conflict (the larger conflict may continue).

### 12.2 — Victory Classification

**VC-1 — Total Victory:**
The opposing force has been completely eliminated (killed, incapacitated, or destroyed) with no retreat. Objectives are fully secured. The opponent cannot reconstitute or respond. Maximum consequence and social weight. Most rare.

**VC-2 — Decisive Victory:**
The opposing force has been decisively defeated — forced to retreat or surrender, objectives fully secured, the opponent's capacity to resume the conflict in the immediate theater is eliminated. The opponent survives but cannot contest the outcome.

**VC-3 — Strategic Victory:**
The overall strategic objectives have been achieved even if individual tactical exchanges were lost. The winning party's position in the larger conflict is significantly improved. Victory is not through total engagement domination but through strategic positioning.

**VC-4 — Technical Victory:**
The opponent formally concedes through surrender or treaty, acknowledging the winning party's superior position without requiring the complete elimination of the opposing force. The outcome is recognized by both parties.

**VC-5 — Pyrrhic Victory:**
Victory conditions are achieved but at a cost that materially weakens the winning party. The winner has achieved the objective but is significantly worse off than before the conflict in terms of forces, resources, political standing, or cultivation capacity. Pyrrhic victories are victories in consequence but defeats in effect.

**VC-6 — No Outcome (Draw):**
Neither party achieves decisive objectives. The conflict terminates through mutual exhaustion, external termination, or deliberate cessation without a recognized victor. See Section 12.4.

### 12.3 — Defeat Classification

**DC-1 — Catastrophic Defeat:**
The defeated faction's military capacity is destroyed, leadership is eliminated, objectives are lost, and no recovery path exists within the current narrative timeframe. The most severe defeat classification.

**DC-2 — Strategic Defeat:**
The faction is forced to withdraw from the theater or conflict; objectives are lost; but the faction's core capacity to eventually recover remains intact. Strategic defeat changes the strategic situation decisively against the defeated party.

**DC-3 — Tactical Defeat:**
A specific engagement or battle is lost, but the larger conflict is not resolved. The faction retains capacity to continue prosecuting the campaign. Tactical defeats are learning events as much as setbacks.

**DC-4 — Honorable Defeat:**
The losing party fought fully and well, lost clearly, and exits with their honor and cultural standing intact. Honorable defeat is not a lesser classification — it is a specific recognized outcome in cultivation society that carries no social shame (Part 25). A faction that fought to its last capacity before accepting defeat under Honorable conditions is not considered diminished.

**DC-5 — Disgraceful Defeat:**
Flight without fighting, surrender before genuine engagement, or violation of rules of engagement during a defeat that was then imposed by the opposing party. Disgraceful defeat carries maximum social and cultural weight (Part 25). Its consequences under Part 27 include reputation damage that persists beyond the immediate conflict.

### 12.4 — Draw Conditions

A **Draw** is recognized when:
1. Mutual Exhaustion (TT-3) terminates combat without objective achievement by either party
2. External Termination (TT-5) ends combat before either party achieves objectives
3. Both parties withdraw simultaneously without one side having achieved decisive advantage
4. A Representative Duel (FD-Type 5) or Formal Duel ends without a recognized winner (both combatants simultaneously incapacitated or the duel is invalidated)

Draws are recognized outcomes, not failures of the classification system. They carry their own consequence weight under Part 27 — a draw that follows a catastrophic battle has different consequences than a draw following a brief skirmish.

### 12.5 — Surrender Protocol

**Individual Surrender:**
A cultivator communicates surrender through: explicit verbal declaration, weapon cast down and hands raised, aura fully and visibly withdrawn and collapsed, kneeling with head bowed (cultural form governed by Part 25), or other universally recognized incapacitation signals. Surrender must be **unambiguous** — a cultivator who is recovering while appearing to have surrendered has not surrendered.

**Force Surrender:**
A command authority surrenders their forces on behalf of all members of that force. Force surrender requires the surrendering authority to have legitimate command over those forces. Force surrender takes effect when the receiving side accepts it (Post-Surrender Protections below); the terms are then established and the surrendering forces visibly stand down.

**Conditional Surrender:**
Surrender with terms attached. The surrendering party proposes conditions (treatment of prisoners, retention of certain assets, immunity of certain individuals). The opposing party may accept, counter-propose, or reject the conditions. Rejection of conditions does not automatically resume combat — the surrendering party may choose to offer unconditional surrender or to resume combat.

**Unconditional Surrender:**
Surrender without terms. The surrendering party accepts the winning party's determination of the post-combat situation. Unconditional surrender is the most extreme form of surrender and is recognized as ending the conflict definitively. The winning party bears no obligation other than those imposed by Part 25 (cultural norms of treatment of defeated) and Part 27 (consequences of how the defeated are treated).

**Surrender Acceptance Requirement:**
A surrender takes effect when the receiving side accepts it (Post-Surrender Protections below). A surrender that is not accepted (the opposing party continues combat despite a valid surrender offer) has not taken effect and is not a valid termination. However, refusing a valid surrender offer is itself the Part 27 violation and carries severe consequence weight.

**Post-Surrender Protections:**
A surrender takes effect when the receiving side accepts it (this is the one statement of the point; the other Areas refer to it). The surrendered parties then gain Protected Status (PS-2, Area 4.5). Continued violence against them is assault, not combat, and carries the full weight of Part 27's consequence law.

### 12.6 — Post-Combat Obligations

After combat terminates, the following obligations apply regardless of victory or defeat classification:

**PO-1 — Wounded Treatment:**
Wounded combatants — of all parties — are entitled to basic treatment under the cultural norms of Part 25. Denying treatment to wounded combatants as a deliberate act is a Part 27 violation.

**PO-2 — Prisoner Handling:**
Captured combatants who have surrendered or been incapacitated retain protected status. Their treatment is governed by Part 25 cultural norms and Part 23 affiliation obligations.

**PO-3 — Body Recognition:**
The dead of all parties are to be recognized — identified if possible, handled according to their cultural death customs (Part 25). Desecration of the dead is a severe cultural violation with consequence implications under Part 27.

**PO-4 — Ceasefire Maintenance:**
Once combat terminates, both parties maintain the termination state until new initiation conditions are met. Re-initiation of combat against the same party requires meeting the conditions of Area 4.3 fresh — the previous conflict does not maintain a permanent state of engagement.

**Aftermath references:** Bodily and energy aftermath: Part 3 Areas 8, 12.3, 13.4; soul damage: Part 4 Area 2.7; mid-battle recovery: Part 11 Area 9.2; each injury is recorded in the character ledger.

## Area 13 — Civil War & Internal Conflict Doctrine

### 13.1 — Civil War Definition

A **Civil War** is an internal conflict classified at E5 or above (for an entity below regional scale, one that meets the CW-3 small-entity test) that takes place within a single political entity (empire, kingdom, sect, faction, clan system) between parties who both claim legitimate authority over that entity. Civil War is distinguished from:

- **External War (E5–E7):** Between separate political entities
- **Internal Purge / Suppression (E3–E4):** A dominant authority suppressing a rebellion without acknowledging the rebellion's legitimacy claims
- **Factional Conflict (E1–E4):** Internal political violence that has not reached E5 scale (or, for an entity below regional scale, the CW-3 small-entity test)

### 13.2 — Civil War Initiation Conditions

A conflict within a political entity escalates to Civil War status when **all three** of the following conditions are met:

**CW-1 — Competing Authority Claims:**
At least two parties within the political entity make active, recognized claims to legitimate authority over the entity. Claims need not be legally valid — they must be recognized as sincere claims by a significant portion of the entity's population or forces.

**CW-2 — Armed Mobilization:**
Both competing authority claims have organized military forces committed to prosecuting the conflict. Unilateral armed mobilization against unresisting rivals is not a Civil War — it is a suppression.

**CW-3 — E5 Threshold:**
The internal conflict has reached or exceeded E5 scale — multiple battles, sustained multi-month prosecution, complete mobilization of institutional resources on at least one side. For an entity below regional scale, CW-3 is met when the internal war has committed all of the entity's combat forces for more than one month.

### 13.3 — Civil War Combat Governance

All escalation tiers (Area 5), force legitimacy rules (Area 4), third-party rules (Area 8), and termination conditions (Area 12) apply to Civil War as they do to external war. Additional specific rules:

**Rule 13.3.1 — Enhanced Non-Combatant Protection:**
In a Civil War, the non-combatant population of the contested political entity retains full protected status (PS-1). The shared political entity means that combatants on both sides share a common civilian population — attacks on civilians are attacks on the political entity's foundation and carry maximum consequence weight under Part 27.

**Rule 13.3.2 — Internal Heritage Protection:**
Historical, cultural, and institutional sites of the contested political entity must be protected by all parties unless designated as military objectives under formal E4+ battlefield declaration. Destruction of a political entity's own cultural heritage in the course of a Civil War is an irreversible consequence that Part 27 treats with maximum severity.

**Rule 13.3.3 — No External Acceleration:**
A Civil War's escalation tier cannot be artificially accelerated by external parties. If an external faction's intervention causes the Civil War to escalate beyond E5, the external party bears co-responsibility for the escalation (Section 13.5).

### 13.4 — Neutral Party Status in Civil War

A faction, sect, clan, or individual entity within the contested political entity may declare **neutrality** during the Civil War. To maintain recognized neutral status:

**Neutrality Requirements:**
1. Non-participation: the neutral party takes no hostile action against any Civil War combatant
2. Non-support: the neutral party provides no direct material support — forces, weapons, resources, intelligence — to either side (humanitarian aid may be provided without losing neutral status)
3. Public declaration: neutrality is publicly communicated to both Civil War parties so that neither misidentifies the neutral party as a hidden ally of the other

**Neutral Party Protections:**
Recognized neutral parties within a Civil War are protected from targeting by either side. Attacking a declared neutral party constitutes Force Legitimacy violation and may trigger external intervention rights for the neutral party's defensive response.

**Neutral Party Loss of Status:**
A neutral party loses neutral status the moment they take any action that qualifies as direct support of either Civil War party. Once lost, neutral status cannot be immediately reclaimed — the party must re-declare through a publicly recognized process and must be accepted as neutral by both parties.

### 13.5 — External Intervention in Civil Wars

An external faction that intervenes in a Civil War becomes a full party to the conflict at the civil war's current escalation tier. External intervention rules (Area 8) apply. Additionally:

- External intervention in an E5 Civil War is classified as E6 (cross-entity conflict) from the intervening external faction's perspective
- External intervention triggers the Alliance Activation rules (Section 8.6) for any party that has alliance obligations with the intervening faction
- An external faction that intervenes cannot subsequently claim non-combatant status or attempt neutrality declaration — intervention is a full and irreversible engagement commitment

### 13.6 — Civil War Resolution

A Civil War terminates when one party achieves control over the contested authority structure — recognized control, not merely military dominance. The termination of military hostilities without political resolution leaves the Civil War in a de facto ceasefire state rather than a genuine termination.

Formal Civil War resolution requires: recognition of the winning authority claim by the surviving losing party (or its remnants), establishment of the new authority's legitimacy claim, and a recognized peace-making act (formal surrender, peace treaty, accession agreement) that creates a stable post-war political state.

## Area 14 — Retreat, Withdrawal & Tactical Reset Rules

### 14.1 — The Three Forms of Leaving Combat

A combatant or faction may leave an active combat state through three distinct mechanisms with different canonical meanings and consequences:

**Form 1 — Tactical Retreat:**
Withdrawal to a more defensible position within the same conflict. The retreating party remains in the engagement — they are changing their position, not leaving the fight. Combat rules remain in full effect. The opposing party may pursue.

**Form 2 — Strategic Withdrawal:**
Deliberate departure from the engagement theater with organized force preservation. The withdrawing party is exiting the current engagement but not surrendering the broader conflict. The engagement terminates from the withdrawing party's side; the larger conflict (if E5+) continues. The opposing party may pursue under pursuit rules but the engagement has technically ended from the withdrawing party's side.

**Form 3 — Combat Flight:**
Disorganized departure under existential pressure — the forced abandonment of position and order under overwhelming assault, where preservation of life takes priority over organized military structure. The most psychologically and socially costly form of departure. Combat Flight is not tactical; it is survival. The fleeing party retains all rights to reconsolidate, regroup, and return unless they have offered and accepted surrender.

### 14.2 — Legitimacy of All Three Forms

All three forms of leaving combat are legitimate under this Part. There is no canon rule that requires a cultivator or force to remain in combat until death or incapacitation. Retreat is not surrender. Withdrawal is not defeat. Combat Flight is not dishonor under canon law, though cultivation society's cultural norms (Part 25) may assign social weight to it.

### 14.3 — Legitimate Retreat Mechanics

**Tactical Retreat Requirements:**
- The retreating party must maintain hostile intent — they are retreating to a better position, not abandoning the fight
- The retreating party's force must remain organized and capable of re-engagement
- The retreating party signals retreat in a recognizable way (movement away from the opponent, defensive formation, etc.)

**Strategic Withdrawal Requirements:**
- The withdrawing party's command authority makes the withdrawal decision
- The withdrawal is executed with organized force preservation as the priority
- The withdrawing party's hostile intent toward the opponent ends with the withdrawal

### 14.4 — Pursuit Rules

**Pursuit Legitimacy:**
Pursuing a retreating or withdrawing force is canonically legitimate in combat. The retreat does not create immunity from being followed and re-engaged. Pursuit is part of combat, not a separate act.

**Pursuit Limits:**
1. **Distance Limit:** Pursuit of a retreating or withdrawing force is legitimate for a distance up to three times the original engagement zone's effective radius. Beyond this distance, continued pursuit constitutes a new initiation event (requiring Area 4.3 conditions) rather than continuation of the original engagement.
2. **Protected Zone Limit:** Pursuit cannot continue into protected zones (Area 9.3). A retreating force that reaches a civilian settlement, sacred ground, or a diplomatic zone (PZ-4) gains the protections of that zone, except where the zone has been declared a military objective. Pursuit that violates a protected zone is a Force Legitimacy violation.
3. **Surrender Response Limit:** If a retreating party formally offers surrender during their retreat (Combat Flight into surrender), the pursuing party must respond — once a valid surrender has taken effect under Area 12.5 (Post-Surrender Protections), continued pursuit of that party is an assault, not pursuit combat.
4. **Power Differential Limit:** A significantly superior-grade cultivator pursuing an overwhelmed fleeing cultivator must recognize the cultivator cannot defend themselves. The 3-Level Rule (Part 3, Area 7.2) does not obligate the superior to stop pursuing, but continuing to pursue and attack a combatant more than 3 minor steps below oneself who cannot meaningfully respond may constitute Force Legitimacy violation under certain cultural frameworks (Part 25).

### 14.5 — Tactical Reset

A **Tactical Reset** is a mutually recognized (or independently executed) pause within an ongoing engagement for both parties to recover, regroup, and reassess — without either party intending to terminate the engagement.

**Tactical Reset Characteristics:**
- Both parties disengage from immediate hostile contact
- Neither party pursues the other during the reset
- Both parties retain their engagement status — the fight is not over
- The reset ends when either party re-initiates hostile contact

**Tactical Reset Recognition:**
A Tactical Reset is most clearly recognized in cultivation combat involving Core Realm and above participants, where Core depletion necessitates recovery periods before continued high-output exchange. Both parties may independently recognize the reset without explicit agreement. The test is intent: if both parties have genuinely stopped for recovery and not for withdrawal, it is a Tactical Reset.

**Tactical Reset vs Strategic Withdrawal:**
The distinction is intent. A Tactical Reset preserves engagement — both parties expect to continue. A Strategic Withdrawal exits engagement — the withdrawing party is done with the fight, even if they may re-engage at a later time under a new initiation. A force that claims Tactical Reset and then completes a Strategic Withdrawal during the reset period has violated the reset's implicit terms and is treated as having executed a deceptive withdrawal (Force Legitimacy implication).

## Area 15 — Escalation Coherence Guidance

### 15.1 — Escalation and In-World Scale

Escalation tiers should be coherent with the current in-world power and political scale of the story. A story moment with city-scale political scope will naturally produce different kinds of conflicts than one with continental or world-scale political scope. This is not a hard restriction — it is guidance to ensure that escalation feels earned and that the story's scale evolution has narrative rhythm.

Three dimensions of in-world scale inform escalation coherence:
- **Power Scale:** The cultivation and technology tier of current major participants and the world stage
- **Political Scale:** The scope of political entities, conflicts, and power structures currently active in the narrative
- **Geographic Scale:** The physical scope of the conflict's reach and consequences

Large-scale escalation that arrives without adequate narrative development feels abrupt. Part 30 (Novel Macro Story Law) provides guidance on story rhythm to help authors develop the world-state before introducing escalation at a new scale.

### 15.2 — Escalation Scale Reference

The following table provides a reference for what escalation tiers tend to feel narratively coherent at various scales of in-world power and political development. These are not hard ceilings — they are calibration tools for authors assessing whether a proposed conflict fits the current story scale or requires further narrative development to feel earned:

| Phase (Part 30) | In-World Political Scale | In-World Power Scale | Naturally Coherent Escalation Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1, early | City / Local | Mortal Tier 1–2 | E1–E2 natural; E3 at story climax |
| Phase 1, later (Part 30 Area 1.3) | Regional to Continental | Mortal Tier 2 (Ruler); no Xiantian on the page | E2–E3 natural; E4 at story climax |
| Later Phases (beyond Phase 1) | Continental | Xiantian | E3–E4 natural; E5 possible |
| Later Phases (beyond Phase 1) | Multi-Continental | Core Realm | E4–E5 natural; E6 possible |
| Later Phases (beyond Phase 1) | World-Scale | Divine Realm | E5–E7; all tiers possible |
| Later Phases (beyond Phase 1) | Star System | Divine Realm; Part 8 technology at star-system reach (Part 8 Area 8.2) | E5–E7 natural; all tiers possible |
| Later Phases (beyond Phase 1) | Star Cluster and beyond | Divine Realm; Part 8 technology at star-cluster reach and beyond (Part 8 Area 8.2) | E6–E7 natural; all tiers possible |

Part 30 sets each Phase's actual scale and power ceiling and prevails over this table.

A conflict that significantly exceeds the story's current scale is not impossible — but it is a signal that the world-state needs further development before the conflict is narrated, or that the conflict itself IS the story event that transforms the world-state.

### 15.3 — High-Power Cultivators in Low-Power Story Moments

When a high-realm cultivator is present in a story context where their full power is not narratively appropriate, their restraint should be rendered authentically:

- Combat scenes involving them must reflect their deliberate restraint — through sealed cultivation, focused engagement, or other canonically valid reasons — without implying their true power ceiling is at the context level
- The prose (Part 2) must render their suppressed presence in ways consistent with both their actual Grade's Heavenly Scale signature (Part 3, Area 3.3) and the current narrative context
- Their existence in a low-power story moment is itself a potential narrative event with consequences (Part 27)

### 15.4 — Escalation Assessment Process

When a proposed combat event seems disproportionate to the current story scale, the following assessment is useful:

**Step 1 — Assess Natural Fit:**
Can the essential story function of the proposed conflict be achieved at a scale that fits the current world-state? If yes, calibrate it down. The story will feel more earned and rhythmically coherent.

**Step 2 — Assess Whether This Is the Scale-Changing Event:**
If the conflict's function genuinely requires a larger scale, is this conflict itself the narrative event that transforms the world-state — introducing a new power, faction, or political reality that opens the next chapter of the story? If yes, develop the world-state transformation narrative that earns this moment.

**Step 3 — Validate Against All Active Parts:**
Once the conflict is designed, validate it against the Simultaneous Multi-Part Compliance Law of Part 28. The outcome must satisfy Part 5 (this Part), Part 3 (power rules), and all other applicable Parts simultaneously.

### 15.5 — Escalation as Story Engine

The most impactful escalation events in the Starward Saga are those where the conflict itself transforms the world — introducing power, political stakes, or consequences that change what the story becomes next.

Part 5's escalation tier system is therefore not only a governance tool but a story rhythm tool. Managing what tier conflicts are classified at, and ensuring they fit — or deliberately break — the current world scale, creates the narrative shape of the series' escalation.

**Principle — Escalation Should Be Earned:**
Escalation from one tier to the next should arise from genuine narrative causation — character choices, faction decisions, world events. The trigger conditions for each tier should be met organically, not for spectacle. This is guidance from Part 30 (Novel Macro Story Law) applied at the combat scale: scale changes should feel rhythmically coherent, not abrupt.

**Principle — Escalation Creates World-State Pressure:**
Every significant escalation creates pressure on the current world-state. A series of large-scale conflicts in a story with limited political scope is pressure toward either the story's resolution or its transformation. This pressure is a narrative engine — it is how the world changes through conflict rather than despite it.

## Area 16 — Writing a Battle

### 16.1 — Scope and Standing

Area 16 is craft guidance for narrating a conflict classified E3 to E7. It adds no mechanic. Tier, footprint, declaration, alignment, command and termination stay with Areas 5, 9, 10, 11 and 12. Prose format stays with Part 2 (Area 3), combat texture with Part 36 Area 6 (for battle scale, Part 36 Area 6.4, named POV strands), and Named Move treatment with Part 6 and Part 28 DP-9. Like Area 15, this Area is a guidance post. The Chapter Spec decides which fights are shown, how many Areas the battle takes and the pacing of each. A battle written another way is reported to the author as information and is never a FAIL.

### 16.2 — The Default Shape: Focal Combats and One Wide Shot

**Rule 16.2.1 — Focal Combats:**
A battle is told through a few fights seen close, in plain words and clear pictures of who does what to whom (plain vocabulary, 2026-10-06). A focal combat is a fight between named combatants on the page, written exchange by exchange with the full treatment of Part 28 DP-1 and DP-9 and Part 36 Area 6 (CT-1 to CT-4). As a default shape, each Area of a battle carries two or three focal combats.

**Rule 16.2.2 — The Wide Shot:**
Between the focal combats the rest of the field is seen once, wide, in one paragraph per Area. The wide shot is an image from one place, not a report. It shows what the viewpoint character can see, hear and feel of the whole field (the lines, the ground, the noise, the smoke, which banner has gone down, who has joined or left), in the world's own units, with a concrete detail in every sentence. It carries the battle facts of Areas 9 and 10 that the scene needs. It never stands in for a fight the story needs shown, and it is not summary in place of scene (Part 28 Area 4.3).

**Rule 16.2.3 — Size Does Not Shrink a Focal Combat:**
A clash of five hundred or of fifty thousand gets the same close treatment in its focal fights. A larger battle takes more Areas, each with its own focal combats and wide shot, and a battle that runs across Areas carries from one to the next under the Area-to-Area carry-over rule of Part 2. It is not squeezed into one Area by summary.

### 16.3 — The Spec and the Viewpoint

**Rule 16.3.1 — The Spec Names the Focal Fights:**
The chapter spec names the focal fights of a battle Area as scene anchors in the Area block (Part 38), and in the Fight line where the spec carries one. Each is chosen for story: the fight that turns the battle, the fight that costs a named character something, the fight a protagonist must win or lose, the fight that shows what this tier's power does. Every other fight on the field stays in the wide shot, and none is owed a scene.

**Rule 16.3.2 — The Tier Is Stated Once:**
The spec states the tier (E3 to E7, with the Formal Duel type if one applies) once, in the Fight line of the Area block. The prose does not name the tier. It shows it through the footprint (Area 9.2), the Battlefield Declaration (Area 9.4), the command structure (Area 5.2, E4), the banners and the time the battle takes. A change of tier inside the battle is stated in the Area where it happens (Rule 5.4.2).

**Rule 16.3.3 — One Place at a Time:**
The viewpoint character takes in their own part of the field and hears the rest. What they know of a far flank reaches them by a channel the world has (noise, a runner, a signal, a change in the line; Part 24) and reaches them late. A wide shot from a height, a tower or a signal post is lawful when the spec puts the viewpoint character there.

**Rule 16.3.4 — The Cost Is Shown Close:**
Casualties, a leader's death (Area 11) and the turn of the battle are shown in a focal fight, or seen in the wide shot beside a named person near enough to feel them. The battle ends on the end class the spec names (Area 12), shown as the last event of the Area. Its aftermath takes its own Area (Rule 12.6; Part 27).
