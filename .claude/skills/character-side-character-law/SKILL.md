**Authority Tier:** 1 (Domain Authority — Character; Parts 2–27, 30–33, 35, 37–38; see `.claude/guardrails/authority/guardrail-authority-tier-system.md`)

## Overview

Part 19 is the Starward Saga Canon's Side Character Law — the governing domain for every named character who is neither a Part 17 hero nor a Part 18 primary villain but who appears in about three or more scenes (a scene is one Area; the count is a guide, never a gate) and contributes meaningfully to the story. Part 16 classifies what a character *is*; Parts 17 and 18 govern the protagonists and primary antagonists; Part 19 governs everyone else who makes the world inhabited — mentors, allies, rivals, faction figures, civilians, and the villain's own people — as fully governed persons rather than plot devices.

Four foundational rules (Area 1) establish that side characters are subject to the full force of the Canon's physical, cultivation, ideology, psychology, and consequence laws; that they are not required to support heroes; that they carry no survival protection and can die; and that they have independent will. On top of these rules sits the four-tier Side Character Depth Spectrum — Tier A (Major), Tier B (Supporting), Tier C (Named Incidental), Tier D (Named Conversational) — which scales governance depth to actual narrative prominence. *(Tier D is a registry row carrying a compact picked code and carries no profile — see Area 1.2, Rules 19.1.2.3 through 19.1.2.7. Nothing promotes automatically, recurrence never promotes, and genuine consequence is flagged to the author, who rules every time — Rule 19.1.2.4.)*

From these foundations, Part 19 builds outward across 15 Areas: the eight primary narrative functions and their governing sub-rules — Mentor Death Law, Ally Cost Law, Rival Credibility Law (Area 2); side character ideology and the ensemble ideological-diversity standard (Area 3); psychology, wounds, and the PIS weight of side character death (Area 4); the consequence architecture under which sacrifices are never free and betrayal is a simultaneous CSS and IE event (Area 5); the mandatory eight-section Side Character Profile and its update triggers (Area 6); relationship law toward the hero, the villain, and other side characters (Area 7); combat governance (Area 8); the lifecycle law of introduction, tier promotion, function evolution, and governed removal (Area 9); the ensemble as the human architecture of the world (Area 10); social and civilizational position (Area 11); position-appropriate information architecture (Area 12); cultivation and technology power governance (Area 13); the eight narration standards N19-1 through N19-8 (Area 14); and the full cross-Part dependency master table (Area 15).

This file is the single authority for the whole of Part 19; there is no layer beneath it. The shared cross-reference table and governing-authority statement sit at the foot in Appendix A and Appendix B.

## Area 1 — Foundational Principles of Side Characters

### 1.1 — What a Side Character Is

**Definition:** A side character is any **named character** in the Starward Saga who does not serve as a protagonist (Part 17 hero) or as a primary antagonist (Part 18 villain) in the current arc, but who appears in three or more scenes and contributes meaningfully to the story through their relationship with those central figures, their role in the world's events, or their own independent existence within the world. A *scene* here is one Area (Part 2). Three is a guide to the typical point for a full ledger, never a gate: the story and the Chapter Spec decide, and Part 37 Threshold C-1 stays the file rule. A named person who appears fewer than three times is Tier C (or Tier D under Area 1.2); the count never gates the tier.

Side characters in the Starward Saga may be:
- Mentor figures who shape the hero's cultivation, ideology, or psychology
- Allies who fight beside the hero and carry their own costs for doing so
- Rivals who push the hero without being villains
- Faction leaders, sect elders, or civilian figures whose choices affect the arc's landscape
- Characters from the villain's perspective — lieutenants, followers, or innocents caught in the villain's path
- Characters who connect the hero's world to larger systems — political figures, scholars, information holders
- Characters who represent what the hero could become, avoid, or return to
- Characters who simply live in the world and whose lives are made real by the hero's passage through it

> **Scope note.** The *"three or more scenes"* threshold in the definition above stands exactly as written for every governed tier. It does **not** reach **Tier D — Named Conversational** (Area 1.2), which is a **conversational** speaking role recorded as one registry row and is not a claim that Part 19 governs the person. Recurrence does not promote and does not end the tier. See Rules 19.1.2.4 and 19.1.2.5.

**Rule 19.1.1.1 — Side Characters Are Fully Governed:** Every named side character who appears in about three or more scenes (a scene is one Area; the count is a guide, never a gate) is subject to the full force of the Canon's physical, cultivation, ideology, psychology, and consequence laws. Their cultivation realm follows Part 3. Their ideology follows Part 20. Their psychology follows Part 26. Their consequences follow Part 27. The world does not apply different physics to side characters.

**Rule 19.1.1.2 — Side Characters Are Not Required to Support Heroes:** A side character does not exist to support the hero. A side character may challenge, oppose, betray, abandon, inspire, comfort, burden, or outlive the hero. Their narrative function is defined by their authentic existence within the world, not by their utility to the protagonist.

**Rule 19.1.1.3 — Side Characters Can Die:** Unlike heroes (Part 17 Rule 17.1.3.1), side characters carry no survival protection. Named side characters can die, be permanently removed from the story, or be fundamentally transformed in ways that end their previous character identity. Side character deaths are major narrative events when the character has been sufficiently developed — precisely because the reader has been given a reason to care.

**Rule 19.1.1.4 — Side Characters Have Independent Will:** Every named side character has their own will, ideology, and agenda. They make choices based on their own ideological profile and psychological state. They are not chess pieces that move to serve the plot. A side character who always happens to do what serves the hero's immediate needs is a canon violation.

### 1.2 — The Side Character Depth Spectrum

> **Tier D rules (single source).** Rules 19.1.2.3 to 19.1.2.7 below are the one statement of the Tier D rules: what the tier is for, that nothing promotes automatically and consequence goes to the author, that the three-or-more-scenes threshold does not reach it, that the person is drawn from registered systems, and the compact code with its finding and 100-row housekeeping rule. Part 37 Area 2.11, Part 39 and the side index point here and do not restate them. A character holding a Tier C or higher registration is never moved down into Tier D (note under Rule 19.1.2.1).

Not all side characters require equal depth. Part 19 defines a **four-tier depth spectrum** based on narrative prominence:

| Tier | Definition | Governance Requirement |
|------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Tier A — Major Side Characters** | Characters who appear across multiple arcs, have sustained relationships with heroes or villains, and who carry significant narrative weight | Full Side Character Profile (Area 6); Ideology Ledger entry (Part 20); Psychological Profile (Part 26); Consequence Arc record (Part 27) |
| **Tier B — Supporting Side Characters** | Characters who appear in a single arc or a concentrated section of an arc and who have a defined relationship and narrative function | Side Character Profile kept as a ledger file (Rule 19.6.1.1), populated as far as the role needs; brief Ideology entry; basic psychological note |
| **Tier C — Named Incidental Characters** | Characters who are named and have individual identity but appear briefly and serve a defined singular function | Character classification (Part 16); at minimum one distinguishing characteristic that makes them a person rather than a prop |
| **Tier D — Named Conversational** | Characters introduced by the drafting agent so that a conversation has two sides — a name, a face, and speech, **drawn from the systems already registered around them** | **One registry row** in `ledger-character-side/_index.md`: name · one-line descriptor · Area of first appearance · **the Code (Baseline) cell, one compact string the drafting agent picks (Rule 19.1.2.7)** · **whether they are a pair-senior or equivalent, where the formation registers one**. **No profile, no ledger file, no Part 39 Layer register, no registered Signature, no prosodic registration, no provenance tag, and no Part 39 apparatus of any kind beyond the picked code.** Written at Gate 2 (`guardrails/audit/guardrail-audit-gate-law.md`), never before. This is the single statement of the Tier D rules; Part 39, Part 37 and the side index point here |

> **THE TIER D ROW.** The same person may be used again, and being used again promotes nothing (Rule 19.1.2.4). The picked code exists so that the same person is the same person three chapters later and for no other purpose. It is not a Layer register, a registered Signature or a miniature Part 39 registration — a pass that writes any of those into a Tier D row has promoted the character without authority (`character-behavioral-synthesis-law` §4.1). **Every hard bar of the tier stands**: they may speak; they may not carry consequence unflagged; no Part 39 registration is required of them or implied about them.

**Rule 19.1.2.1 — Tier Assignment Is Based on Narrative Reality:** A character begins as Tier C and may be upgraded to Tier B or Tier A as their narrative significance increases. Tier assignment must reflect actual narrative prominence, not intended narrative prominence. If a character who was planned as Tier C has organically become a significant presence across multiple scenes, they must be upgraded.

> **Note on this rule's entry point.** Rule 19.1.2.1 says a character *"begins as Tier C,"* and that remains true of every character who is **planned** into the story: an outline or a Chapter Spec that names a person enters them at Tier C or above, registered before drafting at the tier the spec gives. **A person the Chapter Spec names is never Tier D.** A second entry point exists for a person the drafting agent introduces mid-Area purely so a conversation has two sides, who enters at Tier D; that row is written at Gate 2 of the first appearance, never before. A person the drafting agent improvises in the prose may enter at Tier C or at Tier D, and either is registered at Gate 2 from what the prose established, and every Layer or field the prose did not settle is marked `OPEN-AREA` (Part 39 Rules 39.4.2.1 and 39.4.3.1; Part 37 Threshold C-1). **Both entry points are live and neither displaces the other.** A character does not pass *down* into Tier D from Tier C; Tier D is an origin, never a demotion, and Rule 19.9.2.1's transitions run upward from wherever a character entered. No character holding a Tier C or higher registration may be moved down into Tier D, because doing so would strip registered material the ledger already carries. A Tier D person who is about to carry genuine consequence is still flagged to the author (Rule 19.1.2.4). Tier D rows already in the side index before any Area was written are pre-registered plan baseline, confirmed or amended at Gate 2 when the person is first shown.

**Rule 19.1.2.2 — Tier A Characters Require Full Governance:** Tier A side characters are governed with nearly the same depth as heroes, minus the survival protection and arc phase structure of Part 17. Their ideology, psychology, and consequence arc are fully tracked and must be internally consistent.

**Rule 19.1.2.3 — Tier D: What It Is For, and the Failure It Exists to Fix.**
Tier D exists because a named person who cannot speak is not cheaper than a registered one — they are worse than absent. Where a scene asks a man a direct question and canon permits no reply, the drafting agent is forced to narrate the answer *at* him, and the result is a conversation with one side missing. Tier D is the route by which such a person answers in their own voice at the cost of a single line in an index.

- **A Tier D character MAY speak freely in conversation.** This is the whole of the tier's purpose and is not to be narrowed. They may answer, ask, joke, complain, refuse, and be wrong.
- **A Tier D character MAY NOT make a consequential choice, drive a beat, or carry a story-state decision** — **not unflagged, and never on the drafting agent's own judgment.** They talk; they do not turn the plot. **Where genuine consequence arrives anyway, the character is flagged to the author and the author rules** (Rule 19.1.2.4).
- **A Tier D character carries no registered behavioral profile**, and therefore no Part 39 Signature recurrence report or waiting beat is owed for them, and Signature conformance is owed in light form only (Check #20 element 20-2) (see `character-behavioral-synthesis-law` §4.1 and Rule 39.4.3.1a). **The picked code their registry row carries is not a profile and does not become one** (Rules 19.1.2.6 and 19.1.2.7).
- **Recurrence never promotes**, so the tier has no single-appearance limit; **a Tier D character may appear again, and again, at Tier D.** Their registry row is written once, at the Gate 2 of their first appearance, and it is not rewritten for each later use. See Rule 19.1.2.4.

Further promotion from Tier C upward runs under Rule 19.9.2.1.

**Rule 19.1.2.4 — Nothing Promotes Automatically; Genuine Consequence Is Flagged and the Author Rules.**

The author's words, verbatim, are the whole of this rule's substance: ***"Nothing is automatic and flagged when there is genuine consequence - I rule on it everytime."***

**1 — NOTHING PROMOTES AUTOMATICALLY.** There is no automatic promotion out of Tier D, in any circumstance, on any trigger, by any agent. In particular, and stated so that none of the three is ever reinstated by inference:

- **Recurrence NEVER promotes.** A Tier D character who appears in a later Area stays Tier D.
- **Speech NEVER promotes.** Speaking is what the tier is *for*; no amount of it, in any register, at any length, moves the character anywhere.
- **Repeated appearance across Areas, Chapters or Sections NEVER promotes.** Frequency is not a trigger, and no counter of appearances exists at this tier.

**2 — ON GENUINE CONSEQUENCE, THE CHARACTER IS FLAGGED TO THE AUTHOR.** Where a Tier D character is about to make a decision that changes story state — a choice, a beat the story must remember, an act with a consequence attached — **the drafting or auditing agent stops and raises the character to the author via `AskUserQuestion`.** The author rules, **every time**, and the ruling is one of two:

- **PROMOTE TO TIER C** — the mandatory-core six Layers plus one Signature (Part 39 Rule 39.4.2.1), completed before the consequential appearance is drafted, with the picked code carried as Part 39 Rule 39.4.3.2a sets out; or
- **KEEP THEM TIER D** — and let the moment stand as written.

**THE AGENT NEVER DECIDES THIS.** Not by defaulting to promotion, not by defaulting against it, not by reading the act down into conversation so that the question does not arise, and not by treating an inability to ask as licence to choose (`guardrails/quality/guardrail-threshold-consultation.md`, Routing — an agent that finds the trigger reports it to the orchestrating command, which asks the author). **An unflagged consequential act is the violation; the consequence itself is not.**

**3 — TIMING.** **The *question* is the precondition of drafting, not a registration.** The flag is raised at the moment the consequence is identified — at spec time if the spec plans it, at draft time if the draft reaches it, at Gate 1 if an audit finds it — and **drafting of that act waits on the author's answer, not on a registration.** Where the author rules `KEEP THEM TIER D`, nothing is registered and nothing is owed.

**4 — WHAT THIS RULE DOES NOT DO.** It does not lower the bar for any other tier. It does not permit an agent to promote a character on its own initiative in either direction. It does not create a Tier D counter, budget, quota or threshold of any kind. And it does not make Tier D a permanent home by decree either — **a character the story keeps may still be promoted, on the author's word and on nothing else.**

**Rule 19.1.2.5 — The Three-or-More-Scenes Threshold Does Not Reach Tier D.**
Area 1.1's definition of a side character — a named character who *"appears in three or more scenes"* — is the threshold for Part 19's **governed** tiers. It does not reach Tier D: **the three-scene threshold is a test for entry into the governed tiers, and Tier D sits outside them — so a Tier D character may reach three scenes, or thirty, without crossing anything.** **A Tier D row is therefore not a claim that Part 19 governs the person**; it is a record that a named voice was used, **and the address at which the same voice is found again so that it comes back as the same person.**

**Counting a Tier D character's appearances against the three-scene line, in either direction, is the error this rule exists to prevent.**

**Rule 19.1.2.6 — Tier D Is Expressly Negotiable Canon: Draw the Person From the Systems Already Registered Around Them.**

The author's words, verbatim: ***"Not every person gets to Tier C, Tier D is where AI can take benefit of existing knowledge and create information and share for characters which are not defined but defined in existing systems. pick something and use. This is where negotiable part comes in."***

**Tier D is Negotiable Canon under Part 28 Area 2** (`guardrails/authority/guardrail-locked-vs-negotiable-canon.md`), and it is named as such by the author rather than classified there by an agent. That has one operational meaning and it is the whole of this rule:

**1 — WHAT THE DRAFTING AGENT DRAWS FROM.** A Tier D character's behaviour is **rendered out of the systems that are already registered around them**, and the agent is expected to reach for them rather than to ask for a registration:

- the applicable **Collective Behavior Codes** they are visibly inside (Part 39 Rule 39.23.1.1 — the code lives on the collective's ledger);
- the **military or institutional ladder** they sit on, and the rung they sit at;
- their **formation's established culture**, as its own faction ledger holds it;
- their **trade**; and
- **the role itself** — what a person doing that job, in that place, at that rung, does with their hands and their answer.

The author's phrase for this is ***"pick something and use."*** **Pick from what exists, and render it.**

**2 — WHAT IT IS NOT A LICENCE FOR.** This is a licence to **use existing knowledge**, not a licence to invent. It creates **no** authority to coin a new system, a new institution, a new address form, an honorific, a rank, a rank title, a unit rung, a rite, an oath, or anything a higher-tier rule reserves. **Part 28 Area 12's prohibitions, Part 28 Area 20's naming law, Part 29 Area 16's address law and the standing no-coining bar all apply to a Tier D character exactly as they apply to a hero**, and an agent that needs an address form which does not exist **stops and says so** rather than supplying one at this tier because the tier is cheap.

**3 — THE PICKED CODE.** Every Tier D registry row carries the compact code of Rule 19.1.2.7. **The code exists for one reason: so that the same person is the same person three chapters later.** It is not a profile, a summary of a profile, or the beginning of one.

**No Layer register. No registered Signature. No provenance tags. No contradiction pair, no costly behavior, no drift map. No Part 39 apparatus of any kind beyond the picked code of Rule 19.1.2.7.** A pass that writes any of those into a Tier D row has promoted the character without authority, and the remedy is to remove what it wrote, not to complete it (`character-behavioral-synthesis-law` §4.1).

**4 — EVERY HARD BAR STANDS.** They may speak. They may not carry consequence unflagged (Rule 19.1.2.4). No Part 39 registration is required of them or implied about them. **Negotiable does not mean unbounded; it means the material is drawn rather than invented.**

**Rule 19.1.2.7 — The Tier D Compact Code: An Instant Character Is Picked, Not Registered.** *(The grammar, the limits and the Gate 1 validation are `character-behavioral-synthesis-law` Section 3.5.)*

**1 — WHAT IT IS.** A new Tier D person is defined by one compact code: a single comma-separated string of short tokens held in the Code (Baseline) cell of their registry row. It carries a role, an age band, a speech register and two prosodic items, three to six conduct codes with one marked Signature, and the power, class and inner-life baselines. It is what "pick something and use" (Rule 19.1.2.6) produces when it is written down: nothing is elaborated, and every person is defined.

**2 — WHO PICKS, AND WHEN.** The drafting agent picks the code alone, while it drafts a newcomer who speaks and carries no consequence, before Gate 1. It asks no question, and the author has no in-flow veto; `[REVISE AREA]` is the author's remedy for a person, as it is for any minor name. Gate 1 validates the code, and the code is never printed in chat (Part 39 Rule 39.3.5.7). Gate 2 writes the row with the code exactly as validated. The author reads the code in the side index.

**3 — AUTHORITY-BEARING TOKENS.** The tokens that carry power, class and inner-life values are picked within the limits of Part 39 Rule 39.3.5.5 (the Phase ceiling, the formation's ledger, Locked Canon, baseline values only), and each carries an `[AI]` mark. Only the author changes them.

**4 — WHAT IT IS NOT.** The code is not a registration. It is not a profile, a Layer register, a registered Signature or a provenance tag; it opens no Section 11 or Section 12; it is never compared with another character's code; and it never permits an act beyond speech (Rule 19.1.2.4). A person who acts or decides is flagged to the author, not coded.

**5 — THE PURPOSE THAT SURVIVES.** Tier D stays cheap. Speech is what the tier is for. Nothing promotes automatically. Consequence goes to the author, who rules every time. A reading of this Rule that adds an interview, a counter, a promotion trigger or an approval step to the tier is a misreading.

**6 — PROMOTION.** When the author rules promote-to-Tier-C, the code is carried as Part 39 Rule 39.4.3.2a says, and the author sees the `[AI]` tokens first.

**7 — FINDING A PERSON, AND KEEPING THE REGISTRY USABLE.** A Tier D person is found by Grep of the name in the registry in `ledger-character-side/_index.md`; the table is never read whole. When the Tier D registry passes about 100 rows, it moves from `ledger-character-side/_index.md` to `ledger-character-side/_tier-d.md`, and `_index.md` keeps a pointer to it. The figure is a signal to split the file and nothing else: it is not a quota, and no count of Tier D people limits, blocks or fails anything (Rule 19.1.2.4, point 4; Part 39 Rule 39.3.5.6).

## Area 2 — Side Character Narrative Functions

### 2.1 — The Eight Primary Narrative Functions

Every Tier A and Tier B side character has a primary narrative function — the core role they serve in the story's structure. They may serve secondary functions simultaneously.

**Function I — The Mentor:** A character whose experience, wisdom, cultivation mastery, or ideological development is significantly greater than the hero's at the time of their relationship. The Mentor provides knowledge, frames challenges, reflects the hero's growth back to them, and — critically — is not immortal or infallible. Mentors die, fail, lose faith, make critical errors, and reveal limitations. The Mentor who is always right and always available is a narrative crutch, not a character.

**Rule 19.2.1.1 — Mentor Death Law:** Mentors can die, and in many arc structures, they must. The death of a mentor — if the mentor has been properly built — is a Crucible Event (Part 17 Area 7) for the hero and is a permanent event, graded as guidance at about CSS-7 to CSS-8 (a guidance post, not a floor; the story and the Chapter Spec decide). The narrative must have built the mentor sufficiently that their death carries full weight.

**Function II — The Ally:** A character who stands with the hero not because they must but because they have chosen to based on their own ideology, their own relationships, and their own assessment of the conflict. Allies have their own cultivation power, their own faction allegiances, and their own cost for their loyalty. An ally who suffers no cost for their support is not a character — they are a resource.

**Rule 19.2.1.2 — Ally Cost Law:** Every significant ally action has a cost for the ally. They sacrifice cultivation resources, risk their life, damage their faction relationships, or pay an ideological price. These costs are tracked as CSS events (Part 27) for the ally character. Allies who pay no cost exist outside the canonical world.

**Function III — The Rival:** A character who challenges the hero from within the same broad alignment — a fellow sect member, a fellow soldier, a character with similar goals but different methods or ideology. Rivals push the hero's growth through competition, ideological confrontation, and calibrated opposition. The rival is not an enemy; their opposition is fundamentally different in character from villain opposition.

**Rule 19.2.1.3 — Rival Credibility Law:** A rival who is clearly inferior to the hero in all dimensions provides no genuine challenge. Rivals must have areas of genuine superiority — a higher cultivation realm, a different and effective approach, a clearer ideological commitment — that make the competition real.

**Function IV — The Mirror:** A character whose life trajectory parallels the hero's but has taken a different direction — showing what the hero could have become, is avoiding becoming, or fears they are becoming. The Mirror side character is a thematic function: their existence asks the question "Is the hero different from this, or only fortunate?" A mirror or foil relationship is recorded only on the author's ruling (through AskUserQuestion), after each character is designed standalone, and is never used to design either.

**Function V — The World Carrier:** A character whose existence makes the world concrete and human — a sect elder who represents a institution's living culture, a farmer whose village represents what the villain is destroying, a scholar whose knowledge represents what the hero is fighting to protect. World Carriers are the human face of the story's larger systems. They do not need cultivation power to carry narrative weight.

**Function VI — The Consequence Witness:** A character who observes and is affected by the hero's or villain's actions, and whose treatment makes the consequences of those actions real. The Consequence Witness is the mechanism by which Part 27 CSS events become narratively legible: when the hero's action destroys the Consequence Witness's livelihood, the CSS event has a human face.

**Function VII — The Information Carrier:** A character who holds knowledge — of the world, of the villain's history, of a cultivation technique, of a political situation — that the hero needs and must obtain through relationship rather than force. The Information Carrier creates narrative structure around information acquisition (Part 24) and relationship building.

**Function VIII — The Foil:** A character whose qualities, choices, or fate are directly contrasted with the hero's or villain's to illuminate something about the central characters. The Foil does not need to be in direct relationship with the central character — their existence and fate are the narrative commentary. A mirror or foil relationship is recorded only on the author's ruling (through AskUserQuestion), after each character is designed standalone, and is never used to design either.

### 2.2 — Secondary Narrative Functions

Side characters commonly serve multiple functions simultaneously. A Mentor can also be a World Carrier. An Ally can also be a Mirror. These combinations are permitted and often produce the most vivid characters. The primary function governs how the character's arc is structured; secondary functions are additional layers.

## Area 3 — Side Character Ideology

### 3.1 — Side Character Ideology Requirements

**Rule 19.3.1.1 — Every Named Side Character Has an Ideology:** All named side characters who appear in about three or more scenes (a scene is one Area, as in Area 1.1) typically have an ideology governed by Part 20. The count is a guide, never a gate. The ideology need not be mapped in the same depth as a hero's or villain's, but it must exist — it must be internally coherent, and it must be visible in the character's behavior and choices.

**Rule 19.3.1.2 — Side Character Ideology Must Be Independent:** A side character's ideology is their own, not a function of their relationship with the hero. An ally who shares the hero's ideology exactly is less narratively interesting than an ally whose own ideology produces a different relationship with the same events. Ideological alignment between hero and side character should be partial, not total.

**Rule 19.3.1.3 — Side Character Ideology Changes Through IE Events:** Side characters experience IE events (Part 20 Area 3.3). A mentor whose ideology is challenged by the hero's choices may shift. An ally who witnesses a major consequence event may experience an IE-4 recalibration. Side character ideological changes are not required to be tracked with the same detail as heroes, but they must be internally consistent and event-driven.

### 3.2 — Side Character Ideology Diversity

The most effective side character ensemble in the Starward Saga covers the full spectrum of ideological categories (Part 20 Area 2.1) without clustering all allies into a single ideological type. Heroes surrounded only by characters who share their exact ideology are in an ideological echo chamber — this is a narration violation under N19-5.

**Ideological Diversity Standard:** A hero's Tier A ally ensemble (all Tier A allies together) usually spans several distinct primary ideology categories; the number is information only and no minimum is checked. This ensures that the hero regularly encounters ideological perspectives they must engage with genuinely rather than simply reinforce their own.

## Area 4 — Side Character Psychology

### 4.1 — Side Character Psychological Requirements

**Rule 19.4.1.1 — Tier A Side Characters Have Full Psychological Architecture:** Tier A side characters have the four-layer psychological architecture (Part 26: Conscious / Emotional-Somatic / Identity / Will) tracked with sufficient depth to make their behavior internally coherent. PIS events affecting Tier A side characters must be tracked and must visibly affect their behavior.

**Rule 19.4.1.2 — Side Character Psychological Wounds Are Real:** The wounds a Tier A side character carries are not background flavor — they are governance constraints. A mentor who carries a grief-wound (PIS-6) from the loss of their own teacher reacts differently to the hero's decisions than a mentor who carries no such wound. The psychological architecture must be applied.

**Rule 19.4.1.3 — Side Character Psychological Development Is Permitted:** Side characters can grow psychologically across the arc. A Tier A ally who moves from PIS-5 toward genuine resolution through the events of the arc is undergoing real Part 26 resolution pathway progression. This growth is not as rigorously tracked as a hero's but it must be internally consistent.

### 4.2 — Side Character Death and Psychological Governance

When a Tier A side character dies or is permanently removed from the story, the psychological governance of that event applies to all characters who were in relationship with them. The hero's PIS impact from a Tier A ally death is graded by the depth of the relationship (Part 26 Area 2.2). The narrative must apply this impact.

**Rule 19.4.2.1 — Side Character Death Must Be Narratively Real:** The death of a Tier A side character cannot be treated as a plot event that is acknowledged and then functionally ignored. The hero's response to the death — their psychological state, their subsequent behavior, their ideological reconsideration — must reflect the PIS weight of the loss. A hero who loses their Tier 1 Ally (Part 17 Area 6.2, Section 7) and is functionally unaffected in subsequent scenes is a narration violation.

## Area 5 — Side Character Consequence Architecture

### 5.1 — Side Characters and Part 27

All named side characters carry a consequence arc under Part 27. Their actions produce CSS events. The consequences of their choices — to support the hero, to betray them, to sacrifice themselves, to refuse to act — propagate through the world.

**Rule 19.5.1.1 — Side Character Sacrifices Are Not Free:** When a Tier A side character sacrifices something significant — their cultivation path, their faction standing, their life, their relationship — this sacrifice is a real consequence event for every character whose world it changes. The hero who benefits from an ally's sacrifice carries Part 27 debt (CSS consequence weight of the sacrifice). This debt must be narratively acknowledged.

**Rule 19.5.1.2 — Side Character Betrayal Is a CSS Event:** When a Tier A side character betrays the hero or villain they were affiliated with, this is simultaneously a CSS event for the betraying character (the relational consequence of betrayal), a CSS event for the betrayed character (the harm received), and an IE event (Part 20) for both — the betrayer's ideology has produced a rupture; the betrayed character's ideology about trust and loyalty has been challenged.

**Rule 19.5.1.3 — Side Character Consequence Accumulation Is Visible:** Like villain consequences, the accumulating consequences of a side character's choices must be visible in their present state. A Tier A ally who has sacrificed their sect standing, broken a covenant, and used forbidden techniques to support the hero carries those choices — their social position reflects them, their psychology reflects them, their faction relationships reflect them.

## Area 6 — The Side Character Profile

### 6.1 — Side Character Profile Mandate

**Rule 19.6.1.1 — Every Tier A and Tier B Side Character Requires a Side Character Profile:** Every Tier A and Tier B side character requires a canonical Side Character Profile — a governance document tracking the character's complete state. A Tier A or Tier B profile is kept as a full ledger file on the Part 37 Area 2.1 template, its Ledger Type reading Tier A or Tier B (Part 37 Threshold C-1). Tier C characters are not required to have formal profiles.

**Rule 19.6.1.2 — Mandatory Side Character Profile Update Triggers:** The Side Character Profile must be updated when any of the following occur:
- Cultivation breakthrough or technology tier advancement
- Significant combat event
- IE-3+ ideology event
- PIS-4+ psychological event (a PIS-3 event is a one-line tally per Section, Part 26 Area 1.3)
- CSS-3+ consequence event
- Major relationship change (death of a connected character, betrayal, new alliance formation)
- Functional narrative role change (mentor becomes obstacle; ally becomes enemy)

### 6.2 — The Eight Side Character Profile Sections

**Section 1 — Identity Baseline:**
- Full name and aliases
- Depth tier (A or B)
- Primary narrative function (Mentor / Ally / Rival / Mirror / World Carrier / Consequence Witness / Information Carrier / Foil) and secondary functions if applicable
- Current canonical status (Active / Removed / Deceased / Transformed)
- Connection to hero(es) and villain(s) — nature of relationship and current state
- Home faction and location

**Section 2 — Cultivation and Technology Classification:**
- Current cultivation realm and CC-Class (Part 16)
- Affinity type and development level (Part 4)
- Technology tier if applicable (Part 8, Part 16)
- Every other Section 2 field of the Part 37 Area 2.1 side-character template (Part 37 governs the field list). Soul rung, Heart level, military rank, sect position, weapon classification, integration type and fusion stage are Hero Ledger fields (Part 37 Area 2.3); the side ledger carries none of them

**Section 3 — Power and Capability Snapshot:**
- Signature Moves and key combat techniques (Part 6)
- Manual Kit (Part 42): a pointer line to the manual IDs this character holds in the Manual Ledger (`ledger-manuals/character-kits.csv`), or "none"; the Manual Ledger owns the values of every manual-borne move (Part 37 Area 2.1)
- Primary capability differentials relative to the hero — where the side character exceeds and where they fall short
- Known vulnerabilities or limitations

**Section 4 — Ideology Profile:**
- Primary Ideology Category and sub-variant (Part 20)
- Current Ideology Stability State (IS-1 through IS-5)
- Core/Operational/Surface ideology summary
- Active ideological tensions — where their ideology creates friction with the hero or villain
- Key IE events in their history

**Section 5 — Psychological Profile:**
- Current PIS state (Part 26)
- Primary psychological wounds and their origin events
- Primary coping mechanisms
- Psychological relationship to the hero — how the side character psychologically frames the hero

**Section 6 — Consequence Arc:**
- Active consequences (CSS category and severity)
- Notable historical consequence events
- Outstanding consequence debts — who are they indebted to, and through what actions?

**Section 7 — Relationship Web:**
- Relationship to all connected heroes (type, depth, current state, Part 17 Hero Ledger Section 7 cross-reference)
- Relationship to villains if applicable
- Relationship to other significant side characters
- Faction affiliations (Part 23 AFF scale)

**Section 8 — Narrative Arc Status:**
- Current narrative function status — what role are they actively serving at this point in the arc?
- Arc trajectory — what is their likely narrative direction?
- Unresolved narrative threads — what questions their existence raises that must be answered

## Area 7 — Side Character Relationship Law

### 7.1 — Side Character Relationship to the Hero

**Rule 19.7.1.1 — Side Characters Must Change the Hero:** Every Tier A side character must, through their relationship with the hero, change the hero in some meaningful way — shift the hero's ideology, challenge their psychological assumptions, expand their worldview, or force a consequence reckoning. A Tier A side character who exists through the entire arc without changing the hero is a narration violation.

**Rule 19.7.1.2 — Side Characters Are Changed by the Hero:** Equally, the hero changes the side character. The direction of change is not predetermined — the hero may make a side character better, or worse, or simply different. A Tier A mentor who is completely unaffected by the hero they mentored has not been in a real relationship.

**Rule 19.7.1.3 — Side Character Relationships Have Natural Limits:** A side character relationship that serves the hero perfectly, never creates friction, and is always available when needed is not a relationship — it is a resource allocation. Real relationships have limits: mentors have their own struggles, allies have their own agendas, rivals have their own costs. These limits are narrative assets, not narrative problems.

### 7.2 — Side Character Relationship to the Villain

Side characters who are connected to villains — subordinates, former allies, objects of the villain's consequence arc, or characters living in the villain's shadow — are governed under Part 19 for their individual character governance and Part 18 for their relationship to the villain's agenda.

**Rule 19.7.2.1 — Villain-Connected Side Characters Are Not Villain Agents:** A side character who serves a villain faction is not governed as a villain unless they independently meet the criteria for Part 18 governance (named antagonist in about three or more scenes, the count being a guide and never a gate, whose opposition to the protagonist drives the conflict). A soldier in a villain's army is a Tier B or C side character. The general who actively opposes the hero across the arc is a Part 18 villain. *(Pointer: the four-tier Depth Spectrum of Area 1.2 is reused as depth of record by Part 18 Area 6.3, where a tracked minor antagonist or Sub-Villain is a side villain at Tier C or D, and by Part 40 Area 6 for monsters. Nothing in Area 1.2 changes, and a monster serving a villain is not a villain, Part 40 Rule 40.12.4.3.)*

**Rule 19.7.2.2 — Side Character Defection from Villain Is a Major Arc Event:** When a Tier A side character who has been serving or affiliated with a villain defects to the hero's side — or is liberated from the villain's control — this is simultaneously a Part 18 CSS event for the villain, a Part 27 CSS event for the side character, and a Part 20 IE event for all parties involved.

### 7.3 — Side Character Relationships to Each Other

Side characters have relationships with each other that are not merely mediated through the hero. Two allies may conflict. A mentor may have history with a rival. A world carrier may have a family relationship with an information carrier. These inter-side-character relationships are governed by Part 23 (Affiliation Law) and add depth to the world's sense of being populated by people rather than by plot functions.

**Rule 19.7.3.1 — Inter-Side-Character Relationships Must Be Consistent:** If two Tier A side characters have a defined relationship, that relationship must be consistent with their individual ideology profiles, psychological profiles, and history. Two characters whose ideological profiles make close alliance impossible but who are narrated as close allies are a canon violation.

## Area 8 — Side Character in Combat

### 8.1 — Combat Governance for Side Characters

All side character combat scenes are governed by Part 5 (Cultivation — Combat and Fight Law) and Part 6 (Move Taxonomy Law). The cultivation power and technique mastery of side characters follow the same Canon laws as heroes and villains.

**Rule 19.8.1.1 — Side Character Power Must Be Consistent:** A Tier A side character who is established as a Core Formation cultivator does not suddenly perform at Divine Sea level without a canon-valid explanation (breakthrough, Part 28 variance). Narrative convenience cannot override power law.

**Rule 19.8.1.2 — Side Character Combat Serves Narrative Function:** Side character combat scenes must serve the character's narrative function. A Mentor's combat shows mastery the hero is not yet at. An Ally's combat shows a different approach, a different style, a different cost structure. A Rival's combat shows the gap that must be closed. Combat that exists only to show the side character is "impressive" without connecting to narrative function is a narration waste.

**Rule 19.8.1.3 — Side Character Deaths in Combat Are Governed Events:** When a Tier A or major Tier B side character dies in combat, this death is a Crucible Event candidate for the hero (Part 17 Area 7) and a major CSS event for all connected characters. The combat scene producing this death must be given sufficient weight for the death to carry its full narrative impact.

## Area 9 — Side Character Lifecycle Law

### 9.1 — Side Character Introduction Protocol

**Rule 19.9.1.1 — Side Characters Are Introduced With Sufficient Context:** When a Tier A side character is introduced, the narrative establishes: who they are (Part 16 classification), what they want (ideology summary), and why they are in this scene at this moment (faction/location context). This does not require an info-dump — it requires that the character's specificity is communicated through their behavior, speech, and context as the story introduces them (the Chapter Spec decides the pace of the introduction; the number of scenes is information only).

**Rule 19.9.1.2 — Side Characters Are Not Introduced and Forgotten:** A Tier A or Tier B side character who is introduced with narrative investment and then does not appear for extended stretches of the narrative without explanation is a canon continuity issue. If a significant side character disappears from the story, the narrative must account for their absence.

**Rule 19.9.1.3 — New Names Follow the Scene:** An Area introduces as many new named persons as the scene needs. An incidental speaker stays a role (the ferryman, the gate guard), type-governed under Part 16 Rule 16.1.1.2, until the story must call them again. No count, budget or quota applies.

### 9.2 — Side Character Evolution

**Rule 19.9.2.1 — Tier Transitions Are Permitted:** A character who begins as Tier C may become Tier A if the story organically develops them. A character who began as a named soldier and has, through narrative events, become a significant relationship to the hero, must be upgraded to the appropriate tier with retroactive governance applied (their ideology and psychology must be established consistently with their past behavior). The upgrade is made on the author's ruling (AskUserQuestion), prompted by the Section-close list of Part 38 Rule 3.7.2 or by the story; the number of Areas is a guide only, and nothing in that list forces a question.

**Rule 19.9.2.2 — Narrative Function Evolution Is Permitted:** A Tier A side character may shift primary narrative function. A character who begins as a Mentor may become a Consequence Witness when their teaching is done and their own story becomes about the costs of their past. A character who begins as an Ally may become a Mirror when their diverging choices force the hero to examine the difference between them.

### 9.3 — Side Character Removal Law

**Rule 19.9.3.1 — Side Character Removal Must Be Governed:** A Tier A side character is not removed from the story without governance. Removal may occur through: death (Part 27 CSS event, Part 26 PIS event for connected characters), exile or departure (CSS event for the side character, relational consequence for all connected), transformation (the character is fundamentally altered in a way that ends their previous narrative identity), or natural arc completion (their story thread resolves).

**Rule 19.9.3.2 — Unresolved Side Character Threads Are Prohibited:** A Tier A side character whose narrative thread is introduced but never resolved — a mentor who is established and then forgotten, an ally relationship that is built and then dropped without explanation — is an unresolved thread that violates canon continuity.

**Rule 19.9.3.3 — Side Character Deaths Must Have Appropriate Narrative Weight:** A Tier A side character cannot die off-screen or be killed in passing narrative. Their death requires a scene given sufficient weight to honor what was built in the character. The weight of the scene must match the depth of governance invested in the character.

## Area 10 — Side Character and the World

### 10.1 — Side Characters as World Architecture

Beyond their individual narrative functions, the ensemble of side characters in the Starward Saga collectively forms the **human architecture of the world**. Through the side character ensemble, the world is populated by individuals rather than types — by specific people whose specific existence in specific factions and specific relationships makes the world feel inhabited.

**Rule 19.10.1.1 — The Side Character Ensemble Must Span the World's Scale:** The side character ensemble should not all come from the same faction, the same cultivation tradition, or the same social class. The ensemble must represent the breadth of the world's systems — cultivation and technology, elite and civilian, sect-based and independent, high-CCT civilization and low-CCT civilization.

**Rule 19.10.1.2 — Side Characters as Civilizational Representatives:** Many Tier B and Tier C characters are representatives of larger systems — the village elder who represents an entire civilization's relationship to cultivation, the refugee who represents a civilization destroyed by the villain, the sect archivist who represents an institution's accumulated knowledge. These characters make abstract systems human and immediate.

### 10.2 — Side Characters and the Hero's World Expansion

Part 17 Growth Arc Phase 3 (The Widening World) is structurally supported by side characters — it is through new side characters encountered at higher scales that the hero's world expands. New Mentors at higher realms, new Allies from larger factions, new Rivals from civilizations the hero has never encountered — these are the mechanism by which Phase 3 is narratively delivered.

**Rule 19.10.2.1 — World Expansion Side Characters Must Be World-Specific:** A new side character encountered in Phase 3 must reflect the specific qualities and concerns of the higher-scale world the hero has entered. A Core Formation cultivator from a galaxy-spanning sect carries a different relationship to their cultivation, their world, and their ideology than a Core Formation cultivator from a village sect. The specificity of their world must be present in their character.

## Area 11 — Side Character Social and Civilizational Position

### 11.1 — Side Character Social Position

Every named side character's social position (Part 25 social axes: CC-Class, W-Class, L-Class, I-Class, A-Class, Ac-Class) is tracked in their Side Character Profile and governed by Part 16's social class classification. A side character's social position shapes their behavior, their available options, and their relationship to power.

**Rule 19.11.1.1 — Social Position Must Be Consistently Applied:** A Tier A side character who is established as an Outer Disciple of a major sect behaves within the constraints and possibilities of that position. They do not suddenly have access to sect resources reserved for Core Disciples without a governance event (promotion, breach of protocol, cultivation breakthrough).

### 11.2 — Side Character Faction Position

Every Tier A side character who is a member of a named faction (Part 22) has a faction position that is tracked in their Side Character Profile. Their faction loyalty, their rank, and their relationship to their faction's doctrine are governance constraints on their behavior.

**Rule 19.11.2.1 — Side Character Faction Loyalty Is Governed:** A Tier A side character's decision to act against their faction's interest, leave their faction, or betray their faction is an IE event (their ideology is producing action contrary to their institutional doctrine) and a CSS event (the consequence of that action propagates through both their relationship with the faction and their own consequence arc).

## Area 12 — Side Character Information Architecture

### 12.1 — What Side Characters Know and Don't Know

Every side character operates within the information constraints of their position — governed by Part 24 (Information and Secrecy Law). A Tier A side character who is a Core Disciple of a mid-tier sect knows certain things about the world, their faction, and their arc context; they do not know things outside their information access.

**Rule 19.12.1.1 — Side Character Information Must Be Position-Appropriate:** A side character cannot know things that their cultivation realm, faction position, and information access do not permit them to know. Information they should not have constitutes either a Part 24 breach event (they obtained it through intelligence means) or a canon violation.

**Rule 19.12.1.2 — Side Character Knowledge Gaps Are Narrative Assets:** What a side character does not know is as important as what they know. A Mentor who does not know about a threat the villain represents cannot prepare the hero for it — which is a valid and valuable structural gap. Side character knowledge gaps create story problems that the hero must solve independently.

## Area 13 — Side Character Cultivation and Technology

### 13.1 — Side Character Power

All side character cultivation power and technology tier follow the same Canon laws as heroes and villains. There are no governance exceptions for side characters.

**Rule 19.13.1.1 — Mentor Power Credibility:** A Mentor figure must have cultivation or technology capability that genuinely exceeds the hero's in the relevant domain at the time of mentorship. A Core Formation cultivator who is mentoring a Xiantian True God hero is credible in cultivation terms. A Core Formation cultivator who is presented as capable of teaching a Divine Sea hero without an explicit exception (special area of mastery, formation array expertise, etc.) is not credible.

**Rule 19.13.1.2 — Side Character Breakthroughs Are Canon Events:** When a Tier A side character achieves a cultivation breakthrough or technology tier advancement, this is a canon event. It must be the product of the same requirements as hero breakthroughs (Part 3) and must be narratively present — not simply noted in passing.

**Rule 19.13.1.3 — Side Character Power Calibration Shapes the World's Scale:** The cultivation and technology levels of the side character ensemble collectively calibrate the reader's understanding of the world's power scale. Mentors who are three realms above the hero establish what "experienced" looks like. Rivals who are one realm above establish what "achievement" the hero is striving toward. The ensemble's power levels must be internally consistent with the world's cultivation and technology governance.

## Area 14 — Side Character Narration Standards

### 14.1 — Eight Narration Standards for Side Character Content

**N19-1 — The Specificity Mandate:** Every Tier A and Tier B side character must be specifically rendered. They have a specific voice, specific concerns, specific behavioral patterns that reflect their ideology and psychology. A side character who could be replaced by any other character without changing anything about the scene is not specifically rendered. Specificity is mandatory.

**N19-2 — The Independent Agency Mandate:** Side characters act according to their own will, ideology, and psychological state. They do not act to serve the plot. When a Tier A side character's behavior seems coincidentally convenient for the hero, the narrative must justify this as an outcome of the character's genuine choices — not as a contrivance.

**N19-3 — The Relationship Depth Standard:** The relationship between a hero and a Tier A side character must be built through scenes that show the relationship developing — shared experience, shared cost, genuine exchange. A relationship that is stated as deep without being shown as deep is a narration failure. Relationships are proven in scenes, not summarized in backstory.

**N19-4 — The Cost Visibility Standard:** When a side character takes significant action, the cost must be visible. An ally who uses forbidden techniques to save the hero has paid a price — that price must be shown. A mentor who expends their cultivation reservoir to give the hero time carries the consequences of that expenditure. Cost visibility is the mechanism by which the world's consequence structure becomes human.

**N19-5 — The Diversity Mandate:** The side character ensemble must span the ideological, psychological, social, and civilizational range of the world. A hero surrounded only by characters who share their exact ideological profile is in a narrative echo chamber. The side character ensemble must collectively present the hero with genuine ideological diversity — characters whose perspective challenges, enriches, or complicates the hero's own.

**N19-6 — The Death Weight Standard:** When a Tier A or significant Tier B side character dies, the death must be given narrative weight proportional to what was built. Their death must cost the narrative something the reader valued. A well-governed death is a Crucible Event for the connected characters. A poorly governed death is a named character disposed of without narrative engagement.

**N19-7 — The Existence Beyond Function Standard:** Every Tier A side character exists beyond their narrative function. They have a life — concerns, relationships, history, desires, humor, or grief — that extends beyond what they are doing in any given scene with the hero. This excess of existence is what makes them feel like people rather than functions.

**N19-8 — The Consistency Mandate:** Side character behavior must be consistent with their established ideology, psychology, and capabilities at all times. A side character who acts against their ideology without a Part 20 IE event, behaves outside their psychological profile without a Part 26 PIS event, or demonstrates capabilities outside their power classification is a narration violation.
